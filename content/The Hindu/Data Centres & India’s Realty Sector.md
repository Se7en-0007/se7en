## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview</mark>

Data centres have emerged as the top-attracting segment within India's private equity (PE) real estate investments, overtaking the long-standing leader (office spaces) in terms of quarterly share of inflows. This shift reflects a structural change in investor preference toward digital infrastructure assets driven by rising demand for cloud computing, AI, and internet-based services.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Figures (April-June 2026)</mark>

- Total PE investment in India's real estate sector: $2 billion, up 25% year-on-year (YoY)
    
- Data centres' share of this quarterly inflow: 38% — the single largest segment, ahead of office-led investments
    
- H1 2026 (Jan-June) total PE inflows into real estate: $3.2 billion, a 33% YoY surge
    
- Foreign investment directed solely into data centres and hospitality in H1 2026: 69%
    
- Domestic capital share of H1 PE inflows: 52%; foreign capital share: 48% (of which 69% came from US and Canada)
    
- Office segment still captured 68% of domestic investments in H1, concentrated largely in Tier I cities (34% share of equity inflows)
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Sources of Data</mark>

- Savills India — report released in July 2026, primary source for April-June and H1 2026 figures
    
- CBRE — Real Estate Investment Market Outlook, released March 2026, covering 2025 data centre investment trends
    
- Colliers — report on 2025 alternative asset classes (mixed-use, retail, alternatives)
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Data Centre Sector Growth</mark>

- 2025 data centre investment: $0.48 billion, up 27% YoY
    
- New data centre capacity added in H1 2026: 258 MW IT, up 59.3% YoY from 162 MW IT in H1 2025
    
- India's total operational data centre stock: 1.8 GW IT (as of H1 2026)
    
- Projected new capacity addition for full-year 2026: around 500 MW (CBRE estimate)
    
- Projected growth in total operational stock for 2026: around 30% YoY
    

Note: "MW IT" refers to megawatt IT — the maximum electricity available to run server equipment inside a data centre (distinct from total facility power load).

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Data Centres Are Attracting Capital</mark>

- Rising demand from AI applications, cloud computing, streaming, digital payments, and e-commerce
    
- India offers cost advantages, a "location-agnostic" nature, and an expanding infrastructure ecosystem, making it attractive to neo-cloud service providers
    
- Hyperscalers (large cloud companies — Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud) are expanding footprints; Microsoft plans to open its biggest India data centre in 2026
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Challenges Identified</mark>

- Land availability for large-scale data centre development
    
- Power/electricity availability and grid capacity constraints
    
- Need for hyperscalers and operators to explore new geographic clusters beyond existing markets, with support from local governments

In 2025, mixed-use, retail, and alternative assets collectively drew about $1.5 billion, accounting for nearly 17% of total real estate investments that year — signaling a diversifying, maturing investor base moving beyond traditional office and retail assets toward digital and alternative real estate formats.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Relevant Terms</mark>

- PE (Private Equity): Investment funds that provide capital to companies/assets in exchange for equity, typically not through public markets
    
- Hyperscaler: A company operating massive, scalable data centre and cloud infrastructure (e.g., AWS, Azure, Google Cloud)
    
- MW IT: Megawatt of IT load — the actual power capacity available for servers, a key metric for data centre capacity
    
- Tier I cities: India's major metropolitan markets (e.g., Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai) that dominate real estate investment flows
