Critical minerals (lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite, copper and rare earth elements) have shifted from peripheral resource issues to the core of industrial and security strategy. They are foundational inputs for EVs, battery storage, renewable power, semiconductors, defence systems and advanced manufacturing. As decarbonisation and digitalisation accelerate globally, mineral security is becoming as strategically important as oil security once was.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Global Supply Concentration</mark>

- Top 3 refining countries' average market share (across copper, lithium, nickel, cobalt, graphite, REEs) rose from ~82% (2020) to 86% (2024).
    
- Supply bottlenecks are concentrated at a few nodes: Indonesia dominates nickel; China dominates cobalt, graphite and rare earths.
    
- China is the leading refiner in 19 of 20 strategic minerals, with ~70% average market share, making minerals a geopolitical lever rather than a purely commercial commodity.
    
- In 2024, China's processing share was: rare earths and graphite over 90%, cobalt ~75%, lithium chemicals ~70%.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Demand-Supply Outlook and Geopolitical Risk</mark>

- Copper: current project pipeline suggests a potential 30% supply shortfall by 2035.
    
- Lithium: adequately supplied near-term, but market expected to move into deficit by the 2030s.
    
- Rare earths: demand set to rise sharply due to wind power, electronics and magnet applications.
    
- China's 2025 rare earth export controls triggered concern across energy, automotive, defence, aerospace, AI and semiconductor sectors globally.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Global Policy Responses</mark>

|Region/Country|Response|
|---|---|
|European Union|Critical Raw Materials Act — 2030 targets: 10% domestic extraction, 40% processing, 25% recycling, max 65% dependence on single country|
|United States|Diversifying supply chains via international partnerships|
|India|National Critical Mineral Mission, overseas asset acquisition, bilateral frameworks|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Mineral Demand and Domestic Reserves</mark>

Under a net-zero pathway, India's cumulative demand for critical energy-transition minerals could reach ~169 million tonnes by 2070 — about 51% higher than under a current-policy pathway. Copper demand will rise with power systems expansion, while rare earth demand grows with wind energy and advanced manufacturing.

**India's domestic reserves:**

- Cobalt: 44.9 million tonnes
    
- Copper: 163.9 million tonnes
    
- Graphite: 211.6 million tonnes
    
- Nickel: 189 million tonnes
    
- Monazite deposits containing rare earth oxides also present
    

Recycling could eventually meet up to a quarter of copper and graphite demand by mid-century. However, having reserves has not translated into supply security — India remains import-dependent for lithium, cobalt and nickel, and vulnerable in graphite due to China-dominated processing.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Core Problem: Processing, Not Just Reserves</mark>

India's critical gap is midstream capacity (processing and refining) not raw reserves. Unlike the EU, US and Australia (building integrated chains via mandates, partnerships, processing investment), India has strong bulk-mineral experience but lacks high-purity production capability and still imports high-purity critical mineral products.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Structural Constraints</mark>

- Exploration remains relatively shallow.
    
- Regulatory clearances are time-consuming.
    
- Private sector participation is limited.
    
- Remote-region project economics are challenging (infrastructure, logistics costs).
    
- Processing bottlenecks: lack of high-purity input facilities; copper and graphite face smelting, purification and scale constraints.
    
- Recycling cannot substitute for primary supply near-term due to limited feedstock, collection systems and technology.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Policy Response (Since 2023)</mark>

**Key initiatives:**

- 30 critical minerals officially identified by the government.
    
- National Critical Mineral Mission (NCMM) launched to support the entire value chain.
    
- Targets: 1,200 domestic exploration projects by 2030-31; production of at least 15 critical minerals; acquisition of 50 overseas mining assets by Indian companies.
    
- Khanij Bidesh India Limited (KABIL) secured 15,703 hectares in Argentina's Catamarca province for lithium exploration.
    
- 2026-27 Budget proposed rare earth corridors in Odisha, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.
    
- India-U.S. Critical Minerals and Rare Earths Framework signed in May 2026 — adds a diplomatic lever.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward: Mission to Execution</mark>

- Make processing and refining a national industrial priority, backed by infrastructure and targeted incentives.
    
- Improve private capital access via better geological data, predictable approvals and risk-sharing mechanisms.
    
- Operationalise strategic mineral stockpiles.
    
- Accelerate applied R&D through industry partnerships.
    
- Scale domestic capabilities and diversify overseas supply through trusted partners/platforms.
    
- Build a comprehensive strategy: mineral-specific risk thresholds, recycling integration into supply planning, measurable milestones, coordinated institutional framework.
