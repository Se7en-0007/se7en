Mayo Clinic (US) and Bengaluru-based AI startup Sravathi AI Technology have co-developed a new cancer treatment under an intellectual property-sharing arrangement. The treatment targets a protein called GIPC1, which contributes to the growth of several cancer types, including pancreatic cancer. Findings were published in the journal Cell Reports, in a paper titled "AI-driven discovery and validation of a GIPC1 PDZ domain inhibitor for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma."

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Entities Involved</mark>

- Mayo Clinic: US-based medical research centre; had worked on the GIPC1 problem for 12 years before collaborating with Sravathi AI.
    
- Sravathi AI Technology: Bengaluru-based AI startup; founder is Gurram Kishan; CEO is Parag Tipnis.
    
- Debabrata Mukhopadhyay: Senior author of the study, cancer researcher at Mayo Clinic Florida.
    
- Publication: Cell Reports (scientific journal).
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What Is GIPC1 and Why It Matters</mark>

- GIPC1 is a protein that does not cause cancer on its own; its normal presence in the body is not abnormal.
    
- Problems arise when there is faulty expression or signalling of GIPC1 — cancer cells then exploit the "pathways" associated with it to grow excessively or invade other body parts.
    
- GIPC1 was long considered a mostly "undruggable" target due to structural challenges.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Link to Pancreatic Cancer (PDAC)</mark>

- Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is the most common and most aggressive type of exocrine pancreatic cancer.
    
- PDAC has a five-year survival rate under 13.3%, owing to late diagnosis, rapid progression, and therapeutic resistance.
    
- GIPC1 is overproduced in PDAC and drives tumour growth and chemotherapy resistance, but has remained hard to target because of its PDZ domain.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why GIPC1 Is Difficult to Target ("Undruggable")</mark>

- PDZ domain: A structural unit made up of amino acids (building blocks of proteins); found in signalling proteins across bacteria, yeast, plants, viruses, and animals.
    
- According to Sravathi CEO Parag Tipnis, the PDZ domain interacts with multiple proteins through broad, shallow surfaces, making it difficult for conventional small molecules to bind effectively.
    
- GIPC1 acts as a signalling hub — blocking one pathway may not be sufficient, since other active pathways can compensate.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The AI-Driven Drug Discovery Process</mark>

|Step|Description|
|---|---|
|Starting pool|Around 40,000 candidate molecules screened|
|Narrowing|Reduced progressively to 5 candidates|
|Synthesis & testing|2 of the 5 candidates were synthesised and tested|
|Generative AI role|Used to design the initial molecules|
|Predictive AI role|Used to assess properties like toxicity and absorption|
|Further refinement|Molecular modelling and quantum chemistry used to identify molecules most likely to bind GIPC1|
|Final output|Selective, specific small molecule inhibitor targeting the PDZ domain, named GIPCi|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Timeline</mark>

- 2023: Sravathi AI began molecule-discovery work; completed initial research in about five months.
    
- Post-discovery: Mayo Clinic continued research, including animal trials.
    
- Future: If suitable for human use, the treatment could become available in approximately three years.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Significance and Caveats</mark>

- This marks a case of AI (generative + predictive models, molecular modelling, quantum chemistry) enabling drug discovery against a target previously deemed undruggable for over a decade.
    
- Debabrata Mukhopadhyay noted the findings are preclinical, and provide "a strong foundation for the next phase of research" — meaning human trials and further validation are still required before clinical use.
    
- The case is notable as an India-US cross-border AI-pharma collaboration, relevant to India's growing role in AI-driven biotech innovation.
