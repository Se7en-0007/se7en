## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context and Vision</mark>

India's nuclear programme aims for technological self-reliance, leveraging vast thorium reserves to overcome historical fuel embargoes. The goal is to achieve 100 GW of nuclear power capacity by 2047, aligned with the Viksit Bharat vision and Aatmanirbhar Bharat.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Nuclear Energy Matters</mark>

- Provides round-the-clock, low-carbon baseload electricity, complementing renewables and ensuring grid stability
- Reduces dependence on imported fossil fuels
- Supports India's climate commitments
- Baseload electricity = minimum uninterrupted power needed to keep the grid operational; critical for hospitals, defence, communication networks, and industries

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Policy Initiatives</mark>

- **Nuclear Energy Mission (2025–26):** Announced in Union Budget 2025–26; ₹20,000 crore allocated for research, design, development and deployment of indigenous Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)
- **SHANTI Act, 2025:** Accelerates capacity expansion, boosts domestic manufacturing, promotes innovation, enables greater private sector participation

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Nuclear Fuel Basics</mark>

- **Fissile fuel:** Directly sustains chain reaction (e.g., U-235, Pu-239)
- **Fertile fuel:** Converted into fissile fuel inside reactor (e.g., Th-232)
- Common fuels: Natural Uranium, U-235, Low-Enriched Uranium (LEU), Pu-239, Mixed Oxide (MOX) fuel
- Most countries (US, France, China, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Russia) use LEU in Light Water Reactors
- India uses Natural Uranium in PHWRs (no enrichment needed) and MOX fuel in the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR)
- India's uranium reserves are low-grade → supplemented via imports
- India has abundant thorium (Th-232) reserves — found in coastal sands of **Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal, Jharkhand**
- Inside a reactor, Th-232 absorbs a neutron → converts to fissile **Uranium-233**

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Three-Stage Nuclear Power Programme</mark>

Proposed by **Dr. Homi J. Bhabha in 1954** to maximise use of indigenous resources for long-term energy security.

|Stage|Reactor Type|Fuel Used|Output|
|---|---|---|---|
|Stage 1|Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs)|Natural Uranium|Electricity + spent fuel reprocessed to recover plutonium|
|Stage 2|Fast Breeder Reactors (FBRs)|Plutonium (from Stage 1)|Electricity + breeds more fissile material + produces U-233 from thorium|
|Stage 3|Thorium-based reactors|Uranium-233|Harnesses India's abundant thorium reserves|

- **April 2026 milestone:** PFBR at Kalpakkam achieved "first criticality," marking the start of Stage 2
- A breeder reactor produces more fissile material/fuel than it consumes while generating electricity
- **IGCAR (Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research)** led design, development, testing, safety assessment, commissioning and indigenisation of PFBR — achieved nearly 90% domestic manufacturing of reactor equipment/systems

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Nuclear Reaction: Fission vs Fusion</mark>

- **Nuclear fission:** Neutron strikes uranium/plutonium nucleus → splits into smaller atoms → releases heat + more neutrons → sustains chain reaction; heat converts water to steam → drives turbine → generates electricity. Used in nuclear power plants.
- **Nuclear fusion:** Combines two light atoms into a heavier one; powers the Sun and stars; produces far more energy than fission but is still under development for commercial power generation.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Types of Nuclear Reactors in India</mark>

- **PHWRs:** Primary reactor type, uses natural uranium
- **BWRs (Boiling Water Reactors)** and **PWRs (Pressurised Water Reactors):** Also operated
- **FBRs (Fast Breeder Reactors):** Being advanced to use plutonium and transition toward thorium-based reactors
- **SMRs (Small Modular Reactors):** Next-generation technology under development

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)</mark>

- Generate up to 300 MWe via fission
- Compact, modular design → enables factory-based manufacturing, faster construction, better quality, phased deployment
- Key Indian SMR projects:
    
    - **BSMR-200** (220 MWe) — jointly designed by **BARC** and **NPCIL**
    - **SMR-55** (55 MWe)
    - **High-Temperature Gas-Cooled Reactor** — for hydrogen production
- Government target: at least **5 indigenous SMRs operational by 2033**

|Category|Capacity|Application|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Nuclear Waste Management</mark>

- Nuclear waste = radioactive material from plant operation (spent fuel, protective clothing, filters, tools, equipment)
- India follows a **closed nuclear fuel cycle**: spent fuel is reprocessed to recover valuable materials for reuse; remaining high-level waste is immobilised and safely stored
- **Vitrification technology:** India is among few countries possessing this — converts high-level radioactive waste into stable glass form for safer long-term storage, transport, and disposal
