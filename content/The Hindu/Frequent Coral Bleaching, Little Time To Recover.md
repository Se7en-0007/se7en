- **Coral reefs worldwide are experiencing increasingly frequent bleaching events**, leaving insufficient time to recover before the next period of severe heat stress.
- The **seventh Status of Coral Reefs of the World report** found that major disturbances have occurred **every four to six years in many regions since 2010**, compared with **roughly once a decade in the past**.
- **Global hard coral cover has fallen significantly**, raising the risk of **sustained and potentially irreversible decline**.

## Status Of Coral Reefs Of The World

- The assessment drew on **21.1 million observations** from **87,299 surveys** across **36,886 reef sites** between **1980 and 2024**.
- It examined long-term changes in **hard coral** and **macroalgal cover** across the world’s **shallow, warm-water coral reefs**.
- It included detailed assessments across **all 10 regions** covered by the **Global Coral Reef Monitoring Network**.
- **Average global hard coral cover declined by an estimated 9.5 per cent**, with a range of **4 to 14.5 per cent**, relative to the **1980-2009 reference period**.
- The decline has shown a **pronounced stepwise pattern since 2010**.
- This pattern closely followed the increasing **frequency, intensity and geographical extent of marine heatwaves** driven by **human-caused climate change**.
- These conditions have contributed to **four global coral bleaching events since 1998**.

## Recovery Windows Shrink

- Without significant reductions in **greenhouse gas emissions**, global warming would exceed **2 degrees Celsius**, while marine heatwaves would persist and intensify, causing further reef decline.
- **Marine heatwaves**, compounded by **local human and environmental pressures**, have pushed coral reefs towards **sustained global decline**.
- As recovery time shortens, reef degradation is likely to continue unless there is **urgent action to reduce emissions and manage local pressures**.
- **2023 and 2024** were the **two warmest years on record** at the time.
- **2024** was the first calendar year in which the **global average temperature exceeded 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels**.
- Coral reefs are particularly vulnerable to heat stress during **summer marine heatwaves**.
- **Sea-surface temperatures over coral reefs worldwide rose by 0.81°C between 1985 and 2025**.
- Marine heatwaves have become the **single strongest driver of coral-cover decline worldwide**, with their frequency and severity amplified by **global climate change**.

## Degree Heating Weeks

- In **2024**, **87 per cent of reefs** experienced **unprecedented heat stress**.
- More than **40 per cent of reefs** experienced heat stress associated with a **risk of coral mortality**.
- Accumulated heat stress is measured using **degree heating weeks (DHW)**.
- **DHW of 4°C-weeks or more** is associated with **significant bleaching**.
- **DHW of 8°C-weeks or more** can cause **widespread bleaching and mortality**.
- More than **one-quarter of the world’s reef areas** experienced heat stress of at least **4°C-weeks on four or more occasions between 2019 and 2025**.
- This indicates that **near-annual bleaching stress** has become a reality across a substantial proportion of the world’s coral reefs.

## Fourth Bleaching Event Takes Toll

- The effects of the **fourth global mass bleaching event** are likely to be **conservative estimates**, because coral-cover data extended only to **2024** and did not capture the full impact through **2025**.
- Global hard coral cover was estimated to have fallen by **8.9 per cent between 2023 and 2024**, the **largest single-year relative decline recorded to date**.
- In **2024**, **42.6 per cent** of the world’s coral reefs were exposed to heat stress severe enough to risk **reef-wide bleaching and mortality** - **DHW of 8°C-weeks or more**.
- In **2025**, the corresponding share was **27.4 per cent**.
- Global average hard coral cover is likely to continue declining from **2025 onwards**, suggesting that the fourth global bleaching event is on track to be the **most severe event recorded to date**.
- The fourth global mass bleaching event:
    
    - **Began in 2023**.
    - Reached **unprecedented heat-stress levels in 2024**.
    - Was thought likely to have **ended in 2025**.
    - Was described as the **fastest and most widespread global bleaching event on record**.
- Since **1998**, each of the four major global bleaching events has been associated with an estimated **6.5 per cent to 9.9 per cent relative loss** in average global hard coral cover.
- The periods when the largest proportions of reefs experienced severe heat stress were **1998-99, 2010-11, 2016-17 and 2023-24**.

## Caribbean Reefs Hit Hard

- Effects varied significantly between **regions** and **individual reefs**.
- In the **US Virgin Islands**, about **53 per cent of corals** bleached across **33 surveyed reefs**, while coral cover declined by **47 per cent**.
- In the **Dominican Republic**, about **70 per cent of reefs** were left with **less than 5 per cent coral cover**.
- In **Belize**, heat stress caused catastrophic impacts:
    
    - **More than 90 per cent of wild and restored acroporids died**.
    - Long-monitored sites collapsed to **less than 5 per cent coral cover**.
- Across the **Mesoamerican Region**, about **30 per cent of colonies died**.
- Research at **Puerto Morelos, Mexico**, found that the **2023 marine heatwave** eventually resulted in **100 per cent mortality of Acropora palmata**, an important reef-building coral, in the area studied.
- **Acropora** were most affected, with wild and restored populations suffering **rapid tissue loss** and **near-complete collapse** in several countries.

## ROPME Sea Area And Caribbean

- In the **ROPME Sea Area**, reefs lost about **half of their hard coral cover between 2020 and 2024** compared with the **1980-2009 reference period**.
- Hard coral cover in the **Caribbean** had begun declining in the **1980s**, and impaired reef functioning in the region has been extensively documented.
- In **2024**, average hard coral cover stood at:
    
    - **About 15 per cent** in the **ROPME Sea Area**.
    - **7.1 per cent** in the **Caribbean**.
- These low levels indicate that reef functioning could already have been compromised and that future recovery could be constrained by **smaller and increasingly isolated coral populations**.
- **Australia and the Pacific** also recorded moderate declines during the reporting period, particularly after the **third and fourth global bleaching events**, though losses were less severe than in the Caribbean and ROPME Sea Area.

## Future Coral-Cover Trends

- Simulations showed that **shorter recovery periods between major disturbances** would lead to continued declines in **global coral cover**.
- When the frequency of major disturbances exceeds historical patterns, the decline in coral cover is expected to be **more severe than currently documented**.
