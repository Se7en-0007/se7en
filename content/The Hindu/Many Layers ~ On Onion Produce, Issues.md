- Since the **1960s**, India has followed a **balancing act** between keeping food prices **affordably low for consumers** and ensuring **remunerative prices for farmers**.
- The State’s interventions have largely focused on managing price volatility in the **short term**.

## Onion Exports

- The government **banned onion exports** from **December 2023 to May 2024**.
- It then imposed a **minimum export price of $550 per tonne** and a **40% export duty**.
- The export duty was reduced to **20% in September 2024** and finally **abolished in April 2025**.
- **Erratic weather** and the absence of **long-term relief** have made this balancing act increasingly awkward.
- Farmers argue that the government frequently changes its position after they have made important **production-related decisions** based on expected prices.

## Rabi Harvest

- During the **bounteous rabi harvest**, onion farmers in **Maharashtra**, the country’s **principal supplier**, argued that the Centre’s procurement price of **₹12.35 per kg** would not cover cultivation costs.
- The Centre subsequently raised the procurement price to as much as **₹26.45 per kg**.
- However, many farmers could not obtain the higher value because intervention came after prices had already collapsed.
- Some farmers had earlier been forced to sell onions at **₹1 per kg** owing to **low quality**, **lack of storage**, and other factors.
- This exposed the limits of interventions that do not reach **all farmers** or **all grades of produce**.

## Proactive Measures

- Although the government has referred to possible **price manipulation**, the dominant problem is that it continues to **react** rather than take **proactive measures**.
- Required measures include:
- **Improving storage options**
- Maintaining a **less erratic trade policy**
- Moving stocks more efficiently **between regions**
- Protecting farmers from **price shocks**

## Pressures This Year

- **Abnormal rainfall at the time of harvest** and a **5%-7% decline in Maharashtra’s kharif crop** highlighted the flaw in the present approach.
- Other pressures include the known difficulties of **storing onions** and maintaining **large buffers**.

## Tamil Nadu’s Targeted Subsidy

- Tamil Nadu plans to buy **1,000 tonnes of onions** and distribute **1 kg per ration card at ₹35**.
- The measure will place pressure on the Centre to maintain a **steady supply**.
- It is designed to discourage **hoarding** while allowing **private retail prices** to cool down.

## Dry-Grain PDS Network

- Tamil Nadu must guard against the risks of distributing onions through a **dry-grain PDS network**.
- The economic case for the measure could collapse if **post-harvest losses** exceed **10%-15%**, since onions are more susceptible to such losses than **wheat or rice**.
- The case could also weaken if the State must continue the subsidy for a prolonged period.
- If other States adopt similar measures, the **Central buffer** could be quickly exhausted.
- This risk is greater because this year’s onion **storage losses** are around **30%**.
