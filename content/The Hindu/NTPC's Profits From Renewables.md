NTPC, India's largest state-owned power producer, has seen profits grow faster than revenues for three consecutive years - a trend rooted in how thermal power plants are compensated for supporting renewable energy integration.

## Core Puzzle

- NTPC's income fell 0.56% to Rs 1,89,798.56 crore in 2025-26 (from previous year), yet Profit After Tax (PAT) rose 15% YoY to Rs 27,545.76 crore.
- 2023-24: total income grew 1.79%, but profit rose 24.6%.
- 2024-25: income rose 5.35%, but profit grew 12.29%.
- This mismatch (profit growth outpacing revenue growth) has held in each of the last three years except 2022-23, coinciding with India's renewable capacity expansion.

## Why Profits Outpace Revenue

- NTPC CMD Gurdeep Singh attributed this to fixed charges from thermal plants, which boost profitability even when generation falls.
- Fixed charges are payments to thermal generators for keeping plants available to generate power, regardless of actual output.
- Total revenue for thermal generators = Fixed charges + Energy charges (energy charges include fuel costs).
- Company logic: "We generate as per the requirement of the grid. The profit comes from fixed charges."

## Two-Part Tariff Structure

- Long-term Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) use a two-part tariff:
- Fixed capacity charges: paid irrespective of electricity drawn by DISCOMs.
- Variable energy charges: linked to actual consumption.
- As renewable output rises (especially during the day), thermal plants reduce generation or run at lower utilisation, but continue collecting fixed charges as long as capacity stays available to the grid.
- Result: rising renewable share cuts thermal generation without cutting payments to thermal generators.

## Impact on DISCOMs

- DISCOMs (distribution companies) face a growing cost challenge: they must pay capacity charges for thermal capacity regardless of usage.
- Renewable growth does not eliminate the cost of maintaining thermal capacity as backup for low-renewable periods.
- A CEA report flagged a widening gap between DISCOMs' fixed costs and the fixed charges they recover from consumers.
- DISCOM fixed costs include: capacity payments to thermal generators, transmission costs, employee salaries, and infrastructure maintenance - all payable on a lumpsum basis irrespective of energy sales.

### Key DISCOM Financial Data

|Parameter|Share of Total|
|---|---|
|Fixed costs as share of Annual Revenue Requirement (ARR)|38% to 56%|
|Fixed charges recovered from consumers (share of revenue)|Only 9% to 20%|

- This mismatch means DISCOMs recover most fixed costs through variable energy charges tied to consumption, exposing them to financial risk when demand fluctuates even though liabilities remain constant.
- CEA has recommended redesigning retail electricity tariffs to address this structural imbalance.

## Coal Flexibility: The Technical Fix

- Rising renewable capacity requires coal plants to reduce generation when renewable output is high and ramp up quickly when it falls.
- Key requirement: lowering the Minimum Technical Load (MTL) - the lowest stable generation level at which a thermal unit can run without shutting down.
- Lower MTL allows coal plants to back down further during high-renewable periods while staying grid-available.
- CEA roadmap: enable coal plants to operate at 40% MTL by 2030 (phased approach).
- Industry resistance: operating below 55% load risks increased thermal and mechanical stress on turbines and other equipment.
