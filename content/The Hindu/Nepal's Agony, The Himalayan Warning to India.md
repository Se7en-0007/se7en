## The Himalaya as "One Living Rock"

- The Himalaya are **not a series of silos** - they span **Afghanistan Hindu Kush Himalaya, Pakistan Himalaya, Indian Himalaya, Nepal Himalaya, the Himalaya in Tibet-China, and Bhutan**.
- Borrowing a term used by **Vadodara-based painter Gulam Mohammed Sheikh** for the **Elephanta Caves**, the Himalaya are described as **"one living rock"** - silently, broodingly **tense with seismic voltages, glacial stirrings and geological turbulence**.
- The Himalaya "spell their uncoded thought in swipes no one can foretell" - **when, where, and with what ferocity is unknown**.
- Potential disasters include: **rivers plucked from their courses**, **rockfalls and landslides** depositing mud and silt, **turning human habitations into rubble and fertile farms into slime** - none of which can be precisely envisioned in advance.
- Historical precedents cited: the **Nepal-Bihar earthquake of 1934** and the **Assam temblor of 1950**, both remembered as nightmares by those who heard accounts from parents and grandparents.

## The Question of Preparedness

- **Seismologists have specifically warned India** of an **impending earthquake of humungous scale** with potential for **cataclysm across a great swathe of northern India**, which lies under the **quake-shadow of the Himalaya**.
- India's **disaster management agencies** claim to have taken steps to be alert to this risk.
- Key question raised after **26/8** (referring to the Nepal disaster): should what happened in **upper Nepal** have happened in **lower Nepal**, and even lower, and would India's "preparedness" have sufficed to **save riverside habitations from instant death**?
- Critical question on **global warming-triggered GLOFs (Glacial Lake Outburst Floods)**: whether authorities have warned people living in **potential GLOF-affected areas in India** about the danger and advised them how to face it.
- India is described as being in **"seismic, glacial, climatological and geological innocence"** - referring to the people of India, even though **earth scientists know the risks**, but their knowledge (**jnana**) remains **"clasped tight in their acharya-mushti-s"** (knowledge kept in the teacher's fist).
- The full cause of the Nepal disaster on **"that stricken Wednesday"** is yet to be appraised - unclear whether an **earthquake caused the GLOF**, or whether the **GLOF caused the earth to quake and waterbodies to swell**.

## Whatever Happened, India Must Learn - Eight Lessons

**First** - The Himalaya, held sacred since memory has served, **gestate "sure-shot" danger**.

**Second** - These dangers **can no longer be left to be unravelled in seminars and technical papers**.

**Third** - They must be made known **on a war footing** to the clear potential victims (the people).

**Fourth** - Not just increasing knowledge, but making people **part of a new safeguarding**.

**Fifth** - Reference to **scholar-diplomat Shyam Saran's** two articles: **"China's hydel project bodes ill for India"** (The Tribune, **July 14, 2026**) and **"From Nepal to Uttarakhand and China, the mountains are warning us"** (The Indian Express, **August 28, 2026**):

- These explain how **China's construction of Medog**, described as the **world's largest hydropower project**, at the **great bend of the Brahmaputra river**, just across the border and very close to the **Paizhen Fault**, could - should there be a major earthquake in the project's vicinity - cause a **massive discharge of floodwaters and debris into India's northeast**.
- India's initiatives for **mature civility on the borders with China** must now include **frankness about the ferocious danger that Medog poses**.

**Sixth** - An **urgent review of India's own built hazards** such as **dams and reactors in high risk zones**, followed by the **dismantling of the real "baddies"**, can no longer be postponed.

**Seventh** - A **calibrated but time-bound decongestion of human agglomerations in high-risk zones**.

**Eighth** - If **surgical strikes** can be used with precision against an external enemy, their **civilian equivalent should be deployed** to disable that which, though intended for well-being, has become a danger.

## Progress and Danger

- **Marjorie Sykes**, a British devotee of India, is placed in the tradition of **C.F. Andrews, Verrier Elwin, and J.B.S. Haldane**.
- She is quoted as having said with passion: **"Dams are a damnation."**
- Qualification: **not all dams are damnations**, but **some, depending on their location, are**.
- Similarly, **some reactors can become rakshasas**, and **some commercial behemoths** can become **predatory**.
- Concluding thought: **a big and great nation can learn from the experience of a small neighbour**, especially when **both are perched on the same living rock that threatens to turn into a mass grave**.
