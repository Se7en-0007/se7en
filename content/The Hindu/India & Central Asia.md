## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context of The Article</mark>

PM Modi is undertaking a four-day visit to Central Asia, starting with a state visit to Uzbekistan followed by the SCO Heads of State meeting in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan. The author, Pankaj Saran (former Deputy NSA and Ambassador), frames this as an opportunity for India to deepen strategic presence, expand economic ties, and strengthen diplomatic influence in the region.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Central Asia: Regional Profile</mark>

- Landlocked region situated between two major powers: Russia and China.
- Post-1991 generation now dominates governance, focused on finding new internal equilibrium after Soviet collapse.
- Islam is the dominant religion but does not define political identity or serve as a political project.
- Region maintains a strong, uncompromising stance against radicalisation and terrorism.
- Key security reminders include the 2024 Crocus City Hall terror attack in Moscow, China's fears of radicalisation spreading to its border regions, and instability exported from Afghanistan.
- Marked by diversity in ethnicities, languages, geography and resource endowments, shaping distinct domestic and foreign policies (e.g., Turkmenistan's policy of strict neutrality).
- Legacy disputes persist over borders, water resources, and overlapping ethnic groups among regional states.
- External influencers: Türkiye and Iran hold dominant cultural/political influence; US and EU are increasing engagement as Russia's grip weakens and China's grows.

|Year|Development|
|---|---|

|2026|Current visit: Uzbekistan state visit + SCO Summit in Bishkek|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Civilisational Connect</mark>

- Central Asia is described as part of India's extended neighbourhood, alongside South Asia, West Asia, East Asia, and the Indian Ocean Region.
- Lack of overland connectivity does not diminish deep civilisational links in culture, religion, language, and architecture.
- Geographically, India and Tajikistan are separated only by the narrow Wakhan Corridor located in Afghanistan.
- India is described as part of the broader Eurasian landmass continuum.
- Pakistan is characterized as a "historically new piece" attempting to claim historical/religious lineage with the region to isolate India — a tactic termed likely to fail.
- Central Asian nations reportedly view India's plural, moderate, democratic model with admiration rather than apprehension.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Uzbekistan Matters</mark>

- Chosen for the state visit due to steadily improving relations under President Mirziyoyev since 2016.
- Uzbekistan is described as among the most dynamic Central Asian states, most advanced in diversifying its foreign policy.
- Strong people-to-people empathy noted, especially around Tashkent, Samarkand, and Bukhara.
- Strategic value: Uzbekistan's firm anti-terrorism stance, deep knowledge of Afghanistan, and pragmatic engagement with the Taliban.
- India-Uzbekistan discussions include alternative rail/road connectivity routes via Iran and Afghanistan, needing review given recent regional developments.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India and the SCO</mark>

- SCO evolved from the 1996 "Shanghai Five" to a body with 10 members, 15 dialogue partners, and 2 observers (marking its 25th anniversary).
- Modi has attended SCO Heads of State summits regularly, except in 2024.
- India's SCO participation is geography-driven, not ideology-driven.
- India navigates a challenging China-Pakistan dyad within SCO while proposing cooperation in economic, technological, and civilisational domains.
- At the Tianjin summit (last year), Modi articulated a vision centered on security, connectivity, and opportunity.
- For China-dependent states like Kyrgyzstan, the SCO and India's role within it are framed as offering a "valuable geopolitical lifeline."

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Broader Strategic Significance</mark>

- The visit follows Modi's recent trips to the Indian Ocean Region, together illustrating India's simultaneous engagement with both maritime and continental neighbourhoods.
- The author frames this dual engagement as reflecting India's broader geostrategic imperatives and opportunities.
- Suggested areas for India to consolidate its role: economic, trade, defence, natural resources, technology, plus softer domains — culture, education, health, and tourism.
