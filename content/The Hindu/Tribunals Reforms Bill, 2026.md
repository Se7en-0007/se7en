Parliament has passed the Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026, introducing sweeping changes to how tribunals are administered across the country, marking the latest chapter in a decade-long tussle between the Centre and the Supreme Court over judicial independence.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What Are Tribunals</mark>

Tribunals are quasi-judicial bodies set up to provide swift, specialised resolution of disputes and to reduce the caseload of regular courts. Examples include the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), which alone had 24 vacancies against a sanctioned strength of 32, reflecting the chronic staffing crisis tribunals faced.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background: A Decade of Conflict</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Timeline of the Centre-SC Tussle</mark>

|Year|Event|
|---|---|
|2017|Finance Act empowers Centre to frame rules for tribunals|
|2019|Rojer Mathew case: Constitution bench strikes down 2017 rules for undermining judicial independence|
|2020|Centre notifies new rules; SC recommends modifications including a five-year member tenure|
|April 2021|Centre promulgates an Ordinance fixing tenure at four years, minimum age 50, and a two-name panel system for appointments|
|Mid-2021|SC strikes down these provisions as arbitrary|
|August 2021|Parliament passes Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021 — re-enacting the very provisions the Court had just declared unconstitutional|
|November 2025|Two-judge SC Bench strikes down 2021 Act provisions, calling the re-enactment an "impermissible legislative override"|
|2026|Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026 passed, repealing the 2021 Act|

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Supreme Court's November 2025 Ruling</mark>

- Called the government's re-enactment an "impermissible legislative override" of earlier judgments
    
- Criticised the persistent refusal to follow SC pronouncements, noting the legislature kept "reopening the same constitutional debates"
    
- Held that a four-year tenure is "anti-merit" and increases executive interference, jeopardising judicial independence
    
- Ruled that demanding a panel of two names for appointment gave the executive undue discretion
    
- Reiterated the need for a National Tribunals Commission and directed the Centre to set one up within four months
    
- Protected certain existing appointments/selections during the transition to the new framework
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Provisions of the 2026 Bill</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">National Tribunals Commission</mark>

- Core reform: establishes an independent National Tribunals Commission to sever tribunals' dependence on parent ministries for administrative and financial needs
    
- Headed by a former Supreme Court judge or former High Court Chief Justice
    
- Composition: two judicial members plus two technical members
    
- Technical members must have at least 25 years' experience in fields such as public administration, finance, or law
    
- Functions: conducts selection process for vacancies, reviews tribunal performance, oversees inquiries into complaints against members, maintains a National Tribunals Data Grid
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Government's Retained Powers (Limits on Independence)</mark>

- Union government appoints the Commission's chairperson and members (chair and judicial members appointed after consulting the Chief Justice of India)
    
- Centre appoints the Commission's secretary
    
- Centre provides grants/funding to the Commission
    
- Centre retains rule-making powers over qualifications and service conditions of tribunal members
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Tenure and Age Limits</mark>

- Chairpersons and members will hold office for five years, aligning with the SC's mandate
    
- Maximum age: 70 years for chairpersons, 67 years for members
    
- Reappointment is permitted
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Selection Process Reform</mark>

- Search-cum-selection committee headed by a judicial member of the Commission
    
- Committee also includes a retired High Court judge, a government secretary, a technical member, and experts
    
- Committee recommends one candidate per vacancy, with one additional name kept on a waiting list (departure from the 2021 framework's two-name panel)
    
- Government must make the appointment within three months of receiving the recommendation
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Repeal and Transition</mark>

- The 2026 Bill repeals the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021
    
- Contains detailed transitional provisions protecting certain existing appointments
    
- Allows some selection processes begun under the old framework to continue
