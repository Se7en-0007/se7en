## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background</mark>

The Bay of Bengal is one of the world's most disaster-prone regions but lacks coordinated mechanisms to respond to natural disasters. Non-traditional threats (climate-induced disasters, illegal fishing, cyber vulnerabilities, and supply chain disruptions) require regional cooperation that goes beyond bilateral aid or unilateral maritime power. The core problem is a gap between growing capacity and shared norms, which necessitates a stronger maritime security architecture.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What is SHANTI</mark>

- Full form: Securing Holistic Advancement through Norms, Trust and Integrity
    
- Launched by: External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar on July 13, 2026
    
- Occasion: Launch of India's candidature for the UN Security Council (UNSC) for the 2028-29 term
    
- Purpose: Provides a normative framework for shared maritime spaces
    
- Pilot region: Bay of Bengal, chosen as the ideal testing ground before expanding to the wider Indo-Pacific
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Evolution of India's Maritime Vision</mark>

|Framework|Year|Core Idea|
|---|---|---|
|SAGAR (Security and Growth for All in the Region)|2015|Vision of "equity in development"; a statement of intent|
|MAHASAGAR (Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions)|2025|Expanded scope recognizing interconnectedness of security across the wider Indo-Pacific and Global South; a statement of reach|
|SHANTI|2026|Provides the grammar/method of maritime security — norms, trust, integrity — to translate vision into action; a statement of method|

Key shifts SHANTI represents:

- From competition to demand-driven cooperation
    
- From influence to non-prescriptive institution-building
    
- From episodic crisis management to long-term resilience
    
- Overall goal: position India as the "preferred security partner" and "first responder"
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why the Bay of Bengal Matters</mark>

Historically, the Bay connected South and Southeast Asia through trade, culture, religion, and exchange of ideas long before the term "Indo-Pacific" existed. Today it plays several strategic roles:

- Links India's Act East policy with ASEAN
    
- Provides access to the strategically vital Malacca Strait
    
- Connects the eastern Indian Ocean to major global trade and energy routes
    
- Intersection point of economic connectivity, maritime governance, and strategic rivalry (especially with China, given its "Malacca dilemma" — heavy dependence on the strait, driving Chinese port and infrastructure expansion in the region)
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why the Bay is "Governable"</mark>

As a single interconnected maritime system, the Bay of Bengal's cyclones, fish stocks, shipping lanes, and pollution cross national boundaries by default. This means littoral states share remarkably similar challenges:

- Disaster risk reduction and response
    
- Coastal erosion
    
- Fisheries management
    
- Port resilience
    
- Undersea cable protection
    
- Climate adaptation
    

Unlike the western Indian Ocean (marked by active conflict, fragile economies, and contested spaces), the Bay of Bengal's geography naturally encourages cooperation. The region's problem is not a lack of institutions but institutional fragmentation — SHANTI aims to provide a common framework to align existing mechanisms.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recent Institutional Developments (BIMSTEC)</mark>

- July 2026: BIMSTEC National Security Advisers' meeting held in New Delhi
    
- Outcome: Member-states adopted common principles for maritime law enforcement and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief (HADR)
    
- Upcoming: First joint BIMSTEC maritime security exercise scheduled for November 2026 in the Bay
    
- Under discussion: A white shipping information-sharing agreement
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Envisioned Role</mark>

India's geography gives it a natural convening role in the Bay of Bengal region, but the article emphasizes this centrality should rest on regional stewardship — not dominance — built through trust-based, rules-based cooperation among states facing shared challenges and opportunities.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Larger Significance of SHANTI</mark>

The article frames SHANTI's lasting contribution not as another acronym in India's maritime lexicon, but as a redefinition of security along three axes:

- From deterrence to resilience
    
- From rivalry to shared responsibility
    
- From crisis management to governance of the shared maritime commons
