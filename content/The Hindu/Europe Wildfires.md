## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview of the Crisis</mark>

Wildfires have been raging across Europe in 2026, with Greece experiencing some of the most intense fires currently. Last week alone, the fires triggered France's largest peace-time evacuation, while Spain saw 172,000 hectares burnt. Major blazes have also hit Portugal, Italy, Cyprus, and Croatia, producing apocalyptic scenes of ash-covered forests, orange skies over tourist towns, and smoke plumes visible from space.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Natural Role of Wildfires</mark>

- Wildfires naturally clear dead leaves, branches, and dense undergrowth from forest floors.
    
- This process unlocks soil nutrients and enables new plant growth.
    
- Sparks can be natural (lightning) or human-activity related.
    
- Problems arise when fires spread uncontrollably due to factors like climate whiplash.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Concept 1: Climate Whiplash</mark>

Climate whiplash refers to rapid swings between weather extremes — essentially unusually wet periods followed by intense drying.

- An unusually wet period earlier in the year in western Europe encouraged explosive growth of grasses, shrubs, and undergrowth.
    
- Successive heatwaves and drought then dried out this vegetation, creating abundant fuel.
    
- This wet-then-dry sequence is becoming more common as climate change intensifies both ends of the weather spectrum.
    
- The same pattern fuelled last year's California wildfires, showing this is a global phenomenon, not just European.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Concept 2: Pyrocumulonimbus Clouds (PyroCb / "Fire Clouds")</mark>

This is one of the most striking and dangerous features of the 2026 European fires.

**Formation mechanism:**

- Large wildfires release enormous heat.
    
- Hot air, smoke, ash, and moisture from burning vegetation rise rapidly.
    
- As this air climbs, it expands and cools.
    
- Moisture condenses around smoke particles, forming a towering cloud.
    
- If the fire is intense enough, the cloud develops into a full thunderstorm, reaching 10-15 km into the atmosphere, sometimes penetrating the stratosphere.
    

**Dangerous effects:**

- Collisions between ice crystals, water droplets, and ash inside the cloud generate electrical charges, producing lightning.
    
- This lightning can ignite entirely new fires kilometres away from the original blaze.
    
- The storms generate powerful, erratic winds that can suddenly change a fire's direction or speed, trapping firefighters and residents.
    
- In extreme cases, a wildfire effectively creates its own weather system.
    

**Expert view:** A European Climate Pact Ambassador noted that extreme heatwaves and prolonged droughts, intensified by climate change, favor "sixth-generation wildfires" — extremely intense events that destroy biodiversity and release vast CO2, further fuelling global warming.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Non-Climate Causes: Land-Use Change</mark>

Beyond climate change, a major structural cause lies in Europe's changing rural land-use patterns over recent decades.

|Factor|Impact|
|---|---|
|Farm abandonment|Reduces managed, cleared land|
|Decline in livestock grazing|Removes natural vegetation control|
|Rural-to-urban migration|Fewer people maintaining countryside|
|Result|Dense shrubs, young trees, dry vegetation create continuous combustible fuel layers|

The OECD's 2023 report notes that land abandonment in the Mediterranean region has caused significant fuel build-up over the past century, directly increasing wildfire risk.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Economic and Human Consequences</mark>

- Wildfire smoke degrades air quality across thousands of kilometres, far beyond the fire zone itself.
    
- The UNECE (July 30 report) estimates EU wildfires cost around EUR 2.5 billion annually through infrastructure damage, lost tourism revenue, and economic disruption.
    
- Long-term costs include ecosystem degradation, biodiversity loss, reduced agricultural productivity, public health impacts, and expensive landscape restoration.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Shift in Emergency Management Philosophy</mark>

Experts stress that firefighting capability alone is insufficient going forward.

- Fire suppression has improved and the overall number of fires has declined.
    
- However, fires that escape initial suppression now develop with unprecedented intensity and speed.
    
- A researcher from University of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria argues protecting people must become the central objective, not just suppressing fires.
    
- Risk communication, public preparedness, and timely evacuation are now considered as important as firefighting capacity itself.
    
- Long-term solutions require active management of agroforestry landscapes, addressing rural abandonment, and building more diverse, resilient ecosystems.

|Aspect|Europe|India|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Terms</mark>

- **Climate whiplash** – rapid alternation between extreme wet and extreme dry conditions, increasing fuel then drying it out.
    
- **PyroCb (pyrocumulonimbus)** – fire-generated thunderstorm cloud capable of producing lightning and erratic winds.
    
- **Sixth-generation wildfires** – term for extremely intense, biodiversity-destroying fires linked to compounding climate extremes.
    
- **Land abandonment** – decline in agricultural/grazing use of land leading to fuel accumulation, a key non-climatic driver of larger fires.
