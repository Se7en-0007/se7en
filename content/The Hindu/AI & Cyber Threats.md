## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context and Core Issue</mark>

AI has achieved mass adoption faster than any prior technology - the internet took 15 years to reach a billion users, while ChatGPT achieved this in just three years. This rapid diffusion of AI is amplifying cyber threats in speed, scale and sophistication across the entire "cyber kill chain" - the sequential stages of a cyberattack, from reconnaissance to execution. Crucially, AI is evolving from a mere tool into an autonomous agent capable of identifying, planning, adapting and executing offensive cyber operations on its own.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Concept: The AI-Cyber Divide</mark>

The article frames a new geopolitical fault line - it is not only about who _uses_ AI, but who _builds_ it and controls its development. Countries with advanced AI ecosystems gain a dual advantage: stronger offensive cyber capability and stronger defensive capability, since AI enables real-time threat detection, automated response and large-scale data analysis far faster than traditional methods.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">How AI Transforms Each Stage of Cyberattacks</mark>

- Reconnaissance: Previously human-intensive, now automated; AI models (e.g. ChatGPT) can mine social media data to craft precise, personalised spear-phishing emails
    
- Deception: AI generates real-time deepfakes, blurring the line between authentic and fabricated online content
    
- Weaponisation: LLM-generated polymorphic malware - code that AI can autonomously rewrite and restructure - replaces traditional malware that relied on fixed, detectable signatures
    
- Command-and-control: AI systems can act as autonomous cyber agents managing multi-stage attacks with minimal human input
    
- Vulnerability discovery: Frontier AI models can autonomously find zero-day vulnerabilities (previously unknown flaws) at scale and even develop exploits for them

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why This Matters for Critical Infrastructure</mark>

AI-discovered vulnerabilities are especially dangerous for Operational Technology (OT) and Industrial Control Systems (ICS) - systems governing nuclear facilities, energy grids, pharmaceutical manufacturing, chemical processing, oil refineries and communication networks. Risk grows as this infrastructure becomes more integrated with AI, and traditional defences (signature-based antivirus, static patches) are increasingly ineffective against constantly-mutating, AI-generated malware.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Threat Exposure</mark>

- CloudSEK's 2024 report ranked India as the second-most cyber-attacked nation globally, after the US
    
- CloudSEK's 2025 report placed India sixth
    
- India's indigenous AI ecosystem remains "incremental," lagging behind the US and China across the full AI stack - foundational models, GPUs, chip design, and large-scale data-centre infrastructure
    
- This leaves India heavily dependent on the US and other technologically advanced nations for critical AI capability
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Policy Response So Far</mark>

- CERT-In (Indian Computer Emergency Response Team): Since 2025 has adopted AI-driven threat detection, cyber resilience measures, trusted AI frameworks, and citizen-centric malware mitigation
    
- April 2026 Advisory: CERT-In issued guidance recommending organisations remove unnecessary internet-facing services and treat every newly discovered vulnerability as exploitable "within hours, not weeks"
    
- MeitY (Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology): Exploring a consent-based framework for synthetically generated content (deepfakes), curbs on agentic AI autonomy, and clearer liability frameworks for AI models
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Suggested Way Forward (as per Author/Nitin Pai)</mark>

Since India cannot build the full AI stack overnight, its strategic strength should lie in "agile adoption and efficient diffusion" of existing AI capabilities. This should be paired with:

- Supply-chain scrutiny for AI hardware and software
    
- Rigorous security assessments of AI systems
    
- Accountability mechanisms and liability provisions tailored to India's developmental and strategic interests
    
- Active participation in strategic groupings such as "Pax Silica" (referring to global semiconductor/AI-chip alliances)
