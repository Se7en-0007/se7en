## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Food Security Journey</mark>

- Transformed from food-aid dependence in the 1960s to a major global food producer.
- Built substantial buffer stocks and legal entitlements through the National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013, covering ~800 million people.
- Achievement rests on decades of public investment: improved seeds, irrigation, procurement, storage, price support, and Public Distribution System (PDS).
- Current challenge: Ensuring affordable, nutritious supply for 1.4 billion people amid rising temperatures, erratic rainfall, extreme weather, and water scarcity.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Existing Strengths (Advantages)</mark>

|Strength|Detail|
|---|---|
|Production capacity|Foodgrain output crossed 350 million tonnes in 2024-25, with record rice and wheat output|
|PDS reach|Expanded via digitisation and One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC) for portability|
|Institutional depth|Decades of administrative expertise in procurement, storage and distribution|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Climate Vulnerabilities Across the Value Chain</mark>

- Heat stress reduces wheat yields.
- Erratic monsoons cause intra-seasonal floods and droughts.
- Groundwater depletion weakens irrigation buffers.
- Extreme weather damages storage and transport infrastructure.
- Cascading effects create market volatility and reduce household access to nutritious food.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Structural Concern: Rice-Wheat System</mark>

- The rice-wheat procurement model was a rational response to earlier-era production constraints but is now narrowly concentrated in a few crops and geographies.
- Many of these procurement regions face serious groundwater stress.
- Proposed reform: Progressive diversification of procurement across crops and regions, with price support aligned to local water and climate conditions.
- Diversification crops suggested: Millets, pulses, oilseeds, and other nutri-cereals — to reduce risk concentration and strengthen dietary diversity.
- Rice and wheat remain essential but should form part of a broader, more resilient procurement portfolio.
- PDS should progressively reinforce this diversified food basket by including locally appropriate millets and pulses, linking food security with nutrition, water conservation, and climate adaptation.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Policy and Institutional Recommendations</mark>

**Climate information integration:**

- Link India Meteorological Department (IMD) forecasts directly with procurement, buffer-stock, and contingency decisions.
- Expand NFSA's monitoring framework to include indicators for heat, water stress, storage exposure, and supply-chain resilience (beyond just coverage and offtake).

**Farmer-level tools:**

- Climate-resilient seed varieties
- Micro-irrigation
- Watershed development
- Precision farming
- Digital advisories
- Weather-indexed insurance
- Public-private collaboration for research, certification, and last-mile delivery
- Public safety nets to protect smallholders and climate-exposed regions

**Supply chain infrastructure:**

- Flood-resilient warehouses
- Storage systems suited to higher temperatures
- Expanded cold chains
- Robust transport links
- Adaptation incentives/standards should cover procurement, storage, logistics, and market stabilisation — not production alone.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Financing the Transition</mark>

- Requires green bonds, adaptation finance, insurance instruments, and investment in resilient agricultural value chains.
- Integration of food systems into India's net-zero pathway — with measurable emission reduction, carbon sequestration, and resilience targets.
- This enables coordinated action across agriculture, water, energy, transport, and nutrition sectors while unlocking climate finance.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Proposed Concept: "Climate Buffer"</mark>

- Extends the traditional buffer-stock principle (which reduced risk from poor harvests) into a broader risk-management portfolio.
- Components: diversified crops and procurement regions, resilient infrastructure, climate information systems, insurance mechanisms, and improved water management.
- Goal: Reduce systemic risks created by an increasingly unpredictable climate.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Concluding Policy Shift Suggested</mark>

- Reframe national narrative from "food security" to "sustainable food security."
- Embed climate risk management as a first-order design parameter in NFSA planning and monitoring — not as an externality addressed after the fact.
- Argument: This is not a departure from India's established approach but its logical next phase, built on existing institutions and capabilities.
