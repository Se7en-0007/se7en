The UDISE+ 2025-26 report reveals that while India's school education system shows overall progress, deep regional and social disparities persist across access, infrastructure, teachers, and outcomes.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Report Overview</mark>

- Full name: Unified District Information System for Education Plus (UDISE+) 2025-26 report
    
- Coverage: 1.47 million schools, 240 million students, 10.2 million teachers
    
- Areas of progress: gross enrolment, student retention, dropout reduction, teacher availability, infrastructure
    
- Core finding: Regional and social disparities persist despite aggregate progress — described as a "paradox of progress"
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Regional and Social Imbalances</mark>

**Aadhaar Seeding**

- Highest: Andhra Pradesh (99.6%), Chandigarh (99%)
    
- Lowest: Meghalaya (35%)
    
- National average: 90.2%
    

**School and Enrolment Distribution**

- Uttar Pradesh: highest share of schools and student enrolment
    
- Meghalaya and Himachal Pradesh: lowest school-to-enrolment shares (higher student-school ratios)
    

**School Level Composition**

- West Bengal: highest foundational/preparatory schools (79% of total), among lowest secondary schools (11%)
    
- Chandigarh: highest secondary schools (83%), lowest foundational/preparatory schools (5%)
    

**Social Composition of Enrolment**

- Chandigarh and Delhi: highest General category enrolment
    
- Lakshadweep, Mizoram, Meghalaya, Ladakh: high ST enrolment
    
- Punjab: one of the highest SC enrolment shares
    
- Tamil Nadu and Gujarat: particularly high OBC representation
    

**Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) by Social Group (National)**

|Social Group|GER|
|---|---|
|OBC|49%|
|General|27%|
|SC|17%|
|ST|10%|

- Indicates enrolment disparity relative to each group's population share
    

**Gender and Minorities**

- Gender Parity Index: girls' enrolment/participation higher than boys across most States/UTs
    
- Minority community enrolment: over 20% nationally; Muslims and Parsis are major minority groups
    

**Average Enrolment per School**

- Highest: Chandigarh (1,194), followed by Delhi (788)
    
- Lowest: Ladakh (64) — indicates wide variation in school size
    

**Pupil-Teacher Ratio (PTR)**

- Generally: lowest in Union Territories, highest in densely populated States
    
- Foundational/preparatory/middle PTR usually lower than secondary PTR
    
- Highest Secondary PTR: Jharkhand (43), followed by Uttar Pradesh
    
- Lowest Secondary PTR: Sikkim (6), followed by Ladakh
    

**GER by Stage — State Extremes**

|Stage|Highest|Lowest|
|---|---|---|
|Foundational|Meghalaya (131), then Mizoram|Bihar (24), preceded by UP|
|Preparatory|Meghalaya (171), then Manipur|Gujarat (74), preceded by Bihar|
|Middle|Chandigarh & Meghalaya (118), then Delhi|Bihar (70), preceded by Nagaland|
|Secondary|Chandigarh (109), then Goa|Bihar (48), preceded by Nagaland|

**Dropout Rates**

- Preparatory: highest in Bihar (7.9%), then Meghalaya; Delhi, Haryana, Maharashtra report none
    
- Middle: highest in Bihar (9%), then UP; Chandigarh, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh report none
    
- Secondary: highest in Ladakh (14.8%), then Karnataka; lowest in West Bengal (1.5%), preceded by Telangana
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Persistent Structural Gaps</mark>

- Access: overall improved, but remote, hilly, tribal, and border areas still face limited schools and transport (examples: Bageshwar-Uttarakhand, Ganjam and Kandhamal-Odisha, Kathua-J&K, Palghar-Maharashtra)
    
- Vulnerable groups: SCs, STs, minorities, and economically poor families face greater barriers to enrolment, attendance, and completion
    
- Infrastructure: drinking water and electricity access has improved, but availability still varies regionally; educationally backward districts lag urban areas in learning environment quality
    
- Teachers: recruitment has improved in many States, but shortages persist in rural/remote regions; teachers there often handle multiple classes, subjects, and non-teaching duties
    
- High PTR effect: reduces individual attention to students, adversely affecting learning outcomes
    
- Girls' education: enrolment has improved, but dropout reduction, primary-to-secondary transition, and facilities for disabled children remain challenges
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Policy Recommendations</mark>

- Framework reference: National Education Policy (NEP) 2020
    
- Targeted investment needed in educationally deprived regions
    
- Priorities: improved digital infrastructure, adequate teacher deployment, stronger support for disadvantaged groups
    
- Approach required: balanced strategy integrating infrastructure improvement, equitable teacher deployment, inclusive social/economic policies, and dedicated support for disadvantaged social groups
    
- Goal: address the "paradox of progress" and contribute to long-term social, economic, and regional development
