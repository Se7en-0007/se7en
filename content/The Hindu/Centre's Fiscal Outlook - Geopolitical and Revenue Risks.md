The article by C. Rangarajan and D.K. Srivastava examines whether the Central government's fiscal targets for 2026-27 will hold up amid tax reform disruptions and the West Asian crisis. Their overall assessment: fiscal outcomes are likely to stay broadly on track, but escalation of the war could deliver a major shock.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Revenue Receipts: Tax Performance</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Weak Tax Revenue Growth</mark>

- Gross Tax Revenues (GTR) grew only 3.7% in Q1 of 2026-27, a subdued figure.
    
- Cause: major rate rationalisation reforms undertaken in 2025-26 for both Personal Income Tax (PIT) and GST caused an initial "revenue sacrifice."
    
- The expectation was that tax base expansion would offset this loss over time.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">PIT and GST Trends</mark>

- PIT revenue growth in 2025-26 was just 0.037%, implying zero buoyancy.
    
- GST revenue growth in H2 2025-26 was 4.67%.
    
- In Q1 2026-27, PIT growth improved to 6.8%, but GST revenues actually contracted by (-)11%.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Excise Duty Impact</mark>

- The West Asian crisis pushed global crude oil prices up and created supply-side uncertainty.
    
- Government cut excise duties on fuel to shield consumers from rising retail prices.
    
- This caused Union excise duty revenue to contract by 22.4% in Q1 2026-27.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government's Remedial Revenue Measures</mark>

Four corrective steps were taken to offset the revenue shortfall:

- Introduced a new Health Security se National Security (HSNS) Cess from February 1, 2026, replacing the discontinued GST Compensation Cess.
    
- Raised the windfall tax on exports of diesel, petrol, and aviation turbine fuel, effective August 3, 2026.
    
- Increased import duties on gold, silver bullion, and other precious metal articles/sweepings/clad metals.
    
- Benefited from an expected higher nominal GDP growth rate than the budgeted 10.04%.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">GDP Growth Projections</mark>

- Authors project 2026-27 nominal GDP growth at 12.5%-13%, above the budgeted rate.
    
- This reflects an expected real GDP growth of about 7% plus an Implicit Price Deflator (IPD)-based inflation of 5%-5.5%.
    
- This aligns with current CPI inflation of 3.9% and WPI inflation of 9.3% in Q1 2026-27.
    
- Despite higher growth expectations, nominal GDP (2022-23 base series) is estimated at ₹391 lakh crore, slightly below the budgeted ₹393 lakh crore.
    
- Overall conclusion: estimated GTR will likely be realised or fall short only marginally.
    
- Authors recommend the excise duty cut on fuel be restored "at a suitable time."
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Transfers to States</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Tax Devolution</mark>

- Net tax revenue is estimated using a 65% net-to-gross ratio (consistent with 2025-26 and 2026-27 Budget Estimates).
    
- The Sixteenth Finance Commission (FC16) kept the States' share of the divisible pool at 41%.
    
- The new non-shareable HSNS Cess may marginally reduce the shareable pool, though some proceeds could flow to States as non-FC grants.
    
- Q1 2026-27 saw a sharp contraction in tax devolution to States of (-)19.5%, though improvement is expected in later months.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Finance Commission Grants</mark>

- FC16 recommendations mean FC grants to States are budgeted to contract by ₹23,556 crore in 2026-27.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Non-Tax Revenue and Expenditure</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">RBI Dividend Support</mark>

- RBI transferred major dividends to the Centre in May 2026, providing significant fiscal support.
    
- As a result, 77% of the full year's budgeted dividends and profits were already covered within the first three months.
    
- Budgeted non-tax and non-debt capital receipts are expected to be fully realised.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Revenue and Expenditure Dynamics</mark>

- Non-tax revenues contributed 37% of net revenue receipts in Q1 2026-27.
    
- Major subsidies rose 37.4% in Q1 due to the unexpected spike in global crude oil prices.
    
- Revenue expenditure growth was contained at a modest 7.4%.
    
- Capital expenditure was front-loaded, growing 23.7% in Q1 2026-27, a sharp reversal from a (-)23.3% contraction in Q4 2025-26.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Full-Year Subsidy Outlook</mark>

- The West Asian crisis is expected to keep global crude prices elevated for the rest of the year.
    
- If Q1 subsidy levels are extrapolated annually, realised subsidies could exceed the budgeted amount by roughly ₹50,000 crore.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Debt and Deficit Position</mark>

|Metric|Q1 2026-27 Status|Full-Year Estimate|
|---|---|---|
|Fiscal deficit|18.2% of annual budgeted magnitude|₹18.16 lakh crore|
|Revenue deficit|0.4% of annual budgeted magnitude|Impressive balance, aided by non-debt receipts|
|Fiscal deficit-to-GDP ratio|—|4.6% (new GDP series)|
|Debt-to-GDP ratio|—|55.8%|

- These estimates are close to budgeted levels once the new GDP series is factored in.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Risk Factors for Slippage</mark>

- Possible shortfall in tax revenues due to GST contraction and excise duty cuts.
    
- Unbudgeted increase in revenue expenditure from additional fuel/oil subsidies.
    
- Slightly higher external debt burden amid sustained pressure on the Indian rupee.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overall Assessment</mark>

- As things stand, significant deviations from budgeted fiscal outcomes appear unlikely.
    
- The single biggest risk is an escalation of the West Asia war, which could deliver a major jolt to the Indian economy and central government finances.
