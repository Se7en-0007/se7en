The 16th Finance Commission (FC-16), chaired by Arvind Panagariya, covers the period 2026-31 and marks a decisive shift in India's fiscal transfer architecture from an equity-focused approach toward an efficiency/performance-driven one.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background: Role of Finance Commission</mark>

- Constitutional body designed to mediate the fiscal asymmetry between a fiscally dominant Union and structurally constrained States.
    
- Meant to address horizontal inequalities arising from history, geography, and institutional capacity across States.
    
- Core historical mandate: safeguard States' interests to preserve a strong Union, especially given uneven development at Independence.
    
- Each successive Commission has interpreted this mandate differently but has generally sustained India's fiscal federal compact.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Constitutional Basis of Grants-in-Aid</mark>

- Article 275 provides for grants-in-aid - a foundational design element, not an afterthought.
    
- Tax devolution formulas, however sophisticated, cannot capture India's diversity of State-specific needs.
    
- Examples of special State burdens:
    
    - Kerala: "export-oriented" human capital strategy contributes ~23% of India's total remittances but strains State fiscal health via education-related borrowing.
        
    - Punjab: ensures national food security through wheat/rice production (non-taxable crops), while bearing border-State costs.
        
    - Hill States: high infrastructure costs.
        
    - North-eastern States: connectivity constraints.
        
    - Fiscally stressed States: demographic pressures and social sector burdens.
        
- FC-14 and FC-15 retained Revenue Deficit Grants (RDGs), sector-specific grants, and State-specific grants to address these needs.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Changes Under FC-16</mark>

|Parameter|FC-15|FC-16|
|---|---|---|
|Vertical devolution (tax share to States)|41%|41% (retained, despite 18 States demanding 50%)|
|Grants-in-aid (total)|₹10.1 lakh crore|₹9.47 lakh crore|
|Grants share in total FC transfers|19.4%|8.3%|
|Scope of grants-in-aid|RDGs, sector-specific, State-specific grants|Restricted to local bodies and disaster management only|
|Weight to income distance|45%|42.5%|
|New criterion|-|10% weight for contribution to GDP|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Commission's Rationale and Its Problems</mark>

- FC-16 assumes improved fiscal discipline should let States manage finances without gap-filling transfers - presuming fiscal capacity is broadly uniform across States.
    
- Justification for scrapping RDGs: concern that they create "moral hazard" (incentivizing under-collection of revenue or overspending).
    
- Commission cites aggregate data suggesting States overall are not in severe fiscal distress.
    
- Criticism: aggregate data masks deep inter-State disparities; a surplus in one State cannot offset a deficit in another, which was the very purpose RDGs served.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The "Dual Shift" - Cesses vs RDGs</mark>

- While Union insists on fiscal discipline for States (removal of RDGs), it does not recommend a binding rollback of cesses and surcharges (non-shareable Union levies).
    
- Instead, FC-16 proposes a "grand bargain": gradual merger of cesses into the divisible pool in exchange for States accepting a lower devolution share.
    
- This is seen as asymmetric - RDGs removed in the name of discipline, but cesses (a more distortionary instrument for federalism) only gently nudged toward reform.
    
- Net effect: stringency imposed on States while fiscal flexibility is preserved for the Union, raising concerns of widening vertical imbalance.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">States Most Affected</mark>

- Around 8 States - including most north-eastern States and West Bengal - face a reduced share in both tax devolution and grants-in-aid.
    
- Another 6 States have seen a decline specifically in their share of grants.
    
- Compounding factors: reduced weight on income distance, new GDP-contribution criterion, and complete removal of RDGs (which made up ~20% of FC grants in 2024-25).
    
- Combined effect: a "double burden" - lower tax devolution plus loss of compensatory grants - risking widened regional disparities.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Local Government Grants and Conditionalities</mark>

- FC-16 allocates nearly ₹7.2 lakh crore to the third tier (local bodies) - a commendable step for local governance financing.
    
- However, funds are tied to strict performance conditions: targets in water and sanitation, revenue mobilisation, and audited accounts.
    
- This shifts the grant architecture from need-based equalisation to compliance-based incentivisation, reducing fiscal autonomy of local bodies even as accountability improves.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Central Critique: Efficiency vs Equity Tension</mark>

- FC-16 reflects "technocratic confidence" in market-like incentives, even though fiscal federalism is fundamentally a political and constitutional arrangement meant to manage diversity.
    
- Key contradiction: strict stance on RDGs vs accommodative approach to cesses/surcharges, alongside refusal to raise States' divisible pool share to 50%.
    
- This suggests the Commission may (intentionally or not) be protecting Union fiscal space while shifting the adjustment burden onto States - moving away from its equalising constitutional role.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward (Authors' View)</mark>

- Future Commissions should reward high-performing States while continuing to support those facing structural disadvantages.
    
- Fiscal discipline is necessary but "cannot substitute for fiscal justice."
    
- For a diverse country like India, fiscal federalism cannot rest on performance alone - it must be anchored in fairness.
