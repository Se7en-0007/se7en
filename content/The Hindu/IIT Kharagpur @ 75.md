## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background and Genesis</mark>

- The idea for technical institutes like IITs originated in the mid-1940s, during the final phase of World War II, when the colonial government began planning for India's post-war industrial and technical manpower needs.
    
- In 1946, Sir Jogendra Singh (Member, Viceroy's Executive Council, Department of Education, Health and Agriculture) constituted a 22-member committee to explore setting up higher technical institutions in India.
    
- This committee was headed by industrialist Nalini Ranjan Sarkar and came to be known as the Sarkar Committee.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Sarkar Committee Report</mark>

- The committee's interim report noted that after World War II, engineers and technical experts would be in high demand in Europe and America, making it difficult for India to import foreign technical expertise.
    
- It urged that a programme of higher technical education and research be "pushed forward with the utmost speed and determination."
    
- The committee recommended establishing at least four Higher Technical Institutions modelled on MIT (Massachusetts Institute of Technology) - one each in the North, East, South, and West of India.
    
- The eastern institute was proposed for Calcutta, and the western one for Bombay.
    
- These institutions were envisioned as more than ordinary engineering colleges - meant to undertake research, train researchers and technical teachers, and produce graduates comparable to those from top global institutions.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Nehru's Role and Institution-Building</mark>

- After Independence, Jawaharlal Nehru's government made higher technical education central to its vision of building a modern India.
    
- In Bengal, Chief Minister BC Roy and Dr. Humayun Kabir worked actively to fast-track the new college.
    
- Timeline of key milestones:
    

|Date|Event|
|---|---|
|Initial phase|Institute began functioning from 5, Esplanade East, Calcutta|
|September 1950|Shifted to Hijli campus|
|August 18, 1951|Formally inaugurated by education minister Maulana Abul Kalam Azad; name "Indian Institute of Technology" adopted|
|March 3, 1952|Nehru laid the foundation stone of the new building|

- In July 1956, Nehru personally deliberated on the location of the next technological institute, weighing Kanpur and Allahabad, and stated the new institute should be "similar to Kharagpur Institute."
    
- At the institute's first convocation, Nehru remarked on the joy of seeing "visible growth of our big national laboratories and these Institutes," reflecting his broader philosophy of scientific temper and institution-building.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Site: Hijli Detention Camp</mark>

- IIT Kharagpur's location carries deep historical significance tied to India's freedom struggle.
    
- The Hijli Detention Camp was established in 1930 by the British to hold Indian political prisoners as jails overflowed during the intensifying independence movement.
    
- On September 16, 1931, two unarmed detainees - Santosh Kumar Mitra and Tarakeswar Sengupta - were shot dead by colonial police, sparking nationwide outrage.
    
- Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose personally went to the detention camp to take charge of their mortal remains; this incident galvanised national leaders, including Gurudev Rabindranath Tagore.
    
- The camp continued to be used to detain freedom fighters without trial until Independence.
    
- Former President Pranab Mukherjee referenced this history in his 2012 convocation address at IIT Kharagpur, honouring the site's revolutionary legacy.
    
- When IIT Kharagpur's first session began in August 1951, classrooms, laboratories, and administrative offices were housed in the old detention-camp buildings - symbolically transforming a site of colonial repression into a hub of national scientific advancement.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Founding Statistics and Growth</mark>

- First session (1951) details: 224 students, 42 teachers, and 10 departments.
    
- Institutional status was elevated in September 1956, when Parliament's Act granted it national importance, autonomy, and formal incorporation.
    
- Following Kharagpur, IITs Bombay, Madras, and Kanpur were established in the same decade, with more added in subsequent years, forming today's national IIT system.
