
India's EV sector has grown roughly 46 times since 2016, transforming from a niche technology into a strategic pillar of economic growth, energy security, and climate policy.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What is an EV?</mark>

An EV runs on an electric motor powered by a rechargeable battery. There are four broad types:

- Battery EVs (BEVs) – fully electric
    
- Hybrid EVs (HEVs) – combine an internal combustion engine with an electric motor
    
- Plug-in Hybrid EVs (PHEVs) – hybrids with externally rechargeable batteries for extended electric-only range
    
- Fuel Cell EVs (FCEVs) – generate electricity from chemical energy
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Genesis of India's EV Journey</mark>

India's formal EV push began in 2015 with the National Electric Mobility Mission Plan (NEMMP 2020) and the FAME scheme. The strategic drivers were reducing the petroleum import bill, tackling urban air pollution, and cutting greenhouse gas emissions (transport contributes ~9% of India's total emissions).

- NEMMP 2020: roadmap for EV adoption and domestic manufacturing, aimed at energy security plus sustainable mobility
    
- FAME India (2015): incentivized buyers, supported charging infrastructure and manufacturing; Phase I ran till March 2019, Phase II ran five years till April 2024
    
- EV adoption rose from 0.08% (FY15-16) to 8.26% (FY25-26), with FY22 marking the post-COVID acceleration point
    
- Early adoption (FY20-21) was dominated by e-rickshaws; electric two-wheelers became the leading segment by FY25

- Top state markets in 2025: Uttar Pradesh (4 lakh+ units, 18% share), Maharashtra (2.66 lakh, 12%), Karnataka (2 lakh, 9%)
    
- Growth driven mainly by two-wheelers (12.8 lakh units) and three-wheelers (8 lakh units) sold in 2025
    
- EV registrations grew at a CAGR of over 62% in the past decade
    
- Top export destinations: Nepal, Indonesia, Japan
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Charging Infrastructure</mark>

- As of July 2026, 52,718 public charging stations exist, of which 16,561 offer fast-charging
    
- e-Amrit tool (NITI Aayog) helps locate charging stations by location
    
- Private players are expanding networks — e.g., Hyundai Motor India added 11 ultra-fast stations in 2024 across Mumbai, Pune, Ahmedabad, Hyderabad, Gurugram, Bangalore and highways
    
- Target: 1.32 million charging stations across India by 2030
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India Electric Mobility Index (IEMI)</mark>

Launched by NITI Aayog in August 2025, the IEMI benchmarks states/UTs on 16 indicators across three pillars: transport electrification, charging infrastructure readiness, and EV research & innovation.

- Classification tiers: Frontrunners, Performers, Aspirants
    
- Delhi, Maharashtra, and Chandigarh are Frontrunners with mature EV ecosystems
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Domestic Manufacturing Ecosystem</mark>

India is shifting from an assembly-led market to a full manufacturing hub covering battery packs, motors, drivetrains, power electronics, wiring, and charging equipment.

- Domestic OEMs expanding: Tata Motors, Mahindra, TVS, Bajaj Auto (investing in R&D, gigafactories, EV platforms)
    
- Global entrants exploring India manufacturing: VinFast, Tesla, Korean/Japanese battery firms
    
- Milestone: Suzuki's "e VITARA" — first Made-in-India global strategic BEV (launched August 2025), targeted for export to 100+ countries; India became Suzuki's global EV manufacturing hub
    
- ~400 EV startups operating in India, driving charging infra, battery innovation, low-cost manufacturing, and AI-based solutions (voice navigation, traffic insights, route planning)
    
- India's EV ecosystem raised over USD 1.4 billion in FY2025 (~27% higher than 2024); EV manufacturers secured ~USD 1.2 billion; Delhi was the top city for EV investment
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Government Schemes</mark>

|Scheme|Year|Key Features|
|---|---|---|
|National Mission on Manufacturing (NMM)|2025|EVs as "seed" sector; target: raise manufacturing share of GDP from 12.9% (2023) to 25% by 2035, create 143 million jobs, USD 1.2 trillion merchandise exports|
|PM E-DRIVE Scheme|2024|Outlay ₹10,900 crore; covers e-2Ws, e-3Ws, e-trucks, e-buses, e-ambulances; targets 28 lakh+ EVs (22.12 lakh sold by Jan 2026); ₹4,391 crore for 14,028 e-buses; ₹2,000 crore for EVPCS charging stations; ₹780 crore for testing agency upgrades|
|PM e-Bus Sewa–PSM Scheme|2024|Payment security for 12 years per bus; 10,000 e-buses via PPP model; covers depot infra, charging, green mobility|
|SPMEPCI|2024|Promotes e-car manufacturing; minimum investment ₹4,150 crore; 25% Domestic Value Addition (DVA) by year 3, 50% by year 5|
|PLI-Auto Scheme|2021|Minimum 50% DVA; cumulative incentive ₹2,377.56 crore disbursed, ₹2,319.88 crore to EV makers (e-2W/3W/4W/buses) as of Jan 2026|
|PLI ACC Battery Scheme|2021|Outlay ₹18,100 crore; target 50 GWh cumulative ACC capacity; performance phase 2025–2029|

- Advanced Chemistry Cells (ACCs) store energy electrochemically/chemically and convert it back to electricity; batteries form 35-40% of an EV's total cost, making domestic ACC manufacturing critical
    
- GST on EVs is concessional at 5% for all electric cars, two-wheelers, and three-wheelers
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">State-Level Push</mark>

States are key drivers through policy, infrastructure, and investment support.

- As of December 2025, 29 states/UTs had notified EV policies, with 4 more in draft stage
    
- Common incentives: 15-25% capital subsidies, preferential land allotment, stamp duty waivers, up to 100% SGST reimbursement
    
- Major EV manufacturing hub states: Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh
    
- Delhi EV Policy is among India's most progressive; in June 2026, government approved a ₹9,585 crore, two-year scheme to replace ~2.07 lakh old commercial vehicles (1.91 lakh trucks, 16,329 buses) in Delhi-NCR
    
- PM e-Bus Sewa rollout examples: 50 e-buses flagged off in Bhavnagar (Gujarat) in February 2026; also covers Nagpur, Chandigarh, Guwahati
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Future Outlook and Targets</mark>

India's EV strategy aligns with its 2070 net-zero vision and the "Make in India" initiative for domestic EV production.

- India's EV market: valued at USD 3.71 billion (2025), projected to reach USD 191.04 billion by 2034 (CAGR 54.94%)
    
- EV battery market: USD 2.71 billion (2025) to USD 15.90 billion by 2034 (CAGR 21.70%)
    
- Target: 30% of all vehicle sales electric by 2030 (aligned with global EV30@30 initiative)
    
- Upcoming CAFE norms: CAFE III (2027–2032) targeting 91.7 g CO2/km; CAFE IV (2033–2037) targeting 70 g CO2/km, based on WLTP, with a super-credit mechanism favoring EV sales
    
- CAFE norms were originally introduced in 2017 to push OEMs toward fuel-efficient, lower-emission vehicles
