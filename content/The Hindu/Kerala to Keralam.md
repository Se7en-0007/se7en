## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview of the Development</mark>

The Lok Sabha passed the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026 by voice vote on August 11, 2026, amid Opposition protests. The Bill seeks to formally change the state's name from "Kerala" (as it appears in the Constitution) to "Keralam". The Bill must still be passed by the Rajya Sabha and receive Presidential assent before the change becomes law.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background and Legislative Journey</mark>

- August 2023: Kerala Legislative Assembly passed its first unanimous resolution seeking the name change.
    
- June 2024: A second unanimous resolution was passed by the state assembly reiterating the demand under Article 3 of the Constitution.
    
- February 2026: Union Cabinet approved the proposal.
    
- The Bill was then referred to the Kerala state assembly, which unanimously backed it.
    
- August 10, 2026: Bill introduced in the Lok Sabha.
    
- August 11, 2026: Bill passed by the Lok Sabha via voice vote.
    
- Pending: Rajya Sabha passage and Presidential assent.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Kerala Sought the Change</mark>

- "Keralam" is the name of the state in Malayalam, the local language.
    
- "Kerala" is the anglicised version used in English and listed in the First Schedule of the Constitution.
    
- The state assembly argued for aligning the constitutional (English) name with the native Malayalam usage.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Etymology of Kerala/Keralam</mark>

- One of the earliest references appears in Emperor Ashoka's Rock Edict II (257 BCE), which mentions "Ketalaputra" (Keralaputra) alongside the Cholas, Pandyas, and Satiyaputra.
    
- Keralaputra literally means "son of Kerala" in Sanskrit, referring to the Chera dynasty, one of the three major kingdoms of southern India.
    
- German missionary-linguist Hermann Gundert noted a link between the Kannada word "keram" and "cheram," referring to coastal land between Gokarna (Karnataka) and Kanyakumari (Tamil Nadu).
    
- An alternate theory derives the name from the Old Tamil roots "cher" (to join) and "alam" (region or land).
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Formation of Modern Kerala</mark>

- Malayalam-speaking populations were historically fragmented across the princely states of Travancore and Cochin, and the Malabar district of Madras Presidency.
    
- The Aikya Kerala (United Kerala) movement of the 1920s pushed for unification of Malayalam-speaking areas.
    
- 1949: Travancore and Cochin princely states merged to form Travancore-Cochin State.
    
- November 1, 1956: Modern Kerala was formed following the States Reorganisation Commission's recommendations under Syed Fazl Ali.
    
- Malabar district and Kasargod taluk were merged into the new state; four southern Travancore taluks (Tovala, Agastheeswaram, Kalkulam, Vilayankode) and parts of Shenkottai went to Tamil Nadu instead.
    
- Since formation, the state has been called "Keralam" in Malayalam but "Kerala" in English/Constitution — the current Bill seeks to close this gap.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Constitutional Procedure for Renaming a State</mark>

|Step|Detail|
|---|---|
|Empowering provision|Article 3 of the Constitution gives Parliament power to alter a state's name|
|Bill introduction|Can only be introduced in Parliament on the President's recommendation|
|State consultation|If area, boundary, or name is affected, the President refers the Bill to the concerned state legislature for its views|
|Binding nature|The state legislature's opinion is not binding on Parliament|
|Final steps|Bill must pass both Houses of Parliament and receive Presidential assent to become law|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Current Status</mark>

The Bill has cleared the Lok Sabha but still requires Rajya Sabha approval and Presidential assent to formally change the state's constitutional name to "Keralam."
