## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview</mark>

Global food prices are facing upward pressure due to two simultaneous shocks: the intensified Russia-Ukraine war affecting Black Sea shipping, and extreme heat/drought in Europe reducing crop yields. Together, these are pushing the FAO food price index to multi-year highs, adding to existing global trade disruptions like the Strait of Hormuz blockade.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Geographic Chokepoints</mark>

**Black Sea Region**

- Russia and Ukraine together account for a combined 27.4% share of global wheat exports
    
- Their combined share of barley exports: 15.8%; corn: 12.5%; sunflower oil: 61.5%
    
- The Kerch Strait connects the Sea of Azov to the Black Sea and is emerging as a new global chokepoint, alongside the already-watched Strait of Hormuz
    

**Key ports affected**

- Russian ports: Novorossiysk, Rostov-on-Don, Taganrog, Taman, Kavkaz
    
- Ukrainian ports: Greater Odesa region (Odesa, Chornomorsk, Pivdennyi/Yuzhny) - handles ~90% of Ukraine's agricultural exports
    
- Izmail - Ukraine's largest Danube port, located in southwestern Odesa bordering Romania
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recent Military Escalation</mark>

- A Ukrainian drone strike destroyed two major Russian grain export terminals at Novorossiysk, suspending operations with a combined annual export capacity of 15.6 million tonnes
    
- Since mid-July 2026, Ukraine has intensified attacks on grain vessels, maritime infrastructure, and ports along the Sea of Azov and Kerch Strait
    
- Russia has retaliated by targeting Ukrainian port facilities, including the strike on Izmail
    
- Attack intensity comparison: July 2026 alone saw 57 attacks on vessels and 67 on port facilities, compared to just 14 vessel attacks for the entirety of 2025
    
- More than 80% of Russia's grain exports depend on the Sea of Azov and Black Sea route; alternative options include Baltic, Caspian, and Far East sea ports or overland rail links, though these are less efficient
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Europe's Weather Crisis</mark>

**USDA production forecasts (2025-26 to 2026-27, EU)**

- Record-breaking temperatures from late spring through summer 2026 have reduced crop yields across the EU
    
- Drought has lowered river water levels, restricting alternative transport routes like the Danube and limiting inland transportation to ports
    
- This weather crisis is expected to increase the EU's dependence on grain imports
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Impact on Prices</mark>

- The UN FAO's benchmark food price index touched a three-and-a-half year high in July 2026
    
- Following the Novorossiysk strike and Russia's retaliatory hit on Izmail, corn and wheat prices at the Chicago Board of Trade rose by 3.8-4% within days

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Ukraine's Export Outlook</mark>

- Ukraine's Agriculture Ministry has slashed its 2026-27 farm produce export forecast from 64.4 million tonnes to just 29.6 million tonnes
    
- Wheat shipment estimates specifically were downgraded from 17.6 mt to 8.3 mt
    
- The core problem is not a production shortage but a logistics crisis - increased shipowner and crew reluctance, soaring insurance premiums, and reduced vessel calls due to strikes
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Parallel</mark>

- The FAO food price index had previously peaked in March 2022, right when the Russia-Ukraine war began
    
- Prices remained elevated until early 2023, when grain shipments resumed through safe maritime corridors established under a UN-backed initiative (the Black Sea Grain Initiative)
    
- The current 2026 escalation risks repeating or worsening that 2022-23 price shock, especially layered atop the ongoing Strait of Hormuz blockade and broader El Niño-related weather disruptions
