## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Introduction to Makhana</mark>

Makhana (Euryale ferox) is an aquatic crop grown in shallow ponds, lakes, and wetlands, whose edible seed is popped and eaten as "fox nut." India is the world's largest producer, aided by favourable agro-climatic conditions, with cultivation concentrated in Bihar, West Bengal, and Assam.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government Institutional Support</mark>

- National Makhana Board: announced in Union Budget 2025-26; formally launched in Bihar on 15 September 2025 to strengthen and modernize the value chain.
    
- Central Sector Scheme for Development of Makhana: outlay of ₹476.03 crore for 2025-26 to 2030-31; covers research, quality seeds, farmer skilling, harvesting/post-harvest upgrades, value addition, branding, marketing, exports, and quality control.
    
- Scheme allocations: ₹30 crore approved for 2025-26 and ₹90 crore for 2026-27.
    
- Under the scheme (2025-26): 1,010 additional hectares brought under cultivation, 73 hectares under seed production, 89 Front Line Demonstrations conducted, and 8,159 farmers benefited through training at SAUs, CAUs, and ICAR institutes.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Production Landscape</mark>

- Bihar contributes nearly three-fourths of India's makhana production and 80-85% of global supply, concentrated in the Kosi basin (Supaul, Saharsa, Madhepura) and the Mithila/Seemanchal regions.
    
- 2024-25 (final estimate): total production 63,910 MT; productivity 2.03 MT/hectare.
    
- 2025-26 (Second Advance Estimate): total production 80,590 MT; productivity 2.34 MT/hectare; Bihar alone produced 60,000 MT at 2.00 MT/hectare.
    
- Improved varieties Swarna Vaidehi and Sabour Makhana-1, along with field-system farming, have boosted productivity.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Export Profile</mark>

- DGFT introduced a separate HSN code for popped makhana and makhana products in 2025 (previously clubbed under general codes, hiding product-specific data).
    
- 2025-26 exports: 7,264.89 MT, valued at ₹19,296.08 lakh.
    
- Top destinations: United States (40%), Canada (20%), UAE (17%) - together 77% of exports, but at relatively lower unit prices (US $19.5/kg, Canada $15.8/kg, UAE $13.3/kg).
    
- Premium, low-volume markets: Germany ($26.0/kg), Nepal ($21.6/kg), Australia ($21.0/kg) - 1-5% volume share each.
    
- UK offers a balanced profile: 10% share at $20/kg.
    
- Policy implication: opportunity to diversify beyond US/Gulf toward premium-paying markets for better realizations.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Nutritional Profile</mark>

- Rich in carbohydrates, high-quality protein, dietary fibre, and micronutrients (magnesium, potassium, phosphorus, iron); low-sodium, cholesterol-free.
    
- Low glycaemic load - suitable for diabetics; supports heart health, digestion, and has anti-aging properties.
    
- Nutrient profile considered superior to almonds, walnuts, and cashews.
    
- Very low fat (0.33 g/100g), high fibre, ~95% protein digestibility; amino acid index/chemical score comparable to fish.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Value Chain: Seed to Snack</mark>

- Farm-level production: pond preparation, seed broadcasting, crop management, manual (underwater) harvesting - highly labour-intensive, low mechanization.
    
- Primary processing: household/village-level drying, cleaning, high-temperature roasting, popping, polishing, sorting, and grading by size/quality.
    
- Secondary processing/value addition: flavoured makhana, ready-to-eat snacks, makhana flour - a growing segment for value addition and employment.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">National Research Centre for Makhana (NRCM)</mark>

- Develops high-yield makhana and thornless water chestnut varieties; promotes water-efficient/integrated farming and Makhana-cum-fish farming.
    
- Distributed 15,824.1 kg of high-yielding seeds to farmers, KVKs, and organizations (including NABARD, State Fisheries Departments, Bihar Horticulture Development Society).
    
- Trained over 3,000 farmers (2012-2023) in cultivation, processing, and marketing; provided technical support to 24 enterprises.
    
- Proposed a train-the-trainers model with SAUs and Central Agricultural University, Bihar, covering the full value chain (cultivation to branding).
    
- Developed and licensed equipment: makhana seed washer, seed grader, primary roasting machine, popping machine, popped makhana grader.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Significance for the Sector</mark>

Makhana has become one of India's fastest-growing agri-food sectors, driven by health awareness, domestic demand growth, and export potential, positioning it to boost farmer incomes, rural employment, and agricultural exports.
