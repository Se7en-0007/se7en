## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview</mark>

India's distributed solar capacity has grown more than 17 times, from 1.8 GW in FY2018 to 31.5 GW in FY2026, according to a new report by the Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis (IEEFA). This growth was primarily driven by two flagship government schemes: Pradhan Mantri Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana and Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan (PM KUSUM). Despite this surge, Distributed Energy Resources (DER) continue to face regulatory, financing and implementation barriers.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Growth Figures</mark>

- Total distributed solar capacity: 1.8 GW (FY18) to 31.5 GW (FY26) — over 17x growth
    
- Rooftop solar: 1.1 GW (FY18) to 25.7 GW (FY26)
    
- Off-grid solar pumps (PM KUSUM Component B): 0.7 GW (FY18) to 5.8 GW (FY26)
    
- Distributed solar's share in India's total renewable energy portfolio remained broadly unchanged at around 21-22 per cent, since utility-scale solar expanded even faster
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Untapped Potential</mark>

- Rooftop solar potential: estimated 637 GW
    
- Irrigation pumps available for decentralised solarisation: more than 29 million
    
- Small hydropower potential: estimated 21 GW
    
- Bioenergy potential: estimated 42 GW
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Drivers of Growth</mark>

- Government subsidies and concessional finance
    
- Supportive policy frameworks
    
- Diverse business models: CAPEX (capital expenditure), RESCO (renewable energy service company/OPEX), and utility-led implementation models
    
- These models broadened market participation across urban and rural areas
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Comparative Study: India, Australia, Bangladesh</mark>

The IEEFA report compared India with Australia and Bangladesh on DER ecosystem development.

|Country|Strengths|Weaknesses|
|---|---|---|
|India|Comprehensive technical standards; single-window digital platform (PM Surya Ghar); multiple financing mechanisms|Inconsistent approval timelines; uneven smart meter deployment; weak network visibility|
|Australia|Leads in rooftop adoption (~40% of homes have solar panels); emerging residential battery storage phase|Not detailed in report|
|Bangladesh|Rapidly scaling rooftop solar; IEEFA estimates ~667 MW installed across 239 establishments (vs official figure of 418.1 MW)|Data/reporting gaps between actual and official capacity|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Bottlenecks Identified</mark>

**Residential rooftop solar:**

- Delays in approvals and subsidy disbursement
    
- Limited access to affordable finance
    
- Low consumer awareness
    

**Commercial and industrial (C&I) consumers:**

- Uncertainty over net metering regulations, banking, and open access policies
    
- Lengthy and inconsistent approval processes by distribution companies (discoms)
    
- Financing constraints, especially for MSMEs
    

**PM KUSUM implementation:**

- Financing constraints for farmers
    
- Land availability issues
    
- Weak coordination with distribution companies
    
- Slower pace of feeder solarisation
    

**Underutilised technologies:**

- Small hydropower and bioenergy have received relatively limited policy support despite sizable potential
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Approval Timeline Gap</mark>

State regulations typically prescribe 15 to 30 days for rooftop solar approvals, but stakeholders report actual approvals often taking 30 to 90 days — highlighting a significant implementation gap between policy and practice.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Report Recommendations</mark>

- Harmonise net metering regulations across states
    
- Introduce time-bound approval processes
    
- Expand single-window digital platforms beyond residential rooftop solar
    
- Improve access to blended finance and credit guarantees
    
- Strengthen domestic manufacturing capacity
    
- Accelerate deployment of smart meters
    
- Scale virtual power plants (VPPs)
    
- Enable vehicle-to-grid (V2G) integration as EV adoption rises
    
- Expand RESCO models and feeder solarisation for greater system flexibility and consumer participation
