## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Milestone</mark>

In 2023-24, private industry contributed 51.8% of India's national R&D spending, surpassing all levels of government combined for the first time in India's history. Industry now also employs more core researchers than government institutions do.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Sectoral Leaders in R&D</mark>

- Transport: largest corporate investor in R&D; spending was negligible before 2020 and has roughly tripled since
    
- Pharmaceuticals: second-largest contributor
    
- Biotechnology: part of the top research-intensive triumvirate along with transport and IT
    
- Information Technology (IT): rounds out the most research-intensive industries
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Trend</mark>

Through the 2010s, private industry contributed only about a third of national R&D, with the ratio changing slowly. A sharp break occurred between 2020-21 and 2021-22, when private spending nearly doubled from ₹46,388 crore to ₹82,975 crore, and total national R&D spending jumped from ₹1.27 lakh crore to ₹1.95 lakh crore in a single year.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Reasons Behind the Surge</mark>

- Post-pandemic realisation among firms that research strengthens competitiveness
    
- Improved measurement and reporting rather than purely new spending: mandatory sustainability disclosures for large listed firms and tighter RBI norms on research reporting took effect simultaneously
    
- Better capture of spending that was always occurring, such as in foreign subsidiaries of Indian companies and captive centres of multinationals
    
- Genuinely new capital flowing into artificial intelligence, chip design, and semiconductor fabs, though much of this is infrastructure-building that may not strictly belong under R&D
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Structural Concerns and Caution</mark>

Despite the milestone, India's R&D spending stands at just 0.84% of GDP, far below China's 2.58%, the US's 3.45%, and South Korea's 4.94%. India also fields only 354 researchers per million people, compared to several thousand in South Korea and Israel.

A notable irony: private firms spent more on advertising than on research in 2023-24, raising questions about the depth of corporate commitment to R&D as a genuine priority.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward</mark>

- The shift's real value depends on whether it deepens India's pool of specialised workers and builds capacity for sophisticated manufacturing
    
- Success requires moving the economy beyond its long-standing reliance on low-cost services
    
- The outcome depends less on how spending is recorded and more on how many researchers India can actually train
    
- The Anusandhan National Research Foundation, with a ₹50,000-crore corpus largely sourced from private funds, was created for this purpose, but its success now depends on effective delivery
