## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Thesis</mark>

India's aspiration of becoming Viksit Bharat by 2047 depends on whether AI-driven productivity gains are shared broadly or captured only by those already well-connected to digital systems. The design of AI systems (who builds them, whose data trains them, and whose languages they speak) will decide whether AI narrows or widens existing structural inequalities, especially for women.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Scale of The Problem</mark>

- About 82% of working women in India are in informal employment (2018 ILO report), spread across agriculture, home-based production, domestic services, and micro-enterprises.
    
- 76.9% of rural women are engaged in agriculture (PLFS 2023-24).
    
- UN Women's warning: AI is already reshaping reality for billions but "getting women wrong" — without gender-responsive design, it risks amplifying stereotypes, discrimination, and digital violence rather than expanding opportunity.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Policy Backdrop</mark>

- India's AI Governance Guidelines were released in November 2025, laying out seven guiding principles, including fairness and equity.
    
- The challenge is translating these principles into gender-responsive outcomes — described as both a governance priority and a development opportunity.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Evidence from Agriculture</mark>

AI tools show strong potential in precision agriculture, real-time crop health advisories, input optimisation, and market access for farmers.

- A 2024 study of Farmer.Chat, deployed across 12 States, found 61% of women users reported improved quality of life within 45 days of use.
    
- Women users showed engagement levels 2 to 3 times higher than male users.
    
- Key takeaway: when AI tools account for language, land access, mobility constraints, and relevance, women engage meaningfully — suggesting design choices, not technology itself, drive inclusion outcomes.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Three Governance Priorities Proposed</mark>

|Priority|Key Elements|
|---|---|
|Gender impact assessments|Proportionate assessments for AI systems affecting economic opportunity, welfare, or safety; examine variation by sex, location, caste, disability, work status; ensure redressal in regional languages; apply Gender Responsive Budgeting via four questions — which women benefit, which barrier is addressed (access/language/safety/capability), how outcomes are measured, which budget line funds corrective action|
|AI literacy as public infrastructure|Embed AI literacy within DAY-NRLM (Deen Dayal Antyodaya Yojana–National Rural Livelihoods Mission), DDU-GKY (Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Grameen Kaushalya Yojana), Skill India, linked to Mission Shakti's Sakhi network; success measured by outcomes — access to entitlements, platform navigation, better-paid work|
|Digital safety as precondition for participation|Address tech-facilitated gender-based violence (deepfakes, harassment, non-consensual imagery) causing a "chilling effect"; leverage IT (Amendment) Rules, 2021 grievance mechanisms; MeitY's proposed mandatory labelling of AI-generated synthetic content; National Commission for Women's cyber law review offers evidence-based recommendations needing inter-ministerial action|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Existing AI Infrastructure in India</mark>

- IndiaAI Mission
    
- BHASHINI (BHASHa Interface for India) — multilingual platform
    
- AI Governance Guidelines (Nov 2025)
    
- India AI Impact Summit 2026 — showcased a growing ecosystem of practitioners, innovators, and civil society
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Terms</mark>

- Gender Responsive Budgeting (GRB) — budgeting framework tested via four accountability questions on AI spend.
    
- Chilling effect — discouragement of women's digital participation due to fear of online harassment/deepfakes.
    
- Viksit Bharat 2047 — India's vision of becoming a developed nation by its 100th year of independence.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Authors' Conclusion</mark>

The authors argue that by 2047, India's AI success should be measured not by aggregate national indicators alone, but by whether women in informal work gained meaningful, productive access to AI-enabled tools and opportunities — framing this as central to the Viksit Bharat vision.
