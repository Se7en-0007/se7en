## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Issue</mark>

A July 27, 2026 joint letter from the Union Ministry of Tribal Affairs and Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) has sparked concern among forest rights experts that it could dilute Gram Sabha authority under the Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What The Letter Says</mark>

- Issued jointly by two ministries on July 27, 2026, directed at state-level monitoring committees.
    
- Asks states to prepare action plans for Community Forest Resource Management Plans (CFRMP).
    
- Requires these plans be prepared "in consonance with the approved Working Plans" (forest department management plans) and integrated with them.
    
- Framed as "convergence" of central and state scheme resources, including forestry funds, since Community Forest Resource (CFR) areas often lie within larger forest landscapes.
    
- Government's stated rationale: convergence would help forestry funds complement and strengthen community forest management.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Expert Concerns</mark>

- Critics argue the letter subordinates the statutory authority of Gram Sabhas to an administrative forest governance framework.
    
- Y Giri Rao (National Resource Centre for Rights, Conservation and Climate Justice) called it potentially "ultra vires" (beyond legal powers) if treated as binding, since it places statutory community forest rights under an administrative regime.
    
- C R Bijoy (resource conflict/governance researcher) said such communications signal bureaucracy to take de facto control of CFR areas, undermining Section 5 of the FRA, which mandates Gram Sabhas to protect, conserve, manage and regulate forests, wildlife and biodiversity.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Relevant Legal Provisions (FRA)</mark>

- Section 5: Gram Sabha empowered to protect, conserve, manage and regulate forest, wildlife and biodiversity.
    
- Section 6(1), Rule 4: Gram Sabha is the primary authority to initiate determination of forest rights — receiving, verifying, and passing resolutions on claims; also demarcates customary/traditional boundaries.
    
- Gram Sabhas are required to constitute committees under FRA provisions to carry out these functions.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">"Rights Law" vs "Management Scheme" Debate</mark>

- FRA was enacted to recognise and vest forest rights in forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers, correcting "historical injustice."
    
- Experts say FRA envisioned a rights-based, community-centred governance model rooted in local tradition, culture and ecology.
    
- The letter, combined with earlier guidelines, is seen as making the National Working Plan Code, 2023 the basis for CFRMPs — reframing a rights-based law into a bureaucratic management scheme.
    
- Concern: forest dwellers risk being reduced from rights-holders/protectors to mere beneficiaries of state schemes.
    
- The National Resource Centre for Rights, Conservation and Climate Justice plans to submit a memorandum to the Tribal Affairs Ministry urging adherence to FRA's spirit.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Timeline of Related Communications</mark>

|Date|Communication|Key Feature|
|---|---|---|
|June 2021|Joint communication (Tribal Affairs + MoEFCC)|Called for convergence; frontline forest staff to assist CFRMCs; plans to integrate with departmental Working Plans|
|September 2023|Tribal Affairs guidelines on CFR conservation/management|Replaced 2015 guideline (under Section 12, FRA) that gave Gram Sabhas greater autonomy; introduced District Level CFR Monitoring Committees (not in Act/Rules); required district committee authorisation for Gram Sabha bank accounts|
|March 2024|Joint communication|Described CFR management plans as "scientific documents" needing field data; allowed co-option of forest officers; proposed model CFR plans per National Working Plan Code, 2023|
|May 2024|MoEFCC letter to state forest chiefs|Asked sharing of digitised forest boundary maps (shapefiles) with tribal departments for an FRA atlas — advocates say maps not shared in practice|
|July 27, 2026|Latest letter|CFRMPs to align with approved Working Plans|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Takeaways</mark>

- Trend: A progressive administrative/bureaucratic tightening of control over community forest resources since 2021, moving away from the Gram Sabha-centric model envisioned in FRA 2006.
    
- District Level CFR Monitoring Committees and bank account authorisation requirements are flagged as not sanctioned by FRA or its Rules.
    
- Core tension: forest department's "scientific"/administrative Working Plan framework versus FRA's rights-based, community sovereignty framework.
    
- Legal angle: allegations of the letter being "ultra vires" and defying Parliament's intent in enacting FRA.
