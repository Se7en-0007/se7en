### Background

- Matter arose from a plea by 3S and Our Health Society, a Kerala-based non-profit, before the Supreme Court.
- Supreme Court had earlier (February 2026) asked the Centre to consider introducing Front of Pack (FOP) labels.
- FSSAI filed a compliance affidavit (via Kavitha Ramaswamy, Joint Director, FSSAI) before the Supreme Court on Friday, August 28, 2026.

### The Proposal - Front of Pack Nutrition Labelling (FOPNL)

- Red hexagonal symbol to be placed on front of packaged food products.
- Applies to foods high in "nutrients of concern": added saturated fat, sugar, and salt.
- Thresholds based on the Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2024 (issued by ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition).
- Label will carry specific declarations: "high fat", "high sugar", "high salt", and/or "highly sweetened beverage".
- Font size of the warning to be one point larger than the nutrition information table on the back of the pack.
- Objective: simple, prominent, easily comprehensible warning to help consumers make informed food choices, especially for children and vulnerable groups.

### Two-Phase Rollout

|Phase|Coverage|
|---|---|
|Phase 1|Products high in at least two of the three nutrients (fat, sugar, salt)|
|Phase 2|Extended to products high in even one nutrient|

- Stated rationale for phasing: to facilitate consumer acceptability and give industry adequate time for reformulation.

### Exemptions

- Single-ingredient food products.
- Foods inherently rich in fat, sugar or salt - e.g., ghee, edible oil, salt, sugar, jaggery, honey - subject to other food safety/labelling regulations.

### Centre's Objection & Court's Response

- Centre's stance (Additional Solicitor General Brijender Chahar, August 23 hearing): difficult to follow international packaging standards; one-third of MSMEs are based on traditional foods; labelling could mark almost all traditional Indian foods (e.g., namkeen, even eggs) as "unhealthy"; international norms shouldn't be blindly copied since Indian foods are distinct.
- Supreme Court rejected this argument, asking whether India should "remain an underdeveloped country" by not matching international standards, and questioned industry-pressure influence on child health outcomes.

### Constitutional Basis Cited by Court

- Article 21 (Right to Life) - held to encompass the Right to Health, casting a duty on the State to refrain from health-impairing actions and take affirmative steps to protect health.
- Article 47 - casts a duty on the State to improve public health as a primary duty (Directive Principle of State Policy).
