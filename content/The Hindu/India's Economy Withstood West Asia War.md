## Context

- India recorded GDP growth above 7% and a sharp fall in inflation over the past three financial years.
- Analysts expected the US-Israeli war on Iran to disrupt this trend via higher crude oil, fertiliser and import prices; reduced foreign investment; weaker export demand; worsening trade balance; and a weaker rupee.
- El Niño added a compounding risk by threatening food production and inflation.
- Pre-war projections: GDP growth expected to fall below 7% (some predicted ~6%); inflation expected to jump from 2% (2025-26) to near 6%, from RBI's comfort-zone floor to its ceiling.
- On May 11, PM Modi appealed for austerity measures - cutting gold purchases, reducing fuel use, working from home, using public transport.

## The "Goldilocks" Concept

- "Goldilocks economy" refers to an optimal state - not too hot, not too cold - used earlier this year to describe India's macro conditions.
- The term fell out of favour once the war exposed vulnerabilities, especially the sharp rupee depreciation.
- Six months into the war, HSBC's research note observes: growth remains high, inflation remains low, and current account deficit is surprisingly contained - prompting debate on whether India has returned to a Goldilocks state or is masking deeper risks.

## GDP Growth: Current Status

- CMIE noted India likely "sailed through" the June 2026 quarter better than feared, despite the worst energy crisis in Q1 (Apr–Jun) of FY 2026-27.
- HSBC's 100-indicator growth database estimates GDP growth of 7–7.5% for this period.
- SBI Research projects an even higher 8% growth for Q1 FY 2026-27.

### Reasons for Growth Resilience

- Repo rate cut of 125 basis points (1.25 percentage points) between December 2024 and December 2025, which lowers borrowing costs for banks, businesses and individuals, boosting economic activity (transmission effects typically take 2 quarters/6 months).
- GST rate cuts in 2025 lowered prices, improved purchasing power, and increased consumption.
- Rise in India's exports to the US after Trump's tariffs were removed.
- Manufacturers front-loaded production due to anxiety over future energy availability.

## Inflation Trends

- Retail inflation has risen since October 2025 but remains contained, close to RBI's 4% target - far below the feared surge to 6%.
- Inflation has stayed muted despite steady demand and supply pressures (unusual, since low inflation is typically linked to weak growth, not the case here).
- Food and non-food goods inflation is already averaging 5.4% year-on-year (July).
- Services inflation (33% weight in retail inflation calculation) remains "remarkably low" at 2.5%, and is what's currently keeping headline inflation contained.
- Risk flagged by HSBC: if services inflation rises to reflect actual growth levels, headline inflation could surge quickly.

## Current Account Deficit (CAD)

- Current account tracks net money flow from India's trade in goods and services with the world.
- Fast growth plus costlier imports (like oil) typically widens the trade deficit (import spending exceeds export earnings).
- When export earnings (including services) plus remittances fail to cover the goods trade gap, it results in a Current Account Deficit.
- Despite fast growth and costly imports, India's CAD is low - just 0.3% of GDP per RBI's monthly aggregated data, showing no signs of excess.
- Reason: even as the goods trade deficit grows, rising services exports and remittances are offsetting it, keeping overall CAD contained.
- Risk: uncertainty around AI's impact on services exports growth; services export growth has already softened this year, raising doubts on how long services can keep funding a rising goods deficit.

## Risks to the "Resilience" Narrative

|Indicator|Optimistic Read|Risk/Counter-read|
|---|---|---|
|GDP growth|7–8% estimated for Q1 FY27|Credit growth partly driven by government credit guarantee schemes, higher working-capital needs from costlier commodities, and gold loan growth (a stress indicator)|
|Manufacturing|Front-loaded production supported output|Frontloading may be followed by a production lull|
|Agriculture|Stable so far|Could weaken if El Niño intensifies later in the year|
|Inflation|Headline close to 4% target|Food & non-food inflation already at 5.4%; low services inflation (2.5%) is masking true pressure|
|Current Account|CAD low at 0.3% of GDP|Rising services exports/remittances are offsetting a growing goods deficit; sustainability uncertain amid AI-related risks to services exports|

## Key Structural Insight: Role of Services Sector

- Services account for 55% of India's GDP and are currently the key stabiliser across all three indicators - growth, inflation, and external balance.
- Low services inflation and high services export growth are jointly keeping inflation and external imbalances in check.
- If services inflation rises or services export growth weakens, the RBI may be forced to raise interest rates, which would dampen overall GDP growth - creating a potential negative feedback loop.
