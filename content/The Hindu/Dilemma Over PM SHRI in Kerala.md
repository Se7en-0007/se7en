PM SHRI stands for "PM Schools for Rising India," a centrally sponsored scheme meant to upgrade select schools as model institutions. Implementation requires signing a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Union Government, and non-signatory or withdrawing states risk losing associated central funding.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Kerala's Political Timeline</mark>

- October last year: The then LDF government under Chief Minister Pinarayi Vijayan signed the MoU for PM SHRI implementation in Kerala.
    
- The Congress-led UDF (then in Opposition) criticised Vijayan's move as a "betrayal of the national cause" against the NEP 2020.
    
- Kerala Assembly elections were held, and the UDF, led by V.D. Satheesan, came to power.
    
- Once in government, the UDF reversed its earlier stance, arguing it was compelled to continue with PM SHRI since Kerala was already bound by the LDF-era MoU.
    
- A little over a month into government, Satheesan stated only the Union government could withdraw from the MoU, a claim contested by the current Opposition.
    
- The Chief Minister later denied the government had committed to implementing PM SHRI, even as UDF convener Adoor Prakash said the scheme would go ahead.
    
- A UDF Cabinet sub-committee was formed to study the issue; its report is still awaited.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Dilemma for UDF</mark>

The UDF government faces a three-way conflict between fiscal need, coalition unity, and ideological consistency.

- Fiscal pressure: ₹1,158.13 crore in education funds is being withheld by the Centre pending implementation.
    
- Ideological consistency: UDF had earlier opposed the NEP 2020 and criticised the LDF for signing the MoU.
    
- Coalition pressure: Allies like IUML and Muslim organisations are vocal in opposition, creating internal fault lines.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Precedent: Punjab Case</mark>

- Punjab opted out of the PM SHRI scheme in 2023.
    
- It later reversed its decision after the Union government froze related funds, illustrating the leverage central funding gives the Centre over reluctant states.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Curriculum Concerns</mark>

- The PM SHRI framework mandates the curriculum follow the National Curriculum Framework/State Curriculum Framework, itself based on the NEP's structure.
    
- The MoU requires states to implement all NEP provisions "in its entirety."
    
- NEP nominally allows states to design their own curricula/textbooks incorporating state-specific needs, but stipulates the NCERT curriculum as the "nationally acceptable criterion."
    
- Former Kerala Education Minister V. Sivankutty had earlier pointed out that the Union government, in 2022, declared Samagra Shiksha's objective was to help implement the NEP, linking two schemes together.
    
- Apprehension persists that even without direct curriculum interference now, future interventions (curriculum-based programmes, prescribed teaching materials, assessment patterns) could be imposed on states.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">UDF's Available Options</mark>

1. Legal route: Challenge the scheme/MoU in court, as Tamil Nadu has done.
    
2. Negotiation route: Write to the Union government seeking explicit freedom to decide Kerala's own curriculum while remaining in the scheme.
    
3. Non-implementation: Refuse to proceed, risking loss of the withheld ₹1,158.13 crore central funding.
