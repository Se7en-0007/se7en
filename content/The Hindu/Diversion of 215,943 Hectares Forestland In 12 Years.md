## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview</mark>

India approved diversion of 215,943 hectares (ha) of forestland for non-forest use between 2014–15 and 2025–26 (a 12-year period), based on official data from the Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) presented in Parliament. Nearly 62 per cent of this diverted land went towards mining, hydropower, irrigation, and road projects - the four dominant sectors driving forest loss.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Period-Wise Diversion Trends</mark>

The data shows a clear acceleration pattern when broken into three phases:

|Period|Total Diversion (ha)|Annual Average (ha/year)|Daily Rate (ha/day)|
|---|---|---|---|
|2014–15 to 2018–19|74,705.68|14,941|41|
|2019–20 to 2023–24|96,112.90|19,223|53|
|2024–25 to 2025–26 (2 yrs)|45,124.64|22,562|61|

Diversion rose by nearly 29 per cent between the first and second five-year periods.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The "Two Years vs Five Years" Comparison</mark>

The most striking finding is that just two years (2024–25 and 2025–26) accounted for a disproportionately large share compared to entire five-year periods:

- Equal to nearly 60 per cent of the total diverted during the entire 2014–15 to 2018–19 period (74,705.68 ha)
    
- Equal to about 47 per cent of the total diverted during 2019–20 to 2023–24 (96,113 ha)
    
- In 17 states/UTs, the two-year diversion (2024–25, 2025–26) was 60 per cent or more of the entire preceding five-year (2014–15 to 2018–19) total
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Highest-Ever Diversion Year: 2025–26</mark>

2025–26 alone recorded 26,858 ha diverted - the highest in the 12-year dataset and a 47 per cent jump over 2024–25.

- Six states recorded their highest-ever forestland diversion since 2014–15: Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Jharkhand, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Sikkim
    
- In 22 states/UTs, diversion in 2025–26 exceeded 2024–25 levels
    
- Four states - Madhya Pradesh (+116%), Chhattisgarh (+94%), Jharkhand (+82%), Arunachal Pradesh (+43%) - together accounted for over 57 per cent of the total forestland diverted nationally in 2025–26
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Himalayan and North-Eastern States: Sharpest Increases</mark>

Ecologically sensitive Himalayan and North-Eastern regions showed the most alarming spikes:

- Arunachal Pradesh: 6,038.43 ha diverted in just two years (2024–25, 2025–26) - over 8 times the 751.21 ha diverted in the entire 2014–15 to 2018–19 period, and 69 per cent of the 8,744.79 ha diverted in 2019–20 to 2023–24
    
- Sikkim: 778.95 ha diverted in two years - almost 19 times the 41.87 ha diverted in 2014–15 to 2018–19, and 3.7 times the 212.56 ha diverted in 2019–20 to 2023–24
    
- Himachal Pradesh: 1,295.4 ha diverted in two years - about 92 per cent of the 2014–15 to 2018–19 total, and 63 per cent of the 2019–20 to 2023–24 total
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Policy Drivers Behind the Acceleration</mark>

Two regulatory changes are identified as likely contributors to the surge:

- Forest (Conservation) Amendment Act, 2023: Narrowed the legal definition of "forest" and exempted certain strategic linear projects (roads, pipelines, etc.) from forest clearance requirements
    
- Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Amendment Rules, 2025 (notified 31 August 2025): Allow certain defence, strategic, and public-interest projects to seek approvals offline, potentially bypassing state government clearance processes
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Future Projection</mark>

If the current pace continues, forestland diversion during 2024–25 to 2028–29 could exceed 110,000 hectares - around 50 per cent more than the 2014–15 to 2018–19 total, and about 17 per cent higher than the 2019–20 to 2023–24 total.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Terms for Revision</mark>

- Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980: The renamed/amended Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980, under which all these diversions are approved
    
- Data source: Presented by Kirti Vardhan Singh, Minister of State, MoEFCC, in Parliament (July 2026)
    
- Sectors driving diversion: Mining, hydropower, irrigation, roads (~62% combined share)
