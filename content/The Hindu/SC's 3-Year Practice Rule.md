## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Development (2026 Ruling)</mark>

- Supreme Court, by 2:1 majority (August 21, 2026), retained the requirement of prior professional exposure before entering subordinate judiciary but changed how it can be fulfilled.
    
- Majority bench: Chief Justice of India Surya Kant and Justice A G Masih.
    
- Dissenting judge: Justice K Vinod Chandran.
    
- Key change: The "practice" requirement no longer needs to come exclusively from practice at the Bar; it can now include one year of judicial academic training plus one year of supervised clerkship, combined with actual practice, to make up the three-year requirement.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Timeline of the Rule's Evolution</mark>

|Period|Rule|Rationale/Body|
|---|---|---|
|Pre-1993 (varied by state)|Most states required minimum 3 years of legal practice before sitting for Civil Judge (Junior Division) exam|State-level practice|
|1993|SC made 3-year practice rule uniform nationally|SC held that recruiting fresh graduates without lawyering experience "had not proved to be a successful experiment"; book knowledge/pre-service training no substitute for court experience|
|2002|3-year practice requirement scrapped; fresh law graduates allowed to enter exam process immediately after graduation|Based on Shetty Commission recommendations|
|May 2025|3-year practice requirement reinstated; period counted from Bar Council enrolment; time as law clerk countable; 1 year training mandated before regular posting|SC held 20 years of recruiting untrained fresh graduates "has not been a successful experience"|
|August 2026|Central conclusion retained, but requirement can be fulfilled via combination of practice, training, and clerkship (not just Bar practice)|Present ruling; balances maturity requirement with attracting capable young lawyers|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Shetty Commission (Background Context)</mark>

- Chaired by Justice K J Shetty.
    
- Primary mandate: pay and service conditions in subordinate judiciary; additionally reviewed judicial recruitment and qualifications.
    
- Key finding: Law graduates were already exposed to courts via internships and clinical training during law school.
    
- Conclusion: Prescribing 3 years of advocacy practice as a precondition was "futile" for ensuring intimate knowledge of court work.
    
- This recommendation led directly to the 2002 removal of the practice requirement.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">2025 Order - Key Features</mark>

- Reinstated 3-year practice requirement before appearing for judicial service exam.
    
- Practice period counted from date of enrolment with State Bar Council.
    
- Time spent as a law clerk with judges could count toward the requirement.
    
- Mandated at least 1 year of training before assuming a regular judicial post.
    
- Drew review petitions on two main grounds:
    
    - Petitioners argued the Court relied more heavily on affidavits favouring the rule while giving less weight to opposing states, and cited no empirical evidence that fresh graduates perform worse as judges.
        
    - Fairness argument: two decades of law students had planned careers assuming immediate exam eligibility post-graduation; sudden reintroduction disrupted this.
        

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">2026 Order - Detailed Framework</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Rationale Retained</mark>

- A civil judge deals with questions of "life, liberty, property and personal rights" from day one on the bench.
    
- Prior court exposure held to build judicial temperament, patience, empathy, courtroom discipline, and appreciation of Bench-Bar roles.
    
- Court acknowledged unequal paths to establishing oneself at the Bar - women candidates may face social/familial constraints; persons with disabilities may face difficulty accessing meaningful practice opportunities.
    
- However, the Court did not create separate eligibility standards for different candidate categories.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">New Operational Mechanism</mark>

Practical exposure can now be built through a "combination of professional and institutional experience" - not limited to Bar practice alone.

**Transition period (May 20, 2025 to March 31, 2027):**

- Graduates deemed to have completed one year of practice automatically, without needing a certificate.
    
- Selected candidates undergo:
    
    - One year of training as "training judicial officer" at the State Judicial Academy
        
    - One year of supervised clerkship
        
- Both years count toward the overall 3-year requirement.
    

**From April 2027 onwards:**

- Candidates must have at least one year of actual practice, certified under a mechanism prescribed by High Courts, before appearing for the exam.
    
- Successful candidates must still additionally complete:
    
    - One year of academy training
        
    - One year of supervised clerkship
        
- These occur before taking up regular judicial duties.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Additional Rulings</mark>

- Postgraduate legal education (LLM etc.) rejected as a substitute for the practice requirement - academic study cannot replace familiarity with actual functioning of courts.
    
- The new framework will operate for five years, after which its effectiveness will be formally reviewed.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Dissenting Opinion (Justice K Vinod Chandran)</mark>

- Disagreed with counting academy training and clerkship as substitutes for part of the practice requirement.
    
- Agreed courtroom exposure is necessary, but held that the original 2025 order should remain unchanged.
    
- Warned against creating "a cadre of careerists rather than jurists capable of enriching a robust system."
    
- Rejected comparison with other government services: judicial officers exercise independent decision-making from day one, insulated from administrative supervision, correctable only through appeals/revisions.
    
- Argued young lawyers do gain value from early Bar years even without major briefs, using the analogy: senior lawyers/litigants would not entrust cases to fresh graduates, so it is unsafe to entrust them with judicial decision-making.
    
- View: professional judgment develops through lived experience; "training schedules" cannot substitute for "the feel of life and its worries, throbbing in every brief."
