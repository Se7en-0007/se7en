India's nuclear energy programme rests on a safety-first design combined with expanding applications across healthcare, agriculture, mining, semiconductors and green hydrogen, backed by the new SHANTI Act, 2025 and the Nuclear Energy Mission for Viksit Bharat.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Programme Overview and Targets</mark>

- Began with Tarapur Atomic Power Station in 1969; India now operates 24 nuclear power reactors across seven sites with installed capacity of 8.78 GW.
- Nine additional reactors are under construction; preparations continue for 10 more units.
- Nuclear Energy Mission for Viksit Bharat targets 100 GW of nuclear capacity by 2047.
- Union Budget 2025–26 allocated ₹20,000 crore for indigenous Small Modular Reactors (SMRs).
- SHANTI Act, 2025 strengthens the legal/regulatory framework for safe, secure expansion.
- India works closely with the IAEA on safety, security, and safeguards.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Carbon-free Energy Credentials</mark>

- Nuclear power is India's most carbon-efficient clean energy source per unit of installed capacity.
- FY 2025–26 CO2-equivalent emissions avoided per GW: Nuclear 5.4 million tonnes; Hydropower 2.7 million tonnes; Wind 1.6 million tonnes; Solar 0.9 million tonnes.
- Since 1969, cumulative emissions avoided: 851 million tonnes CO2 equivalent — comparable to annual carbon sequestration of over 38 billion mature trees.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Applications Beyond Electricity</mark>

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Healthcare</mark>

- Key institutions: BARC, IGCAR, Tata Memorial Centre (TMC), TIFR, Harish-Chandra Research Institute (HRI) — developing radiopharmaceuticals, imaging tech, and cancer therapies.
- 150-bed Homi Bhabha Cancer Hospital and Research Centre, Muzaffarpur, inaugurated in 2025.
- FY 2024–25: TMC registered 1.3 lakh patients; screened ~5 lakh women for oral, breast, cervical cancers.
- Indigenous radiation technologies sterilised 1.53 crore medical devices.

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Agriculture</mark>

- Radiation-induced mutagenesis plus cross-breeding produces higher-yield, drought/heat/salinity-tolerant crop varieties.
- BARC has developed 70 crop varieties, including TBM-9 banana and RTS-43 sorghum (released 2025).
- Collaboration with ICAR and agricultural universities for crop improvement and food security.

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Food Preservation</mark>

- Radiation extends shelf life of mangoes (enabling sea-route export), onions, and potatoes, reducing spoilage.
- Approved by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI).
- 17 MoUs signed in 2025 to expand irradiation infrastructure; six new gamma radiation processing facilities commissioned, taking the total to 40.

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Mining and Rare Earth Elements</mark>

- Nuclear analytical techniques improve ore assessment, extraction, and quality control for critical minerals.
- India released its first Certified Reference Material for Rare Earth Elements: Ferrocarbonatite (FC) – BARC B1401 — first of its kind in India, fourth globally.

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Semiconductors and Electronics</mark>

- High-purity isotopes and speciality materials support precision fabrication and strategic electronics.
- India's first Electronics-grade (99.8% purity) Boron-11 Enrichment Facility established at Talcher, strengthening the India Semiconductor Mission and reducing import dependence.

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Green Hydrogen</mark>

- Nuclear energy offers carbon-free hydrogen production via electricity plus high-temperature process heat.
- In 2026, India inaugurated the world's first hydrogen production facility using nuclear process heat, at Kalpakkam — supports Net Zero and National Green Hydrogen Mission goals.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Safety Architecture of Nuclear Plants</mark>

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Design-Level Safety (Defence in Depth)</mark>

- Multiple layers of protection: high-quality design, rigorous quality control, fail-safe engineering.
- Continuous monitoring, regular testing, redundant safety systems.
- Multiple physical barriers: ceramic fuel pellets, sealed zirconium alloy fuel rods, pressure vessel/tubes, reinforced concrete containment.
- Independent backup systems ensure emergency shutdown, core cooling, and backup power.
- Plants designed to withstand earthquakes, floods, cyclones, tsunamis.

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Radiological Protection During Operations</mark>

- Follows ALARA principle (As Low As Reasonably Achievable).
- AERB occupational dose limits: average 20 mSv/year over 5 years; cumulative 100 mSv; max 30 mSv in any single year.
- Public annual radiation dose limit in India: 1 mSv.
- Dedicated Health Physics Unit at every plant monitors radiation, exposure, and environmental releases.

### <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Radioactive Waste Management</mark>

- Governed by AERB under the Atomic Energy (Safe Disposal of Radioactive Wastes) Rules, 1987 and AERB Safety Code.
- BARC's Environmental Survey Laboratories provide continuous monitoring.
- Liquid waste: treated, diluted, discharged only within stringent limits.
- Solid waste: processed and disposed of in engineered on-site facilities, matched to radioactivity levels.
- Vitrification technology (developed by BARC) converts high-level waste into stable glass blocks — India is among the few countries with this indigenous capability.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Nuclear Emergency Preparedness Framework</mark>

- Integrated into the National Disaster Management Plan and district disaster management plans.
- DAE leads technical preparedness via a dedicated Crisis Management Plan; AERB regulates.
- Every plant maintains AERB-approved On-site and Off-site Emergency Response Plans.
- Regular mock drills involving plants, district administration, and Disaster Management Authorities.
- Specialised training for police/responders; radiation detection equipment deployed at key locations.
- Medical preparedness via Ministry of Health, DAE, and NPCIL, including Radiation Emergency Medical Networks.
- Plants sited in low-population areas with a 16-km Emergency Planning Zone.
- Environmental Survey Laboratory at every plant monitors air, water, soil, vegetation, food.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">PHWR-Specific Safety</mark>

- Indian Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs) have two independent, diverse shutdown systems.
- Automatic shutdown on detecting abnormal conditions, with dedicated cooling systems to remove residual heat.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Radiation Monitoring Findings (UCIL, Jharkhand)</mark>

- Health surveys around Uranium Corporation of India Limited's mining areas found health issues comparable to other rural areas with similar socio-economic conditions.
- No evidence linking local disease patterns to radiation exposure.
- Continuous health monitoring and environmental surveillance conducted by UCIL with BARC and regulators.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Post-Fukushima Safety Review</mark>

- Following the 2011 Fukushima accident, every Indian nuclear power plant underwent a comprehensive safety review.
- All recommended short-term and medium-term safety enhancements have been completed; long-term upgrades continue.
