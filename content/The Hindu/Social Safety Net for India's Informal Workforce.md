## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context</mark>

Millions of Indians work informally—construction workers, gig workers, domestic help—forming the backbone of urban infrastructure and daily services. The Labour Ministry has integrated the e-Shram portal with State welfare Boards and job platforms, now covering over 300 million registered workers, marking a major milestone in digital inclusion by creating India's first comprehensive registry of unorganised labour.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Registration Alone Is Insufficient</mark>

Digital identity through e-Shram gives recognition but does not automatically protect a family from sudden medical emergencies, workplace injuries, or old-age insecurity. Over 90% of India's working population is in the unorganised sector, where earnings are low and employment insecure. A vast majority of registered informal workers earn less than ₹10,000 a month, leaving little room for savings, meaning a single illness, injury, or seasonal slump can wipe out months of income and push families into predatory debt.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Fundamental Flaw in Welfare Design</mark>

- Geographic and administrative fragmentation: welfare schemes are tied to local residential addresses and fixed occupational categories
    
- Migration breaks access: a worker moving from rural Odisha to a Gujarat factory, or shifting from farming to gig work, loses continuity of welfare access
    
- Inter-State migrant workers (backbone of construction and logistics) are especially penalised since local systems demand local documentation mobile families cannot provide
    
- Result: those who most need protection are excluded because their lives don't fit rigid geographic boundaries
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Proposed Solution: Portable Universal Architecture</mark>

- Single-window "universal social security account" linked to Aadhaar that follows workers across State lines and job changes
    
- Financing via smart, pooled mechanisms rather than fiscal excess:
    
    - Central budget allocations
        
    - Small automated welfare cess on digital platforms, logistics operators, and major infrastructure projects
        
    - Flexible micro-contributions via digital wallets for higher pension tiers
        

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Three Essential Protections to Guarantee</mark>

1. Immediate accident insurance
    
2. Seamless healthcare access
    
3. Baseline old-age pension
    

Health emergencies are cited as the single largest cause of sudden impoverishment among working-class families. Integrating e-Shram profiles with Ayushman Bharat would enable cashless treatment at any empanelled hospital nationwide, independent of local ration card constraints, preventing families from selling assets or pulling children out of school during crises.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Author's Core Argument</mark>

- Digital public infrastructure (e-Shram) has made the invisible visible by giving workers a recognised identity
    
- The next step for policy is converting this digital record into portable, lifelong resilience
    
- Economic development cannot rest on the "fragile shoulders" of unprotected workers
