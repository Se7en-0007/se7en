Despite the US-Israel-Iran war effectively closing the Strait of Hormuz since February 28, 2026 and triggering the biggest global energy shock since 1973-79, India has largely avoided major fertiliser shortages in the current kharif season — though DAP and complex fertilisers remain vulnerable while urea supply stays comfortable.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background: The Shock</mark>

- The Strait of Hormuz closure since February 28, 2026 caused the greatest global energy supply disruption in history, surpassing the 1973 oil crisis and the 1979 crisis.
    
- Before the war, 53-54% of India's LNG imports came from Qatar and UAE via long-term contracts.
    
- These contracts were disrupted due to shipping blockades and damage to liquefaction infrastructure from Iranian missile and drone strikes.
    
- Urea production fell sharply from 24.7 lakh tonnes (lt) in March 2025 to 17.5 lt in March 2026 due to LNG (feedstock) shortages.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Urea: The Success Story</mark>

**Production Recovery**

- Domestic urea output rebounded to 21 lt in April 2026, then 25.2 lt in May and 25.4 lt in June.
    
- Cumulative April-June 2026 production of 71.5 lt was 5.4% higher than 67.9 lt in the same period of 2025.
    

**Government Interventions**

- GAIL (India) Ltd and Indian Oil Corporation diversified LNG sourcing, pivoting to spot market purchases despite higher prices.
    
- New LNG sources included the US, Oman, Nigeria, Angola, Republic of Congo, Indonesia, Trinidad, and Norway — replacing the near-zero share from Qatar and UAE.
    
- Indian Potash Ltd (IPL) and National Fertilizers Ltd floated import tenders on April 4 and May 27, securing 25 lt and 17 lt of urea at $935-959 and $444.9-449.3 per tonne respectively.
    
- Rashtriya Chemicals and Fertilizers issued a third tender for 17 lt on July 29 (10 lt to western ports, 7 lt to eastern coast), with bids opening August 11, 2026.
    
- Total urea imports for April-June 2026 reached 25.1 lt, a massive jump from just 8.4 lt in the same period of 2025.
    
- The government has also absorbed higher import costs rather than passing them to farmers, though this pressures the fertiliser subsidy budget.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">DAP and Complex Fertilisers: The Problem Area</mark>

Understanding the nutrient framework helps clarify why DAP is harder to secure than urea.

|Fertiliser|Nutrient Provided|Status in 2026|
|---|---|---|
|Urea|Nitrogen (N)|Comfortable supply|
|DAP|Phosphorus (P)|Under stress|
|MOP (Muriate of Potash)|Potassium (K)|Reference nutrient|
|Complex fertilisers|N, P, K, Sulphur (S) blends|Declining production|
|SSP (Single Super Phosphate)|Low-P substitute for DAP|Declining production|

**Key Constraints**

- Production of DAP, complex fertilisers, and SSP all declined in April-June 2026 compared to the same period in 2025.
    
- Unlike urea, reduced domestic output has been compounded by lower imports, not offset by them.
    
- The crisis in phosphatic fertilisers stems from raw material shortages, particularly phosphoric acid and sulphur.
    

**Price Escalation of Phosphoric Acid** (cost plus freight, per tonne)

- January-March 2025: $1,055
    
- April-June 2025: $1,153
    
- July-December 2025: $1,258
    
- January-March 2026: $1,290
    
- April-June 2026: $1,360
    
- July-September 2026 (current quarter): $1,700
    

**The Sulphur Crisis — Root Cause**

- Sulphur is essential to produce sulphuric acid, which breaks down rock phosphate into phosphoric acid — without it, DAP, SSP, and complex fertilisers cannot be made.
    
- Landed sulphur prices were already elevated at $500-550 per tonne (versus a normal $150-250 range) even before the Iran war, due to Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian oil refineries and an export ban by a major supplier.
    
- The West Asia conflict further tightened supplies from QatarEnergy, Saudi Aramco, Abu Dhabi National Oil Company, and Iran itself.
    
- Current sulphur import prices stand around $1,100 per tonne.
    

**Ammonia Supply Squeeze**

- Ammonia, used as the nitrogen source for complex fertilisers, saw supply squeezed from QatarEnergy, Saudi Arabia's Maaden and SABIC, and Iran — all previously shipped through the Strait of Hormuz.
    
- Ammonia prices spiked to $850-900 per tonne in May 2026 before easing to $650-700 per tonne.
    

**DAP Import Status**

- Only one major DAP import tender has been contracted: 13.5 lt through IPL's mega-tender on April 28, 2026 at $930-935 per tonne — significantly higher than $725-750 a year earlier.
    
- No fresh DAP imports have been secured since, and prices are expected to rise further if India floats a new large tender.
    
- Industry experts suggest the government should focus on augmenting sulphur supply and providing additional subsidy support for DAP/NPKS manufacturers, similar to the urea model.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The El Niño Factor: Demand-Side Relief</mark>

A crucial reason shortages haven't materialized is weaker demand, not just better supply management.

- All-India rainfall during June-July 2026 was 12.6% below normal, unlike the surplus monsoons of 2024 and 2025.
    
- Area sown under kharif crops is down 4.7% compared to last year's coverage.
    
- In surplus monsoon years (2024, 2025), ample soil moisture and recharged groundwater had prompted farmers to plant aggressively and queue for fertilisers, causing scrambles.
    
- This year's slower fertiliser offtake, combined with proactive government stockpiling, has prevented shortage scenarios despite Iran war disruptions.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Political Context and Outlook</mark>

- With state elections due in Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, and Uttarakhand in February-March 2027, the government is expected to take extra precautions for fertiliser supply during the upcoming rabi (winter-spring) season.
    
- The core risk going forward centers on sulphur and phosphoric acid procurement rather than nitrogen/urea supply.
