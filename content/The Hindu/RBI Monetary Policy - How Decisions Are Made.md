## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Purpose of MPC Reviews</mark>

- The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meets once every two months to review India's economy and decide on interest rates.
    
- The core mandate is maintaining price stability by targeting retail inflation at 4%, with a tolerance band of 2% to 6%.
    
- Persistently high inflation erodes consumer purchasing power; persistently low inflation weakens producers' profit incentive.
    
- The MPC's three possible decisions in any meeting are: hike rates, cut rates, or maintain status quo.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Repo Rate Mechanism</mark>

- The repo rate is the interest rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks.
    
- When growth is weak and inflation is below target, RBI cuts the repo rate, making borrowing cheaper for banks, which then lowers lending rates for consumers and businesses, stimulating growth.
    
- When inflation rises too high (due to excess demand or supply shortages), RBI hikes the repo rate to dampen demand by raising borrowing costs across the economy.
    
- Monetary policy transmission is not instant — changes take several months to percolate through the economy, so RBI must anticipate future conditions, not just react to current data.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Current Inflation and Rate Context (as of August 2026)</mark>

- Retail inflation has risen through 2026 and is now just above the 4% target, though still within the 2%-6% comfort zone.
    
- This creates a dilemma: inflation near target with slowing growth could argue for a cut, while the rising trend could argue for a hike.
    
- Most economists expect RBI to maintain status quo in its August 5, 2026 policy review.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Factors Beyond Headline Inflation</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">1. Crude Oil Prices</mark>

- Higher crude oil prices raise domestic inflation, worsen the trade deficit, weaken the rupee, increase government debt, and slow economic growth.
    
- Since the US-Iran ceasefire in mid-June, crude oil prices have risen roughly 11% due to the ceasefire's inconsistent nature.
    
- If RBI expects oil prices to stay elevated, it may lean toward raising rates or keeping them high for longer.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">2. US-Iran Hostilities</mark>

- Continued conflict threatens energy supply through choke points like the Strait of Hormuz and now the Red Sea shipping routes.
    
- Persistent hostilities discourage any RBI plans to cut rates.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">3. Monsoon and El Niño Risk</mark>

- El Niño conditions are expected to intensify, per the India Meteorological Department.
    
- As of end-July, rainfall is 13% below average, with over 40% of the country in the deficient rainfall category; reservoir levels are lower than the previous year, threatening rabi crop sowing.
    
- Food inflation was already above 5% in June, and these developments could push RBI toward raising or holding rates.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">4. Quality of Growth Momentum</mark>

- RBI examines high-frequency indicators beyond headline GDP to assess the underlying health of growth.
    
- For the quarter ending June 2026, airline passenger traffic, rail freight, diesel consumption, and GST e-way bill growth all slowed compared to June 2025.
    
- Conversely, port cargo traffic, telecom subscriber growth, vehicle registrations, and GST collections maintained upward momentum.
    
- This mixed, uneven picture means growth is not broad-based, making RBI cautious about hiking rates since a hike could hurt sectors already struggling.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">5. Global Central Bank Actions</mark>

- India typically maintains higher interest rates than developed economies due to its higher inflation rate; this rate gap attracts global bond market investors.
    
- If other central banks raise rates, pressure builds on RBI to follow suit to preserve India's attractiveness for debt investment.
    
- However, higher rates also raise government borrowing costs and affect debt sustainability.
    
- As of their July meetings, the US Federal Reserve, European Central Bank, Bank of Japan, and Bank of England all held rates steady, reducing pressure on RBI to hike.
