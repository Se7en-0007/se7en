## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context & Significance of Urban Wetlands</mark>

- Wetlands (incl. urban lakes) cover ~6% of Earth's surface but are among the most productive ecosystems.
- They store a disproportionately high share of global carbon, often outperforming forests per unit area.
- Key functions: carbon sequestration, biodiversity support, climate regulation, human well-being.
- Most surviving urban lakes today are biologically dead or heavily eutrophic.
- Losing lakes erodes cities' natural defences against climate change, harms public health, and threatens urban liveability.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Causes of Lake Destruction</mark>

- Rapid, unplanned urbanisation drives destruction of water bodies.
- Developers and municipal bodies treat lakes as "vacant land" rather than critical ecological infrastructure, making them vulnerable to encroachment.
- Institutional and public apathy: lakes become dumping grounds for untreated sewage, industrial effluents, and municipal solid waste.
- Core issue: lakes die not just from encroachment but from being taken for granted.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Scale of the Crisis (Data Points)</mark>

- CAG reports flag large-scale lake shrinkage in Jammu & Kashmir — over 500 lakes lost since 1967.
- Hyderabad lost ~61% of its lake area since 1979.
- 268 water bodies in Hyderabad disappeared entirely between 2002–2012 (per one study).
- Cities face intensifying urban heat islands and unpredictable flooding as a consequence.
- Call to reframe lakes as "urban climate buffers" and "sponge systems" rather than liabilities.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Economic Drivers — "Taken for Granted"</mark>

- 50 years ago: affordable land, moderate urban expansion, lakes valued as functional civic assets.
- Today: land prices have multiplied manifold; peri-urban land (once agricultural fields/lake catchments) has become prime real estate in Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai.
- This price spike turns lakes into a perceived "opportunity cost," economically incentivising encroachment.
- Result: wetlands filled, fragmented, repurposed for housing, highways, commercial complexes.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Policy Gaps</mark>

| Aspect                   | Reality                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ramsar site designations | Formal, high-level recognition; India expanded its tally, but international status ≠ local conservation success                                                |
| Ground-level issues      | Ambiguous boundaries, ongoing encroachment, disrupted catchment areas                                                                                          |
| Government schemes       | Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT), Jal Shakti Abhiyan, Mission Amrit Sarovar — provide policy skeletons but weak on-ground impact |
| Root causes of failure   | Fragmented accountability, weak inter-departmental coordination, focus on beautification over ecological recovery                                              |
| Municipal priority       | Visible aesthetics (promenades, lighting, fencing) over substantive repair (feeder channels, catchments, water quality)                                        |

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Success Stories</mark>

- HYDRAA (Hyderabad Disaster Response and Asset Protection Agency) — Telangana's institutional initiative:
- Reclaimed encroached lakebeds and cleared illegal structures within Full Tank Level (FTL) zones.
- Desilted long-blocked feeder channels, restoring natural hydrological flows.
- Nearly doubled water spread of Nalla Cheruvu lake — from 16 to 30 acres.
- Grassroots "lake champions" in Bengaluru and Hyderabad revived water bodies such as Puttenahalli, Jakkur, and Kudikunta through community involvement.
- Limitation: These successes remain isolated, lacking institutionalised legal backing, sustained state funding, and statutory enforcement to become replicable models.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward — "Beyond Quick Fixes"</mark>

- Integrate water bodies formally into city planning processes.
- Provide absolute legal protection for lake boundaries and catchments, with strict, consistent penalties for encroachment.
- Streamline fragmented governance under unified, accountable authorities.
- Institutionalise citizen engagement within governance frameworks for permanent local ownership.
- Consequence framing: cities reducing lakes to mere landscaped backdrops will face floods, heat stress, water scarcity; cities restoring lakes as living ecosystems will build resilience for a warming, water-stressed future.
