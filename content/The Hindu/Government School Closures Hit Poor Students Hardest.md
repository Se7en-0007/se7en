## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Argument</mark>

Government schools in India are not neutral public utilities — they are disproportionately used by the poor, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and backward-class communities. School closures under the guise of "rationalisation" have a clear class character that hits the most vulnerable hardest.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Madhya Pradesh Case Study</mark>

- 7.37 lakh children in MP have no school records at all (not enrolled anywhere, private or government), mostly from tribal-dominated districts
    
- 29,000 children in Jhabua and Shivpuri fall in the "Drop Box" category (imminent risk of dropping out)
    
- Across 55 districts: 3,500+ government schools had zero new admissions this session; 6,500 more had fewer than ten
    
- Statewide "Drop Box" category: 11.38 lakh children
    
- 1.23 lakh already recorded as dropouts
    
- 1.55 lakh left school due to family displacement/migration for work
    
- Response: District Education Officers told to paste unmapped children's lists on noticeboards and upload details to state portal
    
- School Chalen Hum and Home Contact Campaign (flagship outreach programmes) failed to reach 750,000 children per the state's own audit

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">State-wise Breakdown (Lok Sabha data)</mark>

- Madhya Pradesh: 1.22 lakh (2018-19) → 99,411 (next year) — drop of ~22,600 in 12 months
    
- Uttar Pradesh: 1.63 lakh → 1.38 lakh in the same year — drop of over 25,000
    
- Two of the largest Hindi-belt states lost school infrastructure at nearly identical pace

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Jammu & Kashmir Rationalisation</mark>

- 4,300+ schools closed or merged under rationalisation scheme
    
- School count fell from 23,167 (2020-21) to 18,785 (2022-23)
    
- ~13,000 vacant teaching posts (reported separately in State Assembly)
    
- Children now travel 4-6 km daily to reach a functioning school — violates RTE's neighbourhood school mandate
    
- Girls are disproportionately withdrawn first when the nearest school becomes inaccessible

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Ground-Level Impact of Consolidation</mark>

- Single teacher often covers five classes simultaneously
    
- Cannot know individual child's background or tailor instruction
    
- Technically breaches RTE Act's own staffing and quality norms

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Centre vs. State Responsibility Debate</mark>

- Government's official position (Lok Sabha): education is on the Concurrent List; responsibility lies with states
    
- Counter-argument: RTE (providing for neighbourhood schools) is a Central Act, so mandates are Centre's responsibility
    
- Centre already influences state education by tying fund allocation to NEP implementation and imposing language policy — so it can intervene against closures if it chooses to

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical & Intellectual Context</mark>

- **Jyotirao Phule (1882 Hunter Commission memorial)**: Criticized colonial laissez-faire education policy — resources poured into higher education for the privileged while primary education of the masses (Shudra, Ati-Shudra cultivators/labourers) was underfunded and left to private/missionary institutions
    
- **Gopal Krishna Gokhale's Compulsory Education Bill (1911)**: Defeated; educationist Anil Sadgopal traces continued resistance to universal free education back to this defeat
    
- **Amartya Sen**: Credits Kerala's educational attainments to strong "public action"

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">RTE Act Limitations</mark>

- In force since 2010; guarantees free, compulsory education from age 6-14
    
- Secondary school and college were never brought under this guarantee — always available to private capital
    
- RTE is silent on quality of classroom delivery once a child is enrolled
    
- Creates entitlements children cannot assert themselves; poor/rural parents often lack legal/institutional capacity to enforce them
    
- Assumes power symmetry between state, school, and family that doesn't exist on ground
    
- School Management Committees (the one formal mechanism for parent/community voice) largely go unconvened
