# How NASA’s Roman Space Telescope Can Help Us Decode The Universe’s Greatest Mysteries

- **NASA launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on August 30** aboard a **SpaceX rocket**.
- It is a **powerful, next-generation space telescope** intended to search for clues to some of the universe’s deepest mysteries, including **dark matter** and **dark energy**.
- The telescope is named after **Nancy Grace Roman**, known as the **“mother of Hubble”** for her role in creating the **Hubble Space Telescope**, which has operated since **1990**.

## What Will The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Do?

- In scientific capability, the **Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope** lies between the **Hubble Space Telescope** and the **James Webb Telescope**.
- It can scan **vast swathes of the cosmos** and may provide new insights into **dark matter**, **dark energy**, and **exoplanets**.

### Exoplanets

- **Exoplanets** are planets located **outside our solar system**.
- They are often found orbiting a **star** or a **“remnant star”** - the remains of a star that has burnt out.
- Because it can scan a **wide area of space**, the Roman Space Telescope may identify many more exoplanets.
- This search could help answer the question: **Are we alone in the universe?**
- So far, **more than 6,000 exoplanets** have been identified.

### Dark Matter And Dark Energy

- Scientists estimate that **ordinary matter** makes up only about **5% of the universe**.
- The rest consists of the little-understood phenomena called **dark matter** and **dark energy**.
- **Dark matter** makes up about **27% of the universe** and is believed to be an **invisible glue that holds galaxies together**.
- **Dark energy** is estimated to make up around **68% of the universe** and is believed to be a **repulsive force** that has expedited the universe’s expansion since the **Big Bang**.
- The telescope will conduct the **most detailed dark matter studies ever**.
- These studies could help map how dark matter is **structured and distributed throughout the cosmos**, especially during the process of **early star birth**.

### Gravitational Lensing

- The telescope will help measure how **gravity subtly bends the path of light across vast distances**.
- This phenomenon is known as **gravitational lensing**.
- The Roman telescope will provide a comprehensive **three-dimensional map** of the distribution of **galaxies** and **galaxy clusters** across the universe.

## How Will The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Work?

- The Roman Space Telescope weighs about **8,000 kgs** and is about the size of a **tour bus**.
- It will operate from **Lagrange Point 2**, a vantage point between the **Earth** and the **Sun**, approximately **1.2 million kilometres from Earth**.
- The **James Webb Space Telescope**, launched in **2021**, also operates from this zone.
- At Lagrange Point 2, gravity from the **Sun** and **Earth** works together with an object’s motion around the Sun, helping the Roman telescope remain **steady**.
- The point also provides **stable optical performance** and an **unobstructed view of the universe** for imaging.
- The telescope will downlink approximately **1.4 terabytes of data per day** to ground stations in **New Mexico, Australia and Japan**.
- Over its **five-year mission life**, it is expected to contribute up to **20 petabytes of data**.

## Wide Field Instrument

- The **Wide Field Instrument** is a **300-megapixel infrared camera**.
- It can capture a patch of sky about **1.5 times bigger than the apparent size of a full Moon**.
- The telescope can process images **1,000 times faster than the Hubble telescope**.

## Coronagraph

- Astronomers use **coronagraphs** to mask the extremely bright light emitted by celestial bodies in order to study their immediate space surroundings.
- Like protective shields that allow humans to see the Sun’s rounded rim without looking directly at it, a coronagraph masks glare.
- The Roman Space Telescope will deploy one of the **most advanced coronagraphs**.
- In principle, it will block the glare from stars and enable scientists to see the **faint reflected light** from planets orbiting them.
- It aims to image the **dusty disks around nearby stars** in visible light.
- This could help see the **older, colder and fainter stars** in close vicinity.

## Who Was Nancy Grace Roman?

- **Nancy Grace Roman** was born in **1925** in the United States.
- She was NASA’s **first chief astronomer**.
- She made significant contributions to building **space-based telescopes** when **ground-based telescopes** were the preferred option.
- Roman died in **2018**.
- NASA is launching an **infrared space telescope** named after her more than a century after her birth.
- Roman was deeply involved in NASA’s **Great Observatories programme**, comprising:
- **Hubble Space Telescope**
- **Chandra X-ray Observatory**
- **Compton Gamma Ray Observatory**
- **Spitzer Space Telescope**
- These observatories took astronomy to newer heights, enabling scientists to study the universe across much of the **electromagnetic spectrum** and complement such study with **ground-based telescopes**.
