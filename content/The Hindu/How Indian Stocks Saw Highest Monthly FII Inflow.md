- **Foreign investors bought Indian equities worth $3.1 billion in August**, the **highest monthly inflow since September 2024**, when they bought shares worth **$6.9 billion**.
- This was the **second straight month of foreign buying**, following a **four-month selloff**.
- Foreign investment in Indian equities was **$2.1 billion in July**.

## Reasonable Valuations

- The renewed interest was driven by **reasonable valuations**, **better-than-expected April-June quarter earnings**, **relatively stable macroeconomic conditions**, and a **shift away from AI-heavy markets** in recent months.
- Between **March and June**, foreign investors sold Indian equities worth **$27.8 billion**, one of the worst four-month stretches in years.
- During this period, the benchmark **Nifty 50** and **Sensex** declined by around **5%**, weighed down by the **conflict in West Asia** and the **AI-led rally in global markets**.
- Since July, both indices are up around **1%**.
- Broader **mid-cap** and **small-cap indices** have gained **5-6%** over the same period, indicating opportunities across a wider range of the market.
- **Higher-than-expected earnings growth in Q1FY27** and reasonable valuations enabled foreign investors to buy Indian equities even while they continued selling stocks in the **AI-heavy South Korean market** through August.

## RBI Kept Policy Stable

- The **Reserve Bank of India** kept the **key interest rate steady** earlier in the month.
- However, the minutes indicated **rate hikes going ahead**, as inflation is expected to rise over **FY27**.
- A prolonged pause in policy eased uncertainty when valuations in several pockets of the Indian market had become reasonable after months of continuous selling.
- According to **Maxiom Wealth**, foreign investors turned buyers largely because the RBI kept policy stable while valuations had already absorbed months of selling pressure.
- The pattern observed earlier in Indian markets is that **heavy, sustained outflows tend to exhaust themselves once valuations catch down to global risk appetite**; then even a modest improvement in the macro picture can draw back some capital.
- The early-August inflow appeared to reflect this pattern rather than any single dramatic catalyst.

## Financial Services, Auto Among FIIs’ Top Picks

- During the **first half of August**, the **financial services sector** attracted the strongest interest, with FIIs buying shares worth **$685 million**.
- The **automobile sector** received inflows of **$462 million**.
- **Consumer services, healthcare and IT** also recorded robust inflows of between **$200 million and $400 million**.
- Except financial services, all these sectors had recorded positive inflows in the previous **15 days**.
- Data for the second half of August had not yet been released, but most of these sectors continued to perform well.
- **Healthcare** gained around **1.2%** in August, while **IT** and **auto** rose by up to **1%**.
- **Financial services** gained as much as **1.6%** in early August before giving up some of those gains.

## Foreign Flows To Remain Volatile

- Despite the revival over the past two months, most market experts view the buying as **temporary and opportunistic**, rather than the beginning of a **structural revival**.
- Foreign inflows are expected to remain volatile and dependent on **crude oil prices**, **geopolitical stability**, and **taxation**.
- The buying value remains insignificant compared with FIIs’ selling over the last **two years**.
- Structural buying may occur if **peace prevails in West Asia** and **oil prices fall by more than 20% from current levels**.
- The inflow coincided with the global **AI rally beginning to crack**, while earnings and other factors improved sentiment.

## Capital Gains Tax And STT

- Many market participants believe FIIs are unlikely to turn structurally positive on Indian equities unless taxation is eased.
- An **IIFL Capital** report argued that **capital gains tax (CGT) on FPIs should be removed** because it has created friction, caused **double taxation**, and imposed a **2.4 percentage-point CAGR hit on post-tax returns for FPIs**.
- India levies both **securities transaction tax (STT)** and **capital gains tax**.
- India is among the few major global markets that charge **STT on FPIs**.
- Removing STT is seen by many market participants, and in some cases bureaucrats, as a measure that would make the Indian market more attractive to FIIs.
- The final decision rests with the **Finance Ministry**.
