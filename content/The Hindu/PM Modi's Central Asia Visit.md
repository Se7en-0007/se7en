## Visit Overview

- PM Modi visited Uzbekistan (Aug 29-30, 2026) on a bilateral visit.
- He attended the 26th SCO Summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyz Republic (Aug 31 - Sept 1, 2026).

## Historical Background of India-Central Asia Engagement

- Indian PMs have visited Central Asia since Jawaharlal Nehru's time.
- India's focused engagement with the 5 Central Asian countries (Kazakhstan, Kyrgyz Republic, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan) began only in 2012 with the "Connect Central Asia" policy.
- Modi became the first Indian PM to visit all five Central Asian countries in July 2015, giving the policy a major boost.
- India invited Central Asian leaders for Republic Day celebrations in January 2022 as another outreach signal.

## Strategic Importance of Central Asia

- Region traditionally seen as Russia's backyard, with 20-30% ethnic Russian population and widespread use of Russian language.
- Chinese President Xi Jinping's September 2022 visit to four of the five Central Asian countries (his first overseas trip post-Covid) underscored the region's strategic weight.
- Region is exceptionally rich in minerals and natural resources, drawing global interest.

## Why India Engages With Central Asia

- Security cooperation, especially after the Taliban takeover of Afghanistan.
- Countering China's growing influence and Belt and Road investments in the region.
- Connectivity plans, particularly the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC).
- Energy needs - Kazakhstan's uranium and Turkmenistan's role in the proposed TAPI (Turkmenistan-Afghanistan-Pakistan-India) gas pipeline.
- Old cultural/civilisational links and trade potential.
- India treats Central Asia as part of its "extended neighbourhood."

## Key Challenges for India

- Lack of overland transport access - Pakistan blocks direct land connectivity to Central Asia.
- India seeks to integrate INSTC with Iran's Chabahar port to bypass this bottleneck.
- Central Asia is seen as the northern boundary of the Islamic world; rise of the Taliban raises risk of radicalism and possible regrouping of the Islamic State.
- Russia-Ukraine conflict has triggered inflow of skilled/wealthy Russians into these countries, which could boost local economies but also create social tension.

## India–Uzbekistan Civilisational and Cultural Links

- Deep historical connect for centuries.
- Kara Tepa (Termez, Uzbekistan) houses the only known Buddhist monastery in Central Asia, an important site during the Kushana era when Buddhism spread into the region.
- Nearly 3,000 shared words exist between Hindi and Uzbek.
- Cuisine parallels: ghee-sariyok, samosa-samsa, naan-non, pulao-pilaf.
- India ranks among Uzbekistan's top 10 trading partners; bilateral trade in 2025-26 was close to USD 1 billion.

## SCO Summit Details (Bishkek)

- 26th meeting of the SCO Council of Heads of State (SCO CHS), held August 31 - September 1, 2026.
- SCO has 10 member states: India, Belarus, China, Iran, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Pakistan, Russia, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan.
- 15 Dialogue Partners: Azerbaijan, Armenia, Bahrain, Egypt, Cambodia, Qatar, Kuwait, Laos, Maldives, Myanmar, Nepal, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Türkiye, Sri Lanka.
- 2 Observers: Mongolia, Afghanistan.
- India has been an SCO member since 2017.
- This edition marks the 3rd SCO+ format meeting, expected to include Azerbaijan, Armenia, Vietnam, Laos, Mongolia, Togo, Turkey, Egypt, Turkmenistan, along with bodies like the UN and Collective Security Treaty Organization (CSTO).
