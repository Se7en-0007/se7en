## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview of the Issue</mark>

The Supreme Court, under Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, deferred hearing a constitutional challenge to the Bodh Gaya Temple Act, 1949 on August 11. The petition, filed by the Buddhist Society of India and tagged with seven similar petitions, seeks repeal of the 1949 Act and demands a new Central law transferring "total management, control and administration" of the Mahabodhi Temple entirely to Buddhists.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Background of Bodh Gaya</mark>

- Prince Siddhartha attained enlightenment (nirvana) and became the Buddha in 589 BCE while meditating under the Bo tree at Bodh Gaya
    
- Emperor Ashoka built a shrine at the site in the 3rd century BCE
    
- The only surviving part of Ashoka's original construction is the Vajrasana (Diamond Throne), a stone slab beneath the Bodhi tree
    
- The broader Mahabodhi Temple Complex is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The 1949 Act: Structure and Governance</mark>

The Bodh Gaya Temple Act was passed by the Bihar legislature in 1949 to regulate management of the temple and its properties.

- Creates an eight-member management committee: four Hindu members and four Buddhist members
    
- Government nominates one secretary from among the committee members
    
- Gaya district magistrate serves as the ex-officio chairperson
    
- Act defines a "Mahanth" as the presiding priest of the Shaivite monastery at Bodh Gaya, who sits as one of the four Hindu members under Section 3(2)
    
- Section 13 excludes the Shaivite monastery's movable and immovable property from the committee's jurisdiction
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Arguments in the Petition</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Structural imbalance despite parity on paper</mark>

- Although the Act provides equal Hindu-Buddhist representation, actual control has skewed toward Hindus because district magistrates have historically been Hindu
    
- Between 1953 and 2007, only one of seven secretaries appointed under the Act was Buddhist
    
- Petitioners argue this shows the State remains the "ultimate owner and controller," since the government can dissolve or reconstitute the committee, or assume direct control, if it finds "gross mismanagement"
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Dilution of Buddhist religious character</mark>

- Section 11 states that "Hindus and Buddhists of every sect shall have access to the Temple and the Temple land for the purpose of worship and pindadan"
    
- Petitioners argue this creates a statutory carve-out for Hindu rituals (like pindadan, the offering of pindas to ancestors) inside a Buddhist place of worship
    
- The committee is also tasked with ensuring proper conduct of "worship" and "pindadan," which the petition says blurs the distinction between the two faiths
    
- The petition objects that the Buddha statue is draped and identified in a manner associated with Hindu worship
    
- It rejects the idea that Buddha is an incarnation of Vishnu, calling it a "misconception and myth," and notes Buddhism "does not involve a belief in any God"
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Role of the Mahanth</mark>

- The Mahanth's statutory inclusion as a Hindu committee member, along with the carve-out for the Shaivite monastery's property, is argued to entrench Hindu influence and reduce Buddhists to merely "symbolic representation"
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Constitutional Grounds Cited</mark>

|Article|Relevance to the Petition|
|---|---|
|Article 13|Pre-Constitution laws inconsistent with fundamental rights are void; since the Act predates the Constitution, it must be tested against constitutional guarantees|
|Articles 25 & 26|Religious freedom and denominational rights; used to argue administration should rest exclusively with Buddhists as a minority community|
|Article 14|Equality before law; alleges discrimination against Buddhists in the Act's structure|
|Article 29|Cultural and educational rights of minorities; cites Bodh Gaya's unique place in Buddhist history|
|Article 49|Obligates the State to protect monuments/places of national importance; basis for petition's demand that the site be declared of "national importance"|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Other Reliefs Sought</mark>

- Declaration of Bodh Gaya as a site of "national importance"
    
- Removal of alleged encroachments on temple land
    
- Separate land allotted specifically for Hindu worship, distinct from the main temple complex
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Link to the Sabarimala Case</mark>

The Supreme Court has deferred the Bodh Gaya hearing to October, pending its verdict in the nine-judge Sabarimala Temple reference. Both cases hinge on Articles 25 and 26 regarding the scope of religious denominational rights and autonomy, and the Sabarimala verdict is expected to clarify these principles before the Bodh Gaya matter is taken up.
