Unified Payments Interface (UPI) was launched as a pilot in April 2016 and became fully operational in August 2016, developed jointly by NPCI (regulated by RBI) and the Indian Banks' Association (IBA). Ten years on, UPI accounts for 86% of all digital transactions in India, used by over 55 crore people, with 703 entities (banks and payment service providers) involved in facilitating transactions.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Origins and Early Vision</mark>

- RBI's 2012-15 "vision document" noted the average Indian made only 6 non-cash transactions annually, and projected that if each citizen did one transaction a month, total transactions could reach 1,200 crore per year - a figure since dwarfed by reality.
    
- By 2025-26, digital transactions in India stood at 28,174 crore, most via UPI.
    
- The RBI's Committee on Comprehensive Financial Services for Small Businesses and Low Income Households (2013) foresaw "spontaneous proliferation" of mobile-based money services following the rise of IMPS (Immediate Payments Service).
    
- Nearly a third of e-commerce purchases were still cash-on-delivery at the time, a key motivator for building a digital alternative.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Growth Trajectory</mark>

- Demonetisation (November 2016) gave a short-term boost, but growth remained slow initially - monthly transaction value stayed under Rs 10,000 crore until December 2017, crossing Rs 1 lakh crore only a year later.
    
- The Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) - a fee merchants paid for accepting digital payments - was identified as a major hindrance to adoption.
    
- The Nandan Nilekani-led High-Level Committee on Deepening of Digital Payments (May 2019) recommended zero MDR for customers/small merchants and government subsidy for transactions up to Rs 2,000.
    
- In 2020, the government introduced an incentive scheme subsidising UPI/RuPay debit card P2M transactions up to Rs 2,000, capped at 0.15% of transaction value, shared among banks, PSPs, and app providers.
    
- COVID-19 pandemic (2020-21) accelerated adoption further as people avoided physical cash handling.
    
- Comparative growth: credit/debit card transactions (excluding ATM withdrawals) grew only 17% between November 2019 and May 2026 (70 crore transactions), whereas UPI transactions grew by 1,800% in the same period.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Investment and Market Structure</mark>

- Private investment in Indian fintech spiked in 2019 and 2021, per a December 2024 BIS paper - 2019's surge tied to UPI adoption and big deals by Paytm/PhonePe; 2021's surge linked to post-pandemic digital payment preference.
    
- Investment was concentrated in firms offering payment services and point-of-sale (PoS) devices.
    
- Market dominance: Walmart-backed PhonePe and Google Pay together handled 80% of UPI transactions by volume and 83% by value in July 2026.
    
- Traditional banks have been squeezed out - SBI Chairman C S Setty admitted "we have definitely missed the payments bus," with SBI handling just 0.1% of transactions by volume (0.2% by value), ranking fourth among banks.
    
- Kotak Mahindra Bank was the top-ranked bank with only 0.6% share by volume and value - showing how non-bank apps dominate.
    
- Market-share caps (30% limit per player) meant to curb duopoly have been postponed repeatedly; current deadline is December 2026.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Cost Crisis</mark>

|Aspect|Detail|
|---|---|
|Annual infra cost|Roughly Rs 20,000 crore/year for technology, banking, compliance|
|Government subsidy|Covers only P2M transactions up to Rs 2,000; called insufficient|
|Industry ask|MDR of 0.3-0.6% on payments above Rs 2,000 to large merchants|
|High-value transaction share|Transactions above Rs 2,000 = only 4% of P2M payments but 68% of total value|

- The Payments and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 has been amended to remove the barrier on charging merchants a fee via UPI, paving the way for reintroducing MDR on large-merchant transactions.
    
- Industry voices (PayU CEO Anirban Mukherjee, Decentro's Sridhar Guntuku) argue zero MDR was right for scaling UPI to small merchants, but the next phase needs "value per transaction" and calibrated MDR for larger merchants to fund growth and enable credit access.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Next Phase of Growth</mark>

- Government's view: subsidy-only model is not viable for the "next wave of growth," which is expected to come from rural and semi-urban India.
    
- Cross-border expansion is a key growth lever - UPI is already operational in nine countries: Bhutan, France, Mauritius, Nepal, Singapore, Sri Lanka, UAE, Qatar, and Cambodia.
    
- NPCI CEO Dilip Asbe wants India to become "self-sufficient" in cross-border payments, similar to its domestic dominance.
    
- Singapore's PayNow has been linked with UPI for over three years - a flagship cross-border model, relevant because traditional international transfers can cost up to 7% of the transferred amount and take days to settle.
    
- Payments Council of India continues pushing for MDR to be decided by "market forces," a position echoed by the Nilekani committee, which noted subsidy-only funding "does not appear to be working."
