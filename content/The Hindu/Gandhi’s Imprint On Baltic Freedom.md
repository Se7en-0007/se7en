- **August 23, 2026 marked the 37th anniversary of the Baltic Way**, the **1989 human chain** that became a watershed in the **Gandhian-inspired Singing Revolution** in **Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia** and culminated in the restoration of Baltic independence in **1990-91**.
- Across the Baltic states, people recalled that they had **studied India’s freedom struggle** and chosen **Gandhian non-violent resistance** to reclaim national freedom from Soviet rule.
- **Richard Attenborough’s film Gandhi** was telecast for **24 hours** on Lithuanian national television.
- Participants recalled joining non-violent protest against the **Soviet army at Vilnius Cathedral Square**, remaining firm even when a bullet passed close by.

## A Turning Point

- The **Singing Revolution (1986-91)** was shaped by **songs and non-violence** during **Mikhail Gorbachev’s perestroika and glasnost**.
- Its turning point was the **Baltic Way**, in which **two million people** formed a **675-kilometre human chain** from **Tallinn**, through **Riga**, to **Vilnius**, demanding freedom from Soviet rule.
- The Baltic Way remains a symbol of **unity, solidarity and freedom**.
- It was organised on the **50th anniversary of the 1939 Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact** to expose its **secret protocol**, which divided Eastern Europe into **German and Soviet spheres of influence** and paved the way for the **Soviet annexation of the Baltic states in 1940**.
- Having gained independence in **1918**, the Baltic states lost it after **22 years**.
- The Baltic Way exposed **Soviet-era repression**, leading to the Soviet Union’s acknowledgement of the secret protocol and the Baltic states’ **re-independence in 1991**.
- Its global media coverage drew international attention. Its inscription in **UNESCO’s Memory of the World Register** and recognition by **Guinness World Records** reinforced its international legacy.

## An Enduring Influence

- Gandhi’s ideas travelled globally, inspiring movements from the **American civil rights struggle** to **South Africa** and beyond.
- The Baltic Way reflects the continuing relevance of Gandhi’s principles of **non-violence, truth and human dignity**, while echoing the **non-cooperation** and **civil disobedience** of the **1930 Dandi March**.
- Gandhi demonstrated that **moral authority could outweigh military power** through **ahimsa, satyagraha, civil resistance, mass mobilisation**, and the principle that a nation’s rule depends on people’s **consent and cooperation**.
- The Baltic Way and the Singing Revolution testify to the success of **people’s power** and **civilian-based defence** drawn from the Gandhi-led Indian freedom movement.
- The leaders of the Singing Revolution drew upon global traditions of non-violent resistance. Latvian scholar **Maija Kūle** observed that, lacking local theorists, they turned to **Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr.**
- Latvia’s earlier cultural links with India may also have encouraged its attraction to Gandhian ideas.
- **Lithuania** played an important role in developing **non-violent civilian-based defence** inspired by Gandhi.
- **Gražina Miniotaitė**, a leading ideologue of Lithuania’s Singing Revolution, recalled that non-violence was widely understood from the outset as essential.
- The **December 1990 “Republic in Danger” declaration** urged adherence to **“non-violence and non-cooperation with the occupational authorities”**.
- Miniotaitė regarded Gandhi as the most successful among thinkers of non-violence in both **theory and practice**, showing that non-violent struggle could effectively resist foreign rule.
- While confronting Soviet troops, people repeated: **“We won’t fight,” “We follow Gandhi,” and “We are peaceful people.”**

## Building Closer Ties

- Since **1992**, the Baltic states have drawn on **Gandhian and cultural ties** to deepen relations with India.
- Cooperation has expanded in **technology, sustainability, the blue economy and culture**.
- In **2025-26**, India’s total bilateral trade was:
    
    - **Lithuania - $490.61 million**
    - **Latvia - $475.66 million**
    - **Estonia - $219.69 million**
- Baltic political discourse reflects Gandhian influence through:
    
    - **Hermann Kallenbach**, Gandhi’s Lithuanian-born Litvak **“soulmate”**
    - **Vydūnas’s** engagement with Gandhian thought
    - **Antanas Poška’s** meetings with Gandhi
    - **Sonja Schlesin’s** secretaryship
    - **Linnart Hall’s** Estonian translations of Gandhi
- These ties continue through:
    
    - Annual commemorations of the **2015 Gandhi-Kallenbach monument** in **Rusnė**, Kallenbach’s birthplace, most recently in **July 2026**
    - The **Kallenbach Memorial Lectures**
    - The **India-Lithuania Friendship Award**, which in 2026 honoured the people of **Goa** during the **400th anniversary** of missionary **Andrius Rudamina**
- India has gifted Gandhi memorials to the Baltic states, including a **bust unveiled in Latvia in 2019** and a **statue installed in Estonia in 2025**.

## The Baltic Way

- The Baltic Way endures as a **chain of freedom and ideas**, linking **courage with conscience**.
- Its contemporary significance is multifaceted:
    
    - A model of **non-violent resistance**
    - A site of **contested memory**
    - A narrative in **Russia-West tensions**
    - A tribute to **Gandhi and India**
