## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Event Overview</mark>

- 25th round of India-China Special Representatives (SR) talks held in Beijing on August 25, 2026, co-chaired by NSA Ajit Doval and Chinese FM Wang Yi.
    
- Discussions covered the boundary dispute plus bilateral, regional and international issues.
    
- India officially agreed to host the 26th SR meeting in Delhi in 2027.
    
- Doval also called on Chinese Vice President Han Zheng ahead of the formal session to discuss maintaining peace and tranquillity at the border.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Special Representatives (SR) Mechanism</mark>

- Instituted in 2023 after the earlier "Joint Working Group" format failed to make progress on the boundary dispute.
    
- Purpose: establish a political framework to resolve the boundary problem; boundary issue remains unsettled but SR dialogue has become an important channel for crisis management.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Background: Post-Galwan to Kazan</mark>

- 2020 Galwan clash in eastern Ladakh (PLA attempted to unilaterally alter status quo at the LAC) plunged relations to their lowest point in decades.
    
- This occurred despite the "strategic guidance mechanism" set up after Modi-Xi meetings at Wuhan (April 2018) and Mamallapuram (October 2019).
    
- Multiple political, diplomatic, and military-commander level meetings led to disengagement, buffer zones, and new patrolling modalities; Depsang sector remained a sticking point.
    
- Breakthrough: Modi-Xi meeting at Kazan, Russia (BRICS Summit, October 2024) opened a "cautious thaw."
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">24th SR Meeting (Delhi, August 2025) — Key Outcomes</mark>

- Produced a 10-point consensus, including:
    
- Implementation of the 2005 Political Parameters and Guiding Principles.
    
- Strengthening border management mechanisms.
    
- Creation of an 'Expert Group' under the Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination to explore "early harvest" boundary delimitation in select sectors.
    
- Separate working group for effective border management.
    
- Continuation of military-diplomatic mechanism and cross-border river cooperation.
    
- Reopening of traditional border trade points.
    
- Tangible outcomes: resumption of direct flights, easing of visa norms, restarting of Kailash Mansarovar Yatra.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">25th SR Meeting — What Was Discussed</mark>

- Both sides positively reviewed bilateral ties, linking progress to the strategic vision set at Kazan (Oct 2024) and Tianjin (Aug 2025) summits.
    
- Reaffirmed commitment to an open dialogue for a "fair, reasonable and mutually acceptable settlement" of the 3,500 km un-demarcated Himalayan border.
    
- Focus areas: preventing miscalculations along the LAC, gradual resumption of institutional exchanges/cooperation post-disengagement.
    
- No fresh measures announced on disengagement, patrolling protocols, or buffer zones.
    
- Divergent readouts: India's MEA statement centred on the boundary process and peace/tranquillity as basis for stable ties; China's readout was broader and political, with Wang Yi stressing that the boundary issue be kept in its "proper place" so it doesn't impede wider cooperation.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Diverging Strategic Approaches (India vs China)</mark>

|Aspect|India's Approach|China's Approach|
|---|---|---|
|Sequencing|Peace and tranquillity at LAC seen as foundational prelude to stable relationship|Holistic approach — boundary issue managed while broader relationship develops in parallel|
|Effect|Boundary resolution treated as precondition|Boundary treated as one issue among many, not a precondition|
|Outcome|Creates a hurdle since boundary remains unresolved|Works to China's advantage — trade and ties grow despite unresolved border|

- This semantic/strategic difference is described as a major hurdle to resolving the boundary dispute.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Ground Realities Despite Diplomatic Thaw</mark>

- Bilateral trade at an all-time high, breaching USD 151 billion, with China enjoying a USD 112 billion trade surplus.
    
- PLA continues building military infrastructure along the LAC, especially opposite Arunachal Pradesh.
    
- No sign of Chinese de-escalation of current deployment — used as leverage/pressure on India.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Mutual Compulsions to Normalise</mark>

- India: needs a stable northern frontier to pursue its economic agenda amid global turbulence.
    
- China: grappling with slowing growth and facing strategic competition from the US.
    
- Despite overlapping stakes, national interests remain divergent.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Underlying Sources of Rivalry</mark>

- China's close relationship with Pakistan.
    
- The Tibet issue.
    
- China's expanding footprint in the Indian Ocean.
    
- India's strategic partnership with the US, Japan, and Australia via the Quad.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Three Phases of India-China Relations (Post-2020)</mark>

1. Confrontation (2020 Galwan crisis and aftermath).
    
2. Disengagement (buffer zones, new patrolling modalities, military-diplomatic talks).
    
3. Cautious reconciliation (post-Kazan thaw, SR dialogue consolidation, current phase).
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overall Assessment</mark>

- The 25th SR meeting is best characterised as consolidation, not a breakthrough — an "uneasy détente" aimed at avoiding another Galwan-type clash.
    
- Given continuing Chinese assertiveness, expansionist behaviour, and the persistent trust deficit between Delhi and Beijing, a full resolution of the boundary dispute appears unlikely even in the future.
