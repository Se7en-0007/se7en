- **The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019** came into force on **September 1, 2019**, marking a defining moment in India’s pursuit of **safer roads**.
- Enacted to address long-standing gaps in India’s road-safety framework, it sought stronger protection for **all road users** - **pedestrians, cyclists, motorists, and commercial drivers**.
- The Act’s anniversary is a checkpoint to assess progress, identify implementation gaps, and chart the course ahead.
- The **Road Accidents in India 2024** report of the **Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH)** records nearly **1.77 lakh lives lost** in road crashes in a single year.
- Despite legislative progress, road safety must remain central to India’s journey towards **Viksit Bharat**.

## Stronger Legal Foundation

- The **2019 Act** marked a meaningful shift through:
    
    - **Enhanced penalties**
    - **Greater accountability**
    - Protection for **Good Samaritans**
    - Provisions for the **“golden hour”** after a crash
    - **Vehicle safety standards**
    - Reinforced **enforcement mechanisms**
- These provisions reflect the recognition that **road fatalities and serious injuries need not be accepted as an unavoidable cost of mobility**.
- However, legislation is only a starting point. Its real test is whether it can **shape behaviour, strengthen institutions, and make daily journeys demonstrably safer**.

## Great Challenge

- Implementation remains **inconsistent across the country**.
- Compliance with **helmet use, seatbelts, and child restraint systems (CRS)** varies widely across States.
- Many children continue to travel in conditions that expose them to **avoidable danger**.
- Sustained and coordinated attention is required for:
    
    - **School transportation standards**
    - **Road-user discipline**
    - **Enforcement capacity**
    - **Road engineering**
    - **Post-crash emergency response**
- No single intervention can guarantee a safe journey.
- Ensuring a child’s safety requires joint understanding and action by **parents, schools, transport operators, enforcement agencies, and governments**.
- **Legislation, enforcement, safer vehicles, better-engineered roads, and responsive emergency care** must function as interconnected parts of one system.
- Key questions include:
    
    - Whether road-safety provisions are interpreted and enforced uniformly across States
    - Whether accident-prone **black spots** are identified and rectified
    - Whether schools and transport operators can make daily school commutes safer
    - Whether enforcement agencies are using technology and data to their full potential
    - Whether timely, life-saving emergency care is available after a crash
- Road safety lies at the intersection of **public policy, enforcement, engineering, technology, and individual behaviour**; progress requires all these elements to move forward together.

## The Way Forward

- India needs a shift from **isolated, piecemeal interventions** to an **integrated, data-driven approach**.
- Enforcement is indispensable but cannot, by itself, deliver safer roads.
- Better data is required to reveal **where and why lives are being lost or seriously compromised** and to enable decisive action.
- Safer school transportation systems and greater awareness among **parents, teachers, and transport operators** are equally essential.
- Technology can help identify risks before they lead to tragedy through:
    
    - **Intelligent traffic systems**
    - **AI-enabled monitoring**
    - **Driver assistance and alertness systems**
    - **Real-time crash reporting**
    - More effective use of **road-safety data**
- Technology must support **sound policy and human judgment**, not substitute either.
- A central principle is that **people will make mistakes**; a well-designed road system ensures that such mistakes **do not cost a life**.

## A Systemic Responsibility

- Road safety should not be viewed as the responsibility of any single **Ministry, department, or enforcement agency**.
- Responsibilities include:
    
    - **Governments** must build safer systems.
    - **Enforcement agencies** must ensure compliance.
    - **Vehicle manufacturers and transport operators** must uphold their duty of care.
    - **Schools** must inculcate safe road behaviour from an early age.
    - **Employers managing large vehicle fleets** must recognise their responsibility towards drivers and fellow road users.
    - **Communities and individuals** must recognise the consequences of everyday choices.
- Important individual practices include **wearing a helmet correctly, buckling a seatbelt, using age-appropriate child restraint systems for children under 12, slowing down, protecting children, and avoiding distractions**.
- Road safety becomes sustainable only when it goes beyond **mere compliance** and becomes an inseparable part of **everyday culture**.
- The Act has provided a stronger legal foundation; the task now is to translate its spirit into **tangibly safer journeys on the ground**.
- The anniversary should be used to examine implementation honestly, reinforce what works, address what does not, and renew collective resolve to reduce **road deaths and serious injuries**.
- India does not need to choose between **stronger enforcement, safer infrastructure, advanced vehicle technology, or greater public participation**. It requires all of them to work in concert.
