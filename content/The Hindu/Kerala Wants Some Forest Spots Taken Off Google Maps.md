## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context of The Issue</mark>

Kerala's Forest Department has declared that indiscriminate geotagging of locations inside forests is illegal, particularly where such tags expose "ecologically sensitive" spots. This decision follows tourists and trekkers using online map platforms (like Google Maps) to locate and enter dangerous or protected forest zones. The trigger point was a request by the Kozhikode Divisional Forest Officer to remove Google Maps listings inside reserve forests in his district.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What Is Geotagging</mark>

- Geotagging is the process of attaching geographical coordinates (latitude/longitude) to a photo, video, or any digital media, making that location visible/searchable on online map platforms.
    
- It becomes "illegal" under this new Kerala directive when the tagged location falls within a sensitive ecological spot inside reserve or protected forests.
    
- Common sources of illegal tags include trekkers, tourists, and even forest department staff trails (e.g., trekking routes used by staff in Silent Valley) that got inadvertently listed online.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Kerala Is Acting Now</mark>

- Mountainous forest regions are prone to natural disasters such as landslides and mudslides, especially during monsoon season.
    
- Tourists chasing scenic "monsoon beauty" content are entering hazardous zones after finding them via map apps.
    
- Wildlife corridors used by elephants, bison, and big cats are getting exposed, increasing risk of human-animal conflict.
    
- Officials want to preserve the "sanctity" of these zones, restricting entry strictly to permitted forest personnel.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Administrative Process Being Followed</mark>

|Step|Action|Responsible Body|
|---|---|---|
|1|Identify illegally geotagged locations|Forest Department IT Wing|
|2|Compile and forward list|Forest Management Wing|
|3|Request removal from map platforms|Forest Department (to Google Maps etc.)|
|4|Identify uploaders and initiate legal action|Forest Department / Forest Force|
|5|Set up local regulatory mechanism|Forest Department with local bodies/panchayats|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Officials and Statements</mark>

- Rajesh Ravindran, Principal Chief Conservator of Forests and Head of Forest Force, Kerala, is the lead spokesperson explaining the rationale.
    
- M Joshil, Kozhikode Divisional Forest Officer, was the first to flag the issue by seeking removal of Google Maps listings of reserve forest locations in his division.
    
- The department plans coordination with local inhabitants near sensitive zones to build a community-level regulatory mechanism preventing illegal entry and tagging.
