## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background: The Legal Trigger</mark>

- **1989 ruling (India Cement Ltd v. State of Tamil Nadu):** Supreme Court had held that royalty on minerals is a "tax," and since mineral rights fall under the Union List, states lacked legislative competence to tax them.
    
- **July 25, 2024 Supreme Court ruling:** Overruled the 1989 judgment; upheld states' power to levy taxes on mineral rights and mineral-bearing land (MBL).
    
- The 2024 verdict also waived interest and penalties on pre-judgment tax demands and allowed staggered payment of dues over 12 years starting April 1, 2026.
    
- This opened the door for mineral-rich states to introduce new levies to boost non-tax revenue.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What the 2026 Amendment Does</mark>

- Passed by both Houses of Parliament in 2026.
    
- Seeks to **restrict states from imposing specified levies** on mineral rights and mineral-bearing land — effectively capping the cumulative burden of state-level mining levies.
    
- Extinguishes unpaid/unrecovered dues from such levies imposed before the Bill takes effect (estimated across the sector at roughly Rs 2 lakh crore).
    
- Does not abolish existing levies outright — Mines Ministry officials note there are around 14 levies currently in the mineral sector (royalty, District Mineral Foundation contributions, National Mineral Exploration and Development Trust contributions, environmental/pollution cesses, MBL tax, etc.); these will continue, but a ceiling ("a certain percentage") on their cumulative burden will be set after consultation with states.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Centre's Rationale</mark>

- Aims to provide **fiscal certainty** to the mining industry by preventing "excessive and multiple" state levies.
    
- Argues unchecked state levies raise mineral costs, feeding inflation and infrastructure expenses.
    
- Industry voices (e.g., former FIMI Director General) argue the MBL tax was the single biggest additional burden on mining companies, beyond royalty and existing cesses.
    
- Officials claim the change is unlikely to cause material revenue loss to states since most contested levies have pending dues tied up in litigation for decades anyway.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">States' Opposition and Federalism Concerns</mark>

- **Jharkhand CM Hemant Soren:** Threatened protests; wrote to the PM stating mining revenue formed approximately 84.9% of the state's own non-tax revenue in 2024-25, and that the MBL cess alone was expected to generate about Rs 11,000 crore annually.
    
- **Kerala CM V D Satheesan:** Raised concerns about implications for India's federal structure.
    
- **Odisha (Naveen Patnaik):** Stated the new law infringes on the state's rights and would cause revenue loss.
    
- Core argument: mineral revenue is not a marginal receipt for these states but a critical fiscal lifeline supporting welfare, developmental, and social-security programs.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Fiscal Significance — Data Points</mark>

- Per CAG report on state finances, states' own non-tax revenue was Rs 3.3 lakh crore in 2024-25.
    
- Of this, 41% (Rs 1.36 lakh crore) came from mineral and petroleum receipts.
    
- Nationally, mineral and petroleum receipts form just 3.4% of all states' revenue receipts, but the share is far higher for resource-rich states:
    
    - Odisha: 23%
        
    - Jharkhand: 13%
        
    - Chhattisgarh: 5%
        

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Expert Perspective</mark>

- Analysts (e.g., Nexdigma) view the amendment as a **cost-containment and investment-certainty** measure for mining companies but caution that revenue-loss concerns for states cannot be dismissed.
    
- Suggested balance: Centre should frame "reasonable and transparent guardrails" through adequate consultation with mineral-producing states rather than a one-sided restriction.
    
- Success of the reform is seen as contingent on reducing industry unpredictability without leaving mining states under-compensated.
