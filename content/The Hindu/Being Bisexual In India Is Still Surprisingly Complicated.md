## Core Theme

The article examines the lived experience of bisexual individuals in India in 2026, arguing that while visibility has increased through dating apps and pop culture, social understanding and acceptance remain shallow, forcing bisexual people to constantly justify their identity.

## Rising Visibility vs. Real Acceptance

- India is more open to sexual fluidity than before, driven largely by dating apps and shifting youth attitudes.
- Tinder's 2023 India survey (1,000 daters, aged 18-25) found 33% reported more fluid orientation than three years prior, and 56% were open to dating people of diverse gender identities/orientations.
- Author clarifies fluidity and bisexuality are not the same thing; the data shows a cultural shift in attitude, not that a third of youth identify as bisexual.
- Despite visibility, bisexual individuals face constant "interrogation" about their identity rather than outright rejection.

## The "Pick a Side" Problem

- A recurring social demand exists for bisexual people to justify or prove their orientation.
- Common suspicions faced:
    
    - From women partners: fear that the person is "secretly gay" and will eventually leave for a man.
    - From gay men: assumption the person is in denial and "not ready" to identify as gay.
    - General stereotype: bisexuality equated with being automatically "sexually adventurous"/promiscuous.
- Core frustration expressed by interviewees: the burden isn't rejection but the interrogation before a relationship even begins.

## Research Evidence on Stigma

|Study|Year/Details|Key Finding|
|---|---|---|
|Humanizing an Invisible Population in India (Archives of Sexual Behavior)|2018 study, published Jan 2019; 22 men in focus groups, 50 in-depth interviews, Mumbai|Bisexual men face secrecy, family pressure, and stigma from both heterosexual AND gay communities|
|Gendered Anti-Bisexual Bias (Journal of Homosexuality)|2022, 1,823 participants|Heterosexual and gay/lesbian people are less willing to date bisexual people than vice versa; bias strongest against bisexual men|
|Bisexual Men's Experiences with Discrimination|2020 longitudinal study, 180 young bisexual men|Men with female partners face more hostility; men with only female partners assumed "sexually irresponsible"; men with only male partners assumed "orientation unstable"|

## Cultural Representation

- Bollywood's _Dostana_: John Abraham's character could be read as bisexual, but audiences simplified both leads as "gay" — reflecting Indian comfort with fixed categories over ambiguity.
- _Margarita with a Straw_: Featured Laila (played by Kalki Koechlin), a woman with cerebral palsy attracted to both men and women; academically analyzed for linking bisexuality, disability, and challenges to heteronormativity.
- General critique: bisexuality in media is often reduced to a "plot point" rather than portrayed as lived reality.

## Fetishisation and Dating Market Paradoxes

- A bisexual woman interviewee reports being persistently fetishised by men, repeatedly propositioned for threesomes — an experience unchanged "in over a decade."
- Counter-intuitive finding: despite theoretically having a larger dating pool (multiple genders), bisexual people face _more_ difficulty, not less, per the 2022 Journal of Homosexuality study.
- A male interviewee's personal theory: dating bisexual women is "easier" because they inherently understand that attraction to one gender doesn't cancel attraction to another — reducing the need for "explaining."

## Diverse Voices Featured

- Anonymous male friend (long-identified bisexual): describes constant questioning from both women and men partners.
- Woman in Bengaluru: experiences deeper emotional bonds with women but more casual attraction to men; faces fetishisation from male partners.
- 50-year-old woman in Kolkata: discovered her bisexuality after her marriage dissolved in 2022; rejects the idea of "choosing" an orientation, framing it instead as basic human connection.

## Offline Infrastructure Gap

- Dating apps (Tinder, Bumble) offer limited functionality for connecting specifically with other bisexual people — no dedicated "bisexual matching" feature.
- Community/support groups mentioned:
    
    - **Bi Collective Delhi** – independent voluntary collective for bisexual, bi+, bi-curious, and pansexual people; functions as a support/safe space.
    - **Umang** (Mumbai) – LBT support group under The Humsafar Trust; includes bisexual women and trans people; runs workshops and informal social gatherings.
- Evidence of decline: a 2025 Reddit thread on Mumbai's bisexual community found no dedicated events; by February 2026, one commenter called the scene "dead."
- Author's caveat: Reddit anecdotes aren't a national survey, but they illustrate the gap between app-based visibility and real-world community spaces.

## Key Takeaway for Analysis

The article frames bisexuality in India through a lens of "administrative burden" — social visibility has outpaced genuine social understanding, leaving bisexual individuals to navigate stigma from multiple directions (heterosexual and homosexual communities alike), fetishisation, dating-market bias, and a thin offline support ecosystem.
