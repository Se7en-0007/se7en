## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Issue</mark>

- 18th BRICS Summit is scheduled in New Delhi on September 12-13, 2026 — India's first time hosting.
    
- Chinese President Xi Jinping's attendance remains officially unconfirmed, though reports suggest he is likely to attend with a large delegation.
    
- If he attends, it would be Xi's first visit to India in seven years.
    
- Context: China had officially confirmed months in advance that Xi would skip the 2025 BRICS summit in Brazil.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Trigger: Arunachal Pradesh Naming Row</mark>

- India recently (August 2026) standardised 27 place names in Arunachal Pradesh.
    
- This drew strong reactions from China's strategic affairs community, seen as a possible signal of a surprise "no-show" decision by Xi.
    
- Liu Zongyi (Shanghai Institute for International Studies), China's leading expert on the India border dispute, said this shows New Delhi's position remains "entrenched" despite recent efforts to improve ties.
    
- China Daily (CCP-controlled) editorial (August 11): called for "caution rather than political posturing" regarding the boundary.
    
- A Chinese foreign affairs blogger accused Modi of "attacking China" ahead of BRICS to gain an "upper hand."
    
- Historical pattern: India has consistently rejected China's renaming of Arunachal places as territorial claim assertions.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Precedents of Xi's Past Absences</mark>

|Summit|Year|Outcome|Suggested Reason|
|---|---|---|---|
|G20, New Delhi|2023|Xi skipped (unusual — never missed a G20 since 2012)|Deteriorated ties post-2020 LAC standoff; China issued fresh renamed places list on LAC days before event; India responded with large military exercises|
|BRICS, Rio de Janeiro|2025|Xi skipped|Officially cited scheduling; meetings with Brazil's Lula; some analysts saw "India angle" — avoiding being overshadowed by Modi, who was concurrently Brazil's state-visit guest of honour|

- Bloomberg noted it "could be embarrassing" for Xi — "the most powerful Chinese leader since Mao" — to be overshadowed by another leader.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Timeline of Bilateral Engagement Leading to 2026</mark>

- 2019: Xi's last visit to India — the "Chennai Connect" informal summit (pre-dates LAC clashes).
    
- 2020: LAC standoff sharply worsened relations.
    
- 2023: Xi skips G20 New Delhi.
    
- 2024: Modi-Xi meeting on sidelines of BRICS Summit, Kazan, Russia.
    
- August 2025: Modi attends SCO leaders' meet in Tianjin, meets Xi — seen as a "reset" gesture; Modi personally delivers BRICS 2026 invitation to Xi.
    
- 2025: Xi skips BRICS Rio.
    
- 2026: Indian Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri visits Beijing to discuss BRICS summit arrangements; Arunachal naming row erupts; Xi's attendance still unconfirmed as of late August.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Possible Strategic Reasons for Ambiguity</mark>

- Analyst Derek Grossman (China Global South Project) suggests China may be using the suspense tactically to pressure India on:
    
    - Relaxation of restrictions on Chinese investment/business.
        
    - Easier visa norms and direct connectivity (flights).
        
    - Tibet and Taiwan-related issues.
        
- China also seeks assurance that India isn't using normalisation with Beijing merely as a tactic while simultaneously deepening military alignment with Washington.
    
- Alternative view: Even amid warming ties, a Xi "no-show" could be a deliberate message that India's Arunachal move is read as political posturing — despite BRICS being central to Xi's push to reshape global power balance.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Broader Significance</mark>

- Regardless of the final decision, the episode raises questions on whether BRICS/BRICS+ can manage bilateral disagreements between its two largest members, who are also neighbours.
    
- As things stand, the article notes it is Beijing — more than New Delhi — that holds the initiative in determining the next phase of the relationship.
