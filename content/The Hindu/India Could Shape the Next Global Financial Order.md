The article argues that as the old Global North-dominated financial architecture strains under climate and development pressures, India is positioned to lead the creation of a new, multipolar, inclusive global financial order centered on green development.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context: Global Financial Order in Transition</mark>

- The prevailing international financial architecture (IMF, World Bank) was built and led by the developed world (Global North) for decades
    
- This system is struggling to meet the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and address the escalating climate crisis
    
- Declining Global North dominance is creating space for a multipolar order, with India positioned at the vanguard
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Drivers of Change</mark>

- Rise of emerging economies — China, Brazil, India — reshaping global economic and political landscape
    
- US-led withdrawal from key global commitments: SDG agenda, UNFCCC, Paris Agreement
    
- Traditional Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs) reluctant to aggressively pivot portfolios toward climate/clean energy
    
- Core insight: future green finance needs come not from governments/institutions providing trillions directly, but from using constrained public budgets to de-risk and mobilise private capital
    
- MDBs struggle both to raise more capital and to design instruments that mobilise private capital for green development

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Role as a Bridge</mark>

- India leverages three platforms to advocate for equitable global order for developing South:
    
- Voice of the Global South Summits (leadership role)
    
- 2023 G20 presidency
    
- Strategic positioning at COP conferences
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Institutional Entrepreneurship</mark>

India has moved from advocacy to actively building new institutional infrastructure — termed "institutional entrepreneurship."

- International Solar Alliance (ISA)
    
- Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI)
    
- Global Biofuel Alliance (GBA)
    
- These are designed to complement, not replace, existing systems — offering focused, scalable, action-oriented models on global public goods
    
- Demonstrate that the Global South can lead on solving climate challenges
    
- 2023 G20 Presidency outcome: Green Development Pact — championed MDB reform, especially voting rights reflecting current economic weight of developing nations
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The BRICS+ Catalyst</mark>

- Expanded BRICS+ coalition comprises over 40% of global GDP and population combined
    
- Central institution: New Development Bank (NDB) — offers an alternative, more equitable governance paradigm for development finance
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Proposed "Twin-Track" Approach for BRICS+</mark>

1. Strengthen the NDB — recapitalise it and strengthen balance sheet to transform it into a leading MDB offering:
    
    - Local currency financing
        
    - Multilateral Risk Guarantee Initiatives (for de-risking green infrastructure)
        
    - Diversifying liabilities, callable capital and assets across countries to reduce borrowing costs, lower lending costs, and bypass structural barrier of global sovereign ratings for developing countries
        
2. Broader country-led efforts to create exclusive green development mechanisms for the Global South
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Additional BRICS+ Financial Sovereignty Measures</mark>

- Harmonising trade regulations
    
- Reducing reliance on Western-dominated payment systems (e.g., SWIFT)
    
- Fostering digital infrastructure
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">A New Vision for Development Finance Institutions</mark>

- Old metric: MDBs judged by size of their own lending books
    
- New metric proposed: DFIs should be judged by how effectively they use public balance sheets to crowd in private capital
    
- India could champion new risk-sharing instruments:
    
- Guarantees
    
- First-loss facilities
    
- Currency hedging
    
- Transition-finance platforms
    
- Goal: convert small public capital amounts into much larger pools of private investment
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Broader Vision</mark>

- Not about replacing old system with new dominance, but creating an inclusive framework letting developing countries guide their own development priorities and decarbonisation
    
- Requires shift from top-down, conditional aid toward collaborative, risk-mitigated investment in local solutions
    
- India combines diplomatic soft power + institutional strength (BRICS) + practical initiatives (ISA, CDRI) to drive next wave of financial reform
    
- End goal: an environmentally sustainable, inclusive global financial system reflective of 21st-century realities
