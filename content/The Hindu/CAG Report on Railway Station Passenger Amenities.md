## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background</mark>

The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) is a constitutional authority under Article 148 of the Indian Constitution, responsible for auditing government expenditure including that of the Ministry of Railways. This particular audit examined passenger amenities at railway stations, marking the first such comprehensive review since 2016, nearly a decade earlier. Despite repeated assurances from the Ministry of Railways after the 2016 audit, the CAG found that key deficiencies had persisted.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Minimum Essential Amenities (MEA) Framework</mark>

The concept of Minimum Essential Amenities was introduced by the Railway Board in 2003 and subsequently revised in April 2018, with a mandate that all facilities be provided by August 31, 2018.

- Drinking-water taps
    
- Seating arrangements
    
- Urinals and toilets
    
- Waiting halls
    
- Water coolers
    
- Fans and clocks
    
- Platform shelters
    
- Public-address systems
    
- Parking-cum-circulatory areas
    
- Electronic train indicator boards
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Scope and Methodology of Audit</mark>

The CAG selected 512 non-suburban stations, representing 8.66% of India's more than 5,900 stations, for detailed examination. This sample-based methodology allowed extrapolation of systemic issues across the national railway network.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Findings - MEA Shortfalls</mark>

Only 54 stations, or under 11% of those audited, spread across 13 railway zones, had fully complied with all minimum essential amenity norms - meaning over 89% of stations had at least one deficiency.

|Amenity Deficiency|Number of Stations Affected|
|---|---|
|Inadequate drinking-water taps|188|
|Insufficient urinals|111|
|Insufficient latrines|59|
|Platform shelters below prescribed area|62|
|No waiting halls|21|
|No clocks|60|
|Fewer water coolers than prescribed|128|
|Dustbins not as per norms|149|

Notably, even major high-revenue stations - including New Delhi, Howrah, Hazrat Nizamuddin Junction, Secunderabad, MGR Chennai Central, Ahmedabad, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, KSR Bengaluru, Surat and Anand Vihar Terminal - with annual revenues exceeding Rs 710 crore and footfalls between 1.03 crore and 11.47 crore, showed amenity shortfalls.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Deficiencies for Divyangjan (Persons with Disabilities)</mark>

The audit revealed significant gaps in disability-friendly infrastructure:

- Standard ramps with railings missing in 44% of cases
    
- Earmarked parking lots absent in 55% of cases
    
- Non-slippery walkways lacking in 32% of cases
    
- Drinking-water taps at alternate water booths on each platform missing in 58% of cases
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Drinking Water Quality Concerns</mark>

Water samples from coolers tested positive for E. coli bacteria at multiple stations including Coimbatore, Palakkad Junction, Hyderabad, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Bhiwandi Road, Kalyan, Lonavala and Madhupur. This is a serious public health finding given the scale of daily rail passenger footfall in India.

Testing and monitoring lapses compounded the risk:

- Chemical analysis of drinking water was not conducted by health inspectors at 179 stations across 11 zones
    
- Prescribed periodicity (twice a year) for testing was not followed at 77 stations across 6 zones
    
- Tests covering all parameters under the Uniform Drinking Water Quality Protocol were not conducted at any of the 512 selected stations
    

These deficiencies occurred despite Indian Railways spending approximately Rs 700 crore annually on station sanitation maintenance, with expenditure actually exceeding budget grants by Rs 72.66 crore in 2022-23 and Rs 9.20 crore in 2023-24.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Financial Underutilisation</mark>

Funds allocated under the 'Customer Amenities' head were underutilised across the five-year audit period, with utilisation ranging only between 56% and 64% of budget grants (i.e., 36% to 44% of funds remained unspent).

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Governance and Monitoring Gaps</mark>

Institutional oversight mechanisms were found to be weak:

- Zonal Railway Users Consultative Councils (ZRUCCs) did not meet at the prescribed frequency of three times a year
    
- Divisional Railway Users Consultative Councils (DRUCCs) similarly failed to meet as mandated
    
- 59% of passenger amenity works were delayed
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Rising Passenger Grievances</mark>

Data from the RailMadad grievance redressal platform showed increasing complaints related to medical assistance, cleanliness, malfunctioning electrical equipment and water availability. Dissatisfaction with complaint resolution also rose, from 37% of complainants in 2022-23 to 42% in 2023-24, indicating deteriorating grievance redressal efficiency.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government's Corrective Response</mark>

Following the report, Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw directed corrective measures, most notably a new 'tank to tap' water filtration system to be installed at around 20,000 locations, including water coolers. The Ministry announced board-level monitoring of water quality, scheduled cleaning and maintenance of storage tanks, and periodic publication of progress and water quality reports for transparency.
