## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Event Overview</mark>

- Devastating flash flood struck Nepal (Bidur/Nuwakot area, Trishuli/Bhote Koshi river basin) around August 26, 2026.
- Death toll surged to 359, with nearly 2,000 people reported missing per Nepal's Disaster Authority; around 288-290 Indians also reported missing.
- Trigger identified as collapse of a hanging glacier, likened by scientists to "a high-intensity bomb blast."

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Mechanism of the Disaster</mark>

- A hanging glacier (a glacier perched on a steep slope, prone to breaking off) collapsed vertically, dropping between 1.5-2 km into a deglaciated valley below.
- Deglaciated valley = an area once covered by ice but now exposed, typically filled with soft moraine and loose debris.
- The steep drop generated immense kinetic energy; the ice mass shattered into smaller pieces/flakes on impact.
- Impact on soft moraine/debris bed released this stored energy explosively, mobilizing debris and water.
- Preliminary satellite assessment (NRSC) suggests possibility of an ice-avalanche temporarily blocking the river, followed by sudden breach releasing impounded water and debris (glacial lake outburst-type mechanism).
- Scientific uncertainty remains on whether glacier collapse or subsequent temporary river blockage/breach was the more decisive factor.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Seismic Evidence & Revised Assessment</mark>

- US Geological Survey (USGS) initially reported a 4.4 magnitude earthquake near the area just before the event, believed at first to be the trigger.
- USGS later revised its assessment: analysis of long-period seismic waves indicated the seismic energy was generated by glacial collapse and debris flow, not a conventional earthquake.
- Revised magnitude of the seismic signal was placed at 5.2.
- This seismic signature is considered clear confirmation that the glacier collapse/ice avalanche occurred on the morning of August 26.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Expert Voices</mark>

- Glaciologist Anil Kulkarni (Indian Institute of Science): identified hanging glacier collapse as most likely cause; described the energy release as bomb-blast-like.
- National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC): released before-and-after satellite imagery showing glacier mass collapse and a visibly swollen Bhote Koshi river channel.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Disaster Management Response</mark>

- National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) announced plans for a comprehensive study of the Nepal event, since similar Himalayan terrain in India carries comparable risk.
- Krishna Swaroop Vatsa (NDMA Member) noted several Indian regions are vulnerable to such glacier/lake-related disasters and stressed the need for detailed study.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">National GLOF Risk Management Programme</mark>

- Initiated after the 2021 Chamoli disaster to mitigate risks from Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOF).
- Covers four Himalayan states: Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, plus Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
- Objectives: strengthen boundaries/embankments of vulnerable glacial lakes and install early warning systems.
- ISRO data (April 2024): out of 2,431 glacial lakes larger than 10 hectares in Indian Himalayan river basins, 676 lakes had expanded notably between 1984-2023, indicating rising glacial melt and risk.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Complementary Mitigation Efforts</mark>

- Parallel focus on developing landslide forecasting capabilities and a landslide early warning system.
- Experimental landslide forecasts are already being conducted in some vulnerable areas.
- NDMA views these landslide and GLOF programmes as mutually reinforcing components of Himalayan disaster preparedness.
