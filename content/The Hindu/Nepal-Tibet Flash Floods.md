## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Event Overview</mark>

- Date: Floods struck the Nepal-Tibet border on Wednesday, August 26, 2026
- Casualties: At least 162 lives lost initially (later reports show toll rising to 359); 133 Indians reported missing initially (later 288-290)
- Location: Lhende Khola and Bhote Koshi river system
- Impact: Roads, bridges and houses destroyed; mudslide crippled Gyirong Port (a major Tibet-Nepal crossing point); power and communications severed

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Precedent</mark>

- This is not the first such disaster on these rivers (Lhende Khola is a tributary of Bhote Koshi)
- Last year (2025), a Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) struck the same river system
- That earlier GLOF damaged four major hydropower plants and affected 8% of Nepal's total electricity supply

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Concept: What Is a Flash Flood?</mark>

- Definition: A sudden, often violent surge of water inundating low-lying areas within a very short time span
- In mountainous terrain, floods carry higher energy and destroy structures in their path
- High velocity gives downstream communities little to no warning or evacuation window

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Mechanism: How an Earthquake/Collapse Can Trigger a Flood</mark>

- Himalayan peaks have massive glaciers and precariously balanced rock formations
- Seismic disturbance (or glacial collapse) can violently displace rock, ice, mud and other debris
- Suspected sequence: ice-rock avalanche/debris entered the Lhende Khola (narrow, high-altitude tributary) and formed a temporary debris dam
- Water backs up rapidly behind this unstable barrier; rising pressure eventually ruptures the dam
- Rupture releases a wall of water combined with solid debris hurtling downstream toward settlements

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Nepal Is Especially Vulnerable: Topographic Factors</mark>

- Nepal combines some of the highest peaks on Earth with narrow, deep river gorges
- This creates natural "funnels" that accelerate floodwater speed, amplifying destructive power

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Compounding Vulnerabilities</mark>

- Rough terrain hampers emergency response — rescue helicopters could not land until floodwaters receded
- Narrow habitable valleys force highly concentrated settlements, so a single flood can geographically isolate entire communities
- Steep gradients cause the flood to dislodge huge volumes of earth and boulders from riverbeds/banks, adding sediment load
- This sediment created logistical challenges for the 574 Chinese rescue personnel deployed to Gyirong port's land crossing

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Regional Hydrology and Transboundary Nature</mark>

- The Lhende Khola is a transboundary river flowing through both Nepal and China (Tibet)
- It is a high-altitude tributary of the Bhote Koshi, part of an interconnected hydrological network including the Trishuli and Narayani rivers
- All three rivers carve deep basins through the mountains before flowing downstream into the plains
- This interconnected flow explains why flood alerts were immediately issued in downstream Indian states — Bihar and Uttar Pradesh

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Economic and International Dimension</mark>

- Fast-flowing Himalayan rivers are highly prized for hydropower generation, attracting foreign investment and foreign workers
- Example: Eight South Korean workers remained unaccounted for after a hydropower plant was struck by the floods
- India responded by sending relief materials and aid; discussions included using Bailey bridges to support Nepal's relief efforts
