## Context

- The **Indian economy has weathered** the storm caused by the **West Asia conflict** and the resultant **energy shock**.
- The **Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)** released **GDP data for the April-June quarter (Q1)** on **Monday, August 31**.
- **GDP** is defined as the **total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders during a specific period**.

## GDP Growth Forecasts

- The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** forecast **GDP growth** in the quarter at **7%**.
- **Economists from various banks and agencies** believe the **headline growth figure may exceed** the RBI's forecast **by at least half a percentage point**, with some predicting growth as high as **8%**.
- **Union Bank of India economists**, led by **Kanika Pasricha**, called an **upswing close to the psychological 8% threshold** - amid **geopolitical tensions** and a **spike in oil prices to an average $97/bbl** - a **"positive surprise"**.
- They project **GDP growth in April-June at 7.9%**, describing it as **"likely a blockbuster beating all odds"**.
- The **economy's performance has been surprising on the upside** for some time, with the **last three quarters recording GDP growth rates of 8.3%, 8%, and 7.8%**.
- The **State Bank of India (SBI)** estimates around **8% GDP growth in April-June**.
- Of the **50-plus economic performance indicators tracked by SBI**, **86% accelerated** in the quarter, **up from 69% in April-June 2025**.

## Decadal High Loan Growth

- The **first quarter of a new financial year** is usually the **weakest** for **corporate performance** and **demand for loans**, since **activity slows after the hectic last quarter** and companies focus on **planning for the year ahead**.
- However, the **first three months of 2026-27** have been the **best for demand for bank loans in more than a decade**.
- **RBI data** shows **outstanding non-food loans of banks** were up **18.3% year-on-year** as of **end-June**.
- The **last time bank loan growth was higher** at the end of Q1 of a financial year was **June 2012**, when it stood at **18.7%**.
- Part of the **high growth in bank credit** is attributed to a **change in the reporting format of the data** (shifted to the **last day of the month**), as well as **high wholesale inflation in April-June** caused by the **war in West Asia**.
- However, there are also **solid underlying reasons driving demand for money**.

### Sector-wise Loan Growth (as of end-June)

- **Loans to industry**: up **19.2% year-on-year** (compared to **6.3% in June 2025**).
- **Loans to services**: up **21.4% year-on-year** (compared to **8.8% in June 2025**).
- **Personal loans**: up **15.8% year-on-year** (compared to **11.7% in June 2025**).

## The Inflection Point

- **Barclays economists Aastha Gudwani and Amruta Ghare** noted that **post-Covid-19 pandemic demand for bank credit from industry** had largely been **lower** than demand from **agriculture, services, and retail sector personal credit** - but this **changed in mid-2025**.
- They observed that the **continued acceleration in industry growth** is now driven by **"large" industry bank credit**, whose pace has **nearly risen 4x**, from **2% outstanding in June 2025 to 16.6% in June 2026**.
- **Upasna Bhardwaj and Harsh Doshi**, economists with **Kotak Mahindra Bank**, noted a **shift in credit offtake from personal loans towards industry and services**, reflecting **strong credit demand in more productive sectors**.
- They stated: **"Economic activity, and as a result credit growth, has held up despite uncertainties from the West Asia war"**.
- This trend is **not limited to India** - **bank credit growth in Asia excluding China** is at **8.5%**, the **highest in 18 years**, according to **Morgan Stanley**.
- **Morgan Stanley economists**, led by **Chief Asia Economist Chetan Ahya**, attributed this to **"surging capex, booming trade and the rise in PPI (Producer Price Index) inflation"**.
- According to them, **Asia is entering its "strongest industrial cycle since the 2000s"**, driven by a **"sustained, multi-year rise in capex"** across **AI and AI-related digital infrastructure spending, energy, defense, and industrial supply chains"**.

## Corporate Performance

- **Companies, large and small**, are **not just taking loans** but also **reporting solid financial results**.
- In **April-June**, **sales of listed private non-financial companies** rose **19.4% year-on-year**, compared to **13.9% in the final quarter of 2025-26** and just **5.5% in April-June 2025**.
- **Operating profits grew 19.3%**, more than **double the 9.8% increase reported in January-March** and **7.3% in the year-ago period**.
- This growth occurred **despite a sharp 25.3% rise in raw material costs** and a **19.3% increase in power and fuel expenditure** during the quarter, both **caused by the West Asia conflict**.
- This data is **based on numbers compiled by the RBI** for **3,247 listed non-government non-financial companies**.
- **Aditi Gupta**, an **economist at Bank of Baroda**, said the **"robust growth in sales, notwithstanding the higher price scenario, suggests that the underlying growth momentum remains strong,"** adding that **both consumption and manufacturing engines are fuelling growth**.
