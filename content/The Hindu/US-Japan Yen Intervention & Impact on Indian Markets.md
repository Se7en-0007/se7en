## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background of The Event</mark>

The US and Japan carried out a joint coordinated currency market intervention, confirmed on Monday, August 3, 2026, to smooth out excessive volatility in the yen. This was the first such joint action since 2011, conducted under the framework of the US-Japan Finance Ministers' Joint Statement issued in September 2025. The intervention took place on the preceding Friday, strengthening the yen against the US dollar, and came against the backdrop of the yen falling to 40-year lows in July 2026.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What Triggered The Intervention</mark>

- The Bank of Japan kept short-term interest rates steady on Friday but signalled a possible near-term rate hike, as underlying inflation threatened to exceed its 2% target.
    
- Japan reportedly sold as much as $59 billion in securities on Thursday to support its currency.
    
- US President Donald Trump publicly confirmed US support, stating Japan "wanted a little bit of help" due to a weakening yen, and "we're always there for Japan."
    
- The yen rose about 1% to 155.23 per dollar (a three-month high) before settling near 156.75, following a 3%+ gain over the prior two trading sessions.
    
- The Fed has so far avoided rate hikes, and the CME Fedwatch tool showed a near 65% probability of a 25-basis-point rate cut at its September meeting.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why the US Has a Stake in This</mark>

**US Treasury Yield Concerns**

- A weaker yen tends to push US Treasury bond yields higher.
    
- Japan holds a large share of US Treasuries; a weak yen may force Japan to sell US bonds, further elevating yields.
    
- With US debt at $40 trillion, even a 10-20 basis point yield increase adds significant pressure on the US balance sheet (one basis point = 0.01%).
    

**Rate Hike Dilemma**

- Japan technically needs to raise interest rates to curb inflation, but central banks are hesitant since rate hikes could dampen demand — making coordinated currency intervention a preferred tool over rate action.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Impact Channel: Yen Carry Trade</mark>

|Aspect|Detail|
|---|---|
|Mechanism|Traders borrow cheap yen to invest in higher-yielding emerging market assets like Indian securities|
|India's exposure (Elara Capital, Aug 2024 estimate)|Around $21 billion, about 2.2% of total FII holdings|
|Risk from stronger yen|Could trigger unwinding of carry trades, potentially causing short-term FII outflows from India|
|Current uncertainty|Analysts unsure of updated exposure figures|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Current State of FII Flows in India</mark>

- India saw positive FII inflows of $2.1 billion in July 2026, ending four consecutive months of outflows.
    
- This inflow is minor compared to the $27.2 billion in foreign outflows India's stock market has recorded so far in 2026.
    
- Analysts note India hasn't been a major beneficiary of FII inflows recently, meaning much of the negative pressure may already be priced in.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Expert Views on Magnitude of Impact</mark>

**Pankaj Pandey (ICICI Securities)**

- Central banks are reluctant to raise rates as it could hurt demand, making intervention the preferred tool.
    
- Believes crude oil price stabilization is a bigger macro concern for India than the yen carry trade unwind.
    

**Sunny Agrawal (SBICAPS Securities)**

- The US likely intervened partly to protect the dollar given the scale of US debt.
    
- Notes the US would need to "dump" another currency (possibly the euro) to buy yen, which would have a cumulative effect on the dollar index, in turn affecting commodities and emerging markets.
    
- Stresses that the exact magnitude of impact is difficult to quantify at this stage.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Takeaways</mark>

- Joint intervention: first since 2011; under Sept 2025 US-Japan Joint Statement.
    
- Trigger: yen at 40-year low in July 2026; Japan sold ~$59 billion in securities to defend currency.
    
- Yen strengthened to 155.23/dollar (3-month high), later around 156.75.
    
- India's yen carry trade exposure: ~$21 billion (~2.2% of FII holdings, 2024 estimate).
    
- India FII flows: +$2.1 billion in July 2026 vs. $27.2 billion in outflows for 2026 so far.
    
- Bigger near-term concern for India: crude oil price stabilization, not just yen movement.
    
- Uncertain variable: which currency the US sells to fund yen purchases, and its knock-on effect on the dollar index and emerging markets.
