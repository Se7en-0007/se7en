## Expansion of Private Sector Dominance

- **Non-government institutions accounted for 86% of Ayurveda colleges** and **85% of homoeopathy colleges** in **2024**.
- The **private sector is leading the rapid expansion** of AYUSH medical education infrastructure.
- **Permitted seats rose by 43%** and **total admission capacity increased by 25%** between **2021 and 2024**.
- During this period, the **Centre's AYURGYAN allocation** (for AYUSH education, training, research, innovation and capacity-building) **increased nearly sixfold**.

## Nature of Quality Concerns

- Concerns for **allopathic medical education** are confined to whether institutions **adequately prepare students for evidence-based practice**.
- For **AYUSH systems**, the concerns extend to both the **quality of training** and **what students are being trained to practise**.
- **Quality issues in India's AYUSH system predate the current boom**.

## Historical Evidence of Quality Deficiencies

- A **2005 audit by the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)** found **insufficient hospital beds, outpatient services, and/or staff** widespread among **homoeopathy colleges**, with **bed occupancy ranging from 1% to 71%**.
- A **2020 Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine article** reported that many institutions had a **shortfall exceeding 50%** in the **teaching staff required by standards** then in place.

## Regulatory Actions

- The regulator, the **National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM)**, has **often withheld or denied permission** to institutions after inspections found **gaps in faculty strength and infrastructure**.
- As of **August 21**, the **NCISM had listed 17 Ayurveda colleges - all private** - whose permissions it had **denied**, several for **"non-compliance with inspection process."**
- The **National Commission for Homoeopathy** has given **41% of all homoeopathy colleges the lowest grade**, including **nearly half of all private institutions**.

## Judicial Observation

- In **Hillside Ayurveda Medical College (2023)**, the **Karnataka High Court** acknowledged that **"often educational institutions are ... guilty of admitting excess students for financial gains."**

## Concerns Arising from Private-Led Expansion

- The **private-sector-led expansion** is of **additional concern** as it is **accompanied by incentives** for institutions to **maximise student intake** while **maintaining faculty size and laboratory infrastructure** (i.e., without proportionately scaling up resources).
- The **expansion of AYUSH education is occurring amid persistent quality issues**, including:
- **Inadequate or disputed faculty strengths**
- **Failures in inspection requirements**
- **Issues with student intake numbers**
- **Allegations of fictitious faculty**

## Key Argument

- It would be **premature to infer** that the **rapid expansion** has **amplified these problems**.
- However, the **persistent regulatory non-compliance warrants**:
- **Tougher quality control**
- **Reexamination of incentives for private institutions**
- **Reexamination of questions about the evidence backing AYUSH medicinal systems**
