## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context</mark>

The article analyses the recent "Cockroach Janta Party" (CJP) movement, a Gen Z-led protest that gathered momentum around Jantar Mantar, driven primarily by unemployment concerns, with Bihar as the epicentre of unrest. The immediate flashpoint was the government's mishandling of the crisis, culminating in the resignation of former Union Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, which the authors describe as a decision taken "just in time" to avoid a point of no return for the NDA government.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government's Response and Misjudgement</mark>

The Centre initially misread the protest as a disorganised, immature middle-class youth movement lacking staying power, expecting it to fizzle out in monsoon heat. Key missteps included:

- Keeping CJP leaders waiting over two hours at Union Health Minister J.P. Nadda's residence
    
- Simultaneously negotiating with Sonam Wangchuk while authorising a brutal lathi charge on July 20
    
- Responding with poorly executed, badly sketched Instagram videos from the Prime Minister instead of substantive engagement
    
- Only conceding after a second failed reel, via Pradhan's resignation
    

Despite the crackdown, the movement gained an "all-India revolt" character by the evening of July 20, drawing an anti-Modi mood even from BJP's traditional upper-class and upper-caste support base.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Argument: Institutional Fragility</mark>

The authors argue the CJP's organisational looseness was compensated by its ability to tap into deep-seated resentment among the middle class and aspirational poor, exposing considerable fragility in India's political institutions. This fragility stems from extreme centralisation of power over the years, which has steadily eroded the resilience of political institutions and legitimate channels for opposition.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The "Modi-Shah Political Playbook"</mark>

Central to the crisis diagnosis is a described pattern of systematically undermining democratic safeguards, including:

- Use of the Enforcement Directorate (ED) and Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) against opponents
    
- Buying and selling of MPs and MLAs to engineer defections
    
- Making courts pliable at the highest level
    
- Controversial implementation of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, raising fears that elections could become a "selection exercise" rather than a genuine contest
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Consequences and Warning Signs</mark>

The authors contend that because the CJP operated outside the framework of institutional opposition politics (i.e., outside formal party/parliamentary channels), its success (Pradhan's resignation) is less a victory and more a warning sign of turbulence within India's democratic architecture. They caution that:

- India is "perilously close" to losing its capacity for peaceful democratic transitions
    
- Mainstream Opposition parties (Congress, Rahul Gandhi, others) had only a tenuous presence during the Jantar Mantar movement, showing institutional opposition's weakened state
    
- Continued erosion of legitimate opposition space could result in future social instability and political chaos unless the described playbook is checked
