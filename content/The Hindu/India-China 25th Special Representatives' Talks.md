## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context and Background</mark>

- Talks held between Special Representatives (SRs) on the boundary question — India's NSA Ajit Doval and China's Foreign Minister Wang Yi meeting in Beijing.
- This was the 25th round of SR-level talks, part of a long-running mechanism to resolve the India-China boundary dispute.
- Both sides reaffirmed efforts of the past two years to maintain peace along the Line of Actual Control (LAC).
- Timeline of normalisation: October 2024 Modi-Xi meeting kickstarted the process; a 2025 meeting in Tianjin sustained momentum; the current talks continue this trajectory.
- Visit occurred ahead of India hosting the BRICS Summit on September 12, with Chinese President Xi Jinping's visit expected — his first in seven years.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Eight-Point Consensus</mark>

An eight-point consensus was released, with key outcomes including:

- Agreement to push for "early and substantial harvest" of boundary delimitation.
- An expert-level mechanism meeting on trans-border rivers to be held next month.
- Continued communication on sharing hydrological data — a long-standing Indian demand.
- Two additional meeting points established for talks between top military commanders.
- Two new military hotlines set up in the Eastern and Middle Sectors of the LAC.
- Reaffirmation of commitment to continuing peace and normalisation along the border.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Institutional Mechanisms</mark>

|Mechanism|Composition|Role|
|---|---|---|
|Special Representatives (SR) talks|High-level political appointees (NSA-level)|Overarching political negotiation on boundary framework|
|Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination (WMCC)|Diplomats and military officials|Operational-level border stability and de-escalation|

- Both tracks have worked in tandem over the past two years to keep the border "generally stable."

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Concerns Raised</mark>

- **Ambiguity on delimitation scope**: Neither side has clarified which specific parts of the border they intend to delimit.
- **Status of 2005 Agreement**: Unclear whether the 2005 Agreement on Political Parameters and Guiding Principles is being reconsidered or merely reaffirmed.
    
    - Article 10 of the 2005 agreement states that delineation of the boundary can only proceed on the basis of an agreed framework — raising the question of whether that framework step has actually been completed.
- **Lack of transparency**: Agreements are being made "without public visibility," which — despite their substantive merit — risks inviting public suspicion.
- **Core editorial argument**: As India and China negotiate the boundary question, both governments must "bring their publics along" through greater transparency.
