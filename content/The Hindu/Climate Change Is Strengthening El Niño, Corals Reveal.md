- **Fossil corals from the Galápagos archipelago reveal that El Niño has intensified by almost 36 per cent over the past four decades compared with 1850.**
- The findings provide the **strongest evidence that global warming is strengthening El Niño**.
- The study examined a **1,000-year archive** preserved in fossil corals and found that the **big El Niño events of the last 40 years are not normal in the context of the last thousand years**.
- The strength of El Niño has changed **in parallel with warming global temperatures**.

## Intensification And Climate Change

- The study **does not directly attribute** the strengthening of El Niño to climate change.
- However, it finds that the intensification coincided with **rapid post-industrial warming**, exceeded the **natural variability of El Niño**, and makes **human-induced warming the most probable possibility**.
- Other coral results from different parts of the Pacific also indicate that **this important source of climate extremes has already changed in recent decades**.
- Climate models simulating the last **1,000 years**, using historical data, volcanic-eruption information and solar variability, found **no major shifts in El Niño**, suggesting that natural processes could not produce such recent changes.

## El Niño

- **El Niño** is a natural phenomenon occurring every **2-7 years**, in which the **tropical Pacific Ocean becomes warmer than usual**.
- Under usual conditions, **trade winds blow east to west along the equator**, pushing warm water from **South America towards Australia**.
- When the trade winds weaken, warm water expands eastwards towards South America, triggering an **El Niño event**.
- This shift moves heavy rainfall from **Indonesia to the central Pacific**, inducing droughts in the **western Pacific**.
- The shift further weakens the trade winds, allowing El Niño conditions to persist for **1-2 years**.
- Although temperature and precipitation fluctuations are confined to the tropical Pacific, their impacts are felt worldwide.

## Climate Extremes

- In the **United States**, deserts in the **Southwest** receive more rain as storm tracks shift southwards, while rainfall decreases in the **Northwest**.
- Globally, El Niño can cause **less rainfall or droughts in countries such as India**, flooding elsewhere, crop failure and wildfires affecting millions of people.
- Forecasters have warned of a **Super El Niño** this year that may become the **strongest in almost a century**.

## Fossil Corals From The Galápagos Islands

- Researchers sampled cores from **13 corals**, including living colonies and boulders of ancient coral, from the **Galápagos Islands**.
- Corals grow **1-2 centimetres per year** by secreting layers of **calcium carbonate**.
- The chemistry of these layers records the **seawater temperature** in which corals grew.
- Because El Niño recurs every few years, researchers selected core samples that were at least **20 years old**.

## Coral Skeleton’s Chemistry

- Researchers examined the **ratio of strontium to calcium**, which is directly linked to the temperature at which corals grew.
- They supplemented this evidence with the **ratio of oxygen isotopes**, another indicator of temperature at the site.
- The resulting temperature history showed that the **Galápagos**, where El Niño has its largest impact, experienced strong El Niños in the last **40-50 years**.
- Earlier El Niños displayed consistent patterns of **lower intensity**.

## Droughts, Floods, Wildfires And Food Insecurity

- If El Niño is becoming stronger, its impacts - **droughts, floods, wildfires and food insecurity** - are also becoming stronger.
- Changes in the **hydrologic cycle** can damage infrastructure through floods that wipe out homes, highways and railroads.
- They can also produce health effects from diseases such as **cholera**.
- Stronger El Niño-driven climate extremes could amplify **ecological, infrastructural and human losses**.
- **No country has the resources to be fully protected from these impacts.**
- The findings underline the need to **mitigate global warming** and move away from **fossil fuels**, described as the root cause of the problem.
