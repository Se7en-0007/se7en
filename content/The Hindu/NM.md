Based on a review of all seven previous-year question papers (NM 2018, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025), here's a comprehensive trend analysis to guide your 2026 preparation.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overall Trend</mark>

The paper size has grown over the years - from 100 questions (2018-2021) to 150 questions (2022-2024), reflecting an expanding syllabus. Recent years (2023-2025) show heavier reliance on multi-statement "consider the following statements" and assertion-reason formats rather than simple one-liners, demanding deeper conceptual clarity rather than rote recall.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Subject-Wise Average Weightage</mark>

Environment & Ecology and Polity have emerged as the highest-weightage sections in recent papers, while Aptitude/Reasoning appears only in earlier years (2018-2019) and has since been phased out.

| Subject               | Approx. Share        | Trend                                                         |
| --------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Environment & Ecology | ~20-22%              | Rising sharply since 2021, now dominant                       |
| Polity & Governance   | ~18-20%              | Consistently high, especially Constitution articles/schedules |
| Science & Technology  | ~15-18%              | Growing focus on emerging tech (AI, space, biotech)           |
| Economy               | ~12-15%              | Steady presence, banking/fiscal terms recurring               |
| History               | ~10-12%              | Ancient & medieval dominate over modern                       |
| Geography             | ~10-12%              | Physical geography (climate, landforms) > human geography     |
| Current Affairs/IR    | ~8-10%               | Country borders, international bodies, awards                 |
| Aptitude/English      | ~10% (only pre-2022) | Discontinued from 2021-22 onward                              |

# <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Environment</mark>

In 2018-2019, environment questions were limited to isolated topics like CITES, wetlands, and MEAs, numbering roughly 8-10 out of 100 questions. By 2021 this rose sharply with a dedicated cluster covering biodiversity loss, cyclones, Ramsar sites, ENSO, and pollution acts, and by 2023-2025 the papers dedicate 25-30+ questions (out of 150) almost entirely to environment, often as consecutive question blocks.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recurring Topics</mark>

- **Protected areas & wildlife**: National Parks with endemic species (Manas–pygmy hog, Keibul Lamjao), Biosphere Reserves (Cold Desert BR, Nilgiri), Community/Conservation Reserves distinctions, Project Tiger/NTCA, Ramsar wetlands (Chilika, Mansar Lake).
- **International conventions/protocols**: CITES, Montreal/Kigali/Kunming-Montreal Protocols, Ramsar Convention, Bonn Convention, UNFCCC/Paris Agreement/Kyoto Protocol, Basel & Rotterdam Conventions.
- **Ecology fundamentals**: Ecological niche, ecotone/ecocline, biomagnification, keystone species, ecological succession, biodiversity hotspots, alpha/beta/gamma diversity, symbiosis (mutualism, lichens).
- **Climate change & meteorology**: El Nino/La Nina (ENSO), jet streams, cyclone formation conditions, Zombie ice, Bonn Challenge, COP outcomes, climate finance (NCQG), Sharm el-Sheikh Plan.
- **Pollution & legislation**: Environment (Protection) Act 1986, Water/Air Acts, Wildlife Protection Act 1972, lead/heavy metal pollution, e-waste, Rhodamine B ban, Air Quality Index pollutants, Eutrophication sequence.
- **Energy & green tech**: Green/Blue Hydrogen, Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022, carbon credit markets, Blue Carbon, BECCS, Small Modular Reactors.
- **Species-specific facts**: Critically endangered species (Great Indian Bustard, vultures, river dolphins), invasive/alien species, Indian Pangolin, bird conservation networks.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">High-Priority Areas for 2026</mark>

Focus areas should include: all Wildlife Protection Act 1972 provisions (National Parks vs Sanctuaries vs Community/Conservation Reserves), India's Ramsar sites and Biosphere Reserves list, major MEAs with their exact provisions (not just names), ecological concepts (succession, ecotone, biomagnification, keystone species), climate mechanisms (ENSO, jet streams, cyclones), and emerging green-tech/policy terms (green hydrogen, carbon markets, BECCS) since these recur almost every year with fresh contextual twists. Given the shift to multi-statement questions, practicing elimination technique on 3-4 statement environment questions will be more valuable than isolated fact memorization.

# <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">S&T</mark>

In 2018-2019, S&T questions were largely factual one-liners covering basic physics, forensic science, and space missions (PET scans, PSLV launches, Nobel laureates). By 2021-2022 the papers introduced more conceptual physics/chemistry (MRI, paramagnetism, seismic waves, isotopes) alongside biotech basics (IVF, PCR, cloning), and by 2023-2025 the focus shifted heavily toward emerging/frontier technologies and multi-statement reasoning formats.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recurring Topics</mark>

- **Space & ISRO missions**: Chandrayaan (2 & 3), Aditya-L1, PSLV/POEM, Gaganyaan, GNSS systems (GPS, GLONASS, IRNSS), James Webb Telescope, asteroid missions (16 Psyche).
- **Biotechnology & genetics**: DNA fingerprinting, PCR, cloning (somatic cell nuclear transfer), gene expression (transcription/translation), CRISPR-style DNA editing tools, biofortification vs bioprospecting vs bioinformatics.
- **Emerging/frontier tech**: Quantum Key Distribution, Generative AI capabilities, Brain-Computer Interfaces, Nanotechnology applications, IoT, superbugs/antimicrobial resistance.
- **Physics fundamentals**: MRI principles, paramagnetism, seismic wave behavior (P/S waves, shadow zones), radioactivity, phosphors, isotopes, PET scan mechanism.
- **Defence & strategic tech**: Missile systems (S-400, cruise vs ballistic missiles), DRDO projects (QRSAM), simulated warfare exercises (INDSPACEEX), aerial targets.
- **Health & medicine**: Vitamin classifications, forensic science (teeth/DNA), streptokinase, biological weapons agents, NEET/NMC-related policy, telemedicine (e-Sanjeevani).
- **Digital/fintech-adjacent tech**: Digital payments (offline payment norms), UPI-linked authentication factors, internet protocol innovations.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Format & Difficulty Shift</mark>

Early papers (2018-2019) tested direct recall ("PET scan images are due to...") while 2023-2025 papers use dense multi-statement formats (e.g., 3-part statements on Kunming-Montreal Protocol, POEM, or BCIs) requiring you to evaluate each sub-statement's technical accuracy before selecting the combination. This mirrors the same "how many of the above are correct" trend seen in Environment, making S&T increasingly analytical rather than memory-based.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">High-Priority Focus for 2026</mark>

Prioritize recent ISRO missions and their technical specifics (payload, purpose, novel components like POEM), foundational biotechnology terms (gene editing, PCR, cloning distinctions), and current buzzwords in AI/quantum/space tech since these appear almost every cycle with fresh context. Also revise basic physics/chemistry NCERT-level concepts (waves, radioactivity, isotopes) as these recur as direct factual questions even in recent papers, and keep a running note of defence technology developments (missile classifications, DRDO/ISRO joint programs) given their consistent appearance.

# <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Polity</mark>

In 2018-2019, Polity questions were direct and factual - testing single Articles, basic Parliament procedures, and fundamental duties/rights lists. From 2021 onward, the section expanded dramatically into a dedicated 15-20 question block featuring Assertion-Reason and Statement-I/II formats on Constitutional bodies, and by 2023-2025 nearly every question uses 3-4 sub-statements demanding fine distinctions between similar provisions.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recurring Topics</mark>

- **Union Executive powers**: President's ordinance-making power, pardoning power, discretionary powers vs Governor's powers on Bills, consultation with Supreme Court (Article 143), electoral college composition.
- **Emergency provisions**: National Emergency (Art 352) effects on Centre-State relations, President's Rule (Art 356), Financial Emergency (Art 360) procedures and salary-reduction powers.
- **Constitutional Amendment process**: Simple vs special majority requirements, President's assent (cannot withhold), state ratification for federal provisions, who can introduce amendment bills.
- **Parliament & legislative procedure**: Money Bill passage, joint sittings, Quorum, deadlock resolution, Parliament's power to legislate on State List subjects (Art 249, 252, President's Rule).
- **Federal features & Constitutional bodies**: Niti Aayog vs Planning Commission status, Inter-State Council, UPSC/State PSC appointment-removal asymmetry, Finance Commission, Election Commission independence.
- **Judiciary**: Supreme Court's original/appellate/advisory jurisdiction, judge appointment (collegium system, ad hoc judges), impeachment procedure, judicial review landmark cases (Kesavananda Bharati, Coelho), Contempt of Court.
- **Schedules & special provisions**: Fifth/Sixth Schedule (tribal areas), Tenth Schedule (anti-defection), Ninth Schedule, Articles 371-371J for special states, District/Regional Councils.
- **Fundamental Rights/Duties/DPSP**: Distinctions between justiciable and non-justiciable rights, specific Article coverage (Art 19-22), fundamental duties list, DPSP-FR conflict resolution.
- **Committees & Commissions**: Fazl Ali Commission, Sarkaria Commission, Rajamannar Commission, Parliamentary committees (Estimates, PAC, Public Undertakings) and their composition.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Format Shift Over Time</mark>

| Year      | Typical Format                     | Example                                                            |
| --------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 2018-2019 | Direct single-answer               | "Which Article empowers Parliament to legislate on State List?"    |
| 2021      | Statement I/II or Assertion-Reason | "Pardoning power can be challenged in SC" true/false pairs         |
| 2022-2023 | Multi-point (3-4 statements)       | Federal features, Constitutional Amendment Bill nuances            |
| 2024-2025 | Dense comparative statements       | President vs Governor assent powers, collegium appointment process |

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">High-Priority Focus for 2026</mark>

Master the fine distinctions between President and Governor's powers (assent, reservation of Bills, discretion), all three emergency provisions with their specific procedural differences, and the exact scope of judicial appointment/collegium mechanics since these recur with increasing granularity. Also revise Constitutional Amendment procedure thoroughly (majority types, who can initiate, President's limited role) and the Fifth/Sixth/Tenth Schedule specifics, as these appear almost every year in varying statement combinations. Given the trend toward "how many of the above are correct" formats, practice statement-elimination technique specifically for Centre-State relations and Constitutional body composition questions.

# <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Economy</mark>

Early papers (2018-2019) tested straightforward facts - bank scheme names, committee recommendations, and basic definitions like fiscal deficit or CAR. By 2021, Economy merged more with general knowledge (Jan Dhan Yojana, business cycles), but from 2022 onward the section became noticeably more analytical, with entire multi-part questions built around GDP calculation methods, inflation mechanics, and RBI tools.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recurring Topics</mark>

- **Banking & monetary policy**: CRR/SLR/Repo Rate, Capital Adequacy Ratio, NPA classification, Payment Banks features, money multiplier, RBI's role in managing public debt and inflation control.
- **Fiscal policy & budget**: Fiscal deficit definition, Consolidated Fund distinctions (charged vs voted expenditure), deficit financing, tax buoyancy, Debt-to-GDP ratio implications.
- **Taxation**: GST (direct vs indirect, destination-based nature), Capital Gains Tax/STT rules, Global Minimum Tax deal, Tobin Tax, Pink Tax concept.
- **National income & growth metrics**: GDP at constant/market price, HDI parameters and India's historical HDI values, inclusive growth, demographic dividend.
- **Government schemes**: PM Awaas Yojana, Jan Dhan Yojana (financial inclusion), MUDRA loans, Stand-Up India, KUSUM, PM Matsya Sampada Yojana, PM CARES Fund.
- **International economic institutions**: IMF (World Economic Outlook, founding members), World Bank, WTO, RCEP, OECD, ICRIER reports.
- **Committees & commissions**: Jalan Committee (RBI surplus transfer), Fazl Ali Commission, Kelkar Commission, Kher Commission.
- **Inflation dynamics**: Effects on bondholders/depositors, inflation-indexed bonds, RBI's inflation-control mechanisms, imported inflation, rupee depreciation linkages.
- **Poverty & welfare indices**: Absolute vs relative poverty lines, poverty gap, Socio-Economic Caste Census, BPL list preparation.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Format Evolution</mark>

| Year      | Typical Question Style              | Example                                                                    |
| --------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2018-2019 | Direct definition/fact              | "Deficit financing refers to..."                                           |
| 2021      | Simple I/II statement pairs         | "Which of following can be effects of fall in Rupee?"                      |
| 2022      | Multi-point technical statements    | GDP at constant market price, product taxes, depreciation                  |
| 2023-2025 | Dense Statement-I/II with reasoning | RBI's public debt role, STT-capital gains linkage, money multiplier impact |

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">High-Priority Focus for 2026</mark>

Prioritize monetary policy transmission mechanisms (how CRR/SLR/Repo changes affect money supply and inflation), GDP measurement nuances (market price vs factor cost, real vs nominal), and current taxation rules (GST destination principle, capital gains/STT) since these appear as increasingly technical multi-statement questions. Also revise India's global economic standing (GDP rankings by nominal/PPP), major international economic reports and their publishers (IMF, WEF, World Bank), and recent RBI/government fiscal actions, as these blend current affairs with core economic theory in the latest papers. Given the shift toward assertion-reason and "how many statements reduce X" formats, practice reasoning through cause-effect chains in monetary/fiscal policy rather than isolated fact recall.
