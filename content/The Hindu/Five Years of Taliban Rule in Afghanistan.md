## Overview

- Afghanistan under Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan (IEA) 2.0 has achieved stability but not modernization - described as a "time-freeze" postponing transition.
- After 47 years of conflict, current stability is a "respite," but progress on political, economic, social fronts remains "glacial and selective."
- Article frames Afghanistan as being at a "fork in the road" - needing choice between hardline theocracy and reform-oriented engagement with the world.

## Positive Developments (Stability Gains)

- IEA 2.0 transition (2021) was chaotic like IEA 1.0 (1996) but avoided mass retribution/civil war.
- Country now largely stable and secure; "Badlands" no longer host groups like al-Qaeda for external operations.
- Territory not being used for terrorism against other countries (key global demand).
- 5.7 million refugees returned from Pakistan/Iran during the five years - about one-eighth of population; managed "reasonably well" despite straining resources.
- These shifts differentiate IEA 2.0 somewhat from the ruinous 1996–2001 rule, partially easing global concern.

## Negative Domestic Policies

- Many IEA 2.0 policies mirror IEA 1.0 (a "carbon copy"), seen as regression from the Western-backed Republic era.
- Key restrictions imposed:
    
    - Ban on music
    - No education for girls beyond class six
    - Gender segregation
    - Lifting of ban on child marriages
    - Narrow power base excluding women, non-Pashtuns, ethnic minorities
- Justification cited by Taliban: Islamic Sharia, Sunni Deobandi Islam, Hanafi jurisprudence, Pashtun traditions.

## Western Response

- West feels "betrayed" - Taliban did not honor commitments under the 2020 Doha Agreement (which enabled US/NATO withdrawal).
- Cost of US 20-year campaign against Taliban: 6,379 deaths, $2.26 trillion - seen as having achieved little.
- Despite Pakistani lobbying, global consensus held to withhold recognition until draconian policies moderate.
- UNSC Resolution 2593 (adopted August 30, 2021) remains cornerstone of global policy - demands:
    
    - No use of Afghan territory for terrorism
    - Human rights upheld (women, children, minorities)
    - Inclusive, negotiated political settlement with full/equal participation of women
    - Adherence to rule of law, building on 20 years of prior gains

## Political Architecture of IEA 2.0

|Feature|Detail|
|---|---|
|Structure|Uncodified unitary theocracy|
|Origin|Evolved from Quetta Shura → renamed Rahbari Shura|
|Legitimizing body|Loya Jirga (Grand Assembly), Kabul, mid-2022; endorsed by 3,000 clerics/tribal leaders|
|Supreme Leader|Hibatullah Akhundzada (Amir al-Mu'minin) - head of state, commander-in-chief, religious leader|
|Top body|26-member Leadership Council|
|PM|Hassan Akhund, plus 3 Deputy Leaders|
|Implementation|Cabinet|
|"Constitution"|Holy Koran; common law = Hanafi jurisprudence|

## Economic Situation - Fragile Progress

**Macro indicators:**

- Corruption/smuggling curbed via draconian measures; Afghani currency stable.
- Consumer inflation: 2% (2025).
- Nominal GDP: $19.66 billion - composition: services 49%, agriculture 26%, manufacturing/mining 13%, construction 12%.
- GDP growth: 4.3% (2025); projected 2.3% (2026).
- GDP fell ~20% right after Taliban's 2021 return to power.
- GDP per capita: only $448 (less than one-sixth of India's).

**Human development / poverty:**

- Population: 49 million; nearly half live below national poverty line, facing food shortages.
- Population grew 11% in 2025 (mainly due to immigration/refugee return) → caused 4% decline in per capita GDP despite healthy growth.
- HDI score: 0.496 - among world's lowest.
- Unemployment: 13.3% formally; most women and a quarter of young men jobless.

**Trade & finance:**

- Foreign trade: $13.9 billion (2025); exports cover only 15% of imports.
- Tax revenues rising, but negligible foreign investment and declining external grants - leaves government with insufficient funds for welfare/infrastructure.
- US froze $9.6 billion of Afghanistan's foreign assets after IEA 2.0 formed (2021).
- Most Western Official Development Assistance (ODA) remains suspended; some NGOs continue humanitarian support.

## International Engagement & Recognition

- IEA claims 40 active diplomatic missions abroad; 20+ countries represented in Kabul.
- Russia: only country to formally recognize IEA and open an embassy in Kabul.
- Pakistan, China, Iran, India: engaged at various de jure levels without formal recognition; some trade/mineral deals made.
- Iran, Central Asian Republics, Gulf countries, Türkiye: trade and logistical links extended.
- Relations stable with most regional countries - exception being Pakistan, currently in conflict with Afghanistan.

## India–Afghanistan Relations

- India shifted from initial scepticism to substantive engagement, while still not extending full diplomatic recognition (aligned with global norms).
- Afghan Foreign Minister visited India (October 2025); followed by visits of three other Afghan Ministers.
- Bilateral trade ~$1 billion annually despite Pakistan denying overland transit; India is one of the largest markets for Afghan exports.
- India providing humanitarian assistance; also mentoring Afghan cricket team's performance improvement.

## Signs of Possible Change & Way Forward

- Some IEA leaders show awareness of unsustainability of hardline agenda - e.g., Taliban now attribute the girls' higher-education ban partly to lack of funds for segregated education (an opening for negotiation).
- Suggested path: World community should create a "critical mass of incentives" to gently encourage IEA reform rather than isolate it.
- India positioned as South Asia's "trendsetter," well-placed to broker normalization - possibly via a multilateral donors' conference.
- Historical irony: Pakistan tried and failed to get world acceptance for IEA 2.0 five years ago (Taliban, flush with victory, saw no need to reform then).
- Conclusion of piece: A progressive, moderate Afghanistan would benefit both India and the world.
