World Elephant Day is observed globally every year on 12 August to highlight the ecological and cultural importance of elephants and the need for sustained conservation action.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview</mark>

- India hosts nearly 60% of the world's wild Asian elephant population, making it central to global elephant conservation.
    
- The Synchronous All India Population Estimation of Elephants (SAIEE) 2021–25 estimated India's wild elephant population at 22,446.
    
- Elephants are a keystone/foundational species — they sustain forests, support biodiversity and maintain ecological balance.
    
- Conservation challenges addressed include habitat loss, fragmentation, poaching, illegal wildlife trade and human-elephant conflict.
    
- India's conservation strategy blends legal protection, habitat conservation, scientific monitoring, technology-driven measures and community participation.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Ecological Importance</mark>

- Elephants are migratory, with home ranges spanning 100–3,000 km².
    
- Their movement creates pathways and water holes benefiting other wildlife.
    
- Feeding habits support nutrient cycling and seed dispersal, aiding forest regeneration.
    
- They help connect fragmented forests, supporting genetic diversity and ecological connectivity.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Cultural and Social Importance</mark>

- North India: Lord Ganesha represents wisdom and removal of obstacles.
    
- Royalty: Elephants historically symbolised authority and prestige as royal emblems.
    
- South India: Temple elephants remain part of religious rituals and festivals.
    
- Northeast India: Communities worship Lord Ganesha as a manifestation of their deities; some regard elephants as elders and symbols of wisdom.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Project Elephant</mark>

- Government of India's flagship elephant conservation initiative, implemented by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC).
    
- Provides financial and technical support to States for:
    
- Protecting elephant habitats
    
- Conserving captive elephants
    
- Mitigating human-elephant conflict
    
- Promotes science-driven conservation via AI-enabled monitoring, radio-collaring, genetic databases and early warning systems.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">SAIEE 2021–25 (Scientific Monitoring)</mark>

- India's first DNA-based synchronised elephant census.
    
- Used dung sample analysis to identify individual elephants via unique genetic fingerprints, helping detect repeat samples and improve accuracy.
    
- Combined DNA profiling, block counts and line-transect surveys for scientific rigour.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Elephant Corridors</mark>

- Corridors connect fragmented elephant habitats, enabling safe movement, reducing human-elephant conflict, and supporting genetic diversity and population viability.
    
- Corridors identified and validated across 14 elephant-range states; currently 150 corridors operational.
    
- Per "Elephant Corridors of India, 2023" (prepared with Wildlife Institute of India support):
    

|Region|Number of Corridors|
|---|---|
|East-Central|52|
|North-East|48|
|Southern|32|
|Northern|18|

- West Bengal has the highest number: 26 corridors (over 17% of national total).
    
- Network includes 19 inter-state corridors and 6 transboundary corridors (India–Nepal).
    
- Corridor usage status: increased use in 59 corridors (40%), stable in 29 (19%), declining in 29 (19%), impaired in 15 corridors.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Elephant Reserves</mark>

- Since 2014, coverage has expanded to 80,777 km² across 14 major elephant-range states.
    
- Additional 8,610 km² brought under protection since 2014.
    
- Expansion has improved scientific management and ecological connectivity.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Management and Planning Frameworks</mark>

- Management Effectiveness Evaluation framework: launched in 2023; piloted in four reserves — Shivalik, Kaziranga–Karbi Anglong, Mayurbhanj, Nilgiri; now being extended nationwide with CAMPA funding.
    
- Regional Action Plans: developed for all elephant-bearing regions, providing a common framework for States to coordinate on habitat connectivity, conflict mitigation and landscape management across jurisdictions.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Railway Safety Measures</mark>

- Joint surveys covered 127 sensitive railway stretches spanning 3,452.4 km.
    
- 77 stretches across 14 States (1,965.2 km) recommended for mitigation involving 705 structures — underpasses, overpasses, ramps, level crossings, bridge modifications.
    
- Technology used: optical-fibre-based intrusion detection, seismic sensors, thermal cameras, and AI-enabled Distributed Acoustic Sensing (real-time alerts to loco pilots, station masters, control rooms).
    
- Every train-elephant collision is jointly investigated with the State Forest Department.
    
- Result: train-hit elephant deaths declined from 26 (2013) to 12 (2024) — a fall of over 50%.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Captive Elephant Safeguards</mark>

- Legal basis: Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 — cases of illegal sale/transport/procurement referred to State Chief Wildlife Wardens.
    
- Gajah Suchana mobile application: national genetic database with DNA profiles, morphological characteristics, and ownership details of every registered captive elephant; used to verify identity before movement and as forensic evidence in legal proceedings.
    
- Captive Elephant (Transfer or Transport) Rules, 2024: regulates movement of captive elephants.
    
- High-Powered Committee: examines interstate transfers, constituted under Supreme Court order dated 3 March 2023.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Community Support and Conflict Management</mark>

Ex-gratia assistance under Centrally Sponsored Schemes "Project Tiger and Elephant" and "Development of Wildlife Habitats":

- ₹10 lakh for death or permanent incapacitation
    
- ₹2 lakh for grievous injury
    
- Up to ₹25,000 for minor injury treatment
    
- Compensation for crop/property loss as per State/UT norms
    

Additional measures:

- Human-Wildlife Conflict Advisory: recommends hotspot mapping, rapid response teams, State/district-level review committees, faster compensation.
    
- Elephant-specific conflict mitigation guidelines issued in 2023.
