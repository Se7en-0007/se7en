## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Article Overview</mark>

The article by Pooja Bhatt argues that the Indo-Pacific Partnership for Maritime Domain Awareness (IPMDA) is the most credible solution to secure unimpeded trade in the Indo-Pacific amid rising maritime threats and a persistent surveillance gap.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context: US-India Relations</mark>

- Diplomatic friction exists due to mounting US sanctions and visa issues affecting Indians.
    
- Despite friction, Washington needs regional powers like India to support shared global interests.
    
- The West Asia (energy) crisis triggered renewed discussions between Indian and US Foreign and Commerce Ministers on reciprocal trade and energy agreements.
    
- Both sides have agreed that Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA) is central to ensuring "unimpeded trade."
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why the Indo-Pacific Matters (Trade Artery)</mark>

- The Indo-Pacific functions as the "circulatory system" of the global economy, carrying nearly $7 trillion in trade annually.
    
- It links energy producers in the Persian Gulf, manufacturing hubs in East Asia, and consumer markets in the Americas.
    
- Key maritime chokepoints located here: Straits of Malacca, Lombok, Sunda, Hormuz, Bab-al-Mandeb, and the Mozambique Channel.
    
- These chokepoints are vital for both energy and container/cargo shipping.
    
- This artery is threatened by state-sponsored coercion, illegal maritime activity, and a surveillance gap that is geographic, jurisdictional, and institutional in nature.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What is IPMDA?</mark>

|Aspect|Details|
|---|---|
|Full form|Indo-Pacific Partnership for Maritime Domain Awareness|
|Launched under|Quad framework (India, Australia, Japan, US)|
|Year launched|2022|
|Nature|Practical, technology-based mechanism|
|Function|Shares near-real-time maritime data across partner nations|
|Purpose|Detect, deter, and respond to threats against free trade flow|
|Anchor|Robust US-India cooperation|
|Related tech deal|India purchased SeaVision technology from the US in 2025 (software, training, logistics support)|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Surveillance Deficit Problem</mark>

- The Indo-Pacific suffers a widening maritime surveillance deficit.
    
- MDA is described as a "multilayered technological assimilation" providing understanding of the maritime domain's impact on security, safety, economy, and environment.
    
- **"Going dark" phenomenon**: Hundreds of vessels disable Automatic Identification System (AIS) transponders to evade sanctions, conceal cargo, or engage in smuggling/ship-to-ship transfers.
    
- Many smaller fishing vessels fall outside mandatory AIS requirements, enabling illegal fishing in other nations' waters or high seas, depleting stocks and threatening food security.
    
- Non-state and state-affiliated actors conduct "grey-zone operations" to intimidate/coerce while evading legal or military response.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">IPMSC - The Newer Layer</mark>

- **IPMSC** = Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration 2026, operating under the IPMDA architecture.
    
- Based on the shared understanding that no single country can monitor the vast oceans alone.
    
- Integrates data from:
    
    - Commercial satellite-based radio-frequency monitoring
        
    - Radar fusion
        
    - Existing partner-nation sensor networks
        
- Creates a layered, near-continuous picture of maritime activities shared among like-minded partners.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Beneficiaries and Stakeholders</mark>

**For the US**: Regional cooperation in the Indian Ocean frees it to focus on its own neighbourhood - the Pacific and Atlantic.

**For India**:

- Its Information Fusion Centre-Indian Ocean Region (IFC-IOR), established in Gurugram in 2018, complements IPMDA's regional hub structure.
    
- IPMDA respects India's sovereignty while amplifying its maritime reach.
    

**Broader partners needed for full potential**:

- Existing Quad members
    
- ASEAN partner states
    
- Pacific Island nations (critical maritime corridors)
    
- The European Union (shown interest since the Hormuz crisis)
    

**Smaller, capacity-constrained states** that stand to benefit significantly: Bangladesh, the Maldives, the Seychelles, Sri Lanka, and Fiji - these have some independent surveillance infrastructure but it remains ineffective against maritime crimes beyond their own jurisdiction, creating room for collaborative solutions.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Takeaway</mark>

- The assumption that open trade is "unilaterally sustainable" has been dismantled by the current strategic environment.
    
- Freedom of navigation, on which global supply chains depend, is increasingly contested - and unrebutted contestation is becoming precedent.
    
- Core thesis: In a region where coercion is becoming the "currency of power," transparency (via mechanisms like IPMDA/IPMSC) must serve as a form of deterrence.
