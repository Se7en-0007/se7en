## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview and Purpose</mark>

Samudra Manthan is a National Offshore Exploration Scheme approved on 31 July 2026, marking a major step in India's energy security and self-reliance journey. It has a Phase-I outlay of ₹84,084 crore extending up to FY 2030–31, aimed at unlocking India's offshore hydrocarbon potential. The scheme focuses on accelerating exploration, deploying modern technology, creating shared infrastructure, and strengthening domestic manufacturing.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Offshore Exploration Matters</mark>

- India is the world's third-largest consumer of crude oil, with an annual import bill of nearly USD 144 billion (about ₹13 lakh crore)
    
- Hydrocarbons (crude oil and natural gas) are organic compounds formed deep below land and seabed, forming raw material for petrol, diesel, LPG, petrochemicals, and fertilisers
    
- India's eastern and western offshore basins extend to depths of up to 3,000 metres and hold an estimated 5,600+ MMTOE of hydrocarbon potential
    
- Recent discoveries in the Cauvery, Mahanadi, and Andaman basins have reinforced this promise
    
- Existing fields naturally decline 6–7 percent annually, making new discoveries essential to sustain output
    
- A deepwater exploratory well costs USD 125–150 million and takes 5–10 years from block award to commercial production
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Four Core Components</mark>

Samudra Manthan integrates reliable data, advanced technology, modern infrastructure, and a resilient supply chain through four components.

- Seismic Data Acquisition, Processing, and Interpretation (₹28,534 crore) – funds large-scale 2D and 3D seismic surveys; AI speeds up processing; old National Data Repository data will be reprocessed
    
- Accelerated Offshore Exploration (₹43,200 crore) – supports drilling of 60 deepwater/ultra-deepwater wells; covers up to 50 percent of eligible drilling cost or ₹675 crore per well, whichever is lower; open to public and private operators
    
- Common Offshore Infrastructure (₹10,000 crore) – shared production and evacuation facilities, particularly for the Mahanadi and Kutch basins, to convert idle discoveries into producing fields
    
- Oil and Gas Manufacturing and Services Zone (₹2,000 crore) – builds domestic capability in manufacturing, repair, warehousing, engineering, and services; supports Make in India
    

An additional ₹350 crore is allocated for monitoring, digital interventions, evaluation, human resources, awareness, and outreach.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Expected Outcomes</mark>

- Larger reserves: over 600 MMTOE addition, expanding resource base from 1.6 billion to 2.2 billion Tonnes of Oil Equivalent
    
- Higher output: annual production targeted to rise from ~62 MMTOE to 80 MMTOE, with incremental production of 10–15 MMTOE and peak potential of 20 MMTOE
    
- Lower import bill: additional output could cut crude imports by nearly ₹1 lakh crore annually
    
- Investment, GDP growth, large-scale employment generation, and strengthened indigenous manufacturing under Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Enabling Ecosystem Provisions</mark>

- Digital programme management for transparent implementation
    
- Capacity building for sector professionals
    
- Adoption of cutting-edge technologies
    
- Continuous stakeholder engagement
    
- International outreach to attract global partners
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Preceding Policy Reforms</mark>

- Opening up offshore acreage: over 99 percent of earlier "No-Go" areas removed; over 1 million sq km of Exclusive Economic Zone now open for exploration
    
- New licensing framework: Hydrocarbon Exploration and Licensing Policy (HELP), 2016, introduced the Open Acreage Licensing Programme (OALP), replacing the nomination-based approach
    
- Simpler contracts: shift from Production Sharing Contracts (PSC) to Revenue Sharing Contracts (RSC) in 2016, making the fiscal framework simpler and reducing administrative intervention
    
- Modern law: Oilfields (Regulation and Development) Amendment Act, 2025, provided contractual stability and stronger dispute resolution
    
- Enabling rules: Petroleum and Natural Gas Rules, 2025, streamlined administration of petroleum leases
    
- Level playing field: standardised Empowered Committee of Secretaries mandate across all contract regimes
    
- Sharper focus: ONGC and Oil India performance parameters reoriented towards exploration
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Geoscientific Data Initiatives</mark>

Seismic surveys send sound waves into the earth to map rock layers below the seabed, forming the basis of good exploration. India has invested in geoscientific knowledge through Mission Anveshan, the National Seismic Programme, the Extended Continental Shelf Survey, the National Data Repository, and the Hydrocarbon Resource Assessment Study, creating one of the world's most comprehensive geoscientific databases.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Progress So Far</mark>

- Nearly 81 percent of active exploration acreage has been awarded after 2014
    
- Under OALP, 172 blocks covering nearly 3.8 lakh sq km have been awarded, with committed investments exceeding USD 4.3 billion
    
- During FY 2025–26, around 674 wells were drilled, five new discoveries made, and seven discoveries monetised
    
- On 25 July 2026, drilling of the first appraisal well (MN-DWN18-1-HD) began in the Mahanadi Offshore Basin, the first of four planned deepwater wells using world-class deepwater drilling technology
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Terms</mark>

- Hydrocarbons: organic compounds of hydrogen and carbon found below land and seabed
    
- Basin: a large natural depression where hydrocarbons collect
    
- Appraisal well: drilled to evaluate quality and commercial value of a hydrocarbon discovery
    
- Spudding: the start of drilling a new well
    
- Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ): sea area where India holds rights over natural resources
    
- MMTOE: Million Metric Tonnes of Oil Equivalent, a unit measuring hydrocarbon reserves/production
