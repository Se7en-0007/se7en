## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context of The Controversy</mark>

The Census 2027 population enumeration questionnaire has sparked political controversy due to detailed personal questions about respondents and their parents. The Congress has alleged this could enable surveillance and serve a "deeper nefarious purpose," with the Left raising similar concerns. This follows an August 15, 2026 report revealing that several new/modified Census questions were also part of the National Population Register (NPR) schedule notified in 2020 - a period when NPR was highly contentious due to protests over the Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) and fears of a nationwide National Register of Citizens (NRC).

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">New Census Questions vs 2011 Census</mark>

The population enumeration questionnaire has 40 total questions, of which 14 are new or modified compared to the 2011 Census.

**Complete list of 14 new/modified questions:**

- Spouse's name
    
- Nationality as declared
    
- Father's and mother's particulars
    
- Scheduled Caste/Scheduled Tribe/caste details
    
- Literacy and digital literacy
    
- Highest educational qualification and stream
    
- Place of Covid-19 vaccination
    
- Number of bank accounts
    
- Mobile number
    
- Aadhaar
    
- Voter ID
    
- Passport
    
- Driving licence
    

**Overlap with NPR (2020 schedule) - 8 questions:**

- Nationality as declared
    
- Father's and mother's particulars
    
- Mobile number
    
- Aadhaar
    
- Voter ID
    
- Passport
    
- Driving licence
    

The parental particulars question is especially significant - the 2020 NPR schedule sought date and place of birth of a person's father and mother, including district/State, or country if born outside India. This specific question was at the centre of the earlier NPR opposition.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why It Is Controversial: Two Core Concerns</mark>

**1. Privacy Concern**  
Census data has traditionally been collected purely for population statistics and planning purposes. The new questionnaire seeks much wider personal information that can identify individuals or link them to other government databases - raising questions about how such data is stored, protected and used, not just how much is collected.

**2. Citizenship Concern**

- NPR is a register of "usual residents," not a citizenship register.
    
- A "usual resident" is defined as someone who has lived in a local area for at least six months, or intends to stay for the next six months - this can include foreigners meeting the residency criterion.
    
- However, NPR is legally linked to preparing the National Register of Indian Citizens (NRIC).
    
- The Citizenship (Registration of Citizens and Issue of National Identity Cards) Rules, 2003 provide for preparation of a Population Register and its verification for creating the citizens' register, and allow individuals with "doubtful" citizenship to be marked for further inquiry.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Table: NPR, CAA, NRC - Distinguishing the Concepts</mark>

|Concept|Nature|Key Point|
|---|---|---|
|NPR (National Population Register)|Register of usual residents|First prepared 2010 (with 2011 Census houselisting), updated 2015|
|CAA (Citizenship Amendment Act)|Amendment to citizenship law|Passed in 2019; triggered protests|
|NRC (National Register of Citizens)|Citizenship verification exercise|Only implemented in Assam so far, excluded ~19 lakh people|
|Citizenship Rules, 2003|Legal framework|Links NPR preparation to NRIC creation|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Timeline: Why 2019-20 Became Politically Explosive</mark>

- NPR itself is not new - first prepared in 2010, updated in 2015 without major controversy.
    
- In 2019-20, NPR revival coincided with passage of CAA and the aftermath of the Assam NRC (which excluded ~19 lakh people).
    
- Union Home Minister Amit Shah repeatedly spoke of a nationwide NRC and urged people to understand the "chronology" - i.e., CAA would be followed by NRC ("aap chronology samajhiye").
    
- This political messaging transformed a routine demographic exercise into one associated with citizenship-questioning and surveillance fears.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government's 2020 Reassurances</mark>

- Then I&B Minister Prakash Javadekar stated that questions on parents' date/place of birth were optional - columns could be left blank if unknown.
    
- Then Union Minister Ram Vilas Paswan clarified that nobody could lose citizenship for not knowing parents' date/place of birth, and no documentary proof would be required.
    
- Government maintained NPR was primarily meant to improve policy formulation and welfare scheme delivery; Shah argued that demographic composition data helps governments plan services and identify beneficiaries.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Contradiction That Fuelled Mistrust</mark>

- In December 2019, Amit Shah stated NPR data would never be used for NRC, claiming the two exercises were governed by different laws.
    
- However, earlier government statements in Parliament, and the Citizenship Rules, 2003 themselves, had explicitly linked the Population Register to preparation of the citizens' register - creating a direct contradiction that undermined official assurances.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government's Rationale for Detailed Data Collection</mark>

- Enables better policy formulation, public service planning, and beneficiary identification.
    
- Improves national security.
    
- Cross-referencing data across databases can reduce duplication/discrepancies and cut paperwork for citizens.
    
- NPR was envisaged to combine basic demographic data with mobile numbers and identity documents for more efficient government services.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Takeaway for Analysis</mark>

The article's core argument is that NPR/NRC exercises are not inherently "nefarious," and India has conducted NPR before without controversy. The 2020 backlash arose specifically because NPR was revived at a moment when public trust on citizenship issues was already damaged by CAA, the Assam NRC exclusions, and the "chronology" remarks - and this same historical baggage is now colouring perceptions of the Census 2027 questionnaire's overlapping questions.
