Regenerative agriculture is a holistic farming approach that goes beyond sustainability by actively restoring soil health, biodiversity, and ecosystem resilience, and the Government of India has scaled up multiple schemes to promote it across natural farming, micro-irrigation, agroforestry, and integrated farming.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Regenerative Agriculture Matters</mark>

India's agriculture and allied sector recorded a decadal growth of 4.45% during FY16–FY25, the highest in previous decades. However, decades of intensive cultivation, chemical overuse, and erratic rainfall have degraded soil health, raising input costs and making harvests uncertain, especially for small and marginal farmers. Regenerative agriculture addresses this by rebuilding soil vitality and strengthening resilience to climate variability, while supporting long-term environmental balance.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What Is Regenerative Agriculture</mark>

- Holistic farming approach centred on restoring soil health and enhancing biodiversity while ensuring long-term productivity and resilience.
    
- Unlike sustainable farming (which maintains existing conditions), regenerative agriculture actively improves them.
    
- Healthy soils support higher food/nutritional output, greater carbon storage, and richer biodiversity.
    

**Core principles:**

- Minimizing soil disturbance
    
- Protecting living roots of perennial crops
    
- Maintaining soil cover
    
- Integrating livestock
    
- Limiting chemical input use
    
- Enhancing biodiversity
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Practices Under Regenerative Agriculture</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Water and Resource Efficiency</mark>

- **Micro-irrigation** (drippers, sprinklers, foggers): delivers water directly to roots, reduces wastage, builds drought resilience.
    
- **Farm mechanization**: automates operations, optimizes water/fuel/fertilizer use, lowers carbon footprint, improves precision and productivity.
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Soil and Nutrient Management</mark>

- **Nitrogen fertilizer management**: reduces nitrous oxide emissions via right fertilizer choice, timing, deep placement of supergranular urea, leaf colour charts, and biological inputs (cyanobacteria, legume intercropping).
    
- **Natural farming**: chemical-free, rooted in traditional knowledge; uses on-farm biological inputs (Beejamrut, Jeevamrut, GhanJeevamrut, Neemastra, Dashparni) from native livestock; promotes traditional seeds, boundary trees, botanical pest control, cover cropping, multi-cropping, mulching, minimal soil disturbance, and livestock integration.
    
- **Soil health practices**:
    
    - Green manuring - adds organic matter/nutrients, improves fertility, structure, carbon sequestration
        
    - Cover cropping - prevents erosion, maintains organic carbon, supports nutrient cycling
        
    - Mulching - conserves moisture, regulates temperature, suppresses weeds, boosts microbial activity
        

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Integrated and Climate-Resilient Systems</mark>

- **Integrated Farming Systems (IFS)**: combines crops, livestock, fisheries, horticulture, agroforestry, apiculture; uses intercropping/crop rotation/mixed farming to diversify livelihoods and cut crop-failure risk.
    
- **Agroforestry**: integrates perennial trees with crops; boosts income, soil moisture, temperature regulation, carbon storage; vital for rejuvenating degraded rainfed land.
    
- **Climate-Resilient Agriculture (CRA)**: covers balanced nutrient/fertilizer use, pest management, judicious water use, soil moisture conservation, appropriate land preparation, plus innovations like water-sharing groups, seed/fodder systems, community nurseries, and collective marketing.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Benefits of Regenerative Farming</mark>

|Category|Benefits|
|---|---|
|Environmental|Soil restoration, biodiversity enhancement, climate mitigation (carbon absorption), water efficiency (reduced runoff/erosion)|
|Economic|Lower input costs, stabilized yields, higher net incomes, new employment in farming/advisory/tech/ecology|
|Social/Resilience|Greater resilience to climate variability and market fluctuations|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Linkage to SDGs</mark>

Regenerative agriculture aligns closely with **SDG 13 (Climate Action)**, particularly **Target 13.1**, which focuses on strengthening resilience and adaptive capacity to climate risks. By helping reverse farm-level greenhouse gas emissions, it directly contributes to this target.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government Schemes Promoting Regenerative Agriculture</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">National Mission on Natural Farming (NMNF) - launched 2024</mark>

- Output-based incentive: ₹4,000/acre/year for two years (up to 1 acre/farmer)
    
- 10,000 need-based Bio-input Resource Centres planned
    
- Natural farming clusters, each supported by 2 Community Resource Persons (CRPs)
    
- As of 5 March 2026: 18,786 clusters formed, covering 8.80 lakh hectares, 18.19 lakh farmers enrolled, 33,676 CRPs trained (at KVKs/Agricultural Universities/local institutions)
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY) - launched 2015</mark>

- Organic farming support: ₹31,500/hectare over three years
    
- As of 31 October 2025: 16.90 lakh hectares covered since inception
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Per Drop More Crop (PDMC) - since 2015-16</mark>

- Promotes drip and sprinkler micro-irrigation
    
- Financial assistance: 55% for small/marginal farmers, 45% for others (plus state top-ups)
    
- As of May 2026: ~109 lakh hectares covered; ₹26,325 crore central assistance released
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Rainfed Area Development Programme (RAD) - since 2014-15</mark>

- Promotes IFS via area-based cluster approach (~20 hectares/cluster in adjoining villages)
    
- Financial assistance: ₹30,000/family for IFS adoption + ₹10,000/cluster for training
    
- As of December 2025: ₹2,251.98 crore Central Assistance released; 9.53 lakh hectares covered; 15.73 lakh farmers benefitted
    
- KVKs conducted 4,416 demonstrations, trained 96,013 farmers (2024-25) on IFS models
    
- FY 2025-26 allocation: ₹343.86 crore
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Agroforestry Component (under PM-RKVY, restructured from erstwhile SMAF, 2023-24)</mark>

- Supports nurseries for Quality Planting Material (QPM); covers new nursery establishment, sapling raising, tissue culture units, skill development, R&D, voluntary carbon market projects
    
- Funding: 100% assistance to government agencies; 50% back-ended credit-linked assistance to private beneficiaries
    
- 2025-26 (as of 31 Oct 2025): ₹78.31 crore sanctioned to 27 States/UTs; 135 new nurseries established; sapling production in 614 existing nurseries; ~176.59 lakh saplings raised; ~25,693 farmers benefitted
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Soil Health Card Scheme (SHC) - launched 2015</mark>

- Provides plot-wise soil test reports every 2 years, assessing 12 key parameters (macronutrients, micronutrients, critical soil properties)
    
- Gives crop-specific recommendations on fertilizers, bio-fertilizers, organic inputs
    
- As of 13 March 2026: 25.89 crore Soil Health Cards generated since inception; 7.17 lakh demonstrations conducted (via ATMA and KVKs); 70,002 Krishi Sakhis trained for grassroots advisory dissemination

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward</mark>

Regenerative agriculture is gradually reshaping Indian farming by restoring soil health while improving resource efficiency and resilience. As adoption scales through these government interventions, Indian agriculture can move towards being both environmentally sustainable and economically rewarding.
