## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview & Launch</mark>

- Scheme name: Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PM-JDY)
- Launched: 2014
- Nature: India's National Mission for Financial Inclusion
- Nodal Ministry: Ministry of Finance, Department of Financial Services
- Significance: World's largest financial inclusion initiative
- Anniversary being marked: 12 years (as of Aug 2026)

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Objectives & Evolution</mark>

- Initial focus (2014): Provide a bank account to "every household"
- Expanded focus (2018): Ensure "every unbanked adult" has access to a bank account
- Core services provided: Basic banking, need-based credit, remittances, insurance, pension
- Target group: Underserved and low-income groups

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Financial Inclusion Matters</mark>

- Helps people manage risks and invest in their future
- Supports livelihood building and greater economic participation
- Reduces income inequalities
- For women specifically: strengthens financial independence and economic empowerment

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Eligibility Criteria</mark>

- Open to all citizens of Indian nationality
- No upper age limit
- One basic savings bank account per unbanked person
- No minimum balance requirement

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Account Opening Process</mark>

- Can be opened at: Bank branch or Business Correspondent/Bank Mitra outlet (extended arm of bank branch)
- Process: Submit account opening form + KYC documents; bank verifies and activates account
- Joint accounts are permitted

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Documents Required (KYC)</mark>

- Aadhaar card
- Government ID proof: voter ID, PAN card, or ration card
- Address proof: passport, driving licence, electricity/telephone/water bill
- Passport-size photograph
- Duly filled and signed PMJDY account opening form
- Any other document notified by the Government

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Benefits & Features</mark>

|Feature|Detail|
|---|---|
|Interest on deposits|Regular savings bank account interest rate applies|
|RuPay Debit Card|Domestic debit card, usable at ATMs and most PoS machines|
|Accident insurance|₹1 lakh (enhanced to ₹2 lakh for accounts opened after Aug 2018); premium-free, paid by NPCI|
|Overdraft facility|Up to ₹10,000 per household (one account holder); available after 6 months of satisfactory account operation, subject to eligibility|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Linked Social Security Schemes</mark>

Accounts under PM-JDY are eligible for convergence with:

- Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)
- Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY)
- Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY)
- Atal Pension Yojana (APY)
- Micro Units Development & Refinance Agency (MUDRA) scheme

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Achievements/Statistics (as of 19 August 2026)</mark>

**Accounts**

- Grew 4x over 12 years: 14.72 crore (2015) → 59.09 crore (2026)
- Rural/semi-urban accounts: 45.95 crore
- Urban/metropolitan accounts: 13.14 crore

**Female Beneficiaries**

- 32.92 crore female account holders — indicates progress on gender equality in financial access

**Deposits**

- Surged ~20x: ₹15,670 crore (March 2015) → ₹3,16,514 crore (Aug 2026)
- Reflects rising savings habit, trust in formal banking, deeper financial participation

**RuPay Cards**

- 41.29 crore RuPay debit cards issued — reflects growth in digital transactions

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Financial Inclusion Index</mark>

- Rose from 53.9 (2018) to 67 (2026)
- Indicates deeper access to credit, insurance, savings, and digital payments

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">International & Global Recognition</mark>

- Guinness World Record (January 2015): 18,096,130 bank accounts opened in a single week under the campaign led by Department of Financial Services
- IMF and World Bank have both highlighted PMJDY's role in expanding formal banking access in India

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overall Impact</mark>

- Transformed financial inclusion from a policy objective into a lived reality for millions
- Expanded access to banking, credit, insurance, pensions, and digital payments
- Strengthened formal financial identities and economic confidence, especially among underserved and rural populations
- Central pillar of India's broader financial inclusion and inclusive growth strategy
