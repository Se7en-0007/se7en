## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview</mark>

Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan (PM-AASHA) is the Government of India's flagship umbrella scheme for price support to farmers, aimed at ensuring remunerative prices while maintaining price stability for consumers. It was launched in September 2018 and brings together multiple pre-existing price-support mechanisms under one unified framework. The scheme's core objectives are strengthening MSP implementation, reducing distress sales, stabilising farm incomes, and ensuring food price stability for consumers.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Facts at a Glance</mark>

- Launch year: September 2018
    
- Budget allocation 2026–27: ₹7,200 crore
    
- Actual expenditure 2024–25: ₹5,437.99 crore
    
- Budget 2025–26: ₹6,941.36 crore
    
- Nodal procurement agencies: NAFED (National Agricultural Cooperative Marketing Federation of India) and NCCF (National Cooperative Consumers' Federation of India Limited)
    
- Digital reforms: Aadhaar-enabled authentication, e-NAM, e-Samriddhi, e-Samyukti
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Four Components of PM-AASHA</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Price Support Scheme (PSS)</mark>

Procures crops at MSP when market prices fall below MSP during harvest; mainly covers pulses, oilseeds, and copra. Procurement is done via NAFED/NCCF at state governments' request, restricted to registered farmers with valid land records to ensure direct benefit transfer without intermediaries.

- From 2024–25: procurement allowed up to 25% of a State/UT's production
    
- Additional procurement (beyond 25%) can be approved by the Committee of Secretaries, up to 25% of national production
    
- Exception: Tur, Urad, and Masur can be procured up to 100% of State production (to boost domestic pulse output and cut imports)
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Price Stabilization Fund (PSF)</mark>

Protects consumers from price volatility by maintaining buffer stocks of commodities like pulses, onions, and potatoes. Commodities are bought at harvest and released in lean seasons to control price spikes. Now merged with PM-AASHA administratively but still managed by the Department of Consumer Affairs.

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Price Deficiency Payment Scheme (PDPS)</mark>

No physical procurement occurs; instead, farmers receive the price difference between MSP and actual market price (capped at 15% of MSP value) directly in their bank accounts. Mainly used for oilseeds; reduces need for procurement infrastructure while retaining MSP protection.

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Market Intervention Scheme (MIS)</mark>

Targets perishable crops without an MSP, such as tomatoes, onions, and potatoes. It activates when market prices fall at least 10% below the previous normal season's rates, and operates via Centre-State cost-sharing through agencies like NAFED and NCCF—particularly useful during glut situations.

|Component|Mechanism|Crops Covered|Trigger Condition|
|---|---|---|---|
|PSS|Physical procurement at MSP|Pulses, oilseeds, copra|Market price below MSP|
|PSF|Buffer stock management|Pulses, onions, potatoes|Price volatility control|
|PDPS|Cash transfer of price difference|Mainly oilseeds|Market price below MSP (up to 15% gap)|
|MIS|Cost-shared procurement|Perishables (no MSP), e.g., tomato, onion, potato|Price fall ≥10% vs. previous season|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">MSP vs Cost of Production (2026–27)</mark>

Higher MSP over production cost strengthens income security and encourages sustained cultivation.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Supporting Infrastructure</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF)</mark>

Sanctioned loans worth ₹96,426 crore for 2,14,437 projects, mobilising investments exceeding ₹1,66,179 lakh crore.

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">e-NAM Platform</mark>

- Integrated 1,656 mandis across 23 States and 4 Union Territories
    
- Facilitated trade worth ₹4,94,847 crore
    
- Registered 4,776 FPOs; onboarded 7,334 FPOs onto ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce)
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Storage and Marketing Infrastructure</mark>

- 50,249 warehouses sanctioned with 992.6 lakh metric tonnes storage capacity
    
- 25,081 agricultural marketing infrastructure projects sanctioned
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recent Reforms</mark>

Biometric farmer authentication, direct procurement from pre-registered farmers, transportation support for TOP crops (Tomato, Onion, Potato), and price differential payments under MIS.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">State-Level Implementation Examples</mark>

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Bihar</mark>

- First-ever organised procurement of masoor (lentil) initiated through NCCF
    
- Implementation via 48 Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS) and FPOs
    
- As of 10 August 2026: NCCF procured 1,042.65 MT masoor (358 farmers registered, 285 benefited); NAFED procured 1,814.13 MT masoor (495 registered, 455 benefited)
    

## <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">Chhattisgarh</mark>

- Network of 200 operational PACS and 12 FPOs
    
- As of 10 August 2026: NCCF procured 18,392.228 MT chana, 22.231 MT masoor, and 1,035.0205 MT mustard (21,721 registered, 13,790 benefited)
    
- NAFED procured 17,020.65 MT chana and 355.05 MT masoor (46,146 registered, 13,673 benefited)
