## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background</mark>

Global trade is heavily dependent on containerised sea transport, with nearly 80% of world merchandise volume moving by sea and containers accounting for close to two-thirds of trade value. Rising geopolitical tensions and supply-chain disruptions have exposed vulnerabilities in global container availability and freight-rate stability. India imports nearly 2 million empty containers annually to meet domestic demand and repositioning needs, making it highly exposed to global market volatility.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What Are Containers?</mark>

Shipping containers are standardized steel freight boxes built to ISO specifications, enabling intermodal transport across ships, rail and road without unloading/repacking cargo. The most common sizes are 20-foot and 40-foot units, measured in TEUs (Twenty-foot Equivalent Units), the standard capacity metric.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview of CMAS</mark>

- Announced in: Union Budget 2026–27
    
- Objective: Build a globally competitive domestic container manufacturing ecosystem, reduce import dependence, strengthen supply-chain resilience
    
- Outlay: ₹10,000 crore over five years
    
- Target: Expand annual domestic manufacturing capacity to about 10 times current levels, up to 7.5 lakh TEUs
    
- Projected market opportunity: Nearly ₹80,000 lakh crore
    
- Alignment: Complements Make in India, Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047, and multimodal logistics development
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Components of Support Under CMAS</mark>

- Capital assistance for new Greenfield manufacturing facilities
    
- Support for expansion of existing Brownfield units
    
- Operational support to improve competitiveness of domestic manufacturers
    
- Support for testing infrastructure, skilling initiatives, and capacity building
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Integrated Maritime Ecosystem Initiatives</mark>

CMAS operates alongside the Bharat Container Shipping Line (BCSL), an MoU signed in February 2026 bringing together SCI, CONCOR, Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority, VOCPA, and Sagarmala Finance Corporation Limited. This envisages investments of around ₹99,149 crore for developing 51 container vessels and domestic container procurement. CMAS also complements PM Gati Shakti (connectivity between ports, rail, highways, industrial centres), the National Logistics Policy (operational efficiency), and the Sagarmala Programme (port-led development).

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Employment and Industrial Impact</mark>

- Direct jobs expected: Around 3,000
    
- Indirect jobs expected: More than 50,000
    
- Ancillary industries likely to benefit: Corner castings, wooden frames, Corten steel

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Broader Maritime Sector Reforms</mark>

- New legislation: Merchant Shipping Act 2025, Coastal Shipping Act 2025, Indian Ports Act 2025
    
- Digital initiatives: One Nation One Port Process (ONOP), Maritime Single Window, e-Samudra
    
- Financial support: ₹70,000 crore Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Package
    
- Major infrastructure projects: Vadhavan Port, International Container Transshipment Port at Galathea Bay, Tuna Tekra Container Terminal, Outer Harbour Container Terminal at V.O. Chidambaranar Port
    
- Performance indicator: Three Indian ports rank among the world's top 30 in the Container Port Performance Index (CPPI) 2025
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward</mark>

CMAS is envisioned as a cornerstone of India's maritime manufacturing strategy, aiming to cut import reliance, build supply-chain resilience, generate large-scale employment, and position India as a competitive global maritime manufacturing and logistics hub when combined with parallel shipbuilding, port, and logistics reforms.
