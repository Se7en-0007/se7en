## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Idea of The Editorial</mark>

The Indian economy is showing signs of slowing industrial growth combined with rising external vulnerabilities, pointing to a phase of "slack demand, higher costs, and moderating growth" ahead. The editorial uses the Index of Core Industries (ICI) and other leading indicators to build this picture.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Data Points: Index of Core Industries (ICI)</mark>

- ICI growth slowed to 5.4% in July 2026, down from 6% in the previous month (June 2026).
    
- This was the second-highest growth rate in the ICI over the preceding seven months, despite the slowdown.
    
- The Manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) eased in July to its lowest level since August 2021, reflecting weak domestic demand.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Sector-Wise Performance (Base Effect Concern)</mark>

A major concern flagged is that much of the "growth" is not organic but a statistical artefact of a low base from the previous year.

|Sector|July 2026 Growth|Context/Base Effect|
|---|---|---|
|Coal|7.6% (11-month high)|Based on a 12.3% contraction in July 2025|
|Refinery products|2.7% (ended 3-month contraction streak)|Based on a contraction in July 2025|
|Iron ore|29.5%|Slower than 44.5% in June; base was a 16.4% (June 2025) and 7.1% (July 2025) contraction|
|Steel|2.9% (sharp slowdown)|Down from 5.6% in June and 15.7% in July 2025|
|Electricity|9%|Slower than double-digit growth in May–June (heatwave-driven demand)|
|Cement|13.1%|Welcome acceleration; one of the few genuine bright spots|

Key takeaway: Coal, refinery products, and iron ore show strong headline numbers, but these are largely due to comparison against a weak (contracted) base in 2025, not real demand strength. Steel's slowdown is a genuine concern since it usually signals construction/manufacturing health.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Persistent Drags: Crude Oil and Natural Gas</mark>

- Domestic crude oil and natural gas production has contracted continuously for at least 14 months (the entire span for which the new ICI series has data).
    
- This reflects a structural, not cyclical, problem in domestic upstream energy output.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Rising External Dependence and Import Costs</mark>

- Crude oil imports rose 13.3% in volume terms in July 2026.
    
- LNG imports grew a more marginal 1.5%.
    
- The crude oil import bill jumped 41% in July, driven by high oil prices - showing that even without much extra volume, cost pressure has surged.
    
- The 20% ethanol-blending programme has not yet meaningfully reduced oil import dependence.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Geopolitical/Trade Risk Factor</mark>

- The US is preparing to levy 100% tariffs on countries (including India) that import Russian oil.
    
- This threatens to further burden Indian exporters, layering a trade-policy risk on top of existing energy-cost pressure.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Bright Spots vs Broader Trend</mark>

- Only two sectors - cement (13.1%) and electricity (9%) - stood out positively within the ICI.
    
- Electricity's strong growth is linked partly to heatwave-driven demand rather than durable industrial expansion.
    
- Overall, positive trends are described as "few and far between," reinforcing the editorial's cautious outlook.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Structured Summary for Revision</mark>

- Core Sector Growth: Slowing (6% → 5.4%), masked by low-base effects in coal, refinery, and iron ore.
    
- Structural Weakness: Continuous 14-month contraction in domestic crude oil and natural gas output.
    
- External Vulnerability: Rising import volumes and costs (41% jump in oil import bill) plus looming US tariff risk on Russian oil imports.
    
- Policy Gap: Ethanol blending (20%) has not materially reduced import dependence.
    
- Genuine Positives: Cement and electricity, though electricity's strength is partly weather-driven.
    
- Overall Outlook: Economy heading into a phase of weak demand, rising costs, and moderating growth.
