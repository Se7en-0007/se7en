- The **Green Climate Fund (GCF)** took **seven years** to approve Nepal’s **$49.9 million** project, **FP272**, aimed at reducing the risks and impacts of **Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs)**.
- The delay came under renewed scrutiny after the **August 26** disaster, when a cascading flood of **ice, rock, mud and debris** tore through the **Bhote Koshi** and **Trishuli** river basins, damaging **settlements, infrastructure and hydropower projects**.
- The project did **not specifically include Bhote Koshi and Trishuli river basins** as intervention sites, but had a broader national mandate to strengthen **GLOF monitoring**, **early-warning systems** and **multi-hazard risk assessments**.
- The **GCF** is the world’s largest dedicated climate fund and was established to support **low-emission, climate-resilient development in developing countries**.

## Nepal Proposal Warned Of Avalanche Risk

- **FP272** entered the GCF pipeline on **February 14, 2018**, was approved on **July 3, 2025**, began implementation on **March 12, 2026**, and is scheduled to run until **March 12, 2033**.
- Valued at **$49.9 million**, approximately **Rs 473.83 crore**, the project is intended to improve safety for about **2.4 million people**.
- Nepal’s proposal warned that under **Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5 and 8.5 scenarios**, temperatures and monsoon rainfall would continue to rise, increasing **lake water volumes** and the probability of **avalanches, rockfalls and dam-wall collapse** that can trigger GLOFs.
- Since the **1970s**, **26 GLOF events** have been recorded across **47 potentially dangerous glacial lakes**, causing catastrophic consequences.
- GLOFs can cause **severe flooding, landslides and mudflows**, damage **infrastructure and private assets**, and disrupt **transport networks, agricultural production, hydropower supplies and tourism**.
- GCF documents acknowledged that Nepal is undergoing **accelerated glacial retreat due to climate change**, intensifying GLOF risks to the **safety, livelihoods and critical infrastructure** of downstream communities.
- The documents also warned that GLOF damage is often **irreparable for decades**, imposing considerable economic costs, particularly on **downstream populations**.
- Glacier melting in Nepal has accelerated sharply due primarily to **fossil fuel-driven climate change**. Glaciers across the **Hindu Kush Himalaya** melted **65 per cent faster in the last decade** than in the preceding decade.

## Hydropower Risk

- Questions were raised about lenders financing hydropower projects in the affected region despite known climate and disaster risks, including the **Asian Development Bank (ADB)**, the **World Bank Group’s International Finance Corporation (IFC)** and **China’s Export-Import Bank**.
- The **South Asia Network on Dams, Rivers and People (SANDRP)** described the disaster as a possible **“watershed moment”** for South Asia’s internationally financed hydropower boom.
- Hundreds of hydropower labourers, engineers and local residents were trapped in tunnels at projects including **Upper Trishuli-1** and **Trishuli 3A**.
- The Nepal Army rescued **more than 350 people**, but many more, including **Indian nationals**, were still trapped inside tunnels.
- The **214 MW Upper Trishuli-1** project, one of Nepal’s largest hydropower projects, was near completion but was badly damaged by the floods.
- The wider **Trishuli basin** has more than **36 hydropower projects**.

## Upper Trishuli-1 Hydropower Project

- The **$647.4 million Upper Trishuli-1** project is among the largest foreign direct investments in Nepal’s history.
- On **November 1, 2019**, the IFC announced that a consortium had finalised a **$453 million debt-financing package** for its construction.
- The IFC had said the plant would increase Nepal’s electricity supply by **one-third** from existing levels.
- The **ADB** and the **Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank** provided combined loans of **$150 million**.
- The remaining debt financing came from institutions including the UK’s **CDC Group**, now **British International Investment**, South Korea’s **Korea Development Bank** and **Export-Import Bank of Korea**, the Netherlands’ **FMO**, and the **OPEC Fund**.
- An earlier climate-change risk assessment had concluded that climate risks to the project were **low**. However, the same report noted that extreme rainfall and a **2025 GLOF event** had damaged facilities under construction and significantly altered **Trishuli river morphology**.
- An **Independent Panel of Experts** report in **June 2025** recommended a **Climate Resilience Assessment** for the project, which campaigners said had not yet been undertaken.
- SANDRP alleged that the IFC and World Bank did not adequately examine **upstream risks** or assess **Tibetan impacts** in the **2020 cumulative impact assessment** of the Trishuli basin.

## Trishuli 3A Hydropower Project

- **China’s Exim Bank** provided a **$149 million concessional loan** for the **Trishuli 3A** hydropower project.
- SANDRP alleged that the bank did not ensure **independent, binding environmental safeguards** and relied on host-country rules or voluntary action.
- This was criticised because China and Nepal share some of the most **fragile and disaster-prone parts** of the transboundary Himalayan basin.

## Climate Finance Criticised

- Campaigners criticised the seven-year approval period as a failure of international climate finance to move quickly enough to protect communities, infrastructure and ecosystems from growing GLOF risks.
- The delay in glacier-adaptation funding, alongside large-scale hydropower financing, was described as **resilience being denied** to vulnerable communities facing **“imminent death”**.
- Campaigners alleged that the GCF’s delay reflected an international climate-finance system structured by industrialised rich countries to remain **inefficient and difficult to access**.
- Ecological economist **Nilanjan Ghosh** questioned the adequacy of the **Cumulative Impact Assessment**, arguing that an integrated cost-benefit analysis should include the probability of **seismic activity, GLOFs, cloudbursts and other extreme events**, along with impacts on **ecosystem services**.
- If these factors were valued and incorporated as costs into the benefit-cost matrix, such projects would be found **economically, socially and financially unviable**, according to Ghosh.
- The disaster raised serious concerns over whether climate-finance systems can provide protection to vulnerable communities **before disaster strikes**.
- A stronger system would need to **shorten the pipeline**, **simplify approvals** and track whether funds reach **high-risk mountain areas** fast enough to matter.
