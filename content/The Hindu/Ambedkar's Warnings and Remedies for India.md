Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, as the chief architect of the Indian Constitution, identified deep structural contradictions in Indian democracy and prescribed specific constitutional and social remedies to address them, many of which remain relevant to contemporary debates on delimitation, reservation, and federalism.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background</mark>

In November 1949, while presenting the Constitution, Ambedkar issued a caution about the gap between political equality and social-economic inequality in India. He warned that from 26 January 1950, India would enter a "life of contradictions"—achieving political equality (one man, one vote) while continuing to deny equality in social and economic life ("one man, one value"). He questioned how long this contradictory existence could be sustained and reminded citizens that independence brought great responsibilities, not just joy.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Contradiction: Political vs Social Democracy</mark>

**The central warning:**

- Political democracy grants formal equality: one man, one vote, one vote, one value.
    
- Social and economic structures continue to deny this equality in practice, creating an inherent contradiction in the new republic.
    
- Ambedkar asked how long India could sustain this dual existence of formal political equality alongside real-world social and economic inequality.
    

**Remedy proposed:**

- Political democracy must be grounded in social democracy.
    
- Social democracy is defined as "a way of life which recognises liberty, equality and fraternity as the principles of life"—not merely a form of government.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">On Society and Caste</mark>

Ambedkar viewed democracy fundamentally as a "mode of associated living," rooted in social relationships rather than parliamentary structures alone. In a 1956 Voice of America interview, he explained that genuine democracy requires community of purpose, mutual sympathy, and cooperative living among people.

**His critique of Indian society:**

- Indian society, he argued, is not a unified body of individuals but "an innumerable collection of castes" that are exclusive, share no common experience, and have no bond of sympathy.
    
- The caste system stands as "a standing denial" of democratic ideals like unity and cooperation.
    
- In his undelivered speech _Annihilation of Caste_, he stated bluntly that no nation or morality can be built on caste foundations—anything built on caste "will crack and will never be a whole."
    

**Remedy:** Annihilation of caste itself was necessary as a precondition for building genuine national unity and functioning democracy.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">On Federalism and Regional Balance</mark>

Ambedkar anticipated tensions between northern and southern states over parliamentary representation, a concern strikingly relevant to today's delimitation debate.

**Key concerns raised:**

- Under India's asymmetrical seat distribution in the Rajya Sabha (based on population rather than equal state representation), he feared northern states could combine to override southern interests.
    
- He contrasted this with the US Senate model, where every state gets equal representation regardless of population size.
    
- He warned that the Rajya Sabha in India had no real power over financial/money matters, unlike the equal-power model between US House and Senate.
    
- He was also concerned about consolidation of northern states versus fragmentation ("balkanisation") of southern states through unequal reorganisation.
    

**Remedies proposed:**

- Every state should have equal representation in the Rajya Sabha, mirroring the US Senate model.
    
- The Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha should have equal voting power specifically on Money Bills.
    
- Larger northern states like Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh should be divided into smaller units for balanced federal representation—he specifically proposed splitting UP into three states with capitals at Meerut, Kanpur, and Prayagraj.
    

**Historical follow-through:** Decades later, Bihar and Madhya Pradesh were indeed bifurcated. In 2011, then-UP CM Mayawati passed an Assembly resolution proposing UP's division into four states (Harit Pradesh, Awadh Pradesh, Bundelkhand, Purvanchal), though the Congress-led UPA government did not support this demand.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Comparative Table: Ambedkar's Federal Remedies</mark>

|Issue|Ambedkar's Concern|Proposed Remedy|
|---|---|---|
|Rajya Sabha representation|Population-based seats let north override south|Equal state representation, as in US Senate|
|Money Bills|Rajya Sabha has no financial power|Equal voting power for both Houses on Money Bills|
|Large northern states|Risk of northern bloc dominance|Split Bihar, MP, and UP into smaller states|
|Southern states|Risk of "balkanisation" amid northern consolidation|Balanced reorganisation to prevent regional imbalance|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">On Society Resisting Constitutional Safeguards</mark>

Ambedkar drew a sharp comparison between how ruling/governing classes in France and Japan responded to demands for equality versus India's governing class.

**Comparative behaviour of governing classes:**

- In France, the post-Revolution governing class voluntarily surrendered its privileges to merge into the national mass.
    
- In Japan, the Daimyo (governing class) similarly gave up privileges in the interest of national unity and nationalism.
    
- In India, by contrast, Ambedkar argued the governing class showed no similar willingness to sacrifice privilege—instead, it "used or misused the slogan of nationalism to maintain its privileges."
    

**Contemporary relevance:** This critique is echoed in modern debates such as resistance to UGC guidelines aimed at preventing caste-based discrimination in universities, where safeguards for reserved-category students faced pushback framed as concerns over "merit" or institutional autonomy—illustrating the ongoing tension between constitutional morality and public/social morality that Ambedkar had identified.
