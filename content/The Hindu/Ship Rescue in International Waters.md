## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context</mark>

- MV Ocean Winner: Panama-flagged bulk carrier, sank August 22, 2026, in Bay of Bengal, ~230 nautical miles off Odisha coast.
    
- Departed from Paradip port carrying 72,100 tonnes of iron ore.
    
- Distress alert issued Saturday morning, ~378 nautical miles east of Visakhapatnam.
    
- Crew composition: 24 total — 20 Chinese, 3 Myanmar, 1 Bangladesh; only 2 (Chinese nationals) rescued initially.
    
- Alert received by Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre (MRCC), Port Blair; search request also came from Victory Star Group Co Ltd (ship's associated company).
    
- Agencies involved: Indian Coast Guard, Indian Navy, China's MRCC.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Maritime Zones — Legal Basis</mark>

- Territorial waters: extend up to 12 nautical miles (~22 km) from coast/baseline; full sovereignty applies.
    
- Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ): up to 200 nautical miles from baseline; sovereignty NOT applicable, but resource/economic rights exist.
    
- International waters: beyond EEZ; no single state has sovereignty.
    
- Key point: Ocean Winner sank in international waters (230 nm from coast) but still fell within India's designated Search and Rescue Region — showing SAR regions are broader than sovereignty-based zones.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Distress Signal Mechanism</mark>

- Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS): communication network developed by the International Maritime Organisation (IMO); enables automatic emergency alerts to shore authorities/other ships.
    
- Digital Selective Calling (DSC): primary alert method within GMDSS; auto-transmits vessel's 9-digit identity number linked to ship's radio; must be crew-triggered.
    
- Emergency Position-Indicating Radio Beacon (EPIRB): floats free from sinking ship, activates automatically on contact with water (does not require crew action).
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Search and Rescue (SAR) Architecture</mark>

|Feature|Detail|
|---|---|
|Total SAR area|4.6 million sq km (Indian Ocean)|
|Formal name|Indian Search and Rescue Region|
|Comparison|Double the size of India's EEZ (~2.3 million sq km)|
|Apex authority|National Maritime Search and Rescue Coordinating Authority|
|Head|Director-General, Indian Coast Guard|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Three Regional MRCCs</mark>

- MRCC Mumbai: covers Western Seaboard and Arabian Sea.
    
- MRCC Chennai: covers Eastern Seaboard and Bay of Bengal.
    
- MRCC Port Blair (now MRCC Sri Vijaya Puram): covers Andaman & Nicobar region, ~9 lakh sq km; deployed Navy's P-8I aircraft (redeployed from routine surveillance sortie) for Ocean Winner search.
    
- Supporting layer: 36 Maritime Rescue Sub-Centres operating locally under the three main MRCCs.
    
- Role of Search-and-Rescue Mission Coordinator: qualified official who takes charge on receiving distress alert; decides on marshalling Coast Guard units, requesting Naval aerial assets, and directing nearby commercial vessels to divert and assist.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">International Legal Framework for Cross-Border Rescue</mark>

**SAR Convention (International Convention on Maritime Search and Rescue)**

- Adopted April 1979; entered into force June 1985.
    
- Purpose: standard operating procedure for SAR operations worldwide.
    
- Divided world's oceans into 13 search-and-rescue areas.
    
- Chapter 3: requires neighbouring states to pool resources and permit immediate entry of foreign rescue units into their territorial waters during a crisis — this is the legal basis allowing Chinese vessels to operate in India's SAR region.
    

**SOLAS (International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea)**

- Mandates that any ship's master must proceed at full speed toward a distress signal, regardless of the nationality/circumstances of the distressed crew.
    
- Relevant provision: Chapter V, Regulation 33 — "Distress Situations."
    

**UNCLOS (United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea)**

- Opened for signature 1982; entered into force 1994.
    
- Popularly called the "Constitution of the Oceans."
    
- Article 98: obligates every coastal State to maintain an operational SAR service; mandates ship captains assist anyone in danger at sea; mandates States cooperate with regional neighbours via mutual arrangements when required.
