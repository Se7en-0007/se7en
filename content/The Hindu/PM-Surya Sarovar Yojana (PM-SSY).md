## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context</mark>

- India's installed solar capacity grew from 3 GW in 2016 to over 160 GW in 2026, driven mainly by large-scale ground-mounted projects.
    
- Land availability for new solar projects is shrinking due to competition from urbanisation, agriculture, and ecological conservation priorities.
    
- This has pushed policymakers to look for land-neutral solar options, of which floating solar is a key alternative.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">PM-SSY: Key Features</mark>

- Full name: Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana; recently approved by the Government of India.
    
- First dedicated national scheme to scale up floating solar adoption in India.
    
- Target: 5,000 MW of floating solar capacity.
    
- Total financial outlay: Rs 5,070 crore.
    
- Includes co-located energy storage systems with minimum 2-hour storage duration, amounting to 10,000 MWh cumulative storage capacity to support grid stability.
    
- Implementation period: 5 years, with financial support available until 2032-33.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What Is Floating Solar?</mark>

- Solar modules mounted on floating platforms placed over water bodies such as reservoirs, lakes, and industrial ponds.
    
- Platforms are anchored to the reservoir bed or banks to maintain position.
    
- Inverters and other electrical equipment may be installed either on the floating platform itself or onshore, depending on system design.
    
- Electricity generated is transmitted to shore via electrical cables.
    
- Since no land is required for installation, floating solar suits industrial ponds, reservoirs, and inland water bodies.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Advantages of Floating Solar for India</mark>

- Land conservation: the biggest benefit, enabling more efficient land use and economic gains.
    
- Reduced water evaporation: areas of water bodies covered by floating panels show lower evaporation than uncovered areas (extent depends on coverage area, climate, and reservoir characteristics).
    
- Improved module efficiency: water beneath the panels keeps them cooler, boosting performance, especially in hot summer conditions.
    
- Complements ground-mounted and rooftop solar rather than replacing them, offering an additional pathway for renewable expansion.

|Project|Location|Capacity|Notes|
|---|---|---|---|
|Ramagundam|Telangana (NTPC)|100 MW|Commissioned 2022; among India's largest|

|Omkareshwar Floating Solar Park|Madhya Pradesh|600 MW planned (278 MW operational)|Expected to generate ~460 crore units of clean energy over 25-year lifetime|

- Current total installed floating solar capacity in India: only 700 MW.
    
- National Institute of Solar Energy assessment: India's floating solar potential is 102 GW, assuming only 20% usage of reservoir areas — indicating a large untapped opportunity.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Role of PM-SSY</mark>

- Until now, floating solar projects have largely been developed by state agencies and public sector undertakings, with limited traction beyond a few large projects.
    
- PM-SSY aims to mainstream floating solar within India's broader renewable energy landscape.
    
- Provides Central Financial Assistance (CFA) of Rs 1 crore per MW for each eligible project upon commissioning.
    
- Provides additional CFA of up to Rs 0.5 crore per project for feasibility studies and preparatory activities, including:
    
    - Hydrographic assessments (study of water body depth and bottom contours).
        
    - Environmental assessments (evaluating impacts on aquatic life and water quality).
        
- Aims to boost domestic manufacturing and generate employment across the floating solar value chain.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Factors Necessary for Success</mark>

- Effective implementation, project bankability, and institutional coordination are as important as financial support.
    
- Clear identification of suitable water bodies and comprehensive site-specific feasibility studies are required.
    
- Climate-resilient system design is essential since floating platforms face extreme winds, waves, and fluctuating water levels.
    
- Ecological caution needed: sensitive water bodies and areas important for biodiversity, bird habitats, and fisheries should be approved only after thorough environmental assessments.
    
- Cost factor: floating solar is generally costlier than ground-mounted solar due to floating platforms, anchoring systems, specialised electrical equipment, and installation costs; adding storage further raises capital costs.
    
- Developers will need affordable financing and long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) despite CFA support.
    
- Institutional coordination required among state agencies, municipal authorities, electricity utilities, water resources and irrigation departments, environmental authorities, and industries.
    
- Clear frameworks needed for allocating water bodies, defining institutional responsibilities, and streamlining approval processes.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward</mark>

- Floating solar offers a path to expand renewable capacity without extra pressure on scarce land resources, complementing ground-mounted and rooftop solar.
    
- PM-SSY provides the policy push to move floating solar from a handful of large installations to systematic, widespread deployment nationwide.
    
- Success will hinge on careful site selection, environmentally responsible and climate-resilient design, and effective coordination among institutions.
    
- India has already proven technical feasibility of floating solar at MW scale; PM-SSY is designed to scale this into a significant share of the renewable energy portfolio while easing land pressure and optimising water resource use.
