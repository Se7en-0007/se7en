## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Argument</mark>

- The India–U.K. relationship is fundamentally a "human relationship" driven by the movement of people, ideas, and ambition — not just diplomacy or trade.
- Students, entrepreneurs, researchers, professionals, and alumni form the "living bridge" between the two nations.
- Education, skills, innovation, and opportunity must move together to strengthen this bridge.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Education as an Economic Growth Engine</mark>

- India–U.K. Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) is a milestone for bilateral trade, but people/talent — not agreements alone — ultimately drive innovation and growth.
- In a knowledge-driven global economy, talent is among the world's most valuable resources for productivity and prosperity.
- Key requirements to build a future-ready talent pool:
    
    - University–business partnerships
    - Industry-informed curricula
    - Collaborative research
- Economic growth is strongest when education and enterprise move in step (author's direct assertion from cross-country industry/academia experience).

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">From Qualifications to Global Careers</mark>

- Success is no longer just about student mobility (numbers crossing borders) but about pathways from education to meaningful global careers.
- Mechanisms enabling exchange of people/ideas:
    
    - Mutual recognition of qualifications
    - Joint and dual degrees
    - Transnational education (TNE) campuses
    - Faculty exchanges
    - Collaborative research programmes
- Value of international students: enrich campuses, bring new perspectives, strengthen research collaboration, unlock business/investment opportunities.
- Real measure of success: whether a student from Chennai or Coventry becomes equipped to work across both economies, build businesses, lead research, and innovate.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Preparing Talent for the Economy of 2035</mark>

- U.K.–India Vision 2035 is described as a blueprint for a future-ready economic relationship, with education central to it.
- Future success will depend on entrepreneurship, creativity, and cross-border/cross-disciplinary problem-solving — not just technical expertise.
- Curricula need to integrate management capability with emerging fields: AI, data analytics, sustainability.
- Practical workplace-readiness tools emphasized: live projects, internships, industry-led research, real-world case studies.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Institutional Infrastructure for Collaboration</mark>

- Strong policy alone is insufficient; institutions must convert ambition into sustained action.
- Key organizations/mechanisms:

|Organization|Role|
|---|---|
|Chartered Association of Business Schools (Chartered ABS)|Leads India–U.K. Business School Dialogue; builds frameworks for sustained collaboration|
|British Council|Co-convenes the Business School Dialogue|
|All India Management Association (AIMA)|Dialogue partner|
|Association of Management Development Institutions in South Asia (AMDISA)|Dialogue partner|
|India Business Group (IBG)|Dialogue partner|
|Indian Academy of Management (INDAM)|Dialogue partner|

- India–U.K. Business School Dialogue outcomes: deeper research collaboration, faculty exchanges, joint programme development, focus on globally relevant careers.
- The Dialogue is grounded in reciprocal partnerships and long-term commitment, not transactional exchange — reflecting evolving nature of TNE (equitable value-generation for institutions, students, societies in both countries).
- The 2026 Dialogue is positioned as advancing both India's National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 and the India–U.K. Vision 2035.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Concluding Vision</mark>

- Success of Vision 2035 should be measured not by trade volumes but by professionals developed, businesses built, research published, and partnerships forged.
- Aspiration: a generation of globally connected entrepreneurs, researchers, innovators, and leaders who embody (not just participate in) the bilateral relationship.
- Call to action: Governments, universities, and businesses must act now, not wait until 2035, to build these partnerships.
