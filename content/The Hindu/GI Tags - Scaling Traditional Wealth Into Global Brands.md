Geographical Indication (GI) tags protect India's cultural heritage while creating economic opportunities for artisans, weavers, and farmers by linking products to their place of origin.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">What is a GI Tag</mark>

- Certifies a product is genuinely made in a specific region using traditional methods, assuring authenticity, quality, and cultural heritage.
    
- Serves as a seal of authenticity, safeguarding artisanal crafts against imitation, misuse, and unauthorized commercialization.
    
- Can raise rural artisans' incomes by 20-30% (per Ministry of Textiles).
    
- Enhances product market appeal, boosts buyer confidence, and helps secure premium market access.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Illustrative Examples</mark>

- Banarasi Saree (Uttar Pradesh) — handwoven with zari work; GI tag prevents undervaluation and imitation.
    
- Channapatna Toys (Karnataka) — received GI recognition in 2006 for wooden lacquerware toys made using traditional techniques.
    
- Muzaffarnagar Jaggery — GI tag helped export 30 metric tons to Bangladesh in 2025 via Brijnandan Agro Farmer Producer Company, benefiting 545 members.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Statistics</mark>

- India has over 800 registered GI products; 607 GIs granted since 2014.
    
- Darjeeling Tea (West Bengal) was India's first GI-tagged product (protection extends to product and logo).
    
- Other early registrations: Aranmula Kannadi (Kerala handicraft), Pochampalli Ikat (Telangana textile).
    
- Authorized users for GI tags rose from 365 to 29,000 between the last decade and January 2025.
    
- Target: 10,000 GI registrations by 2030.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Legal and Regulatory Framework</mark>

- GIs recognised internationally under the Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property and the WTO's TRIPS Agreement.
    
- India's legal framework: Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999, with accompanying Rules notified in 2002; framework operational from 2003, registrations began 2004-05.
    
- Registry: Geographical Indications Registry, Chennai, with all-India jurisdiction.
    
- 2025 amendment reduced GI application filing fees by 80%; renewal fee cut from ₹3,000 to ₹500.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Who Can Apply</mark>

- Any association of producers, organisation, or authority established under law can apply.
    
- Applicant must be a legal entity representing producers' interests; if not a producers' association, must prove representation of producer interests.
    
- Applications may be filed directly, by post, registered post, speed post, or courier.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Exclusions from Registration</mark>

- Generic product names no longer protected in country of origin.
    
- Indications misleading consumers or falsely suggesting different geographical origin.
    
- Indications offending religious sentiments or containing scandalous/obscene matter.
    
- Indications ineligible for legal protection or violating existing law.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Validity, Renewal, and Revocation</mark>

- Registration valid for ten years, renewable periodically.
    
- Revocation governed by Section 27 of the Act; aggrieved persons may apply to Registrar or Appellate Board.
    
- Grounds for revocation: obtained through fraud/misrepresentation, no longer protected in country of origin or fallen into disuse, likely to deceive, violates law, or contains obscene matter.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government Schemes Supporting GI</mark>

- PM Ekta Mall (Unity Mall) — promotes ODOP and GI-tagged products; ₹5,000 crore allocated across states (FY 2023-24).
    
- One Station One Product (OSOP) — railway GI booths giving artisans direct market access.
    
- GI Trails — experiential tourism blending GI clusters with retail, e.g., Muga Silk Trail (Assam, June 2026) with Silk Tourism Park and Muga Utsav festivals.
    
- MSME Innovative Scheme — reimburses up to 100% of GI registration cost, capped at ₹2 lakh per GI.
    
- Office of Development Commissioner (Handicrafts), Ministry of Textiles — up to ₹1.5 lakh for enforcing GI rights and legal expenses.
    
- National Handloom/Handicrafts Development Programme (NHDP) — funds registration, training, and enforcement; 104 GI-registered handloom products and 53 registrations supported under Handloom Marketing Assistance (June 2026).

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">GI and Trade Agreements (FTAs)</mark>

- India-Oman CEPA enabled export of 3 metric tonnes of Indi Lime (Karnataka) to Oman in December 2025.
    
- New Zealand committed to enabling registration of Indian GI products (previously exclusive to EU) — will protect Darjeeling Tea, Basmati Rice, and regional handicrafts.
    
- India-EU FTA talks continue on a dedicated GI agreement.
    
- Leading export destinations for Indian GI products: UK, Italy, UAE, Bahrain, Qatar, USA, South Korea.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward</mark>

India's GI ecosystem is positioned as a bridge between tradition and economic opportunity, aiming for 10,000 GI registrations by 2030, strengthening the heritage economy through export growth, tourism integration, and rural development.
