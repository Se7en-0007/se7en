The National Animal Disease Control Programme (NADCP), launched in September 2019, is a flagship livestock health initiative targeting the eradication of Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) and Brucellosis through nationwide vaccination, surveillance, and digital monitoring.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background</mark>

- As per the 20th Livestock Census 2019, India's total livestock population stands at 535.78 million, including 302.79 million bovines.
    
- Diseases like FMD and Brucellosis historically caused heavy economic losses through reduced milk yield, reproductive failure, and trade restrictions.
    
- FMD is a highly contagious viral disease affecting cloven-hoofed animals (cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats, pigs), reducing milk production, growth, and reproductive/working capacity, and limiting export access.
    
- Brucellosis is a bacterial (Brucella abortus) reproductive disease of cattle/buffaloes with no curative treatment, making vaccination the only sustainable control measure.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Programme Objectives</mark>

- 100% biannual (twice-yearly) FMD vaccination coverage for all susceptible livestock.
    
- One-time Brucellosis vaccination for female bovine calves aged 4–8 months, administered once in their lifetime.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Components/Activities</mark>

- Mass FMD vaccination of bovines and small ruminants every six months.
    
- Public awareness campaigns and official orientation for implementation.
    
- Ear-tagging, animal registration, and data uploads on Bharat Pashudhan Portal (NDLM).
    
- Sero-surveillance and sero-monitoring via pre- and post-vaccination sample testing.
    
- Procurement of vaccines and cold-chain equipment.
    
- Outbreak investigation, virus isolation/typing, and quarantine/check-post regulation of animal movement.
    
- Continuous data generation, monitoring, and impact evaluation.
    
- Vaccinator remuneration (minimum ₹3 per dose).
    

Sero-surveillance assesses population-level antibody prevalence (disease exposure/immunity), while sero-monitoring tracks antibody levels longitudinally to gauge immunity trends.

|Farmers benefited (FMD)|-|8.04 crore|

Additional outcomes:

- No FMD outbreak caused by Serotype Asia-1 in the last three years.
    
- Sero-monitoring shows average protective immunity of 78.1% (Type O), 71.7% (Type A), and 77.6% (Type Asia1) - indicating strong herd immunity.
    
- Brucellosis seroconversion rates (2025): 75.63% in cattle calves, 67.17% in buffalo calves.
    
- Since 2024, FMD vaccination extended to pastoral/migratory sheep and goats in Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, J&K, and Ladakh.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">FMD Serotypes</mark>

- Type O: Most common/widespread globally and in India; causes frequent outbreaks.
    
- Type A: Highly variable/mutates easily, requiring well-matched vaccines.
    
- Type Asia1: Found mainly in South Asia including India, less widespread than Type O.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Institutional and Financial Framework</mark>

- LHDCP (Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme): NADCP, the LH&DC component, and Pashu Aushadhi initiative have been subsumed under this single umbrella scheme for integrated implementation.
    
- Budget outlay for LHDCP (FY 2026-27): ₹2,010 crore.
    
- ICAR institutions receive financial support for vaccine research, quality testing, surveillance, and disease modelling.
    
- Animal Husbandry Infrastructure Development Fund supports private/entrepreneurial vaccine and drug manufacturing facilities.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Digital and Service Delivery Infrastructure</mark>

- Bharat Pashudhan Portal: Integrates livestock registration and vaccination data; each animal gets a 12-digit unique Tag ID; over 39.00 crore livestock registered (as of July 2026).
    
- Mobile Veterinary Units (MVUs): 4,019 units across 29 States/UTs; doorstep services via toll-free number 1962; benefited 136 lakh farmers, treated 276 lakh animals.
    
- MAITRIs (Multi-Purpose Artificial Insemination Technicians in Rural India): 39,810 technicians trained for farmer outreach and doorstep veterinary services.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">One Health and Pandemic Preparedness Linkages</mark>

- One Health approach: Recognizes interdependence of human, animal, and environmental health; NADCP contributes by reducing zoonotic spillover risk and promoting prudent antimicrobial use.
    
- National One Health Mission: Approved by PM-STIAC (21st meeting, 7th July 2022) for cross-ministerial coordination of One Health activities.
    
- Pandemic Fund Project (2024): "Animal Health Security Strengthening in India for Pandemic Preparedness and Response" - US$25 million initiative under the G20 Pandemic Fund, implemented with ADB, FAO, and World Bank; focuses on genomic/environmental surveillance and lab infrastructure upgrades.
    
- Two key documents released under this initiative:
    
    - Standard Veterinary Treatment Guidelines (SVTG) - promotes antimicrobial stewardship, supports National Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance.
        
    - Crisis Management Plan (CMP) for Animal Diseases - national framework for outbreak preparedness and rapid response.
        

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Way Forward</mark>

- Progressive alignment with international standards for FMD-freedom status.
    
- Expansion of laboratory accreditation and genomic surveillance.
    
- AI-enabled disease intelligence and climate-resilient surveillance systems.
    
- Strengthened One Health coordination linked to Viksit Bharat 2047 goals - ensuring food security, farmer prosperity, and pandemic resilience.
