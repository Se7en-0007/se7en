- **The Indian Navy commissioned INS Nipun on August 31 at the Naval Dockyard in Mumbai.**
- It is the **second Nistar-class Diving Support Vessel (DSV)** and will significantly strengthen the **Western Naval Command’s deep-sea diving, underwater intervention, and submarine rescue capabilities**.
- Its crest features a **mighty whale**, symbolising **sustained deep-sea endurance**, and a **modern diving helmet**, representing the diver at the core of the mission.
- **Nipun**, derived from Sanskrit, means **one who is adept, capable, and well versed in a particular discipline**.
- The **9,000-tonne vessel** combines capabilities possessed by only **a handful of navies**.

## Why DSVs Are Needed

- A **DSV** is a specialised platform that enables a navy to sustain **complex underwater operations for extended periods**, particularly in **deep and challenging waters**.
- Naval teams may need to **inspect, repair, recover, or intervene** around **ships, submarines, and other underwater assets**.
- A DSV provides:
    
    - **Diving systems and equipment**
    - **Medical facilities**
    - **Operational support**
    - A platform for the **launch and recovery of divers**
    - Carriage of **related tools and equipment**
- It reduces dependence on **shore-based facilities** and enables a quicker response to **underwater contingencies**.
- The Indian Navy undertakes diving operations in the **Indian Ocean Region** for:
    
    - **Submarine rescue**
    - **Underwater inspection**
    - **Testing or salvage**
    - **Recovery of objects, ships, or aircraft lost at sea**
- These missions often involve divers remaining underwater for **prolonged durations**, creating the need for a suitable support platform.

## Earlier Capability And Construction

- Earlier, the Indian Navy operated **Russian-origin submarine rescue vessels** and relied on a combination of **shore facilities, specialised diving units, and suitably equipped naval vessels**.
- These arrangements lacked **multidomain operational capabilities** and the ability to undertake **long deployments at sea**.
- In **September 2018**, the Indian Navy signed a contract with **Hindustan Shipyard Limited, Vishakhapatnam**, to construct **two DSVs**.
- The first vessel, **INS Nistar**, was commissioned in **July last year**.

## Deep Sea Saturation Diving

- INS Nipun is fitted with diving equipment capable of undertaking **deep sea saturation diving**.
- In saturation diving, **divers live under pressure for long periods** to work deep underwater.
- The ship has a **side diving stage** for diving operations.
- Its **Remotely Operated Vehicles** facilitate:
    
    - **Diver monitoring**
    - **Salvage operations**
- INS Nipun also has a **self-propelled hyperbaric lifeboat**, a pressurised lifeboat capable of independently evacuating:
    
    - **Divers**
    - **Diving compression chambers**
- The ship can undertake **diving and salvage operations**, including recovery of **damaged, sunken, or stranded ships and equipment**.
- It incorporates modern systems for:
    
    - **Navigation**
    - **Platform management**
    - **Precise station keeping**
    - **Diving**
    - **Underwater intervention**
    - **Medical support**
- These systems enable it to function as an **integrated platform for complex operations beneath the surface**.

## Mother Ship For The DSRV

- INS Nipun serves as the **Mother Ship** for the **Deep Submergence Rescue Vessel (DSRV)**.
- A DSRV can be deployed to reach a **submarine in distress** and **evacuate its crew** during an emergency.
- India concluded a contract in **March 2016** with **M/s James Fisher Defence, UK**, for two sets of **non-tethered DSRVs**.
- The DSRVs are capable of effecting **submarine rescue up to depths of 650 metres**.
- The first DSRV was delivered to **Mumbai in April 2018** and the second to **Visakhapatnam in 2019**.
- India has **two DSRVs**, with **one for each seaboard**, both capable of rescue operations to **650 m**.

## Underwater Domain

- The value of DSVs extends beyond individual diving missions because they allow a navy operating across the vast **Indian Ocean Region** to sustain specialised underwater operations at sea.
- In submarine operations, an **accident or technical emergency** can rapidly become a **race against time**.
- DSVs enable the Navy to:
    
    - Maintain an **underwater presence for extended periods**
    - Conduct **interventions** and recover objects in difficult conditions
    - Support **search-and-rescue missions** without being tied to shore facilities
- The induction of **indigenous DSVs** helps the Navy build and sustain specialised expertise and equipment **domestically**.
- It also strengthens India’s capacity to provide **submarine rescue assistance to regional partners**.
- **INS Nistar’s participation in Exercise Pacific Reach 2025** demonstrated this wider role, including **international submarine-rescue operations** and **interoperability with other navies**.
