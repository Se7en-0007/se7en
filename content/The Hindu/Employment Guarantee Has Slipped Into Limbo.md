## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Argument</mark>

The transition from MGNREGA to VB-G RAM G, marketed as an expansion of the employment guarantee scheme, has instead caused an unprecedented crash in rural employment generation, with the poorest states worst affected.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Employment Crash: Key Data</mark>

- July 2026 employment generation under VB-G RAM G fell versus MGNREGA in July 2025
    
- Widely cited decline figure: 50% (based on a premature/provisional figure of 7.7 crore person-days)
    
- Updated figure: 8.3 crore person-days, with a final estimate around 9 crore per the Ministry of Rural Development
    
- Even using the anticipated final figure, the year-on-year decline exceeds 40%
    
- April-July 2026 (first 4 months of FY): only 70 crore person-days generated
    
- Same period in 2024-25: 128 crore person-days; in 2025-26: 119 crore person-days
    
- This represents a 43% decline from the average of the preceding two years
    
- April-July normally accounts for nearly 50% of annual MGNREGA employment (peak summer/agricultural slack season)
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Government's Explanation and Why It Fails</mark>

- Ministry attributed the July 2026 dip to a few states suspending VB-G RAM G under Section 6 of the Act
    
- Authors call this "the mother of all red herrings" for two reasons: those states account for only a small share of total MGNREGA employment, and the decline persists proportionately even excluding them
    
- The July shortfall is simply a continuation of a crisis that began months earlier, not a new or isolated event
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Timeline of the Transition</mark>

- December 2025: Bill replacing MGNREGA with VB-G RAM G rushed through Parliament
    
- Rural Development Minister announced implementation date of April 1, 2026
    
- April 1, 2026: Transition did not happen as planned; Rules had not been framed; MGNREGA continued by default
    
- May 22, 2026: Draft VB-G RAM G Rules released for public consultation (largely a rehash of existing MGNREGA rules/orders)
    
- End of June 2026: Final Rules released
    
- June 30, 2026: VB-G RAM G wage rates notified - minimum norm of ₹300/day (roughly equivalent to the old MGNREGA norm of ₹100/day at 2009-10 prices)
    
- July 1, 2026: Official replacement of MGNREGA by VB-G RAM G
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Ground Reality During Transition</mark>

- April-June 2026 (transition period): widespread confusion; described using a Gramsci-style framing - "the old world of MGNREGA was dying, and the new world of VB-G RAM G was struggling to be born"
    
- In several districts, functionaries refused to open new works
    
- Many areas had no MGNREGA work at all during peak summer season, a serious lapse given normally high demand
    
- Limbo continued into July 2026 as VB-G RAM G made a faltering start
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">State-Wise Variation</mark>

Employment declined across all major states but unevenly.

|Category|States/Details|
|---|---|
|Relatively small decline|Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Telangana|
|Larger than 40% decline|Most major states|
|Severe decline (60-85%)|10 out of 19 major states|
|Virtual standstill|Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand (among India's largest and poorest states)|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Budget Paradox</mark>

- 2026-27 Union Budget allocation for VB-G RAM G: ₹95,692 crore (slightly more than actual MGNREGA expenditure in 2025-26)
    
- Total VB-G RAM G budget including state contributions (40% share for most states): projected to reach ₹1.5 lakh crore
    
- This represented an expected ~70% increase over 2025-26 MGNREGA expenditure
    
- Since wages remained roughly unchanged in real terms, a large increase in employment generation was expected
    
- Instead, the opposite occurred - a historic crash in the first four months of FY 2026-27
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Outlook and Concerns</mark>

- Authors state it is "too early to pass judgement" on VB-G RAM G, but call the launch "a damp squib"
    
- Near-total absence of employment for months in large, poor states is flagged as especially alarming
    
- Doubtful that the projected ₹1.5 lakh crore expenditure target for 2026-27 will be met
    
- New concerns flagged for wage payment reliability: imposition of facial recognition at worksites and Centre-State cost-sharing arrangements
    
- Overall outlook described by the authors as "bleak"
