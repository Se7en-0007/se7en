## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Thesis</mark>

India built three DPI (Digital Public Infrastructure) layers — Identity (Aadhaar), Payments (UPI), and Data (Account Aggregator/DEPA) — as free, interoperable public rails. The article argues intelligence (AI) should become the fourth layer, made cheap/free through deliberate state action, not market charity.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">India's Existing DPI Stack (Unique Global Position)</mark>

- No other country has built all three components at India's scale.
    
- Estonia: strong digital identity, but no UPI-equivalent.
    
- Brazil: Pix (free payments rail) exists alone, without identity/data layers.
    
- Singapore: Singpass and SGFinDex, but not integrated at India's scale.
    
- Europe: open banking and data portability frameworks, but fragmented.
    
- India's edge lies in the integration of Aadhaar + UPI + DEPA/Account Aggregator as interoperable rails.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Precedent: How India Made Data Cheap</mark>

- Between September 2016 and 2019, cost per GB fell from about $4 to under 30 cents.
    
- Outcome: 500 million people came online in five years.
    
- Mechanism: one player absorbed fixed costs of a nationwide 4G network, priced at marginal cost, forcing competitors to match or exit.
    
- Aadhaar: enrolled 1.4 billion people; turned identity verification into a low-cost API call.
    
- UPI: processes around 20 billion transactions/month at near-zero cost.
    
- Key insight: the state built infrastructure and set rules; market competition crashed the price — not subsidy alone.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">The Problem: India as "Digital Quarry"</mark>

- Global AI race runs on an extractive model; India supplies raw inputs but imports finished intelligence at a premium.
    
- India's contribution: engineers build/fine-tune Silicon Valley's frontier models; digitised public life (languages, documents, transactions) becomes training data; universities/diaspora supply research talent.
    
- Indian startups must rent back intelligence as dollar-priced API tokens, subject to export controls, hosted on foreign servers.
    
- Historical parallel: "Ship out the cotton, buy back the cloth" — same colonial-era extractive pattern India recognized in textiles, now recurring in AI.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Proposed Solution: India's AI Token Economy — Three Pillars</mark>

|Pillar|Focus|Key Details|
|---|---|---|
|1. Compute|Making raw compute cheap|IndiaAI Mission, outlay ~₹10,372 crore; PPP model (not state monopoly); 38,000+ GPUs onboarded, target 1,00,000; startups access GPU compute at ~₹65/hour|
|2. Open Models|Preventing dependence on closed foreign models|Government must mandate open-weights licensing for any AI model built using subsidised compute/public data; aggregate anonymised public datasets (legal rulings, agri data, curricula in 22 languages) exclusively for open-source model builders|
|3. Distribution (UII)|Universal access|"Unified Intelligence Interface" — a UPI-like API gateway for AI; common protocol so any app can call any model with shared standards for identity, consent, billing, safety|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Supporting Policy Ideas</mark>

- Fold AI compute demand into the National Electricity Plan; fast-track transmission to data-centre clusters; dedicate renewables (including nuclear) to inference — "cheap electrons are the new cheap spectrum."
    
- National "freemium" model: Aadhaar-verified startups/students get free monthly API tokens; profitable firms move to a paid tier that cross-subsidises the free tier.
    
- Suggestion to divert a tenth of the fertilizer subsidy toward AI tokens for R&D institutions and schools.
    
- Philosophy mirrors UPI: government builds rails and protocol for free; private players (e.g., Google Pay-style firms) compete on user experience layered atop it.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Potential Impact Cited</mark>

- Rural doctors using AI trained on Indian data could treat more patients with fewer errors.
    
- 300 million students could access 24x7 personalised AI tutors in local dialects.
    
- A Karnataka farmer could settle crop-insurance claims by voice in Kannada using AI linked to land records.
    
- 60 million small businesses could gain access to affordable AI tools.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Conceptual Takeaway</mark>

- Free identity and cheap data didn't directly enrich Indians — they enabled frictionless mass participation (UPI scale, short-video platforms, digital vendors).
    
- The author's core argument: the goal of cheap AI is not "better chatbots" but ending cognition as a "class privilege" — democratizing access to intelligence as infrastructure.
