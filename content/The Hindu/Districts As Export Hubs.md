- **Districts as Export Hubs (DEH) Initiative** is a framework for **inclusive export growth** that transforms every district into a **centre of export planning** by identifying products and services with global potential and connecting local producers to international markets.
- Building on the **One District One Product (ODOP)** concept, DEH uses a **convergence-based approach** involving **Central and State schemes, institutions and industry**.
- DEH aims to unlock district-level strengths, drive **employment generation**, strengthen **MSMEs**, and make India’s export growth more **inclusive** and **globally competitive**.

## Why India Needed A District-Led Export Model

- India’s total exports of goods and services reached an all-time high of **US$ 825.25 billion in 2024-25** and are estimated at **US$ 863.11 billion in 2025-26**.
- Export activity had long remained concentrated in a few established industrial clusters and port cities, while small towns, rural districts and remote regions often remained outside shipment networks despite possessing unique, high-quality products.
- Examples of such untapped strength include **Bastar’s iron craft**, **Jalgaon’s bananas** and **Meghalaya’s Mandarin Oranges**.
- The Government introduced the **district-led export strategy** through the **Districts as Export Hubs Initiative**, enabling every district to contribute to export growth by leveraging its unique strengths.

## From ODOP To DEH: Evolution Of The Framework

- DEH seeks to harness the strengths and resources of every district and unlock its export potential.
- Each district is treated as a **unit of export planning, not merely a unit of production**.
- The initiative helps local enterprises access global markets, improve competitiveness and drive district-led economic growth.
- It aligns with the broader vision of **Aatmanirbhar Bharat** and **Vocal for Local**.
- **ODOP** began as a **Uttar Pradesh state initiative in 2018**, built around **Moradabad’s brass industry**, before being scaled nationally.
- ODOP selected, branded and promoted **one distinctive product from each district**, converting local identity into economic opportunity.
- Through DEH under the **Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT)**, the Department of Commerce expanded the focus from **product promotion** to **comprehensive export promotion and ecosystem strengthening**.
- As of **January 2026**, DEH covered **more than 770 districts** across the country.
- DEH is a **convergence framework, not a funding scheme**. It leverages funding available under ongoing Central and State schemes to promote exports, manufacturing and employment generation at the grassroots level.
- It addresses district-specific export challenges and enhances district export competitiveness.

## The Institutional Architecture

- DEH operates through coordination among the **Centre, States and districts**.
- The **Department of Commerce** provides overall policy direction.
- The **DGFT** anchors implementation, facilitates stakeholder coordination and monitors progress.
- **State Export Promotion Committees (SEPCs)** guide implementation at the State level and ensure convergence across departments.
- **District Export Promotion Committees (DEPCs)** identify export opportunities, resolve local challenges and coordinate with industry, exporters and other stakeholders.
- DEPCs prepare **District Export Action Plans (DEAPs)**.
- DEAPs map products and services with export potential, assess infrastructure and logistics, identify export bottlenecks, and recommend interventions to improve competitiveness and market access.
- As of **March 2026**, draft DEAPs had been prepared for **590 districts**, of which **249** had been formally adopted by their respective DEPCs.

- DEH builds exporter capacity and addresses awareness gaps relating to **export-related logistics, quality standards and market access**.

### Meeting Global Standards

- Identified products must meet international quality benchmarks.
- DEH supports awareness generation and capacity building on **quality standards**, testing facilities, packaging, branding and certification.
- This helps local goods meet global compliance requirements.

### Enabling Digital Export

- Digital access has enabled hyper-local reach and expanded market access for businesses, including those in remote regions.
- Partnerships with **Amazon, Shiprocket and DHL** provide MSMEs with cost-effective courier shipping alternatives to buffer against freight-rate volatility.
- **Dak Ghar Niryat Kendras** provide low-cost postal export facilities for documentation, packaging and small consignments.

### Building Capacity & Outreach

- DGFT Regional Authorities and district administrations conduct capacity-building and awareness programmes on **logistics planning, export procedures, packaging norms and regulatory compliance**.
- Ecosystem partners include the **Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India (ECGC)**, **Export Promotion Council for Handicrafts (EPCH)**, **Federation of Indian Export Organisations (FIEO)**, **Ministry of MSME**, **India Post** and **Exim Bank**.
- These programmes provide skills to exporters to address identified concerns.
- They also raise awareness about challenges in e-commerce adoption, including **payment-related issues, trust and buyer verification**.

## Fostering Grassroot Initiatives For Development

- DGFT has partnered with **EXIM Bank** to strengthen export ecosystems in selected districts.
- Six districts selected under EXIM Bank’s **Grassroots Initiatives for Development (GRID)** programme are **Anantapur, Raipur, Solan, Tiruppur, Kanpur and Kolhapur**.
- GRID addresses sector-specific constraints, identifies potential beneficiaries and develops district-specific interventions to improve export competitiveness.
- **Tiruppur**, India’s knitwear capital, achieved **record exports of ₹46,000 crore in FY 2025-26**.
- The **Tiruppur Exporters Association** has set a target of **₹1 trillion in exports by 2030**.
- This aligns with India’s objective to increase textile exports from **US$ 38 billion to US$ 100 billion by 2030**.

## Focused Approach For DEH Implementation

- From **1 June 2026**, the Government adopted an **outcome-oriented, focused and phased approach** for DEH implementation.
- In the first phase, districts across **27 States and Union Territories** were covered with support from **24 DGFT Regional Authorities** and **11 partner agencies**.
- The focus is on measurable outcomes, including **new exporter registrations** and increased export value.
- The approach relies on convergence of ongoing Central and State schemes and leverages **GI products** and **MSME clusters**.
- DEH helps districts overcome long-standing structural constraints and convert local production strengths into export opportunities.

## The Road Ahead

- DEH shifts India’s export strategy from concentrating growth in a few industrial centres to unlocking the potential of every district.
- It combines local strengths with better export capacity, market access and institutional support to create a more **inclusive** and **resilient export ecosystem**.
- As India moves towards its **US$ 1 trillion export ambition**, stronger coordination among the Centre, States and districts is fundamental.
- The initiative seeks to transform **local economies into engines of trade, investment and employment**, expanding export-led growth across the country.
