India's defence sector is expanding, but a combat-proven indigenous tank remains delayed due to institutional shortcomings rather than capability gaps.

## Growth of India's Defence Sector

- India has produced **₹1.54 trillion worth of defence equipment**.
- The sector has seen a **25-fold increase in exports**.
- India has won **92% of domestic defence contracts**.
- These figures are reported in the **Rubix Industry Insights: Defence, May 2026 edition**.
- Despite this growth, a **combat-proven indigenous tank has been delayed for five years**, not due to lack of capability but because of **institutional shortcomings** that make government reforms difficult to implement.

## The Arjun Mk 1A Procurement

- The **Arjun Mk 1A is not an ordinary procurement**.
- Since **2004**, the **Indian Army has deployed 124 Arjun Mk 1 tanks**, which have **consistently outperformed imported options**, according to media reports.
- A **2014 Comptroller and Auditor-General (CAG) report** found an unsettling result: though the **Arjun's benchmarks were deliberately set higher than those of the Russian T-90**, the domestic tank **performed better on crucial factors such as firepower, mobility, and survivability**.
- According to the **CAG Report, 2014**, the **Arjun won despite this disadvantage**.
- **Ravi Kumar Gupta**, former **Director of Public Interface at the DRDO**, stated in an interview that "**DRDO delivered the upgraded tanks 21 days ahead of schedule**".
- After the **Defence Acquisition Council approved two regiments of the Mk 1A in 2010**, the **DRDO was given two years to develop 15 significant upgrades and 72 modifications**.
- Gupta noted: "**The Army trials took three years, more than the whole development cycle of DRDO**".
- The delays "**show a system designed to obstruct, not to help**".

## Operational Relevance

- This matters operationally because **Pakistan displayed aggressive military posturing along the border during Operation Sindoor**.
- **Pakistan has strengthened bases less than five kilometres from Indian border towns in Rajasthan**.
- The **SAMHO missile**, intended for the **Arjun Mk 1A**, has a **maximum range of 5 km**, which is **ideal for this threat geography**.
- The article notes: "**The capability is there. There is a need. What's lacking is execution**".

## Bureaucratic Delays in Contracting

- According to media reports, the **Defence Ministry contracted the Heavy Vehicles Factory in September 2021** for **118 Arjun Mk 1A tanks**, with **delivery anticipated to start in 2024**.
- **By July 2026, no units had been delivered**.
- **Mr. Gupta blamed the delay on a series of bureaucratic blunders**.
- The **requirements for indigenous content were increased from 40% to 50% under the Defence Acquisition Procedure, 2020**.
- Gupta explained: "**Delays increased the cost of imported components**. It was a **good decision taken for Atmanirbhar Bharat, which backfired**".
- In **October 2021, the ordnance factories were corporatised**.
- The **Heavy Vehicle Ordinance Factory is now AVNL (Armoured Vehicles Nigam Ltd.)**.
- The **procurement indent was deemed invalid upon revalidation**, and "**now it requires a purchase order**," according to Gupta.
- **Without this order, AVNL cannot purchase the Power Pack from MTU in Germany**.
- **MTU closed its production line after 11 years of delays**.

## The SAMHO Missile Integration Gap

- Today, the **124 operational Arjun Mk 1 tanks lack an integrated capability that DRDO has successfully tested and proven**: the **SAMHO missile**.
- The **DRDO's Cannon Launched Missile Development Programme produced the SAMHO**, a **gun-launched anti-tank guided missile with a range of 1.5 km to 5 km**.
- **Trials started in September 2020 at the Armoured Corps Centre and School in Ahmednagar**, according to **Defence Ministry records**.
- **Between 2022 and 2024, numerous successful demonstrations were conducted at both minimum and maximum ranges**, according to **DRDO officials**.
- The article states: "**The capability is proven, tested, and operationally available**".

## Cost-Efficiency Advantage Not Exploited

- The **124 tanks come without the SAMHO missile**.
- The **missiles are launched from the tank's main 120mm gun**, **eliminating the need for a separate launcher and additional operational costs**.
- It would be **quicker and cheaper to upgrade the existing fleet than wait for new deliveries**, according to **Defence officials**.
- The **Arjun's dual-capability gun** - capable of firing **conventional and guided munitions from a single platform** - represents a **cost-efficiency advantage that is not being exploited**, according to **defence sources**.

## Broader Pattern of Systemic Dysfunction

- The **Tejas aircraft program also suffered delays**, with **General Electric halting engine production**.
- The overall pattern points to **systemic institutional dysfunction**, since there is **operational need, tested systems, and demonstrated capability**, yet **what is missing is institutional coordination**.
- **India celebrates defence export records** even as its **own combat-proven, tested, indigenous platform, the Arjun, remains underutilised**.
- The article concludes: "**The problem is not that India cannot produce a defence capability. The question is: Why does it not use the capability it already has?**"
