## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Context And Trigger Event</mark>

Heavy rain and thunderstorms disrupted Delhi flight operations, causing 26 flight cancellations and 15 diversions in a single day. The core question examined is why modern aircraft, despite advanced technology, still face disruptions during rain.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Rain Disrupts Flights</mark>

- Visibility is not the primary issue at cruising altitude, since commercial pilots are trained in instrument flying and can operate using cockpit instruments rather than external sight.
    
- The real risk arises during take-off and landing, when heavy showers create runway spray and reduce visibility of lights, markings, and obstacles.
    
- Pilots operate within defined limits called Runway Visual Range (RVR) — the distance over which a pilot above the runway can see markings or lights; operations are restricted if RVR falls below set minima.
    
- On the runway itself, aircraft face risks of sliding off, or colliding with ground vehicles or other planes, since wet surfaces affect braking and directional control.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Runway Friction And Hydroplaning</mark>

- Aircraft tyres rely on friction with the runway surface to decelerate after landing.
    
- Water on the runway reduces this friction; heavy rain can create standing water, raising the risk of hydroplaning (loss of tyre contact/control) and poor braking performance.
    
- The US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) advises pilots to actively monitor runway-condition reports, since braking conditions can worsen rapidly on wet runways.
    
- Slower runway clearance due to these hazards is a major reason behind flight delays and diversions during rain.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Compounding Weather Factors</mark>

Rain rarely occurs in isolation and is often accompanied by thunderstorms and strong winds, which add further complications.

- Thunderstorms can generate wind shear, downbursts, turbulence, hail, and lightning, with the FAA specifically cautioning against take-off or landing near an approaching thunderstorm due to sudden dangerous gusts.
    
- Crosswinds pose a particular danger since runways are aligned with historical wind patterns for each airport (aircraft land and take off into the wind); strong crosswinds force pilots to adjust the aircraft's angle relative to the wind, complicating landing and take-off.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Aircraft Engineering For Rough Weather</mark>

Despite operational disruptions, rain and storms do not make flights inherently unsafe, as aircraft are engineered to withstand severe weather.

- Aircraft carry systems to clear rain from windscreens and to prevent icing.
    
- Engines are certified to operate reliably in precipitation conditions.
    
- Aircraft structures and systems are specifically designed to safely handle lightning strikes.
    
- According to the US National Weather Service, commercial passenger planes are struck by lightning one or two times a year on average.
    
- The aircraft body provides a conductive path allowing electrical current to travel around the structure and exit safely, keeping the interior unaffected.
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">How Pilots Land With Poor Visibility</mark>

This section explains the technological system that allows landing despite low visibility — a key technical concept for exam purposes.

|Concept|Detail|
|---|---|
|Instrument Landing System (ILS)|Uses ground-based radio signals to give aircraft precise lateral and vertical guidance to the runway|
|CAT I|Decision height of at least 200 ft, with specified visibility/RVR minima|
|CAT II|Allows lower decision heights and lower RVR than CAT I|
|CAT III|Permits approaches in much poorer visibility; further divided into IIIA, IIIB, IIIC based on permitted decision height and RVR|
|Regulating body (India)|Airports Authority of India defines these operational categories|

Beyond technology, highly trained pilots, cabin crew, and ground personnel collectively ensure safe operations during adverse weather.
