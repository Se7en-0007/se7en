## Recent Development

- India signed a Letter of Offer and Acceptance (LOA) with the US to procure the Javelin anti-tank guided missile system, announced by the US Embassy in India on August 28, 2026.
- US Ambassador Sergio Gor called it a step forward for US-India defence ties, with potential for future co-production.
- Cost and quantity of missiles in the deal remain undisclosed.
- Purchase made through the US Foreign Military Sales (FMS) process.
- Development follows more than a decade of Indian interest in the system, and comes after India-US signed a 10-year defence framework agreement in October (2025).

## What Is the Javelin System

- An anti-tank guided munition (ATGM) that entered US military service in 1996.
- Standard effective range: 2.5 km.
- Man-portable - can be carried and fired by a single soldier from the shoulder.
- Can also be fired remotely from a launcher mounted on an unmanned ground vehicle or another vehicle platform.
- Designed to destroy heavily armored vehicles like main battle tanks, plus lighter vehicles, fortifications, bunkers, and helicopters.
- Manufactured by the Javelin Joint Venture - a partnership between Raytheon and Lockheed Martin.
- Used by US Army, US Marine Corps, and international customers, most notably Ukraine.

## Key Feature: Fire-and-Forget

- Uses an infrared guidance system that autonomously tracks the target after launch.
- Allows the operator to fire and immediately take cover, relocate, or reload - reducing exposure to counterattack.

## Operating Mechanism

1. Soldier locates target via infrared sight on the launcher.
2. Operator sets a targeting square around the target using a cursor.
3. Target data is transmitted to a guidance computer built into the missile.
4. Operator presses the trigger to fire.

**Weight specifications:**

- Total system weight: ~22 kg.
- Missile weight alone: 8.4 kg.

## Soft Launch Innovation

- Solves the recoil and exhaust problem inherent to shoulder-fired systems.
- Two-motor mechanism:
    
    - First motor: Provides just enough thrust to eject the missile from the tube; fully burns out before leaving the tube, producing no exhaust that could hit the operator.
    - Second (flight) motor: Ignites after the missile clears the tube and propels it along its attack path.
- Benefits: No exhaust hazard to operator, enables firing from confined spaces/buildings, lower recoil, minimal smoke trail (reduces risk of revealing launch position and inviting counterfire).

## Firing Modes

|Mode|Trajectory|Best Used Against|
|---|---|---|
|Direct attack|Missile flies straight at target|Fortified bunkers, helicopters|
|Top attack|Climbs to 150 m altitude, then dives steeply|Tanks (targets vulnerable roof armor)|

## Combat History

- Extensively used by US and allied forces in Iraq and Afghanistan wars.
- Most notable use: Russia-Ukraine war (since 2022) - Ukrainian forces used Javelins effectively against Russian tanks and armored vehicles, despite being numerically inferior.
- NATO Secretary Jens Stoltenberg credited Javelins with helping Ukraine repel Russia's initial invasion.
- US has supplied Javelins to Ukraine since 2018 (pre-dating the 2022 invasion); over 10,000 missiles delivered as of September 2024.
- Cultural symbol: "St. Javelin" - an image of Mary Magdalene holding an FGM-148 missile - became an emblem of Ukrainian resistance.

## Global Reach

- Sold to 18 partner nations besides the US, including Ukraine, France, Qatar, Turkey, and the United Arab Emirates.
- India's acquisition adds it to this growing list of Javelin operators.
