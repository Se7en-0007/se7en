The editorial argues that July 2026's headline GST growth of 15.4% (₹2.11 lakh crore) masks a worrying reliance on imports and inflation rather than genuine domestic production growth, with sharp regional and sectoral disparities.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Headline Numbers</mark>

- Gross GST collection in July 2026: ₹2.11 lakh crore
    
- Year-on-year growth: 15.4% — the second-best growth rate in FY27
    
- Import IGST growth: 26.9% (far outpacing domestic revenue)
    
- Domestic revenue growth: only 4.5%
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Drivers of Import-Led Growth</mark>

- Rupee depreciation of 10%-12% over the past year raised the rupee cost of imports
    
- Crude oil, electronics, machinery, and chemicals together form about 50% of total imports and pushed up the import bill
    
- Global commodity inflation and higher capital goods imports also contributed to post-pandemic IGST buoyancy
    
- Gold imports added to IGST collections even though gold supply hit a six-year low (bullion imports fell 22%)
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Domestic Revenue Growth Looks Weak</mark>

- High Wholesale Price Index (WPI) inflation at the manufacturing level: 7.18% in June 2026 versus 1.52% a year earlier
    
- This inflation inflates GST collected under the ad valorem system even as actual manufacturing activity is weak
    
- HSBC Manufacturing PMI shows a five-year low in manufacturing growth
    
- Services sector recorded its slowest growth in 53 months, with only real estate and business services showing strong charge increases
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Regional and Fiscal Disparities</mark>

- Only 16 States/Union Territories reported post-settlement GST growth above the national average
    
- A little over a dozen States saw higher-than-average GST growth
    
- Growth is concentrated in States with strong manufacturing and organised services
    
- States with larger unorganised sectors struggle to generate tax buoyancy and remain dependent on central transfers and Finance Commission devolution
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Compliance and Structural Issues</mark>

- Domestic refunds are processing faster than IGST refunds, suggesting improved compliance among formal businesses
    
- Businesses are carrying larger input tax credit balances as the GST ecosystem improves
    
- Persistent faultlines remain: input tax credit disputes and unresolved litigation
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Key Takeaway of The Editorial</mark>

|Concern|Editorial's Argument|
|---|---|
|Nature of growth|Should stem from domestic production and incomes, not exchange-rate effects or imported inflation|
|Policy signal|Calls for "GST 3.0" to make growth benefits geographically broad-based and fiscally inclusive|
|"Make in India" critique|Imported inputs, not domestic manufacturing, are doing "the heavy lifting" in GST numbers|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Structural Terms to Remember</mark>

- IGST: Integrated GST, levied on inter-state trade and imports
    
- Ad valorem tax: Tax calculated as a percentage of value, meaning inflation mechanically raises collections even without real growth
    
- GST 3.0: Reference to the next phase of GST reforms aimed at addressing structural and regional imbalances
