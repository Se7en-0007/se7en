## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background and Context</mark>

- Electoral rolls are a legal record of "belonging" — determining participation in democratic contests, not a final determination of citizenship.
- Political parties across the spectrum flagged inaccuracies: inclusion of ineligible/deceased voters and exclusion of eligible ones.
- EC's order dated June 24, 2025 directed SIR in Bihar, citing rapid urbanisation, frequent migration, new young voters, non-reporting of deaths, inclusion of illegal foreign immigrants, illegal cross-border migration, and duplication.
- Bihar was chosen first as Assembly elections were due later in 2025; a nationwide SIR followed.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Two Constitutional Questions</mark>

- Whether the EC has authority to undertake the SIR exercise at all.
- Whether the procedure/methodology (including citizenship ascertainment) adopted was valid.
- The Supreme Court answered both on May 27, 2026.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Question 1: EC's Authority (Article 324 vs 327)</mark>

- Article 324 vests superintendence, direction, and control of electoral roll preparation in the EC.
- Article 327 empowers Parliament to legislate on elections, including roll preparation, but "subject to the provisions of this Constitution."
- Court's interpretation: these are complementary, not competing powers — Parliament's law under Article 327 cannot extinguish or paralyse EC's core Article 324 functions.
- Where laws/rules are silent or inadequate for emerging contingencies, EC can act to preserve electoral purity.
- Conclusion: Petitioners' claim that EC lacked authority (and had no valid reason to launch SIR shortly before Bihar polls) was rejected.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Question 2: Legitimacy and Proportionality</mark>

- Court held SIR pursued a constitutionally legitimate aim: restoring accuracy, completeness, integrity of rolls.
- Accepted EC's justifications — urbanisation, migration, duplication, ineligible/non-genuine electors — as real, non-speculative, cumulative threats to roll integrity.
- Linked accurate rolls to free and fair elections under Articles 325 and 326.
- Additional procedural burdens (identity verification, proof of residence, eligibility confirmation) were not manifestly arbitrary or disproportionate.

### <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Safeguards Noted by Court</mark>

- Expanded range of acceptable documents.
- Multiple avenues for form submission.
- Involvement of booth-level officers and political party representatives.
- Enquiry, notice, speaking orders, and appellate remedies for disputes.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Rational Nexus Doctrine</mark>

- Even though the exercise could inconvenience some voters, measures bore a rational nexus to the objective.
- A measure cannot be struck down merely because a less intrusive alternative might exist.
- Court upheld both legitimacy and proportionality of SIR.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Citizenship Inquiry — Key Clarification</mark>

- Inquiry into citizenship during SIR is limited — only to determine eligibility for roll inclusion, not a conclusive determination of citizenship.
- Excluded voters retain the right to challenge exclusion via judicial review.
- Court directed EC to refer cases of suspected non-citizens to the competent authority under the Citizenship Act, 1955.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Significance</mark>

|Aspect|Court's Finding|
|---|---|
|EC's authority|Valid under Article 324; complementary to Article 327|
|Purpose of SIR|Legitimate — roll accuracy and integrity|
|Procedural burden|Proportionate, not arbitrary|
|Citizenship inquiry|Limited, not final; subject to judicial review and Citizenship Act referral|
|Overall verdict|SIR upheld as constitutional|

- The verdict vindicates EC's stance that SIR purifies electoral rolls and strengthens democracy, addressing political and civil society apprehensions around the exercise.
