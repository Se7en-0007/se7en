India's National Coal Gasification Mission aims to convert 100 million tonnes of coal into methanol, ammonia, and fertilizers by 2030 with a ₹37,500-crore incentive scheme, but the article argues biomass is a more viable feedstock than coal/lignite for this purpose.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Background: The Coal Gasification Mission</mark>

- Target: convert 100 million tonnes of coal into methanol, ammonia, and fertilizers by 2030
    
- Incentive: ₹37,500-crore scheme — the largest public commitment to gasification so far
    
- Objective: improve energy security and cut import dependence amid volatile oil and gas markets
    
- Current strategy relies heavily on lignite as feedstock

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Structural Constraints of Lignite/Coal</mark>

**Resource base and location**

- India's lignite reserves: roughly 47 billion tonnes
    
- Concentrated mainly in Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan, and Gujarat

**Key technical problems**

- High ash fusion temperature: forces entrained-flow gasifiers to run hotter than economically efficient, unlike lower-fusion coals used in China's build-out
    
- High moisture: Neyveli lignite has 45-60% moisture (as-received), lowering efficiency
    
- Low calorific value: 2,600-3,000 kcal/kg, far below imported coal
    
- Feedstock variability: quality differs even within the same field, causing inconsistent syngas composition, raising gas clean-up costs
    
- High sulphur: lignite from Rajasthan/Gujarat can have up to 9% sulphur (dry basis), requiring costly removal and damaging catalysts
    
- High ash in bituminous coal: 25-45% versus 10-20% in imported coal; no major gasifier vendor supports systems above 25% ash
    
- NITI Aayog has flagged ash content, calorific variability, and mineral complexity as persistent barriers

**Technology and implementation risk**

- Current plan depends on imported gasification technologies unproven at commercial scale on Indian lignite
    
- Limited scope for technology transfer; high execution risk
    
- Indigenous technology still at early/pilot stage
    
- Talcher Fertilizers' coal-to-urea project has slipped by years
    
- Bharat Coal Gasification and Chemicals Ltd.'s ammonium nitrate project is the first commercial use of BHEL's indigenous pressurised fluidised bed technology — moving to commercial scale before proven pathways exist

**Environmental and economic issues**

- Coal gasification's "clean" claim depends on Carbon Capture, Utilisation and Storage (CCUS) at scale
    
- India lacks CCUS infrastructure: no storage networks, limited regulatory clarity, no large-scale injection experience
    
- Current incentive scheme does not mandate CCUS
    
- Scale mismatch: ~₹88,000 crore capital committed against only ₹37,500-crore incentive support, implying continued subsidy dependence
    
- China comparison is misleading — China's build-out used lower-ash, lower-moisture, lower-sulphur coal

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Biomass Is a Better Alternative</mark>

**Availability**

- India generates ~990 million tonnes of agricultural residues annually
    
- ~230 million tonnes available as surplus
    
- ~90-100 million tonnes of this surplus is still openly burned annually, a lost resource and major pollution source (notably contributing to North India's winter air pollution/stubble burning issue)

**Process advantages**

- Lower ash content
    
- Negligible sulphur
    
- Moisture manageable through basic pre-treatment
    
- Suitable for fluidised bed gasifiers already used globally; Indian firms are beginning select biomass-to-methanol and industrial syngas projects

**Emissions comparison**

|Feedstock|Downstream Products|Lifecycle CO2 (per tonne methanol)|
|---|---|---|
|Biomass|Methanol, ammonia (same synthesis process as coal)|~0.67 tonnes CO2|
|Coal|Methanol, ammonia|~3 tonnes CO2|

**Deployment model benefits**

- Smaller-scale facilities located closer to feedstock sources
    
- Reduces transport costs; enables hub-and-spoke plant network
    
- Creates rural income via residue aggregation and logistics
    
- Provides a market-based solution to stubble burning

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Recommended Strategy Going Forward</mark>

- Deploy biomass gasification at commercial scale now using established technologies
    
- Build domestic capability in syngas production, clean-up, and downstream synthesis
    
- Continue lignite gasification only at pilot scale with clear, independently validated performance targets
    
- Let progress in CCUS infrastructure inform any future coal-based scale-up decisions
    
- Frame gasification (coal or biomass) as a long-term (two-decade) national technology mission
    
- Draw on institutions like CSIR, IITs, IISERs, ICAR, public sector and private industry — similar to India's earlier space and agriculture capability-building efforts
