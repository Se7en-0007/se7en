## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Core Context</mark>

- India's 10.4 crore Scheduled Tribes derive 20–40% of annual income from Minor Forest Produce (MFP)
    
- Historically, tribals sold MFP to middlemen at distress prices, earning below the cost of their own labour
    
- PMJVM addresses this exploitation by organising tribals into community-owned Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs)
    
- The mission shifts tribals from being raw produce sellers to value-adding entrepreneurs
    
- PMJVM was launched in 2021-22 as a systematic mechanism to improve MFP's market value through skilling, technology, and market linkages
    
- The scheme guarantees Minimum Support Price (MSP) via direct government procurement
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Minor Forest Produce: Legal and Institutional Framework</mark>

- Minor Forest Produce includes non-timber forest produce of plant origin: bamboo, brushwood, stumps, cane, tussar, cocoons, honey, wax, lac, tendu/kendu leaves, medicinal plants, herbs, roots, and tubers
    
- Defined under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (Forest Rights Act)
    
- The Forest Rights Act recognises historical dependence of forest-dwelling communities on forest produce and grants tribals rights to procure and sell non-timber MFP
    
- The Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act (PESA), 1996 separately grants tribal Gram Panchayats ownership rights over MFP
    
- MSP has been notified for 87 MFP items under PMJVM
    
- The Tribal Cooperative Marketing Development Federation of India (TRIFED) is mandated with marketing and development of tribal products
    
- TRIFED purchases non-timber forest produce at pre-determined MSP regardless of prevailing market price, operating through state implementing agencies (tribal development cooperatives, forest development corporations)
    
- Since 2013-14, state governments have procured 2,67,954 metric tonnes of MFP worth Rs. 693.98 crore under the scheme
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Institutional Architecture: Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs)</mark>

|Aspect|Detail|
|---|---|
|Base unit|Self-Help Groups (SHGs) of tribals|
|Cluster structure|15 SHGs (~300 tribals) clustered into one VDVK|
|ST composition requirement|At least 60% of VDVK members must be Scheduled Tribe|
|Origin scheme|Van Dhan Yojana, launched April 14, 2018 (forms foundation of VDVK model, part of PMJVM)|
|VDVKs sanctioned|4,172|
|VDVKs functional|2,911, with sales of ~Rs. 168 crore (as on July 31, 2026)|
|Tribal members linked|12.48 lakh|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Support Mechanisms Under PMJVM</mark>

**Skilling, Funding and Technological Support**

- Training programmes for MFP gatherers, including scientific/sustainable harvesting practices, conducted at VDVKs
    
- Infrastructural support established in a beneficiary's home, or a government/gram panchayat building
    
- Primary processing equipment provided based on locally available MFPs: cutting and sieving tools, decorticators, dryers, packaging tools
    
- Training for 30 members per SHG on sustainable harvesting and value addition, with raw materials and trainee kits supplied
    
- Working capital facilitated through tie-ups with financial institutions, banks, and the National Scheduled Tribes Finance and Development Corporation (NSTFDC)
    
- Storage and transportation support provided on a rental basis
    

**Marketing and Business Support**

- VDVKs are established around local Haat Bazaars (primary MFP procurement points); over 5,000 Haats exist in tribal areas, being modernised in phases with permanent structures, storage, drinking water, shade, and weighing equipment
    
- VDVKs may partner with private entities or non-profits for value addition or marketing
    
- TRIFED disseminates market intelligence (daily rates, e-commerce avenues) to VDVKs for better business decisions
    
- Additional support: branding/promotion via festivals, exhibitions, artisan melas; geo-tagging of VDVKs and scheme assets; Geographical Indication (GI) tagging for tribal produce; IT and e-commerce platform development; research and development support
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Gender Dimension: Women's Empowerment</mark>

- Most collection and sales activities under VDVKs are carried out by women, making PMJVM a key instrument of tribal women's financial empowerment
    
- Case study: Nyigamane, Ho Yorbe, Medumhagdum VDVK in Arunachal Pradesh's Leparada district, established October 2021 with 300 women SHG members and farmers
    
- This VDVK converts local non-timber forest and agricultural produce into processed goods: King Chilli Pickle, Bamboo Shoot Pickle, Turmeric Powder, Ginger Powder, Pineapple Jam, Pineapple Squash, Orange Squash
    
- Has sold Rs. 11 lakh worth of products since 2022-23
    
- Women are joint owners of the business - stakeholders in brand and enterprise, not merely wage workers, reducing dependence on seasonal agriculture alone
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Broader Significance</mark>

- PMJVM balances community ownership with market competitiveness in a forest-based economy
    
- Positions tribal produce as natural, community-owned, and forest-born alternatives to mass-manufactured, plastic-packaged goods
    
- Framed as embodying "Sabka Saath, Sabka Vikaas," extending India's growth story to remote forest communities
    
- Positioned as contributing to the vision of Viksit Bharat@2047
