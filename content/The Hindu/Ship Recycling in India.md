India has become the world's leading ship recycling nation, with its global market share rising from 30.1% in 2024 to 35.4% in 2025, achieving the Maritime India Vision 2030 target five years ahead of schedule.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Overview</mark>

Ship recycling is the safe, environmentally sound and resource-efficient dismantling of ships at the end of their operational life, forming an integral part of the maritime lifecycle. It supports circular economy objectives by recovering steel and other reusable materials while safeguarding the environment and worker health and safety. Stronger legislation and institutional oversight have transformed conventional dismantling into an internationally aligned industry centered on compliance, sustainability, and worker safety.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Why Ship Recycling Matters</mark>

**Environmental significance:**

- Regulated recycling prevents unsafe disposal of end-of-life ships, reducing marine and coastal pollution risk
    
- Ensures safe handling of hazardous materials under strict regulatory supervision
    

**Resource significance:**

- Provides significant ferrous scrap for the domestic steel industry, reducing import dependence and strengthening supply security
    
- Recovery and reuse of steel supports efficient resource utilisation and circular economy
    
- Recycled steel needs substantially less energy than primary production, lowering greenhouse gas emissions and supporting decarbonisation goals
    

**Economic and industrial significance:**

- Generates large-scale employment in dismantling, processing, and material recovery
    
- Sustains ancillary industries such as transport, re-rolling mills, equipment suppliers, and waste management
    
- Recovered steel and components support domestic manufacturing, aligning with Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Alang-Sosiya Cluster</mark>

**Location and scale:**

- Located in Gujarat; recognised as the world's largest ship recycling hub
    
- Accounts for approximately 98% of India's ship recycling activity
    
- Annual recycling capacity of around 6 million GT
    
- Produces around 3.50 million metric tonnes (MMT) of steel per annum without exploiting natural resources
    

**Expansion plans:**

- India aims to nearly double capacity to about 9 million light displacement tons (LDT) through development of the Alang Ship Recycling Yard, backed by a comprehensive master plan
    

**Worker training:**

- The Safety Training and Labour Welfare Institute at Alang-Sosiya has cumulatively trained over 1.5 lakh workers in occupational safety, hazardous material handling, and emergency response
    

**Other yards:**

- Amar Iron Udyog, Kolkata
    
- Steel Industrials Kerala Limited (SILK), Kerala
    

**Modernised yard facilities include:**

- Engineered and demarcated recycling plots
    
- Impermeable working surfaces and drainage systems
    
- Mechanised material handling and lifting equipment
    
- Segregated hazardous waste storage areas
    
- Authorised downstream waste treatment and disposal linkages
    
- Structured safety, health, and emergency response arrangements
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Regulatory and Legal Framework</mark>

**Legal and institutional architecture:**

- **Ship Recycling Act, 2019** – Primary legal framework giving domestic effect to international standards; designates the Directorate General of Shipping as the National Authority; mandates authorisation, certification, inspection, and enforcement
    
- **Ship Recycling Rules, 2021** – Operationalises the Act through facility authorisation, ship recycling plans, hazardous material management, worker safety, training, and reporting requirements
    
- **Ship Recycling Regulations, 2026** – Strengthens implementation through detailed operational, safety, and environmental standards for uniform compliance and monitoring
    

**Alignment with Hong Kong International Convention (HKC):**

- The HKC is the principal international instrument governing safe and environmentally sound ship recycling; came into force on 26 June 2025, making compliance mandatory
    
- India has aligned its domestic framework through the Act, Rules, and Regulations
    
- Financial assistance of ₹53.5 crore provided for yard modernisation, helping 115 facilities achieve HKC compliance
    
- 35 yards at Alang have applied for EU green-listing; EU inspections have begun; Shree Ram Vessel Scrap and Y.S. Investments feature in the draft 16th European List of Ship Recycling Facilities
    

**Inventory of Hazardous Materials (IHM):**

- Identifies and records hazardous substances on board ships throughout their operational life and before recycling
    
- India follows a lifecycle-based compliance approach ensuring transparency, traceability, and risk mitigation
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Ship-breaking Credit Note Scheme</mark>

Introduced under the Ship Building Financial Assistance Scheme (SBFA) 2.0, this scheme encourages shipowners to recycle at compliant Indian yards while promoting new vessel construction domestically. It issues a Credit Note equivalent to 40% of the recycled ship's scrap value, usable toward up to 5% of the cost of a new vessel built at an Indian shipyard, thereby linking recycling with shipbuilding growth.

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Case Study: Ex-INS Viraat</mark>

The recycling of Ex-INS Viraat at Alang stands as one of India's most significant ship recycling projects, showcasing responsible recycling capabilities.

- Served the Indian Navy for 30 years and the Royal Navy for 27 years before decommissioning in March 2017
    
- Arrived at Alang for recycling in September 2020
    
- Recognised by Guinness World Records as one of the world's longest-serving warships
    
- Logged over 22,622 flying hours, spent 2,252 days at sea, and sailed more than 5.88 lakh nautical miles (10.94 lakh km)
    

Its dismantling demonstrated India's capability to safely recover valuable materials from a large, complex vessel while reflecting evolving environmental and occupational safety standards.
