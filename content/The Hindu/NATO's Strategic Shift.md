For the first time since NATO's founding in 1949, the US is asking Europe to take primary responsibility for its own security rather than just increasing defence spending. This marks a fundamental restructuring of the transatlantic security compact, shifting from "whether NATO endures" to "how NATO adapts."

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">US National Security Strategy (Nov 2025)</mark>

- NATO remains America's principal transatlantic alliance, but Washington's role is redefined
    
- Argues the era of US as Europe's primary security guarantor is ending; prosperous European allies must defend their own continent
    
- Endorses raising defence spending benchmark to 5% of GDP for burden-sharing
    
- US retains role as strategic enabler, nuclear guarantor, and political convener — not sole military backbone
    
- Objective: a self-reliant Europe frees up US strategic bandwidth for the Indo-Pacific
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Evidence of US Troop Rebalancing</mark>

- Post-2022 Ukraine invasion: US troop strength in Europe rose to ~100,000
    
- 2025-26 reversal: withdrew ~5,000 troops from Germany, cancelled planned deployment of 4,000 troops to Poland
    
- Brigade Combat Teams in Europe reduced from four to three
    
- Current US presence: ~75,000-80,000 troops, near pre-Ukraine war levels
    
- Reduction also seen in rapid reaction assets: bombers, fighter aircraft, air-refuelling tankers, warships, carriers, attack submarines; deployment of Tomahawk cruise missiles to Germany deferred
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">36th NATO Summit, Ankara (July 2026)</mark>

- Reaffirmed commitment to Article 5 (collective defence)
    
- Pledged €70 billion in military assistance to Ukraine for 2026, with comparable support committed for 2027
    
- Endorsed European-led development of long-range precision strike capabilities
    
- Accelerated efforts to strengthen NATO's defence industrial base
    
- Core message: Europe must assume greater conventional defence responsibility as US pivots to Indo-Pacific
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Historical Framing: Lord Ismay's Dictum</mark>

- Lord Ismay (NATO's first Secretary General) described NATO's Cold War purpose as: "to keep the Russians out, the Americans in, and the Germans down"
    
- Today's updated logic: keeping Russia deterred, America engaged, and Europe prepared
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Three Phases of NATO's Evolution</mark>

|Phase|Period|Key Features|
|---|---|---|
|NATO 1.0|1949-1991|Overwhelming reliance on American military/nuclear power to deter Soviet aggression; transatlantic unity|
|NATO 2.0|1991-2022|Post-Soviet eastward expansion; absorbed former Warsaw Pact members; operations in Balkans and Afghanistan; declining European defence spending; growing dependence on US|
|NATO 3.0|2022-present|Triggered by Russia's Ukraine invasion; more balanced partnership—Europe takes conventional defence lead, US provides nuclear deterrence, intelligence, long-range strike, global power projection|

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Europe's Defence Spending Response</mark>

- 2025 Hague Summit: NATO members committed to raising core defence spending to at least 3.5% of GDP by 2035, with review in 2029
    
- Germany has doubled defence expenditure since 2023, aiming for the 3.5% target by 2029 while rebuilding capability to deter Russia
    
- Russia has simultaneously reorganised its western military posture — new headquarters, formations, and bases facing NATO
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Europe's Central Challenge</mark>

- The harder task isn't spending more money but replacing the American military ecosystem
    
- Europe remains dependent on the US for: intelligence, surveillance, satellite support, command-and-control systems, strategic airlift, missile defence, long-range precision strike, and the nuclear umbrella
    
- Poland's Foreign Minister's framing: Europe doesn't need to match American power, only needs to be "stronger than Russia"
    

## <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">Implications for India</mark>

- NATO's transformation matters to India primarily through the redistribution of American strategic attention toward the Indo-Pacific, reinforcing regional balance amid China's assertiveness
    
- A more capable, self-reliant Europe could become a significant partner for India in defence manufacturing, advanced technologies, AI, cybersecurity, semiconductors, and space
    
- New Delhi should view these shifts as strategic opportunity, not alliance politics — deeper cooperation with both US and Europe is compatible with India's strategic autonomy
    
- Potential benefits: expanded defence industrial partnerships, accelerated technological modernisation, strengthened position as a leading power in a multipolar world
