- **Political consequences of changing patterns of birth, death and movement of people** are being felt worldwide.
- In India, **Census 2027 operations** have created urgency around **uneven population trends across regions and communities**.
- The prospect of **delimitation of Lok Sabha constituencies after the first Census following 2026** adds to this urgency.

## Natalism

- **Natalism or pronatalism** is a belief system or policy stance that encourages **human reproduction and high birth rates**.
- Elections and political debates increasingly involve **fertility, migration, ageing, identity and belonging**.
- **Elon Musk** sees falling birth rates as a major threat to civilisation, particularly the West.
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu** has urged Telugu families to have more children.
- **M.K. Stalin** rhetorically invoked the idea of Tamils having **“16 children”**.
- **Mohan Bhagwat** called for Indian, rather **Hindu, families to have three children**.
- The **Catholic Church** asks its members to have bigger families.
- The Tamil Nadu government extended **one-year maternity leave** to women government employees having a **third child**.
- In **2024**, a remark that Muslims could soon become a majority in West Bengal drew criticism; the party concerned distanced itself from and condemned it.

## Take The Subject Seriously

- **Political demography** studies how population changes - **births, deaths, ageing and migration** - affect **government policies, political power and international security**.
- Although demography generates abundant politics, serious scholarship is constrained by:
    
    - **Conspiracy theorists** seeking political capital.
    - **Demographic sceptics** who treat any discussion as **nativism or xenophobia**.
- The global population will keep growing for some time, but growth is **uneven across political boundaries and social groups**.

## Fertility Transition

- India is moving rapidly through a **fertility transition**.
- India’s **total fertility rate** has fallen below replacement level to **2.0 children per woman**, according to **NFHS-5**.
- Fertility has declined in **every major religious community**; the pace differs across regions and communities, but the **gap is narrowing**.
- Using NFHS data, Pew Research found that **Muslim fertility** declined from **4.4 children per woman in 1992** to **2.4 in 2019-21**.
- Fertility declined among every major religious group and **fertility gaps between religious communities have narrowed**.

## Electoral Process and Migration

- Demographic factors will challenge the existing **electoral process**.
- The principle of **“One person, one vote, one value”** is not applied blindly in India; it operates in consonance with **group rights of representation**.
- **Social federalism** and **political federalism** are both part of India’s democratic design.
- Rich and poor people both move, though for different reasons.
- In recent years, around **two lakh Indians** have been giving up Indian citizenship on average each year.
- Poor people move from places with fewer opportunities to places with greater opportunities.
- According to the **2011 Census**, India had about **45 crore internal migrants**.

## Ageing and Younger Populations

- Some regions are ageing, while others will remain younger for longer.
- **Keralam** has India’s highest median age, estimated at **37 years in 2026**, projected to reach **47 years by 2051**.
- **Uttar Pradesh** has a median age of approximately **26.9 years**, making it one of India’s youngest States.
- India’s projected median age is about **29.2 years in 2026**.
- Even by **2036**, Uttar Pradesh’s median age is projected to reach only **31.7 years**.

## What Is In Focus

- **Natalism is simplistic rhetoric in response to complex realities.**
- No country or society has easily or consistently reversed a sustained decline in fertility.
- **Japan, South Korea, China and Italy** have used financial incentives, childcare support, parental leave and other pronatalist measures, but none restored fertility to earlier levels.
- **South Korea** continues to have extraordinarily low fertility despite extensive family policies.
- **Italy** has continued to experience declining births despite financial incentives and family-support measures.
- Fertility is not merely a question of money; it is tied to:
    
    - **Housing**
    - **Employment**
    - **Education**
    - **Gender relations**
    - **Cost of raising children**
    - **People’s expectations about the future**

## Population Growth

- Continued population growth in many societies increasingly results from **expanding lifespan and population momentum**, rather than rising births.
- This growth is uneven across political boundaries and social groups.
- Wealth creation and distribution are also uneven and will remain so.
- **Fewer people are being born**, people are **living longer**, and they are **moving faster and farther**.
- These developments, together with the demands of **democracy and federalism**, require **cool thinking and new imaginative capacity**.

## Federalism and Development

- The interlinkage between politics and demography remains a marginal field within political science and demography, but it should not remain so.
- In countries such as **India and the United States**, demography and the economy operate alongside questions of **federalism**.
- A balance is needed:
    
    - Across social groups nationally.
    - Across States in **development outcomes, growth, opportunities and political power**.
- Southern States achieved fertility decline earlier and now face the possibility of ageing faster than States with relatively younger populations.
- The political question is not merely **who has more people**, but:
    
    - **Who has invested in development**
    - **Who has fewer young people entering the workforce**
    - **Who needs resources**
    - **How representation should respond to these differences**

## Group Identities

- In a utopian world where all humanity were one, no consideration other than individual identity should matter.
- In practice, these questions are framed in terms of **group identities**.
- Recognition of group identities is not alien to democratic practice; it can be an **integral and essential part** of it.
- The problem is not that groups exist, but how democratic institutions and development planning can accommodate changing group identities **without turning demographic differences into permanent political antagonism**.

## Impact on Planning and Politics

- Changing demographic patterns affect both **planning and politics**.
- Groups with larger populations will claim greater political power, while shrinking groups will feel increasingly insecure.
- As older and retired people grow relative to the working-age population, their care will require a higher commitment of national resources.
- Regional demographic differences can generate friction between States as resources and people move across borders - from regions where there is more to regions where there is less.
- Such differences can cause disputes over:
    
    - **Taxation**
    - **Fiscal transfers**
    - **Development**
    - **Political representation**

## Managing Demographic Change

- Worldwide discussions on demographic shifts have become sources of **diatribe**.
- One group invokes **replacement theory** and fears its race is being wiped out; another dismisses such concerns as conspiracy theories.
- **Demographic change is neither a conspiracy to replace any community nor an imagined phenomenon.**
- It is **real, natural and likely to be difficult to reverse**.
- Politics must shift from trying to **profit from demographic change** towards **sensitively managing and navigating it**.
- The central question is whether there can be a more expansive idea of **“The People.”**
