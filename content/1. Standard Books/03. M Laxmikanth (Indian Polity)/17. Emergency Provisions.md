## INTRODUCTION
* **Location:** Enshrined in **Part XVIII** of the Constitution (**Articles 352 to 360**)
* **Objective:** Enable Central Government to meet abnormal situations effectively; safeguard sovereignty, unity, integrity, security of the country, democratic political system, and Constitution
* **Federal to Unitary:** Converts federal structure into a unitary one **without formal constitutional amendment**
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Constitution can be both **unitary as well as federal** according to requirements of time and circumstances (normal times = federal; emergency = unitary)
* **3 Types of Emergencies:**
  1. **National Emergency (Article 352):** Emergency due to war, external aggression, or armed rebellion (*Constitution terms it ‘Proclamation of Emergency’*)
  2. **President’s Rule (Article 356):** Emergency due to failure of constitutional machinery in states (*also known as ‘State Emergency’ or ‘Constitutional Emergency’; Constitution does not use word ‘emergency’*)
  3. **Financial Emergency (Article 360):** Threat to financial stability or credit of India

## NATIONAL EMERGENCY

### Grounds of Declaration
* **Article 352:** President can declare National Emergency if security of India or a part is threatened by **war**, **external aggression**, or **armed rebellion** (or imminent danger thereof)
* **38th Amendment Act (1975):** President can issue different proclamations on grounds of war, external aggression, armed rebellion, or imminent danger
* **Classifications:**
  * **External Emergency:** Declared on grounds of **‘war’** or **‘external aggression’**
  * **Internal Emergency:** Declared on ground of **‘armed rebellion’**
* **Territorial Operation:** **42nd Amendment Act (1976)** enabled President to limit operation to a specified part of India
* **44th Amendment Act (1978):** Substituted **‘armed rebellion’** for **‘internal disturbance’** (vague phrase used in 1975)
* **Written Recommendation:** President can proclaim ONLY after receiving **written recommendation from Cabinet** (*Cabinet = PM + other Cabinet-rank ministers*; safeguard introduced by 44th Amendment Act, 1978)
* **Judicial Review:** **38th Amendment (1975)** made declaration immune from judicial review → Deleted by **44th Amendment (1978)**
  * ***Minerva Mills Case (1980):*** SC held National Emergency declaration can be challenged in court on grounds of mala fide, extraneous/irrelevant facts, absurdity, or perversity

### Parliamentary Approval and Duration
* **Approval Period:** Must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament** within **1 month** from date of issue (*reduced from 2 months by 44th Amendment Act, 1978*)
* **Lok Sabha Dissolution:** If Lok Sabha is dissolved at declaration time or during 1 month, proclamation survives until **30 days** from 1st sitting of reconstituted Lok Sabha (provided Rajya Sabha approved it)
* **Duration:** Operates for **6 months** once approved; can be extended for **indefinite period** with 6-month periodic parliamentary approval (*periodic approval added by 44th Amendment Act, 1978*)
* **Majority:** Every resolution approving declaration or continuance must be passed by **Special Majority** (majority of total membership + 2/3 members present and voting; *added by 44th Amendment Act, 1978*; previously simple majority)

### Revocation of Proclamation
* **By President:** Can be revoked by President at any time via subsequent proclamation (**no parliamentary approval required**)
* **Mandatory Revocation:** President **must revoke** if **Lok Sabha** passes a resolution disapproving its continuation (*safeguard added by 44th Amendment Act, 1978*)
* **Special Sitting:** If **1/10th members of Lok Sabha** give written notice to Speaker (or President if House not in session), a special sitting must be held within **14 days** to consider disapproving resolution
* **Disapproval vs Approval Resolution:**
  1. Disapproval resolution requires **Lok Sabha only** (Approval requires **both Houses**)
  2. Disapproval passed by **Simple Majority** (Approval passed by **Special Majority**)

### Effects of National Emergency

#### 1. Effect on Centre–State Relations
* **(a) Executive:** Centre acquires power to give executive directions to states on **‘any’ matter** (state governments under complete Central control, but **NOT suspended**)
* **(b) Legislative:** Parliament empowered to make laws on **State List** subjects (state legislature **NOT suspended**, but overridden); Parliamentary laws on State List become inoperative **6 months** post-emergency; President can issue ordinances on state subjects if Parliament is not in session
  * *42nd Amendment (1976):* Extended executive and legislative consequences to non-emergency states as well
* **(c) Financial:** President can modify/reduce/cancel constitutional distribution of revenues between Centre and States until end of financial year (orders laid before Parliament)

#### 2. Effect on Life of Lok Sabha and State Assembly
* **Lok Sabha:** Life extended beyond 5 years by Parliamentary law for **1 year at a time** (any length); cannot extend beyond **6 months** post-emergency (*Example:* Term of 5th Lok Sabha extended twice)
* **State Assembly:** Term extended by Parliamentary law by **1 year at a time** (max 6 months post-emergency)

#### 3. Effect on Fundamental Rights

##### (a) Suspension of Fundamental Rights under Article 358
* **Automatic Suspension:** **Article 19** 6 Fundamental Rights automatically suspended upon proclamation (no separate order required); State freed from Art 19 restrictions
* **44th Amendment Act (1978) Restrictions:**
  1. Art 19 stands suspended **ONLY in External Emergency** (war/external aggression), NOT Internal Emergency (armed rebellion)
  2. Only emergency-related laws and executive actions under such laws are protected from court challenge

##### (b) Suspension of Other Fundamental Rights under Article 359
* **Enforcement Suspension:** Authorises President to suspend **right to move court** for enforcement of specified FRs (rights remain alive, remedy suspended) via Presidential Order
* **44th Amendment Act (1978) Restrictions:**
  1. President **CANNOT suspend** right to move court for **Articles 20 and 21** (protection in respect of conviction for offences & life/personal liberty remain enforceable)
  2. Only emergency-related laws and executive actions under such laws protected

#### Distinction Between Articles 358 and 359
1. **Scope:** Art 358 confined to Art 19; Art 359 extends to all specified FRs (except Arts 20 & 21)
2. **Mechanism:** Art 358 automatically suspends Art 19; Art 359 requires Presidential Order
3. **Emergency Type:** Art 358 operates ONLY in External Emergency; Art 359 operates in both External and Internal Emergency
4. **Duration:** Art 358 operates for entire emergency duration; Art 359 operates for period specified in order
5. **Territorial Extent:** Art 358 extends to entire country; Art 359 extends to whole or part of country
6. **Exception:** Art 358 suspends Art 19 completely; Art 359 cannot suspend enforcement of Arts 20 & 21

### Declarations Made So Far
* **Proclaimed 3 times so far:**
  1. **October 1962:** Chinese aggression in NEFA (Arunachal Pradesh); in force till **January 1968** (covered 1965 Indo-Pak war)
  2. **December 1971:** Pakistan attack
  3. **June 1975:** Internal disturbance (inciting police/armed forces)
* **1962 & 1971:** Declared on ground of **‘external aggression’**
* **1975:** Declared on ground of **‘internal disturbance’** (most controversial)
* **Revocation:** 2nd & 3rd proclamations revoked together in **March 1977**
* **Shah Commission (1977):** Appointed by Janata Govt to investigate 1975 emergency excesses → Led to **44th Amendment Act (1978)** safeguards

## PRESIDENT’S RULE

### Grounds of Imposition
* **Article 355:** Duty of Centre to protect states against external aggression and internal disturbance, and ensure governance in accordance with Constitution
* **2 Grounds for Proclamation:**
  1. **Article 356:** President satisfied state government cannot be carried on per Constitution (acts on Governor's report or otherwise)
  2. **Article 365:** State fails to comply with or give effect to directions from Centre

### Parliamentary Approval and Duration
* **Approval Period:** Must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament** within **2 months** from date of issue
* **Lok Sabha Dissolution:** Survives until **30 days** from 1st sitting of reconstituted Lok Sabha (if Rajya Sabha approved)
* **Duration:** Operates for **6 months**; maximum period = **3 years** (with 6-month periodic parliamentary approvals)
* **Majority:** Approved by **Simple Majority** in either House
* **44th Amendment Act (1978) Restraint beyond 1 Year:** Extension beyond 1 year allowed ONLY if 2 conditions met:
  1. National Emergency in operation in whole of India or whole/part of state; AND
  2. Election Commission certifies general assembly elections cannot be held
* **Revocation:** Revoked by President at any time via subsequent proclamation (**no parliamentary approval required**)

### Consequences of President’s Rule
1. President takes up state government functions and powers of Governor / state executive
2. President dismisses State Council of Ministers headed by CM
3. Governor administers state on President's behalf with Chief Secretary / appointed advisors
4. Parliament exercises powers of state legislature (or delegates to President / specified authority)
5. State Legislative Assembly is either **suspended or dissolved**
6. **High Court Immunity:** President **CANNOT assume powers** of High Court or suspend constitutional provisions relating to it

### Comparing National Emergency and President’s Rule

| Feature | National Emergency (Article 352) | President’s Rule (Article 356) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Grounds** | Security threatened by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion | State government cannot be carried on per Constitution |
| **2. State Organs Status** | State executive & legislature continue functioning (Centre gets concurrent powers) | State executive dismissed; state legislature suspended or dissolved |
| **3. Delegation of Laws** | Parliament legislates on State List directly (cannot delegate) | Parliament can delegate state lawmaking power to President/authority |
| **4. Maximum Duration** | **No maximum period** (indefinite with 6-month approvals) | **Maximum 3 years** |
| **5. Territorial Scope** | Modifies Centre's relation with **all states** | Modifies Centre's relation with **concerned state only** |
| **6. Parliamentary Majority** | Requires **Special Majority** | Requires **Simple Majority** |
| **7. Fundamental Rights** | Affects Fundamental Rights | **No effect** on Fundamental Rights |
| **8. Revocation by Lok Sabha** | Lok Sabha can pass resolution for revocation | No Lok Sabha resolution provision (revoked by President alone) |

### Use of Article 356
* Imposed **over 125 times** since 1950 (first imposed in **Punjab, 1951**)
* **Political Misuse:** Arbitrary imposition for political reasons (1977 Janata Govt dismissed 9 Congress state govts; 1980 Congress Govt dismissed 9 Janata state govts; 1992 3 BJP state govts dismissed post-Babri demolition)
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Hoped Article 356 would remain a **‘dead-letter’** (used as last resort)
* **H.V. Kamath:** *“Dr. Ambedkar is dead and the Articles are very much alive”*

### Scope of Judicial Review
* **38th Amendment (1975):** Made satisfaction final → Deleted by **44th Amendment (1978)**
* ***S.R. Bommai Case (1994) SC Guidelines:***
  1. Presidential proclamation subject to **judicial review** (burden on Centre to justify)
  2. Satisfaction must be based on relevant material; court can restore dismissed government & assembly if unconstitutional
  3. State assembly should be **suspended** (NOT dissolved) until Parliament approves proclamation
  4. **Secularism** = ‘Basic Feature’ → Anti-secular state govt liable under Art 356
  5. Majority confidence of ministry must be tested **on floor of the House**

### Cases of Proper and Improper Use
* **Proper Imposition:**
  1. Hung assembly post-elections
  2. Majority party declines to form ministry & no coalition possible
  3. Ministry resigns after defeat & no alternative ministry possible
  4. Disregard of Central constitutional directions (**Article 365**)
  5. Internal subversion / deliberate action against Constitution
  6. Physical breakdown of constitutional obligations
* **Improper Imposition:**
  1. Governor recommends without probing alternative ministry options
  2. Governor assesses support without allowing floor test
  3. Ruling party defeat in Lok Sabha general elections
  4. Internal disturbance not amounting to subversion or physical breakdown
  5. Corruption / maladministration allegations or financial exigencies
  6. No prior warning given to state to rectify itself
  7. Sorting out intra-party problems of ruling party

## FINANCIAL EMERGENCY

### Grounds of Declaration
* **Article 360:** President can proclaim Financial Emergency if financial stability or credit of India or any part is threatened
* **38th Amendment (1975):** Made satisfaction final → Deleted by **44th Amendment (1978)** (subject to judicial review)

### Parliamentary Approval and Duration
* **Approval Period:** Must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament** within **2 months**
* **Lok Sabha Dissolution:** Survives until **30 days** from 1st sitting of reconstituted Lok Sabha (if Rajya Sabha approved)
* **Duration:** Continues **indefinitely** till revoked (**NO maximum period prescribed**, **NO repeated parliamentary approval required**)
* **Majority:** Approved by **Simple Majority** in either House
* **Revocation:** Revoked by President at any time (**no parliamentary approval required**)

### Effects of Financial Emergency
1. Executive authority of Centre extends to giving directions to states to observe **canons of financial propriety**
2. Directions to reduce salaries and allowances of all/any class of state servants
3. Reservation of all **Money Bills and financial bills** for President's consideration after state passage
4. Directions for reduction of salaries of Union servants and **Judges of Supreme Court and High Courts**
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Patterned after **National Recovery Act of US (1933)** post-Great Depression
* **Status:** **NO Financial Emergency has been declared so far** (not even during 1991 financial crisis)

## CRITICISM OF THE EMERGENCY PROVISIONS
* **Grounds of Criticism:** Federal character destroyed, powers concentrated in Union executive, President could become dictator, state financial autonomy nullified, FRs rendered meaningless
* **Prominent Quotes:**
  * **H.V. Kamath:** *“Foundation of a totalitarian state... peace of the grave and the void of the desert... day of shame and sorrow”*
  * **K.T. Shah:** *“Chapter of reaction and retrogression... arm Centre against units and government against people”*
  * **T.T. Krishnamachari:** *“Form of constitutional dictatorship”*
  * **H.N. Kunzru:** *“Serious threat to financial autonomy of the States”*
* **Protagonists / Defenders:**
  * **Sir Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar:** *“The very life-breath of the Constitution”*
  * **Mahabir Tyagi:** Worked as a *“safety-valve”*
  * **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Acknowledged possibility of misuse, but hoped proper precautions taken
