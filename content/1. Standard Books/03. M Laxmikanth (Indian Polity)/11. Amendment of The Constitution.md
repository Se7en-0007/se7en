## INTRODUCTION
* **Nature:** Neither flexible (like UK) nor rigid (like USA) → **Synthesis of flexibility and rigidity**
* **Location:** **Part XX, Article 368** deals with Parliament's power to amend Constitution & its procedure
* **Scope:** Parliament may in exercise of **constituent power** amend by way of **addition, variation or repeal** any provision
* **Basic Structure Limit:** Parliament **cannot** amend provisions forming **‘basic structure’** of the Constitution (**Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973**)

---

## PROCEDURE FOR AMENDMENT
* **Procedure under Article 368:**
  1. **Initiation:** Introduced **only in either House of Parliament** (NOT in state legislatures)
  2. **Introducer:** By a **minister or private member** (does **not** require prior permission of President)
  3. **Majority:** Must be passed in each House by **special majority** (majority of total membership + 2/3 members present and voting)
  4. **Separate Passage:** Each House must pass bill separately → **NO provision for joint sitting** in case of deadlock
  5. **Federal Provisions:** Requires ratification by legislatures of **half of the states** by **simple majority** (members present and voting)
  6. **Assent:** Presented to President after passage/ratification
  7. **Obligatory Assent:** President **must give assent** (cannot withhold assent or return bill) ← **24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971**
  8. **Act:** Bill becomes Constitutional Amendment Act upon President's assent

## TYPES OF AMENDMENTS
* **Article 368:** Provides **2 types** of amendments (Special Majority; Special Majority + State Ratification)
* **Simple Majority:** Certain provisions amended by simple majority outside Article 368 (**not deemed amendments under Article 368**)
* **3 Ways to Amend:**

### 1. By Simple Majority of Parliament
* **Outside Article 368** (majority of members present and voting):
  1. **Admission or establishment of new states**
  2. **Formation of new states** and alteration of areas, boundaries, or names of existing states
  3. **Abolition or creation of legislative councils** in states
  4. **Second Schedule** (emoluments, allowances, privileges of President, Governors, Speakers, Judges, etc.)
  5. **Quorum in Parliament**
  6. **Salaries and allowances of Members of Parliament**
  7. **Rules of procedure in Parliament**
  8. **Privileges of Parliament**, its members, and its committees
  9. **Use of English language in Parliament**
  10. **Number of puisne judges in Supreme Court**
  11. **Conferment of more jurisdiction on Supreme Court**
  12. **Use of official language**
  13. **Citizenship** (acquisition and termination)
  14. **Elections to Parliament and state legislatures**
  15. **Delimitation of constituencies**
  16. **Union territories**
  17. **Fifth Schedule** (administration of scheduled areas and scheduled tribes)
  18. **Sixth Schedule** (administration of tribal areas)

### 2. By Special Majority of Parliament
* **Requirements:** Majority of total membership of each House + 2/3 members present and voting
* **‘Total membership’:** Total number of members comprising House (irrespective of vacancies/absentees)
* **Lok Sabha Rules:** Require special majority at **all effective stages** of the bill
* **Provisions Covered:**
  1. **Fundamental Rights**
  2. **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)**
  3. **All other provisions** not covered under 1st and 3rd categories

### 3. By Special Majority of Parliament and Consent of States
* **Federal Structure Provisions:** Special majority of Parliament + consent of **half of state legislatures** by **simple majority**
* **Time Limit:** **No time limit** prescribed for states to give consent
* **Provisions Covered:**
  1. **Election of President** and its manner
  2. **Extent of executive power** of Union and states
  3. **Supreme Court and High Courts**
  4. **Distribution of legislative powers** between Union and states
  5. **Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council** (Added by **101st Amendment Act, 2016** / **Article 279-A**)
  6. Any of the lists in **Seventh Schedule**
  7. **Representation of states in Parliament**
  8. **Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution** and its procedure (**Article 368 itself**)

## CRITICISM OF THE AMENDMENT PROCEDURE
1. **No Special Body:** Constituent power vested in Parliament; no provision for **Constitutional Convention** (like USA) or Constitutional Assembly
2. **State Initiation Barred:** State legislatures **cannot initiate** amendments (except resolution for creation/abolition of state legislative council)
3. **Parliamentary Dominance:** Major part amended by Parliament alone; state ratification required in few cases (only **1/2 states** vs **3/4 states in USA**)
4. **No Time Limit:** Silent on time frame for state ratification/rejection & on whether states can withdraw approval
5. **No Joint Sitting:** No provision for **joint sitting** to resolve deadlock over constitutional amendment bill
6. **Ordinary Legislative Similarity:** Amendment process resembles ordinary legislative process (except special majority)
7. **Sketchy Provisions:** Provisions too sketchy → wide scope for **judicial litigation**

---

## EVALUATION & SCHOLARLY VIEWS
* **K.C. Wheare:** Strikes a **good balance between flexibility and rigidity**; *"variety in the amending process is wise but rarely found"*
* **Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru:** Prevents stopping nation's growth → *"no permanence in a Constitution... certain flexibility needed for living, vital, organic people"*
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Refrained from seal of finality/infallibility; provided **facile procedure** for amending
