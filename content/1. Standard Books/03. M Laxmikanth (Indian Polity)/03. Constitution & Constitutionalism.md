## DEFINITIONS OF CONSTITUTION
* **Gilchrist** = Body of rules or laws determining government organization, power distribution and general principles of exercise
* **Gettell** = Fundamental principles determining state form (organization, distribution of sovereign powers, scope and manner of functions, government-people relation)
* **Wheare** = Describes the whole system of government (collection of rules establishing and governing government)
* **Wade and Phillips** = Document with special legal sanctity setting out framework, principal functions and operating principles of Government organs

---

## FUNCTIONS
* **Elliot Bulmer** → 8 core functions of a constitution:
  1. Declare and define boundaries of the political community
  2. Declare and define nature and authority of the political community
  3. Express national community identity and values
  4. Declare and define rights and duties of citizens
  5. Establish and regulate political institutions of the community
  6. Divide or share power between different layers of government or sub-state communities
  7. Declare official religious identity of state and demarcate sacred vs secular authorities
  8. Commit states to social, economic, or developmental goals

---

## QUALITIES
* **Good Constitution** → 7 essential qualities:
  1. **Brevity** = Precise and containing no unwanted provisions (lengthy → confusion in interpretation)
  2. **Clarity** = Provisions specified in clear terms (complicated language ↓ understanding)
  3. **Definiteness** = Definite meaning for provisions (ambiguity ↑ judicial discretion in interpretation)
  4. **Comprehensiveness** = Comprehensive in powers, rights and duties (reduces scope for controversy and litigation)
  5. **Suitability** = Reflects people's needs and aspirations; suitable to historical, socio-cultural, economic and political conditions
  6. **Stability** = Facilitates political stability; resists easy tampering (strengthens citizens' obedience)
  7. **Adaptability** = Dynamic and living document; adapts to changing situations and requirements

## CLASSIFICATION
### 1. Evolved and Enacted
* **Evolved (Cumulative)** = Outcome of slow, gradual evolutionary process (conventions, practices, principles and judicial decisions)
  * *Example:* **British Constitution**
* **Enacted (Conventional)** = Deliberately made by constituent assembly, constitutional council, king, or parliament (provisions in a book/document series)
  * *Examples:* **American and Indian Constitutions**

### 2. Written and Unwritten
* **Written (Documentary / Codified)** = Provisions incorporated in a book/document series; consciously formulated/adopted by a constituent assembly or convention
  * *Examples:* **USA, Canada, Japan, France, India**
* **Unwritten (Non-documentary / Uncodified)** = Most provisions not in a book/document series (conventions, practices, charters, statutes, judicial decisions); product of historical evolution
  * *Examples:* **UK, New Zealand, Israel**
* *Relationship:* Written/Unwritten distinction ≈ Evolved/Enacted distinction

### 3. Rigid and Flexible
* **Rigid (Inelastic)** = Cannot be amended like ordinary laws; requires a special procedure; distinguishes constitutional vs ordinary law
  * *Examples:* **USA, Australia, Switzerland**
* **Flexible (Elastic)** = Amended in the same manner as ordinary laws; no special procedure or distinction between constitutional and ordinary law
  * *Examples:* **UK, New Zealand**
* **Indian Constitution** = Neither rigid nor flexible; a **synthesis of both**
* *Classification Origin:* **Lord James Bryce**

### 4. Federal and Unitary
* **Federal** = Division of power between national and regional governments (operate in respective jurisdictions independently)
  * *Examples:* **USA, Switzerland, Australia, Canada, Russia, Brazil**
* **Unitary** = Concentration of power in national government; regional governments are subordinate agencies exercising only delegated powers
  * *Examples:* **UK, France, Japan, China, Italy, Norway**
* **Indian Constitution** = Contains both federal and unitary provisions
  * *Characterization:* **Quasi-federal** (by **K.C. Wheare**)

### 5. Procedural and Prescriptive
* **Procedural** = Defines legal and political structures of public institutions and sets legal limits on government power to protect democratic processes and fundamental human rights
* **Prescriptive** = Defines government functions + assumes/imposes a broad consensus on common societal goals to be achieved by public authorities
* *Classification Origin:* **Elliot Bulmer**

## CONSTITUTIONALISM AND CONSTITUTIONAL GOVERNMENT

### 1. Meaning
* **Constitution vs Constitutionalism**:
  * Country can have constitution but **not** constitutionalism (e.g., dictatorship)
  * Unlimited power jeopardizes freedom; absolute power corrupts → constitution must be permeated with constitutionalism
  * **Constitutionalism** = **Limited government** / limitation on government (antithesis of arbitrary power)
  * Decentralization of power + other restraints = Constitutionalism exists
  * Polity governed under a constitution ordaining limited government and **rule of law** vs arbitrary, despotic, authoritarian, or totalitarian rule
  * Constitutional government = Necessarily **democratic government**
  * Arbitrary power (even if constitutionally conferred) = Negation of constitutionalism
  * Justification of constitution = Restricting rulers to conform to laws and rules

### 2. Definition
* **Friedrich** = System of effective restraints upon governmental action; rules ensuring fairplay, rendering government responsible
* **Roucek** = Essentially limited government; antithesis of government by unrestrained will of rulers; assumes limitations on government
* **Wheare** = Government according to rule as opposed to arbitrary government; limited by terms of constitution, not by desires and capacities of rulers
* **Ybema** = Form of government where rulers are subject to a body of rules and principles limiting power; antithesis of arbitrary rule

### 3. Elements
* **Louis Henkin** → 8 elements/principles of constitutionalism:
  1. **Popular sovereignty**
  2. **Rule of law**
  3. **Democratic government** (responsible and accountable)
  4. **Separation of powers** (checks and balances)
  5. **Independent judiciary**
  6. **Civilian control of the military**
  7. **Police governed by law and judicial control**
  8. **Respect for individual rights**
