## INTRODUCTION
* **Federal Structure:** Constitution divides all powers (**legislative, executive, financial**) between Centre & States
* **Judicial Exception:** **Integrated judicial system** (no division of judicial power; single system enforces Central & State laws)
* **3 Dimensions of Relations:**
  1. Legislative relations
  2. Administrative relations
  3. Financial relations

---

## LEGISLATIVE RELATIONS
* **Location:** **Articles 245 to 255 in Part XI** + other articles
* **4 Aspects:** Territorial extent, distribution of subjects, parliamentary legislation in state field, Centre's control over state legislation

### 1. Territorial Extent of Central and State Legislation
* **Parliament:** Can make laws for whole or any part of territory of India (States, UTs, acquired areas) + **Extra-territorial legislation** (applies to Indian citizens & property anywhere in world)
* **State Legislature:** Laws apply within state territory; extra-territorial application valid only if **sufficient nexus** exists
* **Restrictions on Territorial Jurisdiction of Parliament:**
  1. **UTs:** **President** can make regulations for peace, progress & good government of Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu and Ladakh (Puducherry when Assembly suspended/dissolved) → Regulation can amend/repeal Parliamentary Acts
  2. **Scheduled Areas:** **Governor** can direct Parliamentary Act does not apply or applies with modifications/exceptions
  3. **Tribal Areas:** **Governor of Assam** (for Assam autonomous districts) & **President** (for Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram autonomous districts) can direct Parliamentary Act does not apply or applies with modifications/exceptions

### 2. Distribution of Legislative Subjects
* **Seventh Schedule (3 Lists):**
  1. **Union List (List-I):** **98 subjects** (originally 97) → Exclusive Parliamentary power (*defence, banking, foreign affairs, currency, atomic energy, insurance, communication, inter-state trade, census, audit*)
  2. **State List (List-II):** **59 subjects** (originally 66) → Exclusive State Legislature power under normal circumstances (*public order, police, public health, agriculture, prisons, local govt, fisheries, markets*)
  3. **Concurrent List (List-III):** **52 subjects** (originally 47) → Joint power of Parliament & State Legislatures (*criminal law, civil procedure, marriage/divorce, population control, electricity, labour welfare, drugs, newspapers*)
* **42nd Amendment Act (1976):** Transferred **5 subjects** from State List to Concurrent List:
  1. Education
  2. Forests
  3. Weights and measures
  4. Protection of wild animals and birds
  5. Administration of justice; constitution & organisation of all courts except SC & HCs
* **101st Amendment Act (2016) - GST Special Provision:** Parliament & State Legislatures have concurrent power to make laws on **GST**; Parliament has **exclusive power** over inter-state supply GST
* **UTs / Acquired Territories:** Parliament can make laws on **any matter** (including State List)
* **Residuary Powers:** Vested exclusively in **Parliament** (includes residuary taxes) ← Derived from **Canadian model** (vs US/Australia where residuary powers go to States)
* **Precedence Rule in Case of Overlapping/Conflict:**
  * **Union List > Concurrent List > State List**
  * **Concurrent List Conflict:** Central law prevails over State law
  * **Exception:** If State law reserved for President's consideration receives **President's assent**, State law prevails in that state (Parliament can still override it by subsequent legislation)

### 3. Parliamentary Legislation in The State Field
* **5 Extraordinary Circumstances:**
  1. **Rajya Sabha Resolution (Article 249):**
     * RS passes resolution by **2/3 members present and voting** in national interest
     * Authorises Parliament to make laws on State List / GST matter
     * Resolution valid for **1 year** (renewable indefinitely, 1 year at a time)
     * Law ceases to effect **6 months** after resolution expires
     * State can still make laws on same subject, but Parliamentary law prevails in case of inconsistency
  2. **During National Emergency (Article 250):**
     * Parliament acquires power to legislate on State List / GST
     * Law becomes inoperative **6 months** after emergency ceases
     * State can still legislate, but Parliamentary law prevails in case of repugnancy
  3. **States Request (Article 252):**
     * Legislatures of **2 or more states** pass resolutions requesting Parliament to enact law on State List
     * Law applies **only to requesting states** (other states can adopt by resolution)
     * State legislatures **surrender power** → Law can be amended/repealed **ONLY by Parliament**
     * *Examples enacted:* Prize Competition Act 1955, Wild Life (Protection) Act 1972, Water Pollution Act 1974, Urban Land Ceiling Act 1976, Transplantation of Human Organs Act 1994
  4. **To Implement International Agreements (Article 253):**
     * Parliament can enact State List laws to fulfill international treaties/conventions
     * *Examples enacted:* UN (Privileges & Immunities) Act 1947, Geneva Convention Act 1960, Anti-Hijacking Act 1982, Environment & TRIPS laws
  5. **During President’s Rule (Article 356):**
     * Parliament empowered to legislate for the state
     * Law **continues to be operative** post-President's Rule until repealed/altered by state legislature

### 4. Centre’s Control Over State Legislation
* **Governor's Reservation:** Governor can reserve state bill for President's consideration (President enjoys **absolute veto**; Articles 200 & 201)
* **Previous Sanction:** Bills restricting trade/commerce freedom require **previous sanction of President** for introduction in state legislature (Article 304)
* **Financial Emergency (Article 360):** Centre can direct states to reserve **Money Bills** and financial bills for President's consideration
* **Ordinances:** Governor needs **Presidential instructions** to promulgate ordinances in certain cases (Article 213)
* **Sarkaria Commission Observation:** Federal supremacy is indispensable to avoid legal chaos, conflict and stultification of two-tier polity

## ADMINISTRATIVE RELATIONS
* **Location:** **Articles 256 to 263 in Part XI** + other provisions

### 1. Distribution of Executive Powers
* Follows legislative distribution:
  * **Centre:** Matters in Union List + rights/authority under treaties/agreements
  * **State:** Matters in State List
  * **Concurrent List:** Executive power rests with **States** by default (unless Constitution or Parliamentary law explicitly confers it on Centre)

### 2. Obligation of States and the Centre
* **2 Constitutional Restrictions on State Executive Power:**
  1. Ensure compliance with Parliamentary laws & existing laws in state (**General obligation**)
  2. Not to impede or prejudice exercise of Centre's executive power in state (**Specific obligation**)
* **Coercive Sanction (Article 365):** Failure by a state to comply with Central directions → Lawful for President to hold that state government cannot be carried on per Constitution → **President’s Rule under Article 356**

### 3. Centre’s Directions to The States
* **4 Specified Subjects for Central Directions:**
  1. Construction & maintenance of means of communication of national/military importance
  2. Measures for protection of railways in state
  3. Facilities for mother-tongue instruction at primary stage for linguistic minorities
  4. Drawing up & execution of welfare schemes for Scheduled Tribes in state
* *Sanction:* Article 365 applies to non-compliance

### 4. Mutual Delegation of Functions
* **Inter-government delegation** mitigates rigid executive division:
  * **Executive Agreement (Consent-based):**
    * **President** (with state govt consent) entrusts Union executive functions to State
    * **Governor** (with Central govt consent) entrusts State executive functions to Union (*Added by 7th Amendment, 1956*)
  * **Parliamentary Legislation (Without State Consent):**
    * **Parliament** (via law on Union List subject) can confer powers/duties on state without state consent
    * *Note:* State Legislature **cannot** delegate functions to Centre without Centre's consent

### 5. Cooperation Between The Centre and States
* **Inter-State River Waters (Article 262):** Parliament provides adjudication for inter-state river water disputes
* **Inter-State Council (Article 263):** Established by President (set up **1990**) to investigate/discuss common interest subjects
* **Full Faith & Credit:** Given throughout India to public acts, records and judicial proceedings of Union and States
* **Inter-State Trade Authority (Article 307):** Parliament can appoint authority for inter-state trade freedom (not appointed yet)

### 6. All-India Services
* **Jointly Controlled:** **IAS, IPS, IFoS** (IFoS created **1966**; 3rd AIS)
  * Recruited & trained by Centre; serve Centre & States by turns
  * **Ultimate control** = Central Government; **Immediate control** = State Government
* **Creation Power (Article 312):** Parliament can create new All-India Services if **Rajya Sabha** passes resolution supported by **2/3 members present and voting**
* **Ambedkar's Defense:** Strategic posts in administrative set-up require high-calibre civil servants with common qualifications and uniform pay scales to maintain standards

### 7. Public Service Commissions
* **SPSC Chairman & Members:** Appointed by Governor, but removable **ONLY by President**
* **Joint State Public Service Commission (JSPSC):** Established by **Parliament** on request of concerned state legislatures; Chairman & members appointed by **President**
* **UPSC Role for States:** Serves state needs on Governor's request + President's approval; assists states in joint recruitment schemes

### 8. Integrated Judicial System
* Single hierarchical system (SC top, HCs below) enforces both Central & State laws
* **HC Judges:** Appointed by President (consultation with CJI & Governor); transferred and removed **ONLY by President**
* **Common High Court:** Established by **Parliament** for 2 or more states (e.g., Maharashtra & Goa; Punjab & Haryana)

### 9. Relations During Emergencies
* **National Emergency (Article 352):** Centre can give executive directions on **'any' matter**; state governments brought under complete Central control (not suspended)
* **President’s Rule (Article 356):** President assumes state government functions & Governor/executive powers
* **Financial Emergency (Article 360):** Centre directs state to observe canons of financial propriety & reduce salaries

### 10. Other Provisions
* **Article 355 (Two Duties of Centre):** (a) Protect states against external aggression and internal disturbance; (b) Ensure state government carried on per Constitution
* **Governor Role:** Appointed by President; holds office during President's pleasure; acts as agent of Centre
* **State Election Commissioner:** Appointed by Governor, but removable **ONLY by President**

### 11. Extra-Constitutional Devices
* **Non-Constitutional Advisory Bodies:** NITI Aayog, National Integration Council, Central Council of Health & Family Welfare, Central Council of Local Govt, Zonal Councils, North-Eastern Council, UGC, etc.
* **Conferences:** Governors' Conference (presided by President), CMs' Conference (presided by PM), Chief Secretaries' Conference (presided by Cabinet Secretary), DGPs/IGPs Conference, Chief Justices' Conference (presided by CJI)

## FINANCIAL RELATIONS
* **Location:** **Articles 268 to 293 in Part XII** + other provisions

### 1. Allocation of Taxing Powers
* **Union List:** Exclusive Parliamentary power (**13 tax entries**)
* **State List:** Exclusive State Legislature power (**18 tax entries**)
* **Concurrent List:** No general tax entries; **101st Amendment Act (2016)** inserted **Article 246A** granting concurrent power for **GST**
* **Residuary Taxing Power:** Vested in **Parliament** (*gift tax, wealth tax, expenditure tax*)
* **Restrictions on State Taxing Powers:**
  1. **Profession Tax:** Max limit **₹2,500 per annum** per person (*raised from ₹250 by 60th Amendment, 1988*)
  2. **Supply of Goods/Services:** State cannot tax supply taking place outside state or in course of import/export (Article 286)
  3. **Electricity Tax:** Cannot tax electricity consumed by / sold to Centre or Railways
  4. **Inter-State River Valley Authority:** State tax on river water/electricity requires Presidential assent

### 2. Distribution of Tax Revenues
* **80th Amendment Act (2000):** Implemented 10th Finance Commission recommendation → **29%** of central taxes/duties devolved to states (**'Alternative Scheme of Devolution'**, w.e.f. April 1, 1996)
* **101st Amendment Act (2016):** Introduced **GST**; subsumed central indirect taxes (excise, service tax, CST, surcharges) and state indirect taxes (VAT, entry tax, luxury tax, entertainment tax); deleted Art 268A (service tax)
* **Present Revenue Distribution Categories:**
  1. **Taxes Levied by Centre but Collected & Appropriated by States (Article 268):** Stamp duties on bills of exchange, cheques, transfer of shares → Assigned to states (not in Consolidated Fund of India)
  2. **Taxes Levied & Collected by Centre but Assigned to States (Article 269):** Taxes on inter-state sale/purchase & consignment of goods
  3. **Levy & Collection of GST in Inter-State Trade/Commerce (Article 269A):** IGST levied & collected by Centre; divided between Centre & States per Parliament law on GST Council recommendations
  4. **Taxes Levied & Collected by Centre but Distributed between Centre & States (Article 270):** All Union List taxes/duties EXCEPT Art 268, 269, 269A, Art 271 surcharges and specific cesses → Distributed per Finance Commission recommendations
  5. **Surcharge on Taxes for Centre Purposes (Article 271):** Parliament can levy surcharges on Art 269 & 270 taxes → Exclusively for Centre (**GST exempted** from surcharge)
  6. **Taxes Levied, Collected & Retained by States:** **18 subjects** in State List (*land revenue, agricultural income tax, state excise on alcohol/narcotics, stamp duty, electricity tax, vehicle tax*)

### 3. Distribution of Non-tax Revenues
* **Centre:** Posts & telegraphs, railways, banking, broadcasting, coinage/currency, CPSEs, escheat & lapse
* **States:** Irrigation, forests, fisheries, SPSEs, escheat & lapse

### 4. Grants-in-Aid to the States
* **Statutory Grants (Article 275):** Parliament makes grants to states in financial need; charged on Consolidated Fund of India; recommended by **Finance Commission** (includes specific grants for ST welfare/scheduled areas)
* **Discretionary Grants (Article 282):** Centre & States can make grants for any public purpose; Centre uses discretionary grants to help states fulfill plan targets
* **Temporary Grants (Export duty on jute):** Provided for 10 years post-1950 to Assam, Bihar, Orissa, West Bengal

### 5. Goods and Services Tax Council
* **Article 279A:** **101st Amendment (2016)** empowered President to constitute GST Council (joint forum of Centre & States)
* **Role:** Recommends GST rates, exemptions, model laws, threshold limits, inter-state apportionment, disaster special rates

### 6. Finance Commission
* **Article 280:** Quasi-judicial body constituted by President **every 5th year** (or earlier)
* **Role:** **Balancing wheel of fiscal federalism**; recommends tax distribution, principles governing statutory grants (Art 275), augmenting State Consolidated Fund for Panchayats/Municipalities (*73rd/74th Amendments, 1992*)

### 7. Protection of The States’ Interest
* **Prior Presidential Recommendation Required for Bills:**
  1. Imposing/varying tax/duty in which states are interested
  2. Varying 'agricultural income' definition
  3. Affecting principles of money distribution to states
  4. Imposing Central surcharges on specified taxes
* **CAG Certification:** Net proceeds certified by **CAG** (certificate is final)

### 8. Borrowing by The Centre and The States
* **Centre:** Can borrow within India or abroad upon security of Consolidated Fund of India (within Parliamentary limits)
* **States:** Can borrow **ONLY within India** upon security of Consolidated Fund of State (within State Legislature limits)
* **Central Loans/Guarantees:** Centre can make loans to states (charged on Consolidated Fund of India)
* **Central Consent Requirement:** State **cannot raise loan without Central consent** if any part of Central loan/guarantee remains outstanding

### 9. Inter-Governmental Tax Immunities
* **Exemption of Central Property from State Tax:** Property of Centre exempt from all state/local taxes (unless Parliament permits); SC/commercial property included; Central corporations/companies **NOT immune** (separate legal entities)
* **Exemption of State Property/Income from Central Tax:** State property & income exempt from Central tax; Parliament can tax state commercial operations; local authorities & state corporations/companies **NOT immune**
* **Customs/Excise Duty (*Sea Customs Case, 1963*):** SC ruled immunity does NOT extend to customs or excise duties (Centre can levy on state goods)

### 10. Effects of Emergencies
* **National Emergency (Article 352):** President can modify/cancel distribution of revenues (tax sharing & grants-in-aid) until end of financial year
* **Financial Emergency (Article 360):** Centre can direct states to observe financial propriety, reduce salaries, reserve money/financial bills for President

## TRENDS IN CENTRE–STATE RELATIONS
* **Pre-1967:** Smooth relations due to one-party rule (Congress) at Centre & States
* **Post-1967:** Congress defeated in 9 states → Non-Congress state governments demanded state autonomy & financial resources → Tensions & conflicts arose
* **14 Key Tension Areas:**
  1. Appointment & dismissal of Governor
  2. Discriminatory/partisan role of Governors
  3. Imposition of President's Rule for partisan interests
  4. Deployment of Central forces in states
  5. Reservation of state bills for President
  6. Discrimination in financial allocations
  7. Role of Planning Commission (till 2015 NITI Aayog)
  8. Management of All-India Services
  9. Use of electronic media for political purposes
  10. Enquiry commissions against Chief Ministers
  11. Sharing of finances
  12. Central encroachment on State List
  13. Implementation of Centrally sponsored schemes
  14. Modus operandi of Central agencies (CBI, ED)

### Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC)
* **1966 Setup:** 1st ARC under **Morarji Desai** (later **K. Hanumanthayya**); M.C. Setalvad study team
* **1969 Report:** 22 recommendations (Establish Inter-State Council under Art 263, non-partisan experienced Governors, max delegation to states, transfer more financial resources, deployment of Central forces on request/otherwise) → **No action taken** by Centre

### Rajamannar Committee
* **1969 Setup:** DMK Tamil Nadu Govt appointed 3-member committee under **Dr. P.V. Rajamannar** (Members: Dr. Lakshmanswamy Mudaliar, P.C. Chandra Reddy)
* **1971 Report Recommendations:**
  1. Establish Inter-State Council immediately
  2. Make Finance Commission a permanent body
  3. Disband Planning Commission (replace with statutory body)
  4. **Totally omit Articles 356, 357, 365** (President's Rule)
  5. Omit 'pleasure of governor' provision for state ministry
  6. Transfer Union/Concurrent subjects to State List; residuary powers to States
  7. **Abolish All-India Services** (IAS, IPS, IFoS)
* *Outcome:* Central Govt completely ignored recommendations

### Anandpur Sahib Resolution
* **1973:** Adopted by **Akali Dal** at Anandpur Sahib (Punjab)
* **Demands:** Restrict Central jurisdiction to **defence, foreign affairs, communications and currency**; vest all residuary powers in States; real federal structure

### West Bengal Memorandum
* **1977:** Published by West Bengal Communist Govt
* **Demands:** Replace 'Union' with **'Federal'**; restrict Centre to defence, foreign affairs, currency, communications, economic co-ordination; repeal Articles 356, 357, 360; 75% central revenue to states; equal powers for Rajya Sabha; **abolish All-India Services** → Rejected by Centre

### Sarkaria Commission
* **1983 Setup:** 3-member Commission under **R.S. Sarkaria** (retired SC Judge; Members: **B. Sivaraman**, **S.R. Sen**)
* **1988 Report:** Rejected curtailing Central powers; emphasized **functional changes** over structural changes; over-centralisation leads to *"blood pressure at the centre and anaemia at the periphery"*
* **Key Recommendations (247 total):**
  1. Permanent Inter-State Council (Inter-Governmental Council) under Article 263
  2. **Article 356 used sparingly as last resort**
  3. Strengthen & create more All-India Services
  4. Residuary taxation powers with Parliament; other residuary powers to Concurrent List
  5. Communicate reasons when President withhold assent to state bills
  6. Consult CM before appointing Governor; Governor's 5-year tenure undisturbed except compelling reasons
  7. Governor cannot dismiss CM enjoying majority in Assembly
  8. Corporation tax net proceeds permissibly shareable with states
  9. Deploy armed forces without state consent (consultation desirable)
* *Implementation:* Central Govt implemented **180 recommendations** (including establishing **Inter-State Council in 1990**)

### Punchhi Commission
* **2007 Setup:** 2nd Commission on Centre-State Relations under **M.M. Punchhi** (former CJI; Members: Dhirendra Singh, V.K. Duggal, N.R. Madhava Menon, Amaresh Bagchi / Vijay Shanker)
* **2010 Report (7 Volumes, 1,456 pages, 310+ recommendations):** Key is **‘Cooperative Federalism’**
* **Key Recommendations:**
  1. Broad agreement with states before legislating on List III (Concurrent List) subjects
  2. Decision within **6 months** for President on reserved state bills (Art 201) & Governor on state bills
  3. Strict Governor selection criteria (outside state, eminent, detached, non-politician in recent past); **5-year fixed tenure**; **impeachment procedure for Governors** (similar to President)
  4. Omit convention of Governors acting as University Chancellors
  5. Incorporate ***S.R. Bommai (1994)*** guidelines into Art 356; introduce **‘Localised Emergency’** framework under Art 355 to avoid full Art 356/352 invocation
  6. **Equality of representation in Rajya Sabha** for all states (irrespective of population size)
  7. Create new All-India Services in **health, education, engineering and judiciary**
  8. Revise mineral royalty rates every 3 years; remove ceiling on profession tax
  9. Set up **Inter-State Trade and Commerce Commission** under Article 307
* *Status:* Examined by Standing Committee of Inter-State Council (2017–2018); circulated to state governments

* **Article 247:** Power of Parliament to provide for the establishment of certain additional courts
* **Article 248:** Residuary powers of legislation
* **Article 249:** Power of Parliament to legislate with respect to a matter in the State List in the national interest
* **Article 250:** Power of Parliament to legislate with respect to any matter in the State List if a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation
