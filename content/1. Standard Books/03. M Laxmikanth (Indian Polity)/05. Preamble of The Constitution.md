## INTRODUCTION
* **Origin:** Based on **‘Objectives Resolution’** (drafted & moved by **Pandit Nehru** on Dec 13, 1946; adopted by Constituent Assembly on Jan 22, 1947)
* **First practice:** **American Constitution** was 1st to begin with a Preamble
* **Concept:** Preamble = Introduction / preface / summary / essence of Constitution
* **N.A. Palkhivala** (jurist) = Called Preamble the **‘identity card of the Constitution’**
* **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976):** Added 3 new words → **Socialist**, **Secular**, and **Integrity**

---

## TEXT OF THE PREAMBLE
* **Exact Wording:**
  > “We, **THE PEOPLE OF INDIA**, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC** and to secure to all its citizens:
  > **JUSTICE**, Social, Economic and Political;
  > **LIBERTY** of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
  > **EQUALITY** of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all;
  > **FRATERNITY** assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;
  > IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949**, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.”

---

## INGREDIENTS OF THE PREAMBLE
1. **Source of authority:** Derived from **The People of India**
2. **Nature of Indian State:** **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republican** polity
3. **Objectives of Constitution:** **Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity**
4. **Date of adoption:** **November 26, 1949**

---

## KEY WORDS IN THE PREAMBLE

### 1. Sovereign
* **Meaning:** Independent state (neither dependency nor dominion of any nation) ← Free to conduct internal & external affairs
  * *15 Aug 1947 – 26 Jan 1950:* Dominion status
  * *26 Jan 1950 onwards:* Sovereign Republic
* **External associations:**
  * **Commonwealth Membership (1949):** Accepts British Crown as head of Commonwealth → Extra-constitutional declaration; does **not** affect sovereignty
  * **UN Membership (1945):** Does **not** limit sovereignty
* **Territorial Power:** Being sovereign, India can **acquire foreign territory** or **cede part of territory** to a foreign state

### 2. Socialist
* **Addition:** Added by **42nd Amendment Act (1976)** (content previously implicit in **DPSPs**)
* **Historical precursor:** **Avadi Session of Congress (1955)** → Resolution passed to establish ‘socialistic pattern of society’
* **Brand:** **Democratic Socialism** (mixed economy where public & private sectors coexist) vs **Communistic/State Socialism** (nationalisation of all means & abolition of private property)
* **SC View (*G.B. Pant University case, 2000*):** Aims to end poverty, ignorance, disease & inequality of opportunity
* **Ideological Blend (*Nakara case, 1982*):** Blend of **Marxism and Gandhism**, leaning heavily towards **Gandhian socialism**
* **1991 New Economic Policy (LPG):** Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation → diluted socialist credentials

### 3. Secular
* **Addition:** Added by **42nd Amendment Act (1976)** (concept previously implicit in **Articles 25–28**; SC 1974)
* **State Types based on Religion:**
  * **Atheistic:** Anti-religion (condemns all religions)
  * **Theocratic:** Pro-religion (e.g., Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Myanmar)
  * **Secular:** Neutral to religion (e.g., India, USA)
* **Indian Concept:** **Positive Secularism** (all religions have equal status and support from state, irrespective of strength)

### 4. Democratic
* **Doctrine:** Based on **popular sovereignty** (possession of supreme power by the people)
* **Types of Democracy:**
  * **Direct:** People exercise supreme power directly (e.g., Switzerland)
    * **4 Devices:** **Referendum**, **Initiative**, **Recall**, **Plebiscite**
  * **Indirect (Representative):** Elected reps exercise power; 2 types: **Parliamentary** & **Presidential**
* **Indian Features:** Representative Parliamentary democracy (Executive responsible to Legislature)
  * *Manifestations:* Universal adult franchise, periodic elections, rule of law, independent judiciary, non-discrimination
* **Broader Scope:** Covers political, social, and economic democracy
  * **Dr. Ambedkar Speech (Nov 25, 1949):** Social democracy = liberty, equality, fraternity (**Union of Trinity**; cannot divorce one from another)
  * **SC (1997):** Envisions egalitarian social order guaranteeing social, economic, political justice

### 5. Republic
* **Classification:** **Monarchy** (hereditary head, e.g., Britain) vs **Republic** (elected head, e.g., USA, India)
* **Indian Head:** **President** elected indirectly for a fixed 5-year period
* **Additional Meanings:**
  1. Political sovereignty vested in the **people** (not a single individual/king)
  2. **Absence of privileged class** → All public offices open to every citizen without discrimination

### 6. Justice
* **3 Distinct Forms:** **Social, Economic, Political** (secured via **FRs & DPSPs**)
* **Social Justice:** Equal treatment without social distinction (caste, race, religion, sex); absence of privileges; improvement of backward classes (SCs, STs, OBCs) & women
* **Economic Justice:** Non-discrimination on economic factors; elimination of wealth, income & property inequalities
* **Distributive Justice:** **Social Justice + Economic Justice**
* **Political Justice:** Equal political rights, equal access to political offices, equal voice in government
* **Source:** Inspired by the **Russian Revolution (1917)**

### 7. Liberty
* **Meaning:** Absence of restraints on individual activities + providing opportunities for personality development
* **Secured:** Liberty of **thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship** (enforceable via **FRs**)
* **Nature:** **Not absolute, but qualified** (enjoyed within constitutional limitations; not license to do anything)
* **Source:** Inspired by the **French Revolution (1789–1799)**

### 8. Equality
* **Meaning:** Absence of special privileges to any section + adequate opportunities for all without discrimination
* **3 Dimensions:** **Civic, Political, Economic**
* **Civic Equality (Fundamental Rights):**
  * **Article 14:** Equality before law
  * **Article 15:** Prohibition of discrimination (religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth)
  * **Article 16:** Equality of opportunity in public employment
  * **Article 17:** Abolition of untouchability
  * **Article 18:** Abolition of titles
* **Political Equality:**
  * **Article 325:** No person ineligible for electoral rolls on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex
  * **Article 326:** Elections on basis of adult suffrage
* **Economic Equality (DPSP):**
  * **Article 39:** Equal right to adequate livelihood & equal pay for equal work

### 9. Fraternity
* **Meaning:** Sense of brotherhood
* **Promotional Mechanisms:** **Single citizenship** + **Fundamental Duties (Article 51-A)**
* **Dual Guarantee:**
  1. **Dignity of the individual**
  2. **Unity and integrity of the Nation** ('integrity' added by **42nd Amendment, 1976**)
* **Dignity of Individual (K.M. Munshi):** Recognises individual personality as sacred; ensured via FRs, DPSPs, and FDs (duty to renounce practices derogatory to women)
* **Unity & Integrity:** Psychological & territorial dimensions; **Article 1** ("Union of States" → no right to secede)

## SIGNIFICANCE OF THE PREAMBLE
* Embodies basic philosophy, fundamental values (political, moral, religious), and noble vision of founding fathers
* **Sir Alladi Krishnaswami Iyer:** *“Expresses what we had thought or dreamt so long”*
* **K.M. Munshi:** *“Horoscope of our sovereign democratic republic”*
* **Pandit Thakur Das Bhargava:** *“Most precious part of the Constitution; soul of the Constitution; key to the Constitution; jewel set in the Constitution; proper yardstick to measure the worth of the Constitution”*
* **Sir Ernest Barker:** *“Key-note”* to the Constitution; quoted Preamble in opening of book *Principles of Social and Political Theory (1951)*
* **M. Hidayatullah (former CJI):** Resembles US Declaration of Independence, but is more; *“soul of our Constitution... pattern of our political society”*

## PREAMBLE AS PART OF THE CONSTITUTION
* **Berubari Union Case (1960):** SC acknowledged Preamble as key to mind of makers, BUT held **Preamble is NOT a part of the Constitution**
* **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973):** SC rejected earlier view; held **Preamble IS a part of the Constitution**
* **LIC of India Case (1995):** SC reaffirmed **Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution**
* **Enactment History:** Enacted by Constituent Assembly **after** the rest of the Constitution was enacted (to ensure conformity)
* **2 Key Principles:**
  1. Preamble is **neither a source of power** to legislature **nor a prohibition** upon legislative powers
  2. Preamble is **non-justiciable** (provisions non-enforceable in courts of law)

---

## AMENDABILITY OF THE PREAMBLE
* **First raised:** **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)**
* **SC Ruling:** Preamble **can be amended** under Article 368, provided the **'basic features' / basic structure** are not altered/destroyed
* **Amendment History:** Amended **only once** so far by **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976)**
  * **3 New Words Added:** **Socialist**, **Secular**, and **Integrity** (held constitutionally valid)
