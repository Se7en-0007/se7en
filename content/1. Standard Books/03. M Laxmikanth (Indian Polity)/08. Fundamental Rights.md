## INTRODUCTION
* **Location:** Enshrined in **Part III** of the Constitution (**Articles 12 to 35**)
* **Inspiration:** Framers derived inspiration from **USA Constitution (Bill of Rights)**
* **Description:** **Part III** = **‘Magna Carta of India’** (contains comprehensive justiciable FRs list)
  * *Origin of Magna Carta:* Charter of Rights issued by **King John of England** in **1215** (1st written document on FRs)
* **Objective:** Promotes ideal of **political democracy**; prevents authoritarian/despotic rule; operates as limitation on executive tyranny & arbitrary legislative laws
* **Aim:** Establishing **‘a government of laws and not of men’**
* **Name Reason:** ‘Fundamental’ because guaranteed & protected by Constitution (fundamental law of land) + essential for all-round development (material, intellectual, moral, spiritual)
* **Original 7 FRs:**
  1. Right to equality (Articles 14–18)
  2. Right to freedom (Articles 19–22)
  3. Right against exploitation (Articles 23–24)
  4. Right to freedom of religion (Articles 25–28)
  5. Cultural and educational rights (Articles 29–30)
  6. Right to property (Article 31)
  7. Right to constitutional remedies (Article 32)
* **Deletion of Right to Property:** Deleted from FR list by **44th Amendment Act, 1978** → Made a legal right under **Article 300-A** (Part XII) → Present total = **6 Fundamental Rights**

---

## FEATURES OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS
1. **Scope of Enjoyment:** Some available only to citizens (Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, 30); others available to all persons (citizens, foreigners, or legal entities) except enemy aliens
2. **Not Absolute:** Qualified in nature → State can impose **reasonable restrictions** (decided by courts) → Balance between individual liberty & social control
3. **Against Whom:** Available against arbitrary state action; some also available against private individuals
4. **Nature:** Negative (limitations on State authority) or Positive (conferring privileges on persons)
5. **Justiciable:** Enforceable by courts upon violation
6. **Guaranteed by Supreme Court:** Aggrieved person can directly approach SC (not necessarily by appeal)
7. **Not Sacrosanct/Permanent:** Parliament can curtail/repeal via **Constitutional Amendment Act** (not ordinary act) without altering **'Basic Structure'**
8. **Suspension:** Suspended during **National Emergency** EXCEPT **Articles 20 and 21**
   * *Article 19 six rights:* Automatically suspended ONLY on grounds of **war or external aggression** (external emergency), NOT **armed rebellion** (internal emergency)
9. **Limited Scope:** Scope restricted by **Article 31A** (acquisition of estates), **Article 31B** (9th Schedule validation), and **Article 31C** (saving DPSPs)
10. **Armed Forces Abrogation:** Application to armed forces, police, intelligence agencies can be restricted/abrogated by Parliament (**Article 33**)
11. **Martial Law Restriction:** Restricted during martial law / military rule in any area (**Article 34**)
12. **Enforcement Legislation:** Most directly enforceable (self-executory); others enforced by Parliamentary law (**Article 35**; State Legislatures cannot legislate) to maintain uniformity

---

## DEFINITION OF STATE (ARTICLE 12)
* **Scope:** Defines 'State' for Part III purposes; includes:
  1. Executive and Legislative organs of Union Government (Govt & Parliament)
  2. Executive and Legislative organs of State Governments (Govt & Legislature)
  3. All local authorities (Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards, Improvement Trusts)
  4. All other statutory or non-statutory authorities (LIC, ONGC, SAIL, etc.)
* **Judicial Expansion:** Private bodies/agencies working as instrumentalities of the State fall within 'State' under Article 12

---

## LAWS INCONSISTENT WITH FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLE 13)
* **Core Rule:** All laws inconsistent with or in derogation of FRs shall be void
* **Express Provision:** Formally establishes **Doctrine of Judicial Review** (conferred on SC under **Article 32** & HCs under **Article 226**)
* **Connotation of 'Law':**
  1. Permanent laws (Parliament / State Legislatures)
  2. Temporary laws (Ordinances by President / Governors)
  3. Statutory instruments / delegated legislation (orders, bye-laws, rules, regulations, notifications)
  4. Non-legislative sources (customs or usages having force of law)
* **Constitutional Amendments:** Article 13 states constitutional amendment is not a law, BUT **Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)** ruled an amendment can be challenged/declared void if it violates FRs forming part of **'Basic Structure'**

## RIGHT TO EQUALITY (ARTICLES 14–18)

### 1. Equality Before Law and Equal Protection of Laws (Article 14)
* **Text:** State shall not deny equality before law or equal protection of laws within territory of India (applies to citizens, foreigners, and legal persons)
* **Equality Before Law:**
  * **Origin:** British origin
  * **Negative concept:** (a) Absence of special privileges, (b) Equal subjection of all to ordinary law courts, (c) No person above law
* **Equal Protection of Laws:**
  * **Origin:** American Constitution
  * **Positive concept:** (a) Equal treatment under equal circumstances, (b) Similar application of same laws to similarly situated persons, (c) Like should be treated alike
* **Permissible Classification:** Forbids class legislation, but permits **reasonable classification** based on intelligible differentia & substantial distinction

#### Rule of Law
* **Propounded by:** **A.V. Dicey** (British jurist)
* **3 Elements:**
  1. Absence of arbitrary power (no punishment except for breach of law)
  2. Equality before law (equal subjection to ordinary law courts)
  3. Primacy of individual rights (constitution is result of individual rights defined/enforced by courts)
* **Indian Adaptation:** 1st and 2nd elements apply in India, 3rd does not (Constitution is the source of individual rights in India)
* **Status:** Rule of Law is a **'Basic Feature'** of the Constitution (cannot be destroyed by amendment)

#### Exceptions to Equality
* **Article 361 (President & Governors Immunities):**
  1. Not answerable to any court for official acts
  2. No criminal proceedings instituted/continued during term
  3. No arrest/imprisonment process issued during term
  4. Civil proceedings require **2 months' prior written notice** for personal acts
* **Article 361-A:** Protection for true publication of Parliament / State Legislature proceedings in media
* **Article 105:** MPs immune from court proceedings for statements/votes in Parliament/committees
* **Article 194:** MLAs/MLCs immune from court proceedings for statements/votes in State Legislature/committees
* **Article 31C:** Exception to Art 14 → Laws giving effect to **Article 39(b) or (c)** DPSPs cannot be challenged on Art 14 violation (*"Where Article 31C comes in, Article 14 goes out"*)
* **Diplomatic Immunity:** Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors, diplomats, UNO and its agencies immune from civil/criminal proceedings

### 2. Prohibition of Discrimination on Certain Grounds (Article 15)
* **Article 15(1):** State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds **ONLY** of **religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth** (or any of them)
* **Article 15(2):** No citizen subject to disability/restriction regarding access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, places of public entertainment, or use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, places of public resort (prohibits discrimination by **both State & private individuals**)
* **4 Exceptions to Non-discrimination:**
  1. **Women & Children (15(3)):** Special provisions allowed (e.g., seat reservation in local bodies, free education)
  2. **Socially & Educationally Backward Classes / SCs / STs (15(4)):** Special provisions allowed for advancement (Added by **1st Amendment Act, 1951**)
  3. **Educational Admission Reservation (15(5)):** Reservation in educational institutions (public & private, aided or unaided, EXCEPT minority institutions) for SEBCs/SCs/STs (Added by **93rd Amendment Act, 2005**)
  4. **EWS Reservation (15(6)):** Up to **10% reservation** in educational admissions (including private, excluding minority) for Economically Weaker Sections (Added by **103rd Amendment Act, 2019**)

#### Reservation for OBCs in Educational Institutions
* **Enactment:** **Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006** → **27% quota** for OBCs in central higher educational institutions (IITs, IIMs)
* **SC Ruling (2008):** Upheld 93rd Amendment & OBC Quota Act, but directed exclusion of **‘Creamy Layer’**
* **Creamy Layer Exclusions (OBC):**
  1. Constitutional posts (President, VP, SC/HC Judges, UPSC, CEC, CAG)
  2. Group A / Class I & Group B / Class II Officers (Services, PSUs, Banks, Private)
  3. Colonel rank and above in Armed Forces / Paramilitary
  4. Professionals (Doctors, Lawyers, Engineers, Artists) & Businessmen/Traders
  5. Agricultural landholders above specified limit & urban vacant land owners
  6. Gross annual income > **₹8 lakh** (revised: ₹1L in 1993 → ₹2.5L in 2004 → ₹4.5L in 2008 → ₹6L in 2013 → ₹8L in 2017)

#### Reservation for EWSs in Educational Institutions
* **Order (2019):** **10% reservation** for EWSs (not covered under SC/ST/OBC quotas)
* **Eligibility Criteria:**
  * Gross family annual income below **₹8 lakh**
* **Exclusions (Regardless of income):**
  1. 5 acres agricultural land and above
  2. Residential flat of 1000 sq. ft. and above
  3. Residential plot of 100 sq. yards and above in notified municipalities
  4. Residential plot of 200 sq. yards and above in non-notified areas
  * *Family definition:* Person, parents, spouse, siblings < 18 yrs, children < 18 yrs

### 3. Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment (Article 16)
* **Article 16(1) & 16(2):** Equality of opportunity in public employment; no discrimination/ineligibility based **ONLY** on **religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence**
* **4 Exceptions:**
  1. **Residence Requirement (16(3)):** Parliament can prescribe residence requirement (*Public Employment Act 1957* expired 1974; currently active only in **Andhra Pradesh & Telangana** under Art 371D)
  2. **Backward Class Reservation (16(4)):** Reservation in appointments for inadequately represented backward classes
  3. **Religious Office Incumbent (16(5)):** Incumbent/governing body member of denominational institution can be required to belong to that religion
  4. **EWS Reservation (16(6)):** Up to **10% reservation** in public employment for EWS (Added by **103rd Amendment Act, 2019**)

#### Mandal Commission and Aftermath
* **1979:** **Second Backward Classes Commission** appointed under **B.P. Mandal** (under Article 340) by Morarji Desai Govt
* **1980 Report:** Identified **3,743 castes** as SEBCs (~52% population); recommended **27% job reservation** for OBCs
* **1990:** **V.P. Singh Govt** declared 27% job reservation for OBCs
* **1991:** **Narasimha Rao Govt** introduced 2 changes: (a) economic preference within OBC 27%, (b) 10% quota for other economically backward sections
* **Indra Sawhney Case / Mandal Case (1992) SC Rulings:**
  * Upheld **27% OBC reservation**; rejected 10% extra economic reservation
  * **Creamy layer** must be excluded from OBC quota
  * **No reservation in promotions** (confined to initial appointments; existing allowed for 5 years till 1997)
  * Total reserved quota **must not exceed 50%**
  * **Carry forward rule** for backlog vacancies valid (subject to 50% rule)
  * Statutory body needed for OBC inclusion/exclusion complaints
* **Government Follow-up Actions:**
  * **Ram Nandan Committee (1993):** Identified creamy layer
  * **NCBC (1993):** Statutory body created; granted **constitutional status** by **102nd Amendment Act, 2018** (**Article 338-B**)
  * **77th Amendment Act (1995):** Added **Article 16(4A)** → Allowed reservation in promotion for SCs/STs
  * **85th Amendment Act (2001):** Provided **consequential seniority** for promoted SC/ST candidates retrospectively from June 1995
  * **81st Amendment Act (2000):** Added **Article 16(4B)** → Ended 50% ceiling on **backlog vacancies** (treated as separate class)
  * **76th Amendment Act (1994):** Placed Tamil Nadu Reservation Act (69% quota) in **9th Schedule**
  * **82nd Amendment Act (2000):** Amended **Article 335** → Allowed relaxation in qualifying marks / evaluation standards for SCs/STs in promotions

### 4. Abolition of Untouchability (Article 17)
* **Core Provision:** Abolishes 'untouchability' & forbids practice in any form; enforcement of disability = punishable offence
* **Act:** **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** (originally *Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955* amended & renamed in 1976)
* **Definition:** 'Untouchability' **not defined** in Constitution or Act
  * *Mysore High Court Ruling:* Refers to social disabilities historically imposed on certain classes by birth in specific castes (does not cover social boycott)
* **Nature:** Direct right available against **private individuals**; State has constitutional obligation to enforce (*PUDR case, 1982*)

### 5. Abolition of Titles (Article 18)
* **Prohibitions:**
  1. State prohibited from conferring titles (except military or academic distinctions) on citizen or foreigner
  2. Citizen prohibited from accepting title from foreign state
  3. Foreigner holding office of profit under State cannot accept foreign title without President's consent
  4. No citizen/foreigner holding office of profit can accept present/emolument/office from foreign state without President's consent
* **Banned Titles:** Hereditary nobility titles (Maharaja, Raj Bahadur, Rai Saheb, Dewan Bahadur)
* **National Awards (Balaji Raghavan case, 1995):**
  * SC upheld validity of **Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri**
  * Ruled awards are **decorations, not 'titles'**; do not violate equality
  * **Constraint:** Cannot be used as prefixes/suffixes to names (violators forfeit awards)
  * *History:* Instituted 1954; discontinued by Janata Govt 1977; revived by Indira Gandhi Govt 1980
  * *Limits:* Max **3 Bharat Ratna** & **120 Padma Awards** per year

## RIGHT TO FREEDOM (ARTICLES 19–22)

### 1. Protection of Six Rights (Article 19)
* **Guaranteed to Citizens Only:** Protects 6 freedoms against State action (not private individuals):
  1. **19(1)(a):** Freedom of speech and expression
  2. **19(1)(b):** Freedom to assemble peaceably and without arms
  3. **19(1)(c):** Freedom to form associations, unions, or co-operative societies (*Co-operatives added by 97th Amendment, 2011*)
  4. **19(1)(d):** Freedom to move freely throughout territory of India
  5. **19(1)(e):** Freedom to reside and settle in any part of territory of India
  6. **19(1)(g):** Freedom to practice any profession, or carry on any occupation, trade or business
  * *Note:* Original 19(1)(f) (right to acquire, hold, dispose of property) **omitted by 44th Amendment Act, 1978**

#### Freedom of Speech and Expression (19(1)(a))
* **Implied Rights (SC Rulings):** Press freedom, commercial ads, right against phone tapping, right to telecast, right against bandh, right to know govt activities, freedom of silence, right against pre-censorship, right to demonstrate (no right to strike), fly national flag, voters' right to know antecedents, express gender identity, access internet (*Anuradha Bhasin case, 2020*)
* **Reasonable Restrictions (19(2)):** Sovereignty & integrity of India, security of State, friendly relations with foreign states, public order, decency/morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence

#### Freedom of Assembly (19(1)(b))
* **Scope:** Peaceful & unarmed assembly on public land; no right to strike
* **Restrictions (19(3)):** Sovereignty & integrity of India, public order
* **Legal Tools:** **Section 144 CrPC** (magistrate order to prevent danger/riot) & **Section 141 IPC** (unlawful assembly of 5+ persons)

#### Freedom of Association (19(1)(c))
* **Scope:** Form political parties, companies, societies, clubs, trade unions; covers negative right NOT to join; no guaranteed right to strike/lockout
* **Restrictions (19(4)):** Sovereignty & integrity of India, public order, morality

#### Freedom of Movement (19(1)(d))
* **Scope:** Internal movement inside India (External movement / travel abroad protected under **Article 21**)
* **Restrictions (19(5)):** General public interest & **protection of Scheduled Tribes** (protect culture, language, property)

#### Freedom of Residence (19(1)(e))
* **Scope:** Temporary stay or permanent settlement anywhere in India
* **Restrictions (19(5)):** General public interest & **protection of Scheduled Tribes**

#### Freedom of Profession, etc. (19(1)(g))
* **Scope:** Livelihood means; excludes immoral/dangerous trades (trafficking, harmful drugs, explosives)
* **State Powers (19(6)):** Prescribe technical qualifications; create complete/partial State monopolies

### 2. Protection in Respect of Conviction for Offences (Article 20)
* **Guaranteed to all:** 3 key protections against arbitrary/excessive punishment:
  1. **No Ex-Post-Facto Law:** Cannot be convicted except for violation of law in force at time of act, nor subjected to greater penalty
     * *Limitation:* Applies **ONLY to criminal laws**, NOT civil or tax laws; immunity doesn't apply to preventive detention
  2. **No Double Jeopardy:** No person prosecuted & punished for same offence more than once
     * *Limitation:* Available **ONLY before courts of law / judicial tribunals**, NOT administrative authorities
  3. **No Self-Incrimination:** No accused compelled to be a witness against himself
     * *Limitation:* Extends to oral & documentary evidence; does **NOT cover** physical objects, thumb impression, blood specimens, body exhibition; applies **ONLY to criminal proceedings**

### 3. Protection of Life and Personal Liberty (Article 21)
* **Text:** No person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**
* **AK Gopalan Case (1950):** Narrow interpretation → Protection available **ONLY against arbitrary executive action**, NOT legislative action; 'procedure established by law' strictly applied
* **Maneka Gandhi Case (1978):** Wider interpretation → Introduced American **‘Due Process of Law’** → Procedure must be **reasonable, fair, and just**; protection available against **both executive & legislative action**; 'Right to life' includes **living with human dignity**

#### Key Rights Declared Under Article 21
* Right to live with human dignity, decent environment/pollution-free air/water, livelihood, privacy (*Puttaswamy case 2017*), shelter, health, free education up to 14 yrs, free legal aid, speedy trial, emergency medical aid, travel abroad (*Satwant Singh Sawhney 1967*), sleep, marry person of choice, die with dignity (passive euthanasia - *Common Cause 2018*), climate change protection

### 4. Right to Education (Article 21A)
* **Provision:** State shall provide free & compulsory education to all children aged **6 to 14 years** (Elementary Education = FR)
* **Added by:** **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** (*"Dawn of second revolution"*)
* **Consequential Changes:** Changed DPSP **Article 45** (early childhood care < 6 yrs) & added Fundamental Duty **Article 51A(k)** (parental duty 6-14 yrs)
* **Precursor:** **Unni Krishnan case (1993)** recognised FR to primary education under Art 21 (overruled *Mohini Jain case 1992* which extended it to higher education)
* **Enforcing Legislation:** **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009** (Enforced w.e.f. **April 1, 2010**)

### 5. Protection Against Arrest and Detention (Article 22)
* **Two Types of Detention:**
  * **Punitive:** Punish person after trial and conviction
  * **Preventive:** Detain person without trial/conviction to prevent future offence (precautionary measure)

#### Ordinary Law Safeguards (Article 22 First Part)
1. Right to be informed of grounds of arrest
2. Right to consult and be defended by legal practitioner
3. Right to be produced before magistrate within **24 hours** (excluding journey time)
4. Right to release after 24 hours unless magistrate authorises detention
* *Exceptions:* **Not available to enemy aliens** or persons under **preventive detention**

#### Preventive Detention Safeguards (Article 22 Second Part)
1. Detention cannot exceed **3 months** unless **Advisory Board** (HC Judges) reports sufficient cause
   * *44th Amendment 1978:* Sought to reduce limit to 2 months (unnotified; original 3 months continues)
2. Grounds of detention communicated to detenu (except facts against public interest)
3. Detenu given opportunity to make representation
* **Jurisdiction:** Parliament has exclusive power for defence, foreign affairs, security of India; Concurrent power with States for public order, state security, essential supplies

#### Major Preventive Detention Acts
* Preventive Detention Act (1950, expired 1969), MISA (1971, repealed 1978), COFEPOSA (1974), NASA (1980), PBMSECA (1980), TADA (1985, repealed 1995), PITNDPSA (1988), POTA (2002, repealed 2004), **UAPA (1967, amended 2004, 2008, 2012, 2019)**

## RIGHT AGAINST EXPLOITATION (ARTICLES 23–24)

### 1. Prohibition of Traffic in Human Beings and Forced Labour (Article 23)
* **Prohibitions:** Traffic in human beings, *begar* (unpaid forced labour under zamindars), and other forced labour
* **Traffic Includes:** Selling/buying humans, prostitution, devadasis, slavery → Enforced via **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956**
* **Forced Labour:** Compelling work against will (physical, legal, or economic compulsion like working below minimum wage) → Enforced via **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976**, **Minimum Wages Act 1948**, **Equal Remuneration Act 1976**
* **Exception:** State can impose **compulsory service for public purposes** (military/social service) without payment, provided no discrimination on religion, race, caste, or class

### 2. Prohibition of Employment of Children in Factories, etc. (Article 24)
* **Prohibition:** Employment of children below **14 years** in factories, mines, or hazardous activities (construction, railways)
* **Key Legislation:** **Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986** (Amended 2016)
* **1996 SC Ruling:** Established **Child Labour Rehabilitation Welfare Fund** (offending employer fine = **₹20,000 per child**)
* **2005 Act:** **Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005** (NCPCR, SCPCR, Children's Courts)
* **2006 Govt Ban:** Banned child domestic servants & workers in dhabas, hotels, restaurants, shops

## RIGHT TO FREEDOM OF RELIGION (ARTICLES 25–28)

### 1. Freedom of Conscience, Profession, Practice, Propagation (Article 25)
* **4 Dimensions:**
  1. **Freedom of Conscience:** Inner freedom to mould relation with God
  2. **Right to Profess:** Open declaration of religious belief
  3. **Right to Practice:** Performance of rituals, ceremonies, worship
  4. **Right to Propagate:** Disseminate religious beliefs (does **NOT include right to convert**; forced conversions impinge on conscience)
* **Scope:** Covers beliefs + practices; available to citizens & foreigners
* **Restrictions:** Public order, morality, health, and other FRs
* **State Powers:** Regulate economic/secular activities associated with religion; throw open Hindu public religious institutions to all sections
* **Explanations:** (1) Wearing/carrying *kirpans* included in Sikh profession; (2) 'Hindus' includes **Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists**

### 2. Freedom to Manage Religious Affairs (Article 26)
* **Religious Denomination Rights:**
  1. Establish & maintain religious/charitable institutions
  2. Manage own internal religious affairs
  3. Own & acquire property
  4. Administer property per law
* **Individual vs Collective:** Art 25 = Individual freedom; Art 26 = Collective freedom of religious denominations
* **Restrictions:** Public order, morality, health (NOT subject to other FRs)
* **Denomination Criteria (SC):** Collection of individuals with belief system, common organisation, designated by distinctive name (*Ramakrishna Mission & Ananda Marga = Denominations; Aurobindo Society = NOT a denomination*)

### 3. Freedom from Taxation for Promotion of a Religion (Article 27)
* **Rule:** No person compelled to pay taxes for promotion/maintenance of any particular religion
* **Meaning:** Tax money cannot favour one religion over another (can be spent on promoting all religions equally)
* **Tax vs Fee:** Prohibits **TAX**, does **NOT prohibit FEE** (fees levied for secular administration/safety of pilgrims)

### 4. Freedom from Attending Religious Instruction (Article 28)
* **4 Categories of Educational Institutions:**
  1. **Wholly maintained by State:** Religious instruction **completely prohibited**
  2. **Administered by State but established under Trust/Endowment:** Religious instruction **permitted**
  3. **Recognised by State:** Religious instruction **permitted on voluntary basis**
  4. **Receiving aid from State:** Religious instruction **permitted on voluntary basis** (minor consent required for minors)

## CULTURAL AND EDUCATIONAL RIGHTS (ARTICLES 29–30)

### 1. Protection of Interests of Minorities (Article 29)
* **2 Provisions:**
  1. Any section of citizens with distinct language, script, or culture has right to conserve the same
  2. No citizen denied admission to State-maintained/aided educational institutions on grounds **ONLY of religion, race, caste, language**
* **Scope:** Protects **both religious & linguistic minorities** + majority ("section of citizens"); includes right to agitate for language protection

### 2. Right of Minorities to Establish and Administer Educational Institutions (Article 30)
* **Exclusive to Minorities (Religious & Linguistic):**
  1. Right to establish & administer educational institutions of choice
  2. State compensation for compulsory land acquisition must not abridge this right (Added by **44th Amendment, 1978**)
  3. State shall not discriminate in granting aid
* **Definition:** 'Minority' **not defined** in Constitution
* **3 Types of Minority Institutions:**
  1. Seeking recognition + aid from State (subject to academic/staff/sanitation regulations)
  2. Seeking recognition only (subject to regulatory power)
  3. Seeking neither recognition nor aid (free internal administration; subject to general contract/labour/tax laws)
* **Principles (*Malankara Syrian Catholic College Case, 2006*):** Equality with majority (no reverse discrimination); regulations for academic excellence permissible; right to choose governing body & staff

## RIGHT TO CONSTITUTIONAL REMEDIES (ARTICLE 32)
* **Ambedkar's View:** *"Most important article... heart and soul of the Constitution... without which it would be a nullity"*
* **Status:** Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right & **'Basic Feature'** of Constitution
* **4 Provisions:**
  1. Right to move SC by appropriate proceedings guaranteed
  2. SC empowered to issue directions, orders, or writs
  3. Parliament can empower other courts to issue writs (without prejudice to SC)
  4. Right to move SC suspended **ONLY during National Emergency (Article 359)**
* **Nature:** SC is defender & guarantor of FRs; jurisdiction is **Original but NOT Exclusive** (concurrent with High Courts under **Article 226**)

### Writs—Types and Scope
* **Origin:** Borrowed from English law (**Prerogative Writs**)
* **SC (Art 32) vs High Court (Art 226) Writ Comparison:**
  1. **Purpose:** SC issues writs **ONLY for FRs**; HC issues for FRs **AND ordinary legal rights** (HC purpose scope wider)
  2. **Territorial:** SC jurisdiction = **Entire India**; HC jurisdiction = **State territory** (unless cause of action arises outside) (SC territorial scope wider)
  3. **Dismissal:** SC **CANNOT refuse** to exercise writ power (Art 32 is FR); HC writ power is **discretionary** (HC may refuse)

#### 1. Habeas Corpus
* **Literal Meaning:** "To have the body of"
* **Purpose:** Order to produce detained person to examine detention legality; sets person free if illegal → Bulwark of individual liberty
* **Issued Against:** Both **public authorities & private individuals**
* **Not Issued When:** Detention is lawful, for contempt of court/legislature, by competent court, or outside jurisdiction

#### 2. Mandamus
* **Literal Meaning:** "We command"
* **Purpose:** Command to public official/body/court to perform mandatory official duty failed/refused
* **Not Issued Against:** Private individuals, non-statutory instructions, discretionary duties, contractual obligations, **President or State Governors**, sitting HC/SC judges

#### 3. Prohibition
* **Literal Meaning:** "To forbid"
* **Purpose:** Higher court orders lower court/tribunal to prevent exceeding jurisdiction (directs **inactivity**)
* **Issued Against:** **Judicial & quasi-judicial authorities ONLY** (NOT administrative authorities or private bodies)

#### 4. Certiorari
* **Literal Meaning:** "To be certified / informed"
* **Purpose:** Higher court quashes order of lower court/tribunal passed in excess/lack of jurisdiction or error of law
* **Prohibition vs Certiorari:** Prohibition issued *before* final order (preventive); Certiorari issued *after* final order (curative & quashing)
* **Issued Against:** Judicial, quasi-judicial, **AND administrative authorities** affecting individual rights (SC expanded scope)

#### 5. Quo-Warranto
* **Literal Meaning:** "By what authority or warrant"
* **Purpose:** Inquires into legality of claim of a person to a public office; prevents illegal usurpation
* **Conditions:** Substantive permanent public office created by statute/Constitution (NOT ministerial or private office)
* **Locus Standi:** Can be sought by **any public-minded person** (not necessarily aggrieved person)

## ARMED FORCES AND FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLE 33)
* **Power:** Parliament empowered to restrict/abrogate FRs of Armed Forces, Paramilitary, Police, Intelligence agencies, and non-combatants (cooks, barbers, tailors) to ensure discipline
* **Exclusive Authority:** Only **Parliament** can legislate (State Legislatures cannot); laws immune from FR violation challenge
* **Enacted Acts:** Army Act (1950), Navy Act (1950), Air Force Act (1950), Police Forces (Restriction of Rights) Act (1966), BSF Act
* **Court Martial:** Excluded from writ jurisdiction of SC & HCs regarding FRs

---

## MARTIAL LAW AND FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLE 34)
* **Concept:** Restrictions on FRs while martial law (military rule) is in force in any area
* **Indemnity:** Parliament can pass **Act of Indemnity** protecting government servants for acts done to restore order
* **Definition:** 'Martial Law' **not defined** in Constitution; borrowed from English Common Law; implies suspension of ordinary law & government by military tribunals
* **Martial Law vs National Emergency:**
  * Martial Law affects **only FRs**; National Emergency affects FRs, Centre-State relations, revenue distribution, Parliament tenure
  * Martial Law **suspends govt & ordinary courts**; Emergency continues govt & courts
  * Martial Law imposed to **restore breakdown of law & order**; Emergency imposed for **war, external aggression, armed rebellion**
  * Martial Law imposed in **specific area**; Emergency in **whole country or part**
  * Martial Law has **no explicit constitutional provision**; Emergency has **explicit provisions (Art 352)**

---

## EFFECTING CERTAIN FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLE 35)
* **Exclusive Parliamentary Power:** Power to make laws giving effect to specified FRs vests **ONLY in Parliament** (ensures national uniformity):
  1. Residence requirement for employment (Art 16)
  2. Empowering lower courts to issue writs (Art 32)
  3. Abrogating FRs for Armed Forces (Art 33)
  4. Indemnifying acts during Martial Law (Art 34)
  5. Prescribing punishment for untouchability (Art 17) & human trafficking/forced labour (Art 23)

---

## PRESENT POSITION OF RIGHT TO PROPERTY (ARTICLE 300A)
* **Original Status:** FR under **Article 19(1)(f)** & **Article 31**
* **Abolition:** **44th Amendment Act, 1978** repealed Art 19(1)(f) & Art 31 from Part III; inserted **Article 300A in Part XII**
* **Current Status:** **Legal / Constitutional Right** (NOT Fundamental Right, NOT part of Basic Structure)
* **Implications:**
  1. Can be modified/curtailed by ordinary Parliamentary law (no constitutional amendment needed)
  2. Protects property against executive action, BUT NOT legislative action
  3. Violation remedy via **High Court (Art 226)**, NOT direct SC writ (Art 32)
  4. No guaranteed constitutional right to compensation (EXCEPT under **Art 30** minority institutions & **Art 31A** ceiling-limit agricultural land)

---

## EXCEPTIONS TO FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (ARTICLES 31A, 31B, 31C)
* **Article 31A (Saving of Estate Acquisition Laws):** Saves 5 categories of laws (land reforms, taking over management, amalgamating corporations) from Art 14 & 19 challenges (Requires President's assent for state laws)
* **Article 31B (Validation of 9th Schedule Acts):** Immunises acts in 9th Schedule from all FRs
  * **Judicial Review (*Kesavananda 1973, Waman Rao 1980, IR Coelho 2007*):** Laws placed in 9th Schedule **after April 24, 1973** are open to challenge if violating **Basic Structure** or Art 14, 15, 19, 21
  * *Count:* Originally 13 acts; currently **282 acts**
* **Article 31C (Saving DPSP Laws):**
  1. Laws implementing **Article 39(b) or (c)** DPSPs protected from Art 14 & 19 violation
  2. Declaration clause immunities declared unconstitutional in *Kesavananda Bharati 1973* (restored judicial review)
  3. 42nd Amendment attempt to extend to all DPSPs struck down in ***Minerva Mills 1980***

---

## CRITICISM OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS
1. **Excessive Limitations:** Innumerable exceptions, restrictions ("grants with one hand, takes with other")
2. **No Social & Economic Rights:** Lacks right to work, social security, employment, rest/leisure
3. **No Clarity:** Vague terms ('public order', 'reasonable restriction'); "Paradise for lawyers" (*Sir Ivor Jennings*)
4. **No Permanency:** Can be curtailed by Parliament (except Basic Structure limitation)
5. **Suspension During Emergency:** Suspension cuts at democratic roots
6. **Expensive Remedy:** Judicial process too costly for common man
7. **Preventive Detention:** Negates individual liberty; state-centric
8. **No Consistent Philosophy:** Complex formulae based on no single philosophy (*Jennings*)

---

## SIGNIFICANCE OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS
1. Bedrock of democratic system & bulwark of individual liberty
2. Facilitates Rule of Law & protects minorities/weaker sections
3. Strengthens secular fabric & ensures human dignity
4. Checks governmental absolute authority & lays foundation for social equality

---

## RIGHTS OUTSIDE PART III (CONSTITUTIONAL / LEGAL RIGHTS)
1. **Article 265 (Part XII):** No tax levied/collected except by authority of law
2. **Article 300A (Part XII):** No person deprived of property save by authority of law
3. **Article 301 (Part XIII):** Trade, commerce, intercourse throughout India shall be free
4. **Article 326 (Part XV):** Elections to Lok Sabha & Legislative Assemblies based on adult suffrage
* *Difference from FRs:* In case of violation, aggrieved person **cannot go directly to SC under Art 32**; must approach High Court under **Art 226** or file ordinary suit
