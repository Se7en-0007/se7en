## INTRODUCTION
* **Executive Scheme:** President = **Nominal Executive Authority** (*de jure* executive, Head of State); Prime Minister = **Real Executive Authority** (*de facto* executive, Head of Government)

---

## APPOINTMENT OF THE PRIME MINISTER
* **Constitutional Provision:** **Article 75** states Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President (no specific selection procedure in Constitution)
* **Parliamentary Convention:** President appoints leader of majority party in Lok Sabha as PM
* **Discretionary Appointment (No Clear Majority):**
  * President appoints leader of largest party/coalition & asks to seek vote of confidence in Lok Sabha within **1 month**
  * *First Exercise:* **1979** - President Neelam Sanjiva Reddy appointed **Charan Singh** (coalition leader) after Morarji Desai's Janata Party govt fell
  * *Subsequent Examples:* Charan Singh (1979), V.P. Singh (1989), Chandrasekhar (1990), P.V. Narasimha Rao (1991), A.B. Vajpayee (1996), Deve Gowda (1996), I.K. Gujral (1997), A.B. Vajpayee (1998)
* **Sudden Death of Incumbent:**
  * President exercises individual judgment if PM dies suddenly with no obvious successor
  * *1984 Example:* President Zail Singh appointed **Rajiv Gandhi** upon Indira Gandhi's assassination (bypassed caretaker precedent)
  * *Caretaker Precedent:* Death of Jawaharlal Nehru (1964) & Lal Bahadur Shastri (1966) → **Gulzari Lal Nanda** appointed as acting/caretaker PM twice
* **Judicial Rulings on Appointment:**
  * **Delhi HC (1980):** Constitution does not require proving majority *before* appointment → Appoint first, prove majority within reasonable time
  * **SC (1996):** Person non-member of Parliament can be appointed PM for max **6 months**; must get elected to either House within 6 months or cease to be PM
* **House Membership:**
  * PM can be member of **either House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha)
  * *Rajya Sabha Members as PM:* **Indira Gandhi (1966)**, **Deve Gowda (1996)**, **Manmohan Singh (2004)**
  * *British Comparison:* UK PM **must** belong to Lower House (House of Commons)

## OATH, TERM AND SALARY

### Oath of Office and Secrecy
* **Administered by:** **President**
* **Oath of Office:** True faith & allegiance to Constitution; uphold sovereignty & integrity of India; discharge duties faithfully; do right to all without fear or favour, affection or ill will
* **Oath of Secrecy:** Not reveal Union Minister matters directly or indirectly except as required for discharge of duties

### Term of Office
* **Not fixed**; holds office during **pleasure of the President**
* Cannot be dismissed so long as enjoying majority support in Lok Sabha
* Loss of Lok Sabha confidence → Must resign or President dismisses (*e.g., V.P. Singh 1990, Deve Gowda 1997*)

### Salary and Allowances
* Determined by Parliament (**Salaries and Allowances of Ministers Act, 1952**)
* Draws MP salary & allowances + sumptuary allowance, free accommodation, travel, medical facilities
* *2001 Amendment:* Sumptuary allowance increased from ₹1,500 to **₹3,000 per month**

## POWERS AND FUNCTIONS OF THE PRIME MINISTER

### In Relation to Council of Ministers
1. Recommends ministers for Presidential appointment (President appoints only recommended persons)
2. Allocates and reshuffles portfolios among ministers
3. Can ask minister to resign or advise President to dismiss minister in case of differences
4. Presides over meetings of Council of Ministers & influences decisions
5. Guides, directs, controls, and coordinates activities of all ministers
6. Resignation or death of PM → Automatically dissolves Council of Ministers & creates vacuum (resignation/death of minister merely creates a vacancy)

### In Relation to the President
1. **Principal Channel of Communication (Article 78):**
   * Communicate all decisions of Council of Ministers regarding administration & legislation
   * Furnish administration & legislative info called for by President
   * Submit for Council consideration any matter decided by a minister but not considered by Council (if President requires)
2. Advises President regarding key appointments: **Attorney General of India**, **CAG**, **UPSC Chairman & Members**, **Election Commissioners**, **Finance Commission Chairman & Members**

### In Relation to Parliament
* Leader of the Lower House (Lok Sabha):
  1. Advises President on summoning & proroguing Parliamentary sessions
  2. Recommends dissolution of Lok Sabha to President at any time
  3. Announces government policies on floor of the House

### Other Powers & Functions
1. Ex-officio Chairman of **NITI Aayog** (succeeded Planning Commission), **National Integration Council**, **Inter-State Council**, **National Water Resources Council**
2. Shapes foreign policy of the country
3. Chief spokesman of Union Government
4. Crisis manager-in-chief at political level during emergencies
5. Leader of the nation (meets diverse sections, receives memoranda)
6. Leader of the ruling party
7. Political head of the services
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar Quote:** *“If any functionary under our constitution is to be compared with the US President, he is the Prime Minister and not the President of the Union.”*

## RELATIONSHIP WITH THE PRESIDENT
* **Article 74:** Council of Ministers with PM at head to aid and advise President (President shall act in accordance with advice; may ask for reconsideration once)
* **Article 75:** (a) PM appointed by President; other ministers appointed on PM's advice; (b) Ministers hold office during pleasure of President; (c) Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Lok Sabha
* **Article 78:** Duties of PM regarding furnishing information to President (communication of decisions, furnishing info, submitting single-minister decisions to Council)

## CHIEF MINISTERS WHO BECAME PRIME MINISTERS
* **6 Chief Ministers became Prime Ministers:**
  1. **Morarji Desai:** CM of erstwhile Bombay State (1952–56) → 1st non-Congress PM (March 1977)
  2. **Charan Singh:** CM of undivided UP (1967–68, 1970) → PM (1979)
  3. **V.P. Singh:** CM of UP → PM in National Front Govt (Dec 1989–Nov 1990)
  4. **P.V. Narasimha Rao:** CM of Andhra Pradesh (1971–73) → 1st PM from South India (1991–96)
  5. **H.D. Deve Gowda:** CM of Karnataka → United Front PM (June 1996)
  6. **Narendra Modi:** CM of Gujarat 4 times (2001–2014) → PM (May 2014 onwards)

## CARETAKER GOVERNMENT

### Meaning
* Not explicitly provided in Constitution; situational arrangement & functional necessity
* Feature of parliamentary system post-dissolution of Lok Sabha until induction of new ministry after general elections
* Purpose: Ensure free & fair polls & carry on routine administration
* Term describes status of Council of Ministers that resigned/lost confidence but asked to continue till alternative arrangement

### Limited Role
* Manages **routine day-to-day activities** only
* Not expected to take major policy decisions, launch new projects, or grant financial concessions (except urgent national interest/security)
* **Tarkunde / J.P. Committee (1974–75):** Recommended caretaker govt should not announce new policies, start new projects, grant loans/allowances, or hold official minister functions
* **President N. Sanjeeva Reddy Communique (Aug 1979):** Confirmed caretaker govt would not take new policy decisions or major spending
* **Calcutta High Court (Dec 1979):** Caretaker govt carries on day-to-day administration necessary for making alternative arrangements
* **Constraints:** Lack of parliamentary accountability & prohibition against gaining electoral advantage
