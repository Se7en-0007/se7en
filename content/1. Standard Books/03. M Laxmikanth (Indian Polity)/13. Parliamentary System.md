## INTRODUCTION
* **Constitutional Basis:** **Articles 74 & 75** (Centre) and **Articles 163 & 164** (States)
* **Classification Basis:** Nature of relations between executive & legislative organs
* **Parliamentary vs Presidential:**
  * **Parliamentary System:** Executive is responsible to legislature for its policies and acts
  * **Presidential System:** Executive is NOT responsible to legislature; constitutionally independent in tenure
* **Alternative Names for Parliamentary System:**
  * **Cabinet Government:** Cabinet = nucleus of power (*Ivor Jennings*)
  * **Responsible Government:** Cabinet accountable to Parliament & stays in office while enjoying confidence
  * **Westminster Model:** Named after location of British Parliament (origin place)
* **Prevalence:**
  * **Parliamentary:** Britain, Japan, Canada, India, etc.
  * **Presidential:** USA, Brazil, Russia, Sri Lanka, etc.
* **Prime Ministerial Government:** PM's power & dominant role increased significantly (*Cross-man, Mackintosh*); shifted from *‘primus inter pares’* (first among equals) to dominant leader (applies to both UK & India)

## FEATURES OF PARLIAMENTARY GOVERNMENT

### 1. Nominal and Real Executives
* **Dual Executive:**
  * **Nominal Executive (De jure / Titular):** President (Head of State)
  * **Real Executive (De facto):** Prime Minister (Head of Government)
* **Article 74:** Council of Ministers headed by PM to aid and advise President
* **Binding Advice:** Advice of Council of Ministers is **binding** on President (**42nd & 44th Amendment Acts of 1976 & 1978**)

### 2. Majority Party Rule
* **Government Formation:** Political party securing majority seats in Lok Sabha forms government
* **Appointments:** Leader appointed as PM by President; other ministers appointed by President on PM's advice
* **Coalition:** If no single party gets majority, coalition of parties invited to form government

### 3. Collective Responsibility
* **Bedrock Principle:** Fundamental pillar of parliamentary system
* **Article 75:** Ministers collectively responsible to Parliament in general & **Lok Sabha in particular**
* **Team Concept:** Act as a team; **“swim and sink together”**
* **Removal:** Lok Sabha can remove Council of Ministers by passing a **Vote of No-Confidence**

### 4. Political Homogeneity
* **Shared Ideology:** Usually members of Council of Ministers belong to same political party
* **Coalition Exception:** Ministers bound by **consensus** in coalition governments

### 5. Double Membership
* **Dual Role:** Ministers are members of **both Legislature and Executive**
* **Mandatory Rule:** Minister must be an MP; non-MP appointed as minister must get elected to Parliament within **6 consecutive months** or cease to be a minister

### 6. Leadership of The Prime Minister
* **Crucial Role:** PM is leader of Council of Ministers, leader of Parliament and leader of ruling party

### 7. Dissolution of The Lower House
* **Executive Right:** Lower House (**Lok Sabha**) can be dissolved by President on recommendation of PM before term expiry
* **Implication:** Executive enjoys right to get legislature dissolved

### 8. Secrecy
* **Principle:** Ministers operate under secrecy of proceedings, policies and decisions
* **Oath:** **Oath of Secrecy** administered by President before assuming office

## FEATURES OF PRESIDENTIAL GOVERNMENT
*(Based on American Presidential System)*
1. **Single Executive:** President is both Head of State (ceremonial) and Head of Government (leads executive organ)
2. **Fixed Tenure:** Elected by electoral college for **4-year fixed term**; removed by Congress only via **impeachment** for grave unconstitutional act
3. **Kitchen Cabinet:** Governs via non-elected advisory body (Departmental Secretaries); selected, appointed and removable solely by President
4. **Non-Responsibility:** President & secretaries not responsible to Congress; do not hold membership or attend sessions
5. **No Dissolution:** President **cannot** dissolve House of Representatives (Lower House of Congress)
6. **Separation of Powers:** Strict doctrine (*Montesquieu - 'The Spirit of Laws', 1748*) → Legislative, executive and judicial powers separated into independent organs

---

## MERITS OF THE PARLIAMENTARY SYSTEM
1. **Harmony Between Legislature & Executive:** Cooperation & interdependence; eliminates friction and disputes between organs
2. **Responsible Government:** Ministers accountable to Parliament via Question Hour, discussions, adjournment motions, no-confidence motions
3. **Prevents Despotism:** Executive authority dispersed among Council of Ministers (not single individual); accountable to Parliament
4. **Ready Alternative Government:** Opposition invited to form govt if ruling party loses majority without fresh elections (*Jennings: “Leader of opposition is alternative PM”*)
5. **Wide Representation:** Cabinet accommodates representatives of diverse sections, interests and regions

---

## DEMERITS OF THE PARLIAMENTARY SYSTEM
1. **Unstable Government:** No guaranteed tenure; vulnerable to no-confidence motions, defections, coalition friction (*Examples:* Governments of Morarji Desai, Charan Singh, V.P. Singh, Chandra Sekhar, Deva Gowda, I.K. Gujral)
2. **No Continuity of Policies:** Policy shifts upon party changes (*Example:* Janata Govt 1977 reversed Congress policies; Congress 1980 reversed Janata policies)
3. **Dictatorship of the Cabinet:** Absolute majority can lead to cabinet tyranny (*Laski*; *Ramsay Muir* in *'How Britain is Governed'* = **‘Dictatorship of the Cabinet’**; witnessed during Indira Gandhi & Rajiv Gandhi eras)
4. **Against Separation of Powers:** Fusion of legislative and executive powers (*Bagehot*: *“Cabinet is a hyphen that joins, the buckle that binds executive and legislative departments”*)
5. **Government by Amateurs:** Ministers are non-experts/politicians; PM's selection restricted to MPs; ministers overburdened with political/parliamentary work

---

## REASONS FOR ADOPTING PARLIAMENTARY SYSTEM
1. **Familiarity with System:** Operated in India during British rule; constitutional traditions became parliamentary (*K.M. Munshi argument*)
2. **Preference to More Responsibility:** *Dr. B.R. Ambedkar* noted democratic executive must balance **stability vs responsibility**; American system = more stability, less responsibility; British system = **more responsibility, less stability** → Draft Constitution preferred **more responsibility**
3. **Need to Avoid Legislative–Executive Conflicts:** Infant democracy could not afford perpetual cleavage or conflict between organs
4. **Nature of Indian Society:** Highly heterogeneous and complex plural society; parliamentary system offers wider representation to diverse sections, promoting national unity
* **Swaran Singh Committee (1975):** Re-examined system and affirmed parliamentary system is performing well; rejected switch to presidential system

---

## DISTINCTION BETWEEN INDIAN AND BRITISH MODELS
1. **Republican vs Monarchical:**
   * **India:** Republican system with **elected Head of State** (President)
   * **Britain:** Monarchical system with **hereditary Head of State** (King/Queen)
2. **Sovereignty of Parliament:**
   * **Britain:** Doctrine of Parliamentary Sovereignty (Parliament supreme)
   * **India:** Parliament **not supreme**; limited by written Constitution, federalism, judicial review and Fundamental Rights
1. **Prime Minister's House Membership:**
   * **Britain:** PM **must** belong to Lower House (House of Commons)
   * **India:** PM can belong to **either House** of Parliament (*Examples from Rajya Sabha:* **Indira Gandhi (1966)**, **Deve Gowda (1996)**, **Manmohan Singh (2004)**)
4. **Minister Qualification:**
   * **Britain:** Only MPs can be appointed as ministers
   * **India:** Non-MP can be appointed minister for max **6 consecutive months**
5. **Legal Responsibility & Countersignature:**
   * **Britain:** System of legal responsibility; ministers must countersign official acts of Head of State
   * **India:** **No legal responsibility**; no requirement for ministerial countersignature
6. **Shadow Cabinet:**
   * **Britain:** Unique institution formed by opposition to balance ruling cabinet and prepare future ministers
   * **India:** **No Shadow Cabinet** institution
