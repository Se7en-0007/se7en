## INTRODUCTION
* **Classification Basis:** Nature of relations between national and regional governments
  * **Unitary Government:** All powers vested in national government; regional governments (if existing) derive authority from national government (*Examples:* Britain, France, Japan, China, Italy, Norway, Sweden)
  * **Federal Government:** Powers divided between national and regional governments by Constitution; both operate independently in respective jurisdictions (*Examples:* US, Switzerland, Australia, Canada, Russia, Brazil, Argentina)
* **Term Origin:** 'Federation' derived from Latin word ***foedus*** = 'treaty' or 'agreement' (new political system formed via treaty/agreement among units)
  * *Unit Names:* States (US), Cantons (Switzerland), Provinces (Canada), Republics (Russia)
* **2 Modes of Federation Formation:**
  1. **Integration:** Militarily weak / economically backward independent states unite → Strong Union (*Example:* USA – 1787, 1st & oldest federation, post-American Revolution 1775–83; grown from 13 to 50 states)
  2. **Disintegration:** Big unitary state converted into federation by granting autonomy to provinces (*Example:* Canada – 1867, grown from 4 to 10 provinces)
* **Indian Choice:** Adopted federal system due to **large size of country** and **socio-cultural diversity** (reconciles national unity with regional autonomy)
* **Constitutional Terminology:** Term 'Federation' **nowhere used** in Constitution
  * **Article 1:** Describes India as a **‘Union of States’**
  * **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's 2 Reasons for 'Union of States':**
    1. Indian federation is **not the result of an agreement** among states (unlike American federation)
    2. States have **no right to secede** from the federation (indestructible Union)
* **Model Adopted:** Based on **Canadian Model** (NOT American Model)
  * *Resemblance to Canadian Model:* (i) Formation by disintegration, (ii) Preference for term 'Union', (iii) Centralising tendency (vesting more power in Centre)

## Comparing Features of Federal and Unitary Governments

| Federal Government | Unitary Government |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1. Dual Government (national & regional) | 1. Single government (national government; may create regional governments) |
| 2. Written Constitution | 2. Constitution may be written (France) or unwritten (Britain) |
| 3. Division of powers between national & regional government | 3. No division of powers; all powers vested in national government |
| 4. Supremacy of the Constitution | 4. Constitution may be supreme (Japan) or not supreme (Britain) |
| 5. Rigid Constitution | 5. Constitution may be rigid (France) or flexible (Britain) |
| 6. Independent judiciary | 6. Judiciary may be independent or not independent |
| 7. Bicameral legislature | 7. Legislature may be bicameral (Britain) or unicameral (China) |

## FEDERAL FEATURES OF THE CONSTITUTION

### 1. Dual Polity
* **Structure:** Union at Centre + States at periphery (each endowed with sovereign powers assigned by Constitution)
* **Jurisdiction:**
  * **Union:** Matters of national importance (*defence, foreign affairs, currency, communication*)
  * **States:** Matters of regional/local importance (*public order, agriculture, health, local government*)

### 2. Written Constitution
* **Lengthiest Written Constitution:** Specifies structure, organization, powers, functions and limits of both Central & state governments
* **Size:** Originally Preamble + 395 Articles (22 Parts) + 8 Schedules; Presently Preamble + ~470 Articles (25 Parts) + 12 Schedules

### 3. Division of Powers
* **7th Schedule 3 Lists:**
  * **Union List:** **98 subjects** (originally 97)
  * **State List:** **59 subjects** (originally 66)
  * **Concurrent List:** **52 subjects** (originally 47)
* **Conflict Rule:** Central law prevails over State law on Concurrent List subjects
* **Residuary Powers:** Vested in the **Centre**

### 4. Supremacy of The Constitution
* **Supreme Law:** Laws enacted by Centre and States must conform to Constitution
* **Judicial Enforcement:** Non-conforming laws declared invalid by Supreme Court or High Courts via **judicial review**

### 5. Rigid Constitution
* **Federal Structure Amendment:** Provisions concerning federal structure (Centre-State relations, judicial organisation) require **special majority of Parliament** + **ratification by half of state legislatures**

### 6. Independent Judiciary
* **Dual Purpose:** (1) Protect supremacy of Constitution via judicial review; (2) Settle disputes between Centre and States or between States
* **Safeguards:** Security of tenure, fixed service conditions for judges

### 7. Bicameralism
* **Two Houses:** Upper House (**Rajya Sabha**) + Lower House (**Lok Sabha**)
* **Rajya Sabha:** Represents states of Indian Federation; maintains federal equilibrium by protecting state interests against Central interference

## UNITARY FEATURES OF THE CONSTITUTION

### 1. Strong Centre
* **Inequitable Power Division:** Union List has more subjects + more important subjects
* **Overriding Power:** Centre overrides States on Concurrent List
* **Residuary Powers:** Vested in Centre (unlike US where vested in states)

### 2. States Not Indestructible
* **No Territorial Integrity:** Parliament can unilaterally alter area, boundaries, or name of any state by **simple majority** (**Article 3**)
* **Dictum:** India is **“an indestructible Union of destructible states”** (vs USA: “an indestructible Union of indestructible states”)

### 3. Single Constitution
* **Unified Frame:** Single Constitution embodies both Centre and States (States cannot frame own Constitution)
* *Historical Exception:* Erstwhile Jammu & Kashmir had own Constitution (abolished in 2019)

### 4. Flexibility of The Constitution
* **Unilateral Amendment:** Bulk of Constitution amended by Parliament alone (simple or special majority)
* **Initiation:** Power to initiate constitutional amendments lies **only with Centre** (states cannot propose amendments, unlike US)

### 5. No Equality of State Representation
* **Population Basis:** State representation in Rajya Sabha varies from **1 to 31** seats
* *Comparison (USA):* US Senate grants equal representation = 100 seats (2 per state for 50 states)

### 6. Emergency Provisions
* **3 Types:** National (**Art 352**), State / President's Rule (**Art 356 & 365**), Financial (**Art 360**)
* **Structural Shift:** Converts federal structure into **unitary one** without formal constitutional amendment

### 7. Single Citizenship
* **Single Polity:** Only **Indian Citizenship**; no separate state citizenship (derived from Canadian model)
* *Comparison:* USA and Australia have dual citizenship (national + state)

### 8. Integrated Judiciary
* **Single Hierarchy:** Supreme Court at top → High Courts below
* **Enforcement:** Single court system enforces both Central and State laws (vs USA: separate federal and state court systems)

### 9. All-India Services
* **Common Services:** IAS, IPS, IFoS common to Centre and States
* **Central Control:** Recruited, trained and ultimately controlled by Centre → violates traditional federal principle

### 10. Integrated Audit Machinery
* **CAG Control:** Comptroller and Auditor-General of India audits accounts of both Centre and States
* **Appointment:** Appointed/removed by President without state consultation (restricts state financial autonomy)

### 11. Parliament’s Authority Over State List
* **Rajya Sabha Resolution:** Parliament can legislate on State List subject if Rajya Sabha passes resolution in national interest
* **Non-Emergency Competence:** Extends Central legislative power without formal amendment

### 12. Appointment of Governor
* **Head of State:** Appointed by President; holds office during **pleasure of President**; acts as agent of Centre (Canadian model)

### 13. Integrated Election Machinery
* **Election Commission:** Conducts elections to both Central and State legislatures; constituted by President without state say

### 14. Veto Over State Bills
* **Presidential Reservation:** Governor can reserve certain state bills for President's consideration
* **Absolute Veto:** President enjoys absolute veto over reserved state bills (can withhold assent even in second instance)

## CRITICAL EVALUATION OF THE FEDERAL SYSTEM

### Scholarly Opinions & Descriptions
* **K.C. Wheare:** Described Indian Constitution as **“quasi-federal”**
  * *Quote:* “Indian Union is a unitary state with subsidiary federal features rather than a federal state with subsidiary unitary features.”
* **K. Santhanam:** Identified **2 factors** causing centralisation/unitary bias:
  1. Dominance of Centre in financial sphere & state dependence on Central grants
  2. Emergence of powerful Planning Commission (now **NITI Aayog** since 2015) controlling state development
* **Paul Appleby:** Characterised as **“extremely federal”**
* **Morris Jones:** Termed as **“bargaining federalism”**
* **Sir Ivor Jennings:** Described as **“federation with a strong centralising tendency”** (mainly federal with unique safeguards for national unity)
* **C.H. Alexandrowicz:** Stated India is **“sui generis”** (unique in character)
* **Granville Austin:** Called it **“cooperative federalism”** (strong Centre did not make states weak/administrative agencies)

### Views of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
* **Dual Polity:** Establishes a dual polity; Union and States both created by Constitution and derive co-equal authority from it
* **Flexible Mould:** Avoids tight mould of federalism; can be both unitary and federal depending on time & circumstances
* **Rejection of Over-Centralisation Charge:** States are in no way dependent on Centre for legislative/executive authority; partition of powers is by Constitution itself, not Central law

### Supreme Court Ruling in S.R. Bommai Case (1994)
* **Basic Feature:** SC held Constitution is federal and federalism is a **‘basic feature’**
* **State Autonomy:** States have independent constitutional existence; not satellites/appendages/agents of Centre
* **Supreme in Allotted Sphere:** Within allotted sphere, states are supreme; emergency overrides are exceptions, not the rule

### Compromise & Balancing Factors
* **Dual Considerations:** (i) Normal division of powers & state autonomy vs (ii) Need for national integrity and strong Union in exceptional situations

### Trends Reflecting Federal Spirit in Practice
1. Territorial disputes between states (*e.g., Maharashtra & Karnataka over Belgaum*)
2. Inter-state river water disputes (*e.g., Karnataka & Tamil Nadu over Cauvery*)
3. Emergence of regional parties coming to power in states (*e.g. andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu*)
4. Creation of new states for regional aspirations (*e.g., Mizoram, Jharkhand, Telangana*)
5. Demand by states for increased financial grants from Centre
6. Assertion of state autonomy and resistance to Central interference
7. Supreme Court's procedural limits on use of **Article 356** (President's Rule)
