## EMERGENCE OF THE BASIC STRUCTURE
* **Shankari Prasad Case (1951):**
  * **Challenge:** Constitutional validity of **1st Amendment Act (1951)** (curtailed Right to Property)
  * **SC Ruling:** Parliament can amend FRs under Article 368; 'law' under **Article 13** includes only ordinary laws, NOT constituent laws (amendment acts) → Parliament can abridge/take away FRs
* **Sajjan Singh Case (1964):**
  * **SC Ruling:** Re-affirmed *Shankari Prasad* stand → Constitutional Amendment Act is NOT 'law' under Article 13
* **Golak Nath Case (1967):**
  * **Challenge:** Validity of **17th Amendment Act (1964)** (inserted certain state acts into 9th Schedule)
  * **SC Ruling (Reversed earlier stand):** FRs occupy **‘transcendental and immutable’** position → Parliament **cannot** abridge or take away FRs; Amendment Act **IS a 'law'** under Article 13 and void if violating FRs
* **24th Amendment Act (1971):**
  * **Parliament's Reaction:** Amended **Articles 13 and 368** → Declared Parliament has power to abridge/take away FRs under Art 368; such act is NOT 'law' under Art 13
* **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973) (Fundamental Rights Case):**
  * **SC Ruling:** Overruled *Golak Nath*; upheld validity of **24th Amendment Act (1971)** → Parliament can abridge/take away FRs
  * **Doctrine Innovated:** Laid down **‘Basic Structure’ (or ‘Basic Features’)** doctrine → Constituent power under Art 368 does NOT enable Parliament to alter 'basic structure' of Constitution (cannot take away a FR forming part of basic structure)
* **Indira Nehru Gandhi Case (1975) (Election Case):**
  * **SC Ruling:** Reaffirmed & applied Basic Structure doctrine → Invalidated provision of **39th Amendment Act (1975)** (kept election disputes of PM & Lok Sabha Speaker outside court jurisdiction)
* **42nd Amendment Act (1976):**
  * **Parliament's Reaction:** Amended **Article 368** → Declared no limitation on constituent power of Parliament; no amendment can be questioned in any court
* **Minerva Mills Case (1980):**
  * **SC Ruling:** Invalidated 42nd Amendment provision excluding judicial review → **Judicial review** & **limited amending power** are basic features
  * **Principle:** Constitution conferred limited amending power; donee of limited power cannot expand it into absolute/unlimited power to repeal/destroy basic features
* **Waman Rao Case (1980):**
  * **SC Ruling:** Adhered to Basic Structure doctrine; clarified it applies to constitutional amendments enacted **after April 24, 1973** (*Kesavananda Bharati* judgment date)

## ELEMENTS OF THE BASIC STRUCTURE
* **Present Position:** Parliament under Art 368 can amend any part of Constitution (including FRs) without affecting 'basic structure'; SC has not exhaustively defined basic structure
* **Emergent Basic Features (Synthesised List):**
  1. **Supremacy of the Constitution**
  2. **Sovereign, democratic and republican nature** of the Indian polity
  3. **Secular character** of the Constitution
  4. **Separation of powers** between legislature, executive, and judiciary
  5. **Federal character** of the Constitution
  6. **Unity and integrity** of the nation
  7. **Welfare state** (socio-economic justice)
  8. **Judicial review**
  9. **Freedom and dignity of the individual**
  10. **Parliamentary system**
  11. **Rule of law**
  12. **Harmony and balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles**
  13. **Principle of equality**
  14. **Free and fair elections**
  15. **Independence of Judiciary**
  16. **Limited power of Parliament to amend the Constitution**
  17. **Effective access to justice**
  18. **Principles (or essence) underlying fundamental rights**
  19. **Powers of Supreme Court** under **Articles 32, 136, 141, and 142**
      * *Art 32:* Remedies for enforcement of FRs including writs
      * *Art 136:* Special leave to appeal by Supreme Court
      * *Art 141:* Law declared by SC binding on all courts
      * *Art 142:* Enforcement of decrees and orders of SC
  20. **Powers of High Courts** under **Articles 226 and 227**
      * *Art 226:* Power of High Courts to issue certain writs
      * *Art 227:* Power of superintendence over all courts by High Court
