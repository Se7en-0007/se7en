## INTRODUCTION
* **Location:** Enshrined in **Part IV** of the Constitution (**Articles 36 to 51**)
* **Borrowing Source:** Borrowed from **Irish Constitution of 1937** (which copied from Spanish Constitution)
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Described DPSPs as **‘novel features’** of the Indian Constitution
* **Granville Austin:** Described DPSPs + Fundamental Rights as the **‘Conscience of the Constitution’**
* **Core Philosophy:** DPSPs + FRs contain the philosophy and soul of the Constitution

---

## FEATURES OF THE DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES
1. **Constitutional Instructions:** Ideals to keep in mind while formulating policies & enacting laws (instructions/recommendations to legislative, executive, administrative organs)
2. **Definition of State (Article 36):** Same meaning as Part III (includes Union & State Govts/Legislatures, local & public authorities)
3. **Resemblance to 1935 Act:** Resemble **‘Instrument of Instructions’** issued to Governor-General/Governors under **GOI Act of 1935** (*Ambedkar*)
4. **Socio-Economic Democracy:** Comprehensive economic, social & political programme → Aims at establishing a **‘Welfare State’** (not a colonial ‘Police State’)
5. **Non-Justiciable (Article 37):** Not legally enforceable by courts for violation, BUT declared **fundamental in governance** → Duty of State to apply in lawmaking
6. **Judicial Utility:** Helps courts determine constitutional validity of laws (law implementing DPSP deemed 'reasonable' under **Article 14 or 19**)

## CLASSIFICATION OF THE DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES
* **Note:** Constitution contains **no classification**; classified informally based on content/direction into 3 broad categories:

### 1. Socialistic Principles
* **Ideology:** Democratic Socialism → Frame welfare state & ensure social and economic justice
* **Article 38:** Promote welfare of people by securing a social order (justice: social, economic, political) & **minimise inequalities** in income, status, facilities, opportunities
* **Article 39:** Secure:
  1. Right to adequate means of **livelihood** for all citizens
  2. **Equitable distribution** of material resources for common good
  3. Prevention of **concentration of wealth** & means of production
  4. **Equal pay for equal work** for men & women
  5. Preservation of **health & strength** of workers & children against abuse
  6. Opportunities for **healthy development of children** & protection against exploitation
* **Article 39A:** Promote equal justice & provide **free legal aid** to the poor
* **Article 41:** Secure **right to work, to education, and to public assistance** (unemployment, old age, sickness, disablement)
* **Article 42:** Provision for **just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief**
* **Article 43:** Secure a **living wage**, decent standard of life, and social/cultural opportunities for workers
* **Article 43A:** Take steps to secure **participation of workers in management** of industries
* **Article 47:** Raise level of **nutrition & standard of living** and improve **public health**

### 2. Gandhian Principles
* **Ideology:** Gandhian programme of reconstruction during national movement
* **Article 40:** Organise **village panchayats** with powers to function as units of self-government
* **Article 43:** Promote **cottage industries** on an individual or co-operation basis in rural areas
* **Article 43B:** Promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control, professional management of **co-operative societies**
* **Article 46:** Promote educational & economic interests of **SCs, STs, and other weaker sections** & protect from social injustice/exploitation
* **Article 47:** Prohibit consumption of **intoxicating drinks and drugs** injurious to health
* **Article 48:** Prohibit slaughter of **cows, calves, milch & draught cattle** and improve breeds

### 3. Liberal–Intellectual Principles
* **Ideology:** Liberalism
* **Article 44:** Secure a **Uniform Civil Code** throughout the country
* **Article 45:** Provide **early childhood care and education** for all children until age of **6 years**
* **Article 48:** Organise **agriculture and animal husbandry** on modern & scientific lines
* **Article 48A:** Protect & improve **environment** and safeguard **forests & wild life**
* **Article 49:** Protect **monuments, places, and objects of artistic/historic interest** declared of national importance
* **Article 50:** **Separate the judiciary from the executive** in public services of the State
* **Article 51:** Promote **international peace and security**, maintain just/honourable relations, foster respect for international law/treaties, encourage arbitration of disputes

## NEW DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES

### Added by 42nd Amendment Act of 1976 (4 Directives)

2. Equal justice and free legal aid to poor (**Article 39A**)
3. Participation of workers in management of industries (**Article 43A**)
4. Protection/improvement of environment, safeguarding forests & wildlife (**Article 48A**)

### Added by 44th Amendment Act of 1978 (1 Directive)
* Minimise inequalities in income, status, facilities, and opportunities (**Article 38**)

### Modified by 86th Amendment Act of 2002
* Changed subject matter of **Article 45** to early childhood care/education (< 6 years); made elementary education (6–14 yrs) a Fundamental Right (**Article 21A**)

### Added by 97th Amendment Act of 2011
* Promotion of voluntary, autonomous, democratic, professional management of co-operative societies (**Article 43B**)

## SANCTION BEHIND DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES
* **Origin of Split:** **Sir B.N. Rau** (Constitutional Advisor) recommended dividing rights into **justiciable (Part III)** & **non-justiciable (Part IV)**
* **Real Sanction:** **Political / Public Opinion** (electorate)
  * *Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar:* No ministry responsible to people can afford to ignore Part IV
  * *Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:* Govt ignoring DPSPs will answer to electorate at election time
* **3 Reasons for Non-Justiciability:**
  1. Lack of sufficient **financial resources**
  2. Vast **diversity and backwardness** in the country
  3. Newly independent State would be crushed under burden if compelled on timeline/mode

## CRITICISM OF THE DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES
1. **No Legal Force:** Non-justiciable character
   * *K.T. Shah:* **“Pious superfluities”** & **“a cheque on a bank, payable only when resources permit”**
   * *Nasiruddin:* **“No better than new year’s resolutions broken on 2nd of January”**
   * *T.T. Krishnamachari:* **“A veritable dustbin of sentiments”**
   * *K.C. Wheare:* **“Manifesto of aims and aspirations”**, **“moral homily”**
   * *Sir Ivor Jennings:* **“Pious aspirations”**
2. **Illogically Arranged:** Lack of logical arrangement or consistent philosophy
   * *N. Srinivasan:* Neither properly classified nor logically arranged; mixes modern with old & reason with sentiment
   * *Sir Ivor Jennings:* No consistent philosophy
3. **Conservative:** Based on 19th-century political philosophy
   * *Sir Ivor Jennings:* **“Ghosts of Sydney Webb and Beatrice Webb stalk through the pages... Fabian Socialism without the socialism”**; outdated for 21st century
4. **Constitutional Conflict (*K. Santhanam*):** Leads to potential conflicts between:
   * Centre and States
   * President and Prime Minister
   * Governor and Chief Minister

## UTILITY OF DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES
* **Fundamental to Governance:** Life-giving provisions embodiment of social justice
* **Scholarly Views:**
  * *L.M. Singhvi:* **“Life giving provisions... stuff of the Constitution and its philosophy of social justice”**
  * *M.C. Chagla:* If fully carried out, India would be **“a heaven on earth”** (welfare state)
  * *Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:* Lays down goal of **“economic democracy”** (distinguished from political democracy)
  * *Granville Austin:* **“Furthering the goals of the social revolution”**
  * *Sir B.N. Rau:* **“Moral precepts... educative value”**
* **M.C. Setalvad (former AG) & General Utility:**
  1. Acts as an **‘Instrument of Instructions’** reminding authorities of new socio-economic order
  2. Serves as **beacon-lights / guides** to courts in judicial review
  3. Dominating background to all State legislative/executive action
  4. Amplifies the **Preamble** (justice, liberty, equality, fraternity)
  5. Facilitates **stability and continuity** in domestic & foreign policies despite party changes
  6. **Supplementary to Fundamental Rights** (fills vacuum by providing social/economic rights)
  7. Creates atmosphere for full enjoyment of FRs
  8. Enables **Opposition** to exercise control/criticism over government
  9. Serves as **crucial test** for government performance
  10. Common **political manifesto** for ruling parties

## CONFLICT BETWEEN FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS AND DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES
* **Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951):**
  * SC ruled **FRs prevail over DPSPs**; DPSPs run as subsidiary to FRs
  * Held FRs can be amended by Parliament (led to **1st, 4th, 17th Amendments**)
* **Golaknath Case (1967):**
  * SC ruled Parliament **cannot take away or abridge FRs** to implement DPSPs (FRs are sacrosanct)
* **24th (1971) & 25th (1971) Amendment Acts:**
  * **24th Amendment:** Empowered Parliament to abridge FRs via constitutional amendment
  * **25th Amendment (Inserted Article 31C):**
    1. Law implementing **Article 39(b) or (c)** DPSPs cannot be declared void for violating **Article 14, 19, or 31**
    2. Declaration in such law cannot be questioned in court on grounds of not giving effect to policy
* **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973):**
  * SC upheld **1st provision** of Article 31C
  * Declared **2nd provision** of Article 31C unconstitutional/invalid (Judicial Review = Basic Structure)
* **42nd Amendment Act (1976):**
  * Extended 1st provision of Article 31C to give primacy to **ALL DPSPs** over Articles 14, 19, 31
* **Minerva Mills Case (1980):**
  * SC struck down 42nd Amendment extension → Restored **DPSPs subordinate to FRs** (except Art 39(b) & (c) over Art 14 & 19)
  * Held Constitution is founded on **bedrock of balance & harmony between FRs and DPSPs** (**“two wheels of a chariot”**; balance = Basic Structure)
* **44th Amendment Act (1978):** Omitted Article 31 (Right to Property)
* **Present Position:** **FRs enjoy supremacy over DPSPs**; Parliament can amend FRs to implement DPSPs without altering **Basic Structure**

## IMPLEMENTATION OF DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES
1. **Planning & Development:** **Planning Commission (1950)** replaced by **NITI Aayog (2015)** for planned socio-economic growth
2. **Land Reforms:** Abolition of intermediaries (zamindars), tenancy reforms, land ceilings, distribution of surplus land, cooperative farming
3. **Labour Protections:** Minimum Wages Act (1948), Payment of Bonus Act (1965), Contract Labour Act (1970), Bonded Labour Abolition Act (1976), Child & Adolescent Labour Act (1986/2016), Factories Act (1948), Mines Act (1952)
4. **Women Protections:** Maternity Benefit Act (1961), Equal Remuneration Act (1976)
5. **Financial Wealth Distribution:** Nationalisation of life insurance (1956), commercial banks (1969), general insurance (1971); abolition of Privy Purses (1971)
6. **Free Legal Aid & Lok Adalats:** **Legal Services Authorities Act (1987)** established free legal aid & Lok Adalats (statutory civil court status, binding awards)
7. **Cottage Industry Promotion:** Khadi & Village Industries Board/Commission, Small-Scale Industries Board, NSIC, Coir Board, Silk Board
8. **Rural & Employment Schemes:** CDP (1952), IRDP (1978), Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (1989), MGNREGA (2006)
9. **Environment & Wildlife:** Wildlife Protection Act (1972), Forest Conservation Act (1980), Water/Air Pollution Control Acts, National Forest Policy (1988)
10. **Panchayati Raj:** **73rd Amendment Act (1992)** constitutionalised 3-tier local government
11. **Weaker Sections Protection:** Protection of Civil Rights Act (1976), SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act (1989), **65th Amendment (1990)** NCSCST → **89th Amendment (2003)** split into NCSC & NCST
12. **Commissions:** NCBC (1993, constitutionalised by **102nd Amendment 2018**), National Commission for Minorities (1993), NCW (1992), NCPCR (2007)
13. **EWS Reservation:** **103rd Amendment Act (2019)** provided 10% reservation for EWS
14. **Judiciary-Executive Separation:** **CrPC (1973)** separated judicial powers from executive district authorities (Collectors/Tehsildars)
15. **Monuments Protection:** Ancient & Historical Monuments Act (1951)
16. **Health:** Primary Health Centres, disease eradication schemes (malaria, TB, AIDS)
17. **Cow Slaughter Prohibition:** Enacted in various states
18. **Foreign Policy:** Non-alignment and Panchsheel (**Article 51**)

## DIRECTIVES OUTSIDE PART IV
* **1. Article 335 (Part XVI):** Claims of SCs and STs to services/posts (taking into account maintenance of efficiency of administration)
* **2. Article 350-A (Part XVII):** Facilities for instruction in **mother-tongue** at primary stage of education to children of linguistic minority groups
* **3. Article 351 (Part XVII):** Duty of Union to promote spread and development of **Hindi language**
* **Status:** Non-justiciable in nature, but given equal importance and attention by judiciary

## TABLES AT A GLANCE

### Distinction Between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles
| Fundamental Rights | Directive Principles |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1. Negative (prohibit State from doing certain things) | 1. Positive (require State to do certain things) |
| 2. Justiciable (legally enforceable by courts) | 2. Non-justiciable (not legally enforceable by courts) |
| 3. Aim at establishing **political democracy** | 3. Aim at establishing **social and economic democracy** |
| 4. Have **legal sanctions** | 4. Have **moral and political sanctions** |
| 5. Promote individual welfare (**individualistic**) | 5. Promote community welfare (**socialistic**) |
| 6. Automatically enforced (no legislation required) | 6. Require legislation for implementation |
| 7. Courts bound to declare violative laws unconstitutional | 7. Courts cannot declare violative laws unconstitutional (can only uphold law enacted to give effect to DPSP) |
