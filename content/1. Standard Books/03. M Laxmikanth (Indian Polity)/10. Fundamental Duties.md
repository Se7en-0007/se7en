## INTRODUCTION
* **Rights & Duties:** Correlative and inseparable
* **Original Constitution:** Contained Fundamental Rights (Part III) & DPSPs (Part IV), but **NO Fundamental Duties**
* **Addition:** **42nd Amendment Act (1976)** added Part IVA & Article 51A (**10 duties**); **86th Amendment Act (2002)** added **11th duty**
* **Inspiration:** Borrowed from Constitution of erstwhile **USSR**
* **Democratic Constitutions:** Major democracies (USA, Canada, France, Germany, Australia) do **not** contain duties; **Japan** is perhaps the **only** democratic constitution with a list of duties
* **Socialist Constitutions:** Give equal importance to rights and duties (e.g., USSR Constitution)

## SWARAN SINGH COMMITTEE RECOMMENDATIONS
* **1976 Setup:** Congress Party appointed **Sardar Swaran Singh Committee** during internal emergency (1975–1977)
* **Objective:** Recommend need and necessity of fundamental duties
* **Recommendation:** Incorporate a separate chapter on fundamental duties in Constitution → Enacted via **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**
* **New Part:** **Part IVA** (consisting of single **Article 51A**) specifying 10 fundamental duties
* **Committee vs Enacted:** Swaran Singh Committee suggested **8 duties**; 42nd Amendment included **10 duties**
* **Rejected / Unincorporated Recommendations of Swaran Singh Committee:**
  1. Parliament may impose **penalty or punishment** for non-compliance / refusal to observe duties
  2. No law imposing such penalty or punishment shall be questioned in any court on grounds of FR violation or repugnancy
  3. **Duty to pay taxes** should be a Fundamental Duty

## LIST OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES
* **Article 51A:** Duty of every citizen of India:
  1. **51A(a):** Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem
  2. **51A(b):** Cherish and follow the noble ideals that inspired the national struggle for freedom
  3. **51A(c):** Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India
  4. **51A(d):** Defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so
  5. **51A(e):** Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India transcending religious, linguistic, and regional or sectional diversities; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women
  6. **51A(f):** Value and preserve the rich heritage of the country’s composite culture
  7. **51A(g):** Protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife, and have compassion for living creatures
  8. **51A(h):** Develop scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform
  9. **51A(i):** Safeguard public property and abjure violence
  10. **51A(j):** Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement
  11. **51A(k):** Provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years (*Added by **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002***)

## FEATURES OF THE FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES
1. **Moral vs Civic Duties:**
   * **Moral Duty:** Cherishing noble ideals of freedom struggle
   * **Civic Duty:** Respecting Constitution, National Flag, and National Anthem
2. **Indian Ethos:** Codification of tasks integral to Indian way of life, tradition, mythology, and practices
3. **Confined to Citizens:** Applicable **ONLY to citizens** (do not extend to foreigners, unlike some FRs)
4. **Non-Justiciable:** Not directly enforceable by courts; no legal sanction in Constitution for violation (Parliament free to enforce via legislation)

---

## CRITICISM OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES
1. **Non-Exhaustive:** Omits key duties like voting, paying taxes, family planning (*duty to pay taxes recommended by Swaran Singh Committee*)
2. **Vague and Ambiguous:** Contains complex phrases difficult for common man to understand (*‘noble ideals’, ‘composite culture’, ‘scientific temper’*)
3. **Code of Moral Precepts:** Non-justiciable character renders them mere moral guidance
4. **Superfluous:** Law-abiding citizens perform duties naturally without constitutional listing (*C.K. Daphtary, A.K. Sen*)
5. **Secondary Status:** Added as an appendage to Part IV (DPSPs); critics argue placement after Part III would put them on par with FRs

---

## SIGNIFICANCE OF FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES
1. **Civic Reminder:** Reminds citizens of duties owed to nation or society while enjoying rights
2. **Warning Beacon:** Warning against anti-national and anti-social activities (flag burning, destroying public property)
3. **Source of Inspiration:** Promotes discipline, commitment, and active participation in national goals
4. **Judicial Interpretation:**
   * **Mohan Kumar Singhania case (1991):** SC ruled Art 51A can be used to interpret ambiguous laws and determine constitutionality
   * **Ramlila Maidan Incident case (2012):** SC observed common thread runs through Parts III, IV, and IVA
1. **Legal Enforcement:** Parliament can legislate penalties or punishments for failure to fulfill duties
2. **Political Consensus:**
   * **H.R. Gokhale (Law Minister):** Sobering effect on anti-national subversive agitations
   * **Indira Gandhi (PM):** Establishes democratic balance between rights and duties
   * **Janata Govt (Morarji Desai):** Retained FDs via 43rd & 44th Amendments (1977, 1978), demonstrating cross-party consensus

## VERMA COMMITTEE OBSERVATIONS
* **Verma Committee on Fundamental Duties of Citizens (1999):** Identified existing legal provisions implementing Fundamental Duties:
  1. **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act (1971):** Prevents disrespect to Constitution, National Flag, and National Anthem
  2. **Criminal Laws (IPC):** Punishments for encouraging enmity between groups (religion, race, language, place of birth)
  3. **Protection of Civil Rights Act (1955):** Punishments for offences related to caste and religion
  4. **Indian Penal Code (IPC):** Declares assertions prejudicial to national integration as punishable
  5. **Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (1967):** Declaration of communal or anti-national organisations as unlawful associations
  6. **Representation of People Act (1951):** Disqualification of MPs or MLAs for corrupt practices (soliciting votes on religion or caste or promoting enmity)
  7. **Wildlife (Protection) Act (1972):** Prohibits trade in rare and endangered species
  8. **Forest (Conservation) Act (1980):** Checks indiscriminate deforestation and forest land diversion
