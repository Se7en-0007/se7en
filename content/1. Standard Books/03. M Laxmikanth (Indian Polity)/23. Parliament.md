## INTRODUCTION & ORGANISATION OF PARLIAMENT
* **Location:** Enshrined in **Part V** of the Constitution (**Articles 79 to 122**)
* **Organ of Union:** Legislative organ of Union Government (**Westminster Model**)
* **3 Parts of Parliament:** **President**, **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** and **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**
* **Adoption of Hindi Names:** Adopted in **1954** for Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha respectively
* **Role of President:** Integral part of Parliament (assent required for bill to become law; summons, prorogues, dissolves Lok Sabha, addresses Parliament, issues ordinances); President is **NOT a member** of either House
* **Global Comparison:**
  * **British Pattern:** Crown + House of Lords (Upper) + House of Commons (Lower) → *‘Crown-in-Parliament’*
  * **American Pattern:** President is **NOT** part of Congress (Senate + House of Representatives) due to strict separation of powers

## COMPOSITION OF THE TWO HOUSES

### Composition of Rajya Sabha
* **Maximum Strength:** **250** (238 elected indirectly from States/UTs + 12 nominated by President)
* **Present Strength:** **245** (225 States + 8 UTs + 12 nominated)
* **Allocation of Seats:** **Fourth Schedule** of the Constitution
* **Representation of States:**
  * Elected by **elected members of State Legislative Assemblies** via **Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote**
  * Seats allocated based on **population** (e.g., UP = 31, Tripura = 1; *vs USA Senate = equal representation of 2 seats per state, 100 total*)
* **Representation of Union Territories:** Elected indirectly by electoral college constituted for the purpose (Delhi = 3, Puducherry = 1, J&K = 4; other UTs lack representation due to small population)
* **Nominated Members:** **12 members** nominated by President from art, literature, science and social service

### Composition of Lok Sabha
* **Maximum Strength:** **550** (originally 552; 530 from States + 20 from UTs)
  * *Discontinuation of Anglo-Indian Nomination:* **104th Amendment Act (2019)** discontinued nomination of 2 Anglo-Indians (w.e.f. Jan 25, 2020)
* **Present Strength:** **543** (524 States + 19 UTs)
* **Representation of States:** Directly elected by people from territorial constituencies on basis of **Universal Adult Franchise**
  * *Voting Age:* Reduced from 21 to **18 years** by **61st Amendment Act, 1988** (w.e.f. 1989)
* **Representation of UTs:** Direct election per **Union Territories (Direct Election to the House of the People) Act, 1965**

## SYSTEM OF ELECTIONS TO LOK SABHA

### Territorial Constituencies
* **Uniformity of Representation:**
  1. Ratio between seats allotted and population is same for all states (*not applicable to states with population < 6 million*)
  2. Ratio between constituency population and seats allotted is uniform throughout the state

### Readjustment after each Census
* **Delimitation Commission Acts:** Enacted in **1952, 1962, 1972 and 2002**
* **42nd Amendment Act (1976):** Froze seat allocation and constituency division till 2000 at **1971 level**
* **84th Amendment Act (2001):** Extended seat freeze till **2026**; allowed constituency rationalisation based on **1991 census**
* **87th Amendment Act (2003):** Provided delimitation of constituencies based on **2001 census** (without altering total state seat count)

### Reservation of Seats for SCs and STs
* **Basis:** Reservation provided based on **population ratios** (no separate electorate; elected by all voters; SC/ST candidate can contest general seats)
* **104th Amendment Act (2019):** Extended SC/ST seat reservation in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies until **2030**
* **84th (2001) / 87th (2003) Amendments:** Refixed reserved seats on 2001 census basis

### First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) System
* **Adopted for Lok Sabha:** Single-member territorial constituencies; candidate securing simple majority declared elected
* **Proportional Representation (PR) Rejected for Lok Sabha:** Rejected due to high voter illiteracy, complexity and risk of government instability from party proliferation (*PR used for Rajya Sabha, State Legislative Councils, President, Vice-President*)

## DURATION OF TWO HOUSES

### Duration of Rajya Sabha
* **Permanent Body:** Continuing chamber; **not subject to dissolution** (first constituted in 1952)
* **Retirement Cycle:** **1/3rd of members retire every 2nd year** (eligible for re-election/renomination indefinitely)
* **Term of Member:** **6 years** per **Representation of the People Act (1951)** (*Constitution left term determination to Parliament*)
* **1st Rajya Sabha Order:** President issued *Rajya Sabha (Term of Office of Members) Order, 1952* → Order of retirement in 1st batch decided by **lottery**

### Duration of Lok Sabha
* **Normal Term:** **5 years** from date of 1st meeting after general elections (automatically dissolves)
* **Early Dissolution:** President authorised to dissolve Lok Sabha anytime (irrevocable & non-challengeable in court)
* **National Emergency Extension:** Parliament can extend term by law for **1 year at a time** (cannot extend beyond **6 months** post-emergency; *5th Lok Sabha extended twice*)

## MEMBERSHIP OF PARLIAMENT

### Qualifications (Article 84)
1. Citizen of India
2. Oath or affirmation before person authorised by Election Commission
3. Age: Not less than **30 years for Rajya Sabha** and **25 years for Lok Sabha**
4. Additional qualifications under **Representation of the People Act (1951)**:
   * Must be registered as an **elector for a parliamentary constituency** (*2003 amendment dispensed with state residency requirement for RS, upheld by SC 2006*)
   * Must belong to SC/ST to contest reserved seats (can also contest general seats)

### Disqualifications (Constitutional & Statutory)
* **Constitutional Grounds:**
  1. Holds office of profit under Union or State Govt (except Minister or exempted office)
  2. Unsound mind (declared by court)
  3. Undischarged insolvent
  4. Non-citizen or voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship
  5. Disqualified under any parliamentary law
* **Representation of the People Act (1951) Grounds:**
  1. Convicted for electoral offences or corrupt practices
  2. Convicted for offence resulting in **imprisonment for 2 or more years** (*preventive detention is NOT a disqualification*)
  3. Failed to lodge election expense account in time
  4. Interest in government contracts, works or services
  5. Director/managing agent in corporation with ≥25% government share
  6. Dismissed from government service for corruption or disloyalty
  7. Convicted for promoting enmity or untouchability/dowry/sati
* **Decision on Disqualifications:** President’s decision is **final** (must obtain binding opinion of **Election Commission**)

### Disqualification on Ground of Defection (Tenth Schedule)
* **Grounds:** Voluntarily giving up party membership; voting/abstaining contrary to party direction; independent member joining any party; nominated member joining party **after 6 months**
* **Deciding Authority:** **Chairman (Rajya Sabha)** / **Speaker (Lok Sabha)**
* **Judicial Review:** Deciding authority's decision is subject to **judicial review** (***Kihoto Hollohan case, 1992***)

### Vacating of Seats
1. **Double Membership (RPA 1951 & Rules):**
   * Elected to both Houses → Must intimate choice within **10 days** (default: **RS seat vacant**)
   * Sitting member elected to other House → **1st House seat vacant**
   * Elected to 2 seats in same House → Must exercise option (default: **both seats vacant**)
   * Elected to Parliament + State Legislature → Resign state seat within **14 days** (default: **Parliament seat vacant**)
2. **Disqualification:** Under Constitution or 10th Schedule
3. **Resignation:** Addressed to Speaker/Chairman (accepted unless non-voluntary/not genuine)
4. **Absence:** Absent from all meetings for **60 consecutive days** without permission (excluding prorogation/adjournment > 4 days)
5. **Other Cases:** Election declared void by High Court (*RPA 1951*); expelled by House; elected President/VP; appointed Governor

### Oath or Affirmation (Article 99)
* Administered by **President** or appointed person; failure to take oath bars voting/proceedings/privileges
* Penalty for sitting/voting without oath/disqualified = **₹500 per day**

### Salaries and Allowances
* Determined by Parliament (**Salary, Allowances and Pension of Members of Parliament Act, 1954**; amended 2018):
  * Salary = **₹1,00,000/month**
  * Constituency Allowance = **₹70,000/month**
  * Office Expenses = **₹60,000/month**
  * Daily Allowance = **₹2,000/day**
  * Pension = **₹25,000/month** (+₹2,000/month for every excess year served beyond 5 years)
* **Officers of Parliament Salaries:** Determined by Parliament (**Salaries and Allowances of Officers of Parliament Act, 1953**; amended 2018); **charged on Consolidated Fund of India** (non-votable); RS Chairman salary = **₹4,00,000/month**

## PRESIDING OFFICERS OF PARLIAMENT

### Speaker of Lok Sabha
* **Election & Tenure:**
  * Elected by Lok Sabha from amongst its members; election date fixed by **President**
  * Vacates office if: (a) Ceases to be LS member, (b) Resigns in writing to **Deputy Speaker**, (c) Removed by resolution passed by **Effective Majority** (majority of all the then members) with **14 days' advance notice**
  * Cannot preside during removal resolution (can speak, take part and **vote in 1st instance**, but not casting vote)
  * Does **NOT vacate office upon LS dissolution** (continues till 1st meeting of new LS)
* **Powers & Functions:**
  1. Maintains order and decorum in House (final authority)
  2. Final interpreter of Constitution, Rules of Procedure and Parliamentary Precedents within House
  3. Adjourns House or suspends meeting in absence of **quorum (1/10th)**
  4. Casting vote in case of a tie
  5. Presides over **Joint Sitting** of Parliament
  6. Allows secret sitting on request of Leader of House
  7. Final authority to decide whether a bill is a **Money Bill** (endorses certificate)
  8. Decides 10th Schedule defection questions (*subject to judicial review, Kihoto Hollohan 1992*)
  9. Ex-officio chairman of Indian Parliamentary Group & Presiding Officers Conference
  10. Appoints chairmen of parliamentary committees; chairs **Business Advisory, Rules and General Purposes Committees**
* **Independence & Impartiality:** Security of tenure; charged expenses; conduct discussed only on substantive motion; 7th rank in Precedence (equal to CJI, above Cabinet Ministers except PM/Deputy PM)
* **Historical Origins:**
  * GOI Act 1919 (1921): President & Deputy President → **Frederick Whyte** (1st President), **Sachidanand Sinha** (1st Deputy President)
  * **Vithalbhai J. Patel:** 1st Indian and 1st elected Speaker of Central Legislative Assembly (**1925**)
  * GOI Act 1935 renamed to Speaker/Deputy Speaker (adopted 1947)
  * **G.V. Mavalankar** & **Ananthasayanam Ayyangar:** 1st Speaker and 1st Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha

### Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha
* Elected by LS from amongst members; election date fixed by **Speaker**
* Vacates office if: ceases member, resigns to **Speaker**, or removed by **Effective Majority** (14 days notice)
* Assumes all powers of Speaker when office vacant or Speaker absent; presides over Joint Sitting in Speaker's absence
* **NOT subordinate to Speaker** (directly responsible to House)
* **Special Privilege:** Automatically becomes Chairman of any parliamentary committee if appointed member
* Convention: Speaker from ruling party/alliance, Deputy Speaker from **opposition**

### Panel of Chairpersons of Lok Sabha
* **Nominated by Speaker** (max **10 members**); presides in absence of Speaker/Deputy Speaker
* Cannot preside when office of Speaker/Deputy Speaker is **vacant** (President appoints member for vacancy)

### Speaker Pro Tem
* Appointed by **President** (usually seniormost member) when last LS Speaker vacates before 1st meeting of new LS
* Administers oath to new members & enables election of new Speaker; temporary office

### Chairman of Rajya Sabha
* **Ex-Officio Chairman:** **Vice-President of India**
* Removed ONLY when removed from Vice-President office
* Powers similar to Speaker **EXCEPT**:
  1. Speaker decides Money Bill (Chairman has no such power)
  2. Speaker presides over Joint Sitting (Chairman **NEVER presides**)
* **NOT a member** of Rajya Sabha; casting vote only; cannot preside during removal resolution
* Salary charged on Consolidated Fund of India (**₹4 lakh/month**)

### Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha
* Elected by Rajya Sabha from amongst members
* Vacates if ceases member, resigns to **Chairman**, or removed by **Effective Majority** (14 days notice)
* Not subordinate to Chairman; performs duties when Chairman office vacant or VP acting as President

### Panel of Vice-Chairpersons of Rajya Sabha
* **Nominated by Chairman**; presides in absence of Chairman or Deputy Chairman

### Secretariat of Parliament
* Separate secretarial staff for each House; recruitment/conditions regulated by Parliament
* Headed by **Secretary-General** (permanent officer, appointed by Presiding Officer)

## LEADERS IN PARLIAMENT

### Leader of the House
* **Lok Sabha:** Prime Minister (if LS member) or a nominated Cabinet Minister
* **Rajya Sabha:** Nominated Cabinet Minister belonging to RS
* Exercises direct influence on conduct of business; nominates Deputy Leader

### Leader of the Opposition
* Statutory recognition under **Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977** (Cabinet Minister rank & salary)
* First recognised in **1969**
* **Requirement:** Leader of opposition party having greatest numerical strength (at least **1/10th total strength** of House)
* **British Equivalent:** **Shadow Cabinet** (Leader of Opposition = *‘alternative Prime Minister’*, Ivor Jennings)

### Whips
* Based on **parliamentary conventions** (mentioned neither in Constitution nor Rules of House)
* Assistant floor leader appointed by political party; ensures member attendance & voting per party directions
* **Government Chief Whip:** Minister of Parliamentary Affairs (LS) / MoS Parliamentary Affairs (RS)
* *Facilities Act 1998:* Secretarial facilities for recognised party (≥55 LS / ≥25 RS seats) & group (≥30 LS / ≥15 RS seats)

## SESSIONS OF PARLIAMENT

### Summoning and Recess
* **Summoning:** President summons each House; **max gap between sessions = 6 months** (must meet at least twice a year)
* **3 Annual Sessions:** Budget Session (Feb–May), Monsoon Session (July–Sept), Winter Session (Nov–Dec)
* **Recess:** Period between prorogation of a House and its reassembly in new session

### Adjournment vs Prorogation vs Dissolution

| Feature | Adjournment | Prorogation | Dissolution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Effect** | Terminates a **sitting** | Terminates a **sitting AND session** | Ends the **very life** of Lok Sabha |
| **2. Done By** | **Presiding Officer** | **President** | **President** |
| **3. Lapsing** | Does not affect pending business | Pending notices lapse; **bills do not lapse** | All pending business/bills **lapse** (subject to exceptions) |

* **Adjournment Sine Die:** Terminating sitting for an indefinite period (no day named for reassembly)

### Lapsing of Bills on Lok Sabha Dissolution
* **Bills that LAPSE:**
  1. Bill pending in Lok Sabha (originating or transmitted by RS)
  2. Bill passed by Lok Sabha but pending in Rajya Sabha
* **Bills that DO NOT LAPSE:**
  1. Bill pending in Rajya Sabha but **not passed by Lok Sabha**
  2. Bill passed by both Houses but **pending President's assent**
  3. Bill passed by both Houses but **returned by President for reconsideration**
  4. Bill where **Joint Sitting notified by President** before LS dissolution

### Quorum, Voting and Language
* **Quorum:** **1/10th of total membership** of House (LS = 55, RS = 25); Presiding officer must adjourn/suspend if no quorum
* **Voting:** Simple majority of members present and voting; Presiding Officer exercises **casting vote** only
* **Languages:** **Hindi and English** (Presiding Officer can permit mother tongue); simultaneous interpretation for 8th Schedule languages; English extended indefinitely by **Official Languages Act, 1963**
* **Rights of Ministers & Attorney General:** Right to speak/participate in either House, Joint Sitting, or Committees without voting right (unless member)
* **Lame-Duck Session:** Last session of preceding Lok Sabha post-elections before new Lok Sabha constituted; non-reelected members = *'lame-ducks'*

## TYPES OF MAJORITY

### 1. Simple Majority
* Majority of members **present and voting** (ordinary / functional / working majority; Article 100 general rule)
* **Used for:** Ordinary/Money/Financial Bills, Adjournment/No-Confidence/Confidence/Censure/Thanks Motions, President's Rule (Art 356), Financial Emergency (Art 360), VP removal in LS (Art 67), Speaker/Deputy Speaker/Deputy Chairman elections, disapproving National Emergency

### 2. Effective Majority
* Majority of **effective strength** (Total membership minus vacant seats); *"majority of all the then members"*
* **Used for:** Removal of Vice-President in RS (Art 67), removal of Deputy Chairman RS (Art 90), removal of Speaker & Deputy Speaker LS (Art 94)

### 3. Absolute Majority
* Majority of **total membership** of House (>50% of total strength, regardless of vacancies/absentees)
* **Used for:** Component of Special Majority-I (not standalone)

### 4. Special Majority
* **Special Majority-I:** >50% total membership + **2/3rd members present and voting**
  * *Used for:* Constitutional Amendment (Art 368), SC/HC Judges removal (Art 124/217), CAG removal (Art 148), CEC/SEC removal, National Emergency approval (Art 352)
* **Special Majority-II:** **2/3rd of total membership** of House
  * *Used for:* Impeachment of President (Art 61)
* **Special Majority-III:** **2/3rd of members present and voting in Rajya Sabha**
  * *Used for:* Creating New All-India Services (Art 312), Parliamentary legislation on State List (Art 249)

## DEVICES OF PARLIAMENTARY PROCEEDINGS
* **Question Hour:** First hour of sitting; Starred (oral, supplementaries allowed, green), Unstarred (written, no supplementaries, white), Short Notice (<10 days notice, oral, pink), Questions to Private Members (yellow)
* **Zero Hour:** Indian innovation (**1962**); informal device immediately post Question Hour; not mentioned in Rules
* **Motions:** Substantive (independent, e.g. impeachment), Substitute (replaces original), Subsidiary (Ancillary, Superseding, Amendment)
* **Closure Motion:** Simple Closure, Closure by Compartments, Kangaroo Closure (important clauses only), Guillotine (undiscussed clauses put to vote due to time limit)
* **Privilege Motion:** Censures minister for breach of privilege / withholding facts
* **Calling Attention Motion:** Draws minister's attention to urgent public importance (Indian innovation **1954**, mentioned in Rules)
* **Adjournment Motion:** Draws attention to definite urgent public importance matter; requires **50 members' support**; element of censure (**Lok Sabha only**); duration ≥ 2 hrs 30 mins
* **No-Confidence Motion:** Based on **Article 75(3)** (collective responsibility); **Lok Sabha only**; requires **50 members' support**; CoM resigns if passed
* **Confidence Motion:** Procedural device to prove majority in hung parliament / coalition
* **Censure Motion vs No-Confidence Motion:**
  * *Censure:* Must state reasons; moved against individual/group/all ministers; censures specific policies; CoM **need not resign** if passed
  * *No-Confidence:* Need not state reasons; moved against **entire CoM only**; tests majority confidence; CoM **must resign** if passed
* **Motion of Thanks:** Discussion on President's inaugural address; defeat amounts to government fall
* **Point of Order:** Raised when rules/constitutional business violated; suspends proceedings; **no debate allowed**; Speaker decides
* **Half-an-Hour Discussion:** Elucidates factual matter from previous debate (3 days/week)
* **Short Duration Discussion:** Two-hour discussion on urgent public importance (2 days/week, since 1953)
* **Special Mention:** Raising matter in Rajya Sabha (*LS equivalent = Rule 377*)
* **Resolutions:** Private Member's (alternate Friday afternoons), Government, Statutory; all resolutions are substantive motions

## LEGISLATIVE PROCEDURE IN PARLIAMENT

### Public Bill vs Private Bill
* **Public Bill:** Introduced by minister; reflects govt policy; 7 days notice; drafted by department/law ministry
* **Private Bill:** Introduced by non-minister MP; 1 month notice; drafting responsibility of member

### Ordinary Bills (5 Stages)
1. **First Reading:** Title & objectives read; publication in Gazette (no discussion)
2. **Second Reading:** Scrutiny stage: (a) General Discussion, (b) Committee Stage (Select/Joint Committee clause-by-clause), (c) Consideration Stage (voting on clauses & amendments)
3. **Third Reading:** Debate confined to acceptance/rejection as a whole; passed by **simple majority**
4. **Bill in Second House:** Options: Pass, pass with amendments, reject, or keep pending (6 months deadlock → **Joint Sitting**)
5. **Assent of President:** Assent (Act), Withhold (fails), or Return for reconsideration (**Suspensive Veto**)

### Money Bills (Article 110)
* **Definition:** Provisions dealing **ONLY** with tax imposition/alteration, govt borrowing, Consolidated/Contingency Fund custody/appropriation, charged expenditure. (*Excludes fines, license fees, local taxes*)
* **Speaker's Decision:** Final & non-challengeable in court, Parliament, or President
* **Procedure:** Introduced **ONLY in Lok Sabha**, **ONLY on President's recommendation**, **ONLY by a minister**
* **Rajya Sabha Role:** Restricted powers; **cannot amend or reject**; 14-day return limit (recommendations optional for LS)
* **Assent:** President gives assent or withholds (**cannot return** for reconsideration); **NO Joint Sitting**

### Financial Bills

| Feature                  | Financial Bill (I) - Art 117(1)             | Financial Bill (II) - Art 117(3)                              |
| :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Content**              | Art 110 matters + general legislation       | Expenditure from Consolidated Fund (no Art 110 matter)        |
| **Introduction**         | **Lok Sabha only**                          | **Either House**                                              |
| **Prior Recommendation** | Required for introduction                   | Not required for introduction; **required for consideration** |
| **Passage Procedure**    | Same as Ordinary Bill (RS can amend/reject) | Same as Ordinary Bill (RS can amend/reject)                   |
| **Joint Sitting**        | **Applicable**                              | **Applicable**                                                |

## JOINT SITTING OF TWO HOUSES (Article 108)
* **Extraordinary Mechanism:** Resolves deadlock over Ordinary or Financial Bills (**NOT Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills**)
* **Deadlock Conditions:** Bill rejected by second House; Houses disagree on amendments; >6 months elapsed without passage
* **Presiding Order:** Speaker → Deputy Speaker → Deputy Chairman RS → Person determined by sitting (*RS Chairman NEVER presides*)
* **Quorum:** **1/10th combined strength** of both Houses; passed by **simple majority** of total members present & voting (Lok Sabha usually prevails)
* **3 Historical Instances of Joint Sitting:**
  1. **Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1960** (sitting held May 1961)
  2. **Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977** (sitting held May 1978)
  3. **Prevention of Terrorism Bill, 2002** (sitting held March 2002)

## BUDGET IN PARLIAMENT

### Concept and Constitutional Provisions
* **Term:** Referred as **‘Annual Financial Statement’ (Article 112)**; financial year = April 1 to March 31
* **Railway Budget Merger:** Merged into General Budget in **2017** (separated in 1924 on **Acworth Committee Report, 1921**)
* **Key Provisions:** Demands for Grants on President's recommendation (**Art 113**); withdrawal only via Appropriation Act (**Art 114**); tax money bills require President's recommendation (**Art 117**); Parliament can reduce/abolish tax, **cannot increase** (**Art 117**)

### Charged Expenditure
* **Non-Votable:** Can only be discussed by Parliament (**Article 113**)
* **Includes:** President's emoluments; Presiding Officers' salaries/allowances; SC judges' salaries/pensions; HC judges' pensions; CAG salary/pension; UPSC Chairman/members' salaries/pensions; debt charges; court decrees/judgments

### 6 Stages in Budget Enactment
1. **Presentation:** Advanced to **1st February** (since 2017); Budget Speech by Finance Minister; laid before RS
2. **General Discussion:** 3–4 days in both Houses; general principles discussed; **no voting or cut motions**
3. **Scrutiny by Departmental Standing Committees:** Houses adjourned for 3–4 weeks; **24 DRSCs** examine Demands for Grants in depth
4. **Voting on Demands for Grants:** **Exclusive to Lok Sabha**; votable part only; Cut Motions:
   * **Policy Cut Motion:** Disapproval of policy; demand reduced to **₹1**
   * **Economy Cut Motion:** Affects economy; demand reduced by **specified amount**
   * **Token Cut Motion:** Ventilates specific grievance; demand reduced by **₹100**
   * *Guillotine:* Speaker puts all remaining undiscussed demands to vote on last allotted day
5. **Passing of Appropriation Bill:** Authorises withdrawal from Consolidated Fund of India (**Article 114**)
   * *Vote on Account (Art 116):* Advance grant for 2 months (1/6th total estimation) pending budget passage; largely avoided post-2017 advancement
6. **Passing of Finance Bill:** Legalises income/taxation proposals; must be enacted within **75 days** (*Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 1931*)

### Other Grants
* **Supplementary Grant:** Authorised amount insufficient for current year
* **Additional Grant:** Expenditure needed upon new service not in budget
* **Excess Grant:** Money spent in excess of grant (*voted by LS post-financial year; requires **PAC approval** first*)
* **Vote of Credit:** Blank cheque for unexpected demand of indefinite character
* **Exceptional Grant:** Special purpose non-routine grant
* **Token Grant:** Reappropriation of funds for new service (**₹1 nominal demand**)

### 3 Constitutional Funds
1. **Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266):** All revenues, loans raised, & loan repayments credited; payments require parliamentary law
2. **Public Account of India (Article 266):** Provident funds, judicial deposits, savings bank deposits; operated by **executive action** (banking transactions)
3. **Contingency Fund of India (Article 267):** Placed at President's disposal (*Contingency Fund of India Act 1950*); held by Finance Secretary; advances for unforeseen expenditure pending parliamentary authorisation; operated by **executive action**

## MULTIFUNCTIONAL ROLE OF PARLIAMENT & INEFFECTIVENESS OF CONTROL
* **Roles:** Legislative, Executive, Financial, Constituent (subject to **Basic Structure**, *Kesavananda Bharati 1973*), Judicial (impeach President, remove VP/judges/CAG/CEC, punish contempt), Electoral, Deliberative
* **Rule of Lapse:** Unspent granted money expires at financial year-end and returns to Consolidated Fund → Causes **‘March Rush’** (heavy year-end spending)
* **Factors for Ineffectiveness of Control:** Lack of time & technical expertise, executive legislative leadership, massive unmanageable size, executive majority support, post-mortem nature of PAC, guillotine usage, delegated legislation, frequent ordinances, lack of strong opposition

## POSITION OF RAJYA SABHA
* **Equal Status with Lok Sabha:** Ordinary bills, Constitutional amendment bills, Financial bills (I) passage, President election/impeachment, VP election/removal initiation, SC/HC judges/CAG/CEC removal, Ordinances & Emergency proclamations approval, Minister selection
* **Unequal Status to Lok Sabha:** Money bills (no introduction, amendment, or rejection; 14-day limit), Speaker decides Money Bill, Speaker presides Joint Sitting, Demands for Grants voting (LS exclusive), National Emergency discontinuance resolution (LS only), No-confidence motion (LS only)
* **Special Powers of Rajya Sabha (4 Exclusive Powers):**
  1. Authorises State List legislation (**Article 249**, 2/3rd present & voting)
  2. Authorises creation of new All-India Services (**Article 312**, 2/3rd present & voting)
  3. Initiates Vice-President removal (**Article 67**)
  4. Approves Emergency proclamations when Lok Sabha is dissolved (**Articles 352, 356, 360**)

## PARLIAMENTARY PRIVILEGES & SOVEREIGNTY

### Parliamentary Privileges
* **Meaning:** Special rights, immunities, exemptions enjoyed by Houses, committees & members (**extends to Ministers & Attorney General; does NOT extend to President**)
* **Collective Privileges:** Right to publish/prohibit debates (*44th Amendment 1978* restored press freedom for true reports except secret sittings); exclude strangers; make rules; punish for contempt/breach; receive arrest info; courts barred from inquiring
* **Individual Privileges:** Immunity from civil arrest during session & **40 days before/after** (*no immunity in criminal/preventive detention*); freedom of speech in Parliament (**Article 105**); exemption from jury service
* **Sources:** Constitutional provisions, Parliamentary laws, Rules of Houses, Conventions, Judicial decisions (*44th Amendment 1978 dropped direct text reference to British House of Commons*)

### Sovereignty of Parliament
* **British Parliament = Sovereign** (A.V. Dicey: unlimited lawmaking, no distinction between ordinary & constitutional law, no judicial review)
* **Indian Parliament = NOT Sovereign**; authority is defined, limited and restrained by:
  1. **Written Constitution** (supreme law)
  2. **Federal System** (legislative division of powers)
  3. **Judicial Review** (SC & HCs can declare laws ultra vires)
  4. **Justiciable Fundamental Rights** (**Article 13** prohibits taking away FRs)
* *Comparison:* Indian Parliament resembles **US Congress** in limited jurisdiction
