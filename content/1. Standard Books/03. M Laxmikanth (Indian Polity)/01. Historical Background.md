## INTRODUCTION
* **1608: British arrival** (as traders, East India Company / EIC) ← **1600: Queen Elizabeth I Charter** (exclusive trading rights)
* **1764: Battle of Buxar** → **1765: Diwani Rights** (revenue & civil justice over Bengal, Bihar, Orissa) granted by Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II** → EIC = territorial power
* **1857: Sepoy Mutiny** → **1858: British Crown** assumes direct responsibility → Continues to **Aug 15, 1947 (Independence)**
* **Independence** → Need for Constitution → **1946: Constituent Assembly** formed → **Jan 26, 1950: Constitution** came into being

## THE COMPANY RULE (1773–1858)

### Regulating Act of 1773
* **Constitutional Significance:**
  * 1st step by British Gov to control & regulate EIC affairs in India
  * 1st recognition of EIC's political & administrative functions
  * Laid foundation of central administration in India
* **Core Features:**
  * **Governor of Bengal → 'Governor-General of Bengal' (GGB)** + Executive Council of 4 members to assist (1st GGB = **Lord Warren Hastings**)
  * **Governors of Bombay & Madras Presidencies** subordinated to GGB (ended independent presidency era)
  * **1774: Supreme Court at Calcutta established** = 1 Chief Justice + 3 other judges
  * **Ban on Private Trade:** EIC servants prohibited from private trade & accepting presents/bribes from 'natives'
  * **Control ↑:** British Gov control ↑ over EIC via **Court of Directors** (governing body) → mandatory reporting on revenue, civil, & military affairs in India

### Amending Act of 1781 (Act of Settlement)
* **Objective:** Rectify defects of the Regulating Act of 1773
* **Core Features:**
  * **Jurisdiction Exemption:** **GGB-in-Council** & EIC servants exempted from SC jurisdiction for official acts
  * **Revenue Excluded:** Revenue matters & collection methods excluded from SC jurisdiction
  * **Calcutta Inhabitants:** SC jurisdiction extended to all Calcutta inhabitants under personal laws (Hindus → Hindu law; Muslims → Mohammedan law)
  * **Appeals Shift:** Appeals from Provincial Courts → GG-in-Council (not SC)
  * **Regulations power:** GG-in-Council empowered to frame regulations for Provincial Courts & Councils

### Pitt’s India Act of 1784
* **Origin:** Introduced in British Parliament by PM **William Pitt**
* **Core Features:**
  * **Separation of Powers:** Distinguished commercial vs political functions of EIC
  * **System of Double Government:**
    * **Court of Directors** = managed Commercial affairs
    * **Board of Control** (new body) = managed Political affairs
  * **Supervision power:** Board of Control empowered to supervise/direct all operations of civil, military gov, or revenues
* **Significance:**
  * 1st time: EIC territories called **‘British possessions in India’**
  * British Government given supreme control over EIC affairs & administration in India

### Act of 1786
* **Origin:** **Lord Cornwallis** appointed GGB; placed 2 demands to accept post:
  1. Overriding power over Council in special cases
  2. To be the Commander-in-Chief
* **Outcome:** Act enacted both provisions

### Charter Act of 1793
* **Core Features:**
  * **Overriding Power Extended:** Cornwallis's override power extended to all future GGs & Governors of Presidencies
  * **Control ↑:** GG given more control/power over subordinate Bombay & Madras Presidencies
  * **Monopoly Extension:** EIC trade monopoly in India extended for **20 years**
  * **Commander-in-Chief:** Not a member of GG’s Council unless specifically appointed
  * **Salaries:** Board of Control members & staff paid out of Indian revenues

### Charter Act of 1813
* **Core Features:**
  * **Monopoly Ended:** EIC trade monopoly in India abolished (trade thrown open to all British merchants)
  * **Exceptions:** Trade in **tea** & trade with **China** remained monopolised by EIC
  * **Crown Sovereignty:** Asserted sovereignty of the British Crown over EIC's Indian territories
  * **Religion:** Allowed Christian missionaries to enter India for 'enlightenment'
  * **Education:** Provided for spread of western education among inhabitants
  * **Taxation:** Authorised Local Governments to impose taxes on persons (with penalty for non-payment)

### Charter Act of 1833
* **Significance:** Final step towards centralisation in British India
* **Core Features:**
  * **Governor-General of Bengal → 'Governor-General of India' (GGI)** + vested with all civil & military powers (1st GGI = **Lord William Bentinck**)
  * GGI vested with authority over entire territorial area possessed by British in India
  * **Legislative Monopoly:** Bombay & Madras Governors deprived of legislative powers; GGI got exclusive legislative powers for entire British India
  * **Terminology Change:** Laws made under previous acts = **Regulations**; Laws made under this act = **Acts**
  * **EIC Status:** Ended EIC commercial activities → became purely administrative body (territories held ‘in trust for His Majesty, His heirs and successors’)
  * **Civil Service Attempt:** Attempted to introduce open competition for civil servants (no debarring of Indians) → **Negated** due to opposition from Court of Directors

### Charter Act of 1853
* **Significance:** Last of the series of Charter Acts (1793–1853); key constitutional landmark
* **Core Features:**
  * **Council Separation:** 1st time separated legislative & executive functions of GG's Council
  * **Indian (Central) Legislative Council:** Established separate 6-member legislative council ('mini-Parliament') using British Parliament procedures
  * **Legislation = Special function** requiring special machinery & process
  * **Civil Service Open:** Introduced open competition system for civil service recruitment (covenanted civil service open to Indians)
    * **1854: Macaulay Committee** (Committee on Indian Civil Service) appointed
  * **Rule Extension:** Extended EIC rule (trust for Crown) without specifying time limit → indicated EIC rule could be terminated anytime by Parliament
  * **Local Representation:** 1st time introduced local representation in Central Legislative Council → 4 of 6 members appointed by provincial governments of Madras, Bombay, Bengal, Agra

## THE CROWN RULE (1858–1947)

### Government of India Act of 1858
* **Origin:** Enacted in wake of **Revolt of 1857** ('sepoy mutiny' / First War of Independence)
* **Alias:** **"Act for the Good Government of India"**
* **Core Features:**
  * **EIC Abolished:** Transferred government, territories, & revenues to British Crown
  * **Designation Change: GGI → Viceroy of India** (direct representative of British Crown; 1st Viceroy = **Lord Canning**)
  * **Double Government Ended:** Abolished Board of Control & Court of Directors
  * **Secretary of State for India (SoS):** New office created; member of British Cabinet, ultimately responsible to British Parliament, vested with complete authority over Indian administration
  * **Council of India:** 15-member advisory body established to assist SoS (SoS made Chairman)
  * **Corporate Body:** SoS-in-Council constituted as body corporate (capable of suing & being sued in India & England)
* **Nature:** Act confined to improving machinery in England for superintendence & control; did not substantially alter the system of government in India

### Indian Councils Act of 1861
* **Context:** Policy of association (seeking Indian cooperation post-1857 revolt; 1861, 1892, 1909)
* **Core Features:**
  * **Representative Institutions:** Viceroy to nominate Indians as non-official members to expanded legislative council
    * **1862 nomination:** Viceroy **Lord Canning** nominated: **Raja of Benaras**, **Maharaja of Patiala** and **Sir Dinkar Rao**
  * **Decentralisation Begun:** Restored legislative powers to Bombay & Madras Presidencies (reversed 1773–1833 centralisation climax) → culminated in provincial autonomy in 1937
  * **New Councils:** Established legislative councils for **Bengal** (1862), **North-western Provinces** (1886) and **Punjab** (1897)
  * **Rules & Portfolio:** Viceroy empowered to make rules/orders for council business transaction. Recognized Canning's **‘Portfolio’ system** (introduced 1859) → member made in-charge of department(s), can issue final orders
  * **Ordinances:** Viceroy empowered to issue emergency ordinances (valid for 6 months) without legislative council concurrence

### Indian Councils Act of 1892
* **Core Features:**
  * **Membership ↑:** Increased additional (non-official) members in Central & provincial legislative councils (maintained official majority)
  * **Functions ↑:** Legislative councils given power of discussing the budget (budget system introduced 1860) & addressing questions to executive
  * **Nomination Process:** Non-official members nominated by:
    * **Central Council:** Viceroy on recommendation of provincial legislative councils & Bengal Chamber of Commerce
    * **Provincial Councils:** Governors on recommendation of district boards, municipalities, universities, trade associations, zamindars, & chambers
  * **Indirect Election:** Limited & indirect provision for elections (word "election" not used; described as nomination on recommendation)

### Indian Councils Act of 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)
* **Namesakes:** **Lord Morley** (SoS) + **Lord Minto** (Viceroy)
* **Core Features:**
  * **Size ↑:** Central legislative council size increased from **16 to 60** members; provincial council sizes also increased (non-uniform)
  * **Majority Rules:** Retained official majority in Central Council; allowed non-official majority in provincial councils
  * **Deliberative Powers ↑:** Councils could ask supplementary questions, move resolutions on budget, etc.
  * **Executive Association:** 1st time associated Indians with executive councils of Viceroy & Governors
    * **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** = 1st Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council (appointed as **Law Member**)
  * **Communal Representation:** Introduced 'separate electorate' for Muslims (Muslim members elected only by Muslim voters)
    * **Lord Minto** = **"Father of Communal Electorate"** (legalised communalism)
  * **Separate representation:** Granted to presidency corporations, chambers of commerce, universities, & zamindars

### Government of India Act of 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms)
* **Context:** Aug 20, 1917 British declaration (objective: gradual introduction of responsible government); came into force 1921
* **Namesakes:** **Montagu** (SoS) + **Lord Chelmsford** (Viceroy)
* **Core Features:**
  * **Devolution Rules:** Classified administrative subjects into **Central** & **Provincial** lists → relaxed central control but structure remained centralised & unitary
  * **Provincial Dyarchy (Double Rule):** Divided provincial subjects into:
    * **Transferred subjects** (health, education, local govt, agriculture) → administered by Governor with Ministers responsible to legislative council
    * **Reserved subjects** (police, justice, prisons, land revenue, finance) → administered by Governor & Executive Council (not responsible to legislative council)
    * *Result:* Dyarchy experiment largely unsuccessful
  * **Bicameralism & Direct Elections:** Indian Legislative Council replaced by bicameral legislature:
    * **Upper House** (Council of State)
    * **Lower House** (Legislative Assembly)
    * *Note:* Majority of members in both houses chosen via direct election
  * **Executive Indianization:** Required 3 of 6 members of Viceroy’s Executive Council (excluding Commander-in-Chief) to be Indian
  * **Communal Electorate Extended:** Separate electorates granted to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans**
  * **Franchise:** Granted to a limited population based on property, tax, or qualifications
  * **High Commissioner:** Created office of High Commissioner for India in London (transferred SoS functions)
  * **Public Service Commission:** Provided for PSC; **1926: Central Public Service Commission** established for civil servant recruitment (on **1923–24 Lee Commission** recommendation)
  * **Budget Separation:** Separated provincial budgets from Central budget; provincial legislatures authorised to enact their own budgets
  * **Statutory Commission:** Provided for commission to report on working of Act after 10 years
  * **Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal):** Inaugurated 1921 (120 members: 108 Princes + 12 state reps); headed by Viceroy for consultation on common interest

### Simon Commission
* **1927 (2 years early):** 7-member statutory commission appointed under **Sir John Simon** to report on new Constitution
* **Boycott:** All members British → boycotted by all political parties
* **1930 Report Recommendations:** Abolish dyarchy, extend responsible government in provinces, establish federation of British India & princely states, continue communal electorate
* **Process:** 3 Round Table Conferences → 'White Paper on Constitutional Reforms' prepared → reviewed by Joint Select Committee → incorporated (with changes) into GOI Act of 1935

### Communal Award
* **Aug 1932:** British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** announced minority representation scheme
* **Extension:** Separate electorates continued for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans AND extended to **depressed classes (Scheduled Castes)**
* **Protest:** Gandhi undertook fast unto death in Yerawada Jail (Poona) against SC separate electorates
* **Poona Pact:** Agreement between Congress & **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** (depressed classes leader) → retained Hindu joint electorate + gave reserved seats to depressed classes
* **Outcome:** British Gov accepted and modified Communal Award; provisions incorporated into GOI Act of 1935

### Government of India Act of 1935
* **Significance:** Lengthy & detailed document (321 Sections, 10 Schedules); 2nd milestone to responsible government
* **Core Features:**
  * **All-India Federation:** Proposed federation of provinces & princely states. Power division via 3 lists:
    * **Federal List** (59 items)
    * **Provincial List** (54 items)
    * **Concurrent List** (36 items)
    * *Note:* Residuary powers vested in Viceroy. Federation never materialised (princely states refused to join)
  * **Provincial Autonomy:** Abolished provincial dyarchy; introduced provincial autonomy
    * Responsible government in provinces (Governor acts on advice of ministers responsible to legislature) → active **1937**, discontinued **1939**
  * **Central Dyarchy:** Proposed dyarchy at Centre (Reserved vs Transferred subjects) → never came into operation
  * **Bicameralism in Provinces:** Introduced bicameralism (Legislative Council + Legislative Assembly) in 6 out of 11 provinces: **Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam and United Provinces**
  * **Extended Separate Representation:** Reserved seats for depressed classes (SCs), women, & labour (workers)
  * **Council Abolished:** Abolished Council of India (est. 1858); SoS provided with advisory team
  * **Franchise:** Extended to about **14 per cent** of the total population
  * **RBI:** Established **Reserve Bank of India** to control currency & credit
  * **Public Service Commissions:** Established Federal PSC, Provincial PSC, & Joint PSC (for 2 or more provinces)
  * **Federal Court:** Established (set up in **1937**)
  * **Burma Separated:** Burma (now Myanmar) separated from India
  * **New Provinces:** Created **Orissa** & **Sind**
  * **Other bodies:** Established Federal Railway Authority & appointed Auditor-General of India

### Indian Independence Act of 1947
* **Context:**
  * **Feb 20, 1947:** British PM **Clement Attlee** declared British rule ends by June 30, 1948
  * **June 3, 1947:** British Gov declared Constitution cannot apply to unwilling parts
  * **June 3, 1947: Mountbatten Plan** (partition plan) proposed by Viceroy Lord Mountbatten; accepted by Congress & Muslim League
  * **Enactment:** Passed as Indian Independence Act 1947 (Bill introduced July 4, received Royal Assent July 18, in force Aug 15, 1947)
* **Core Features:**
  * **End of Rule:** Declared India independent from August 15, 1947
  * **Two Dominions:** Created independent dominions of **India** and **Pakistan** (right to secede from Commonwealth)
  * **Viceroy Abolished:** Replaced by Governor-General for each dominion, appointed by King on advice of dominion cabinet
  * **Sovereign Assemblies:** Constituent Assemblies of both dominions empowered to frame/adopt any constitution & repeal any British Act (including Independence Act)
  * **Interim Legislation:** Assemblies empowered to legislate for respective territories till new constitutions drafted (no British Parliament act applied unless extended by dominion legislature)
  * **SoS Abolished:** Transferred SoS functions to Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs
  * **Paramountcy Lapsed:** Declared lapse of British paramountcy over princely states & treaty relations with tribal areas (Aug 15, 1947)
  * **States' Freedom:** Princely states free to join India, Pakistan, or remain independent
  * **GOI 1935 as Interim:** Dominions governed by GOI Act of 1935 till new constitutions drafted (authorized to make modifications)
  * **Royal Veto:** King's right to veto bills/reserve bills abolished; GG given full power to assent in King's name
  * **Nominal Heads:** GG and provincial governors designated nominal (constitutional) heads (must act on advice of respective Council of Ministers)
  * **Titles:** Dropped title of 'Emperor of India' from King's royal titles
  * **Civil Services:** Discontinued SoS civil service appointments & post reservations; pre-Aug 15 appointees retained all benefits
* **Transition details:**
  * Midnight of **14–15 August, 1947**: Power transferred
  * **Lord Mountbatten** = 1st Governor-General of Dominion of India
  * **Jawaharlal Nehru** sworn in as 1st Prime Minister
  * **Constituent Assembly** (formed 1946) became Parliament of Indian Dominion
  * **Boundary Commission:** Headed by **Radcliffe**; determined borders
  * *Pakistan territory:* West Punjab, Sind, Baluchistan, East Bengal, NWFP, Sylhet district of Assam (NWFP & Sylhet voted in favor via referendum)
