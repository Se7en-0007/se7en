## INTRODUCTION
* **Location:** Enshrined in **Part V** of the Constitution (**Articles 52 to 78**) dealing with the Union Executive
* **Union Executive Composition:** President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, and Attorney General of India
* **Head of State:** President = Head of the Indian State, **1st citizen of India**, symbol of unity, integrity, and solidarity of the nation

---

## ELECTION OF THE PRESIDENT

### Electoral College (Article 54)
* **Nature:** Indirect election by an Electoral College consisting of:
  1. **Elected members of both Houses of Parliament** (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha)
  2. **Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies** (MLAs)
  3. **Elected members of Legislative Assemblies of UTs of Delhi and Puducherry** (*Added by 70th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992, w.e.f. June 1, 1995*)
* **Exclusions (Do NOT participate):**
  * Nominated members of Parliament (LS & RS)
  * Nominated members of State Legislative Assemblies
  * Members (both elected & nominated) of State Legislative Councils (in bicameral legislatures)
  * Nominated members of Puducherry Legislative Assembly
  * Members of dissolved Legislative Assemblies (cease to vote even if fresh elections not held before presidential election)
* **Discontinuation of Anglo-Indian Nominations:** **104th Constitutional Amendment Act (2019)** discontinued nomination of Anglo-Indians to LS & State Assemblies (w.e.f. Jan 25, 2020)

### Uniformity and Parity System (Article 55)
* **Uniformity & Parity Principle:** Maintains scale uniformity among states & parity between States as a whole and the Union
* **Value of Vote of an MLA:**
  **Value of Vote of an MLA** = (Total population of state / Total elected members in state assembly) × (1 / 1000)
  * *Population Definition:* Ascertained at **1971 Census** (*84th Amendment Act 2001* fixed 1971 population figures till first census taken after 2026)
* **Value of Vote of an MP:**
  **Value of Vote of an MP** = Total value of votes of all MLAs of all states / Total elected members of Parliament (LS + RS)

### Electoral System and Voting
* **System:** Proportional representation by means of **Single Transferable Vote** via **Secret Ballot**
* **Electoral Quota:** Candidate must secure a fixed quota of votes to be declared elected:
  **Electoral Quota** = { Total valid votes polled / (1 + 1 = 2) } + 1
* **Voting Mechanism:** Single ballot paper with preferences (1, 2, 3, etc.); 1st preference votes counted first; if no candidate hits quota, candidate with least 1st preference votes is eliminated & 2nd preferences transferred until quota achieved

### Election Disputes (Article 71)
* **Adjudication:** All doubts & disputes inquired into and decided **exclusively by Supreme Court** (SC decision = **final**)
* **Incomplete Electoral College:** Election **cannot** be challenged on ground of any vacancy in electoral college
* **Acts Done Prior to Void Declaration:** If election declared void by SC, acts done by President prior to declaration date remain **valid and in force**

### Reasons for Indirect Election
1. **Harmony with Parliamentary System:** President is nominal executive (real powers with Council of Ministers headed by PM) → Direct election with nominal powers would be anomalous
2. **Cost & Time Factor:** Direct election for vast electorate highly expensive, time & energy consuming for a symbolic head
3. **Union-State Balance:** Parliamentary-only election would favour ruling central party; current Electoral College represents Union and States equally

### Critique of Terminology
* *Proportional Representation:* Misnomer (used when filling 2+ seats; single vacancy = preferential/alternative vote)
* *Single Transferable Vote:* Misnomer (voters hold plural votes of varying values)

## QUALIFICATIONS, OATH AND CONDITIONS

### Qualifications for Election as President
1. Citizen of India
2. Completed **35 years of age**
3. Qualified for election as a member of **Lok Sabha**
4. Must **not hold any office of profit** under Union Govt, State Govt, or local/public authority
   * *Exemptions (Not deemed office of profit):* Sitting President, Vice-President, Governor of any state, Union or State Minister
* **Nomination Requirements:**
  * Subscribed by at least **50 electors as proposers** and **50 electors as seconders** (*increased in 1997 from 10 each*)
  * Security deposit = **₹15,000** in Reserve Bank of India (*increased in 1997 from ₹2,500*)
  * Forfeited if candidate fails to secure **1/6th of total valid votes polled**

### Oath or Affirmation by the President (Article 60)
* **Swears/Affirms to:**
  1. Faithfully execute the office
  2. Preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution and the law
  3. Devote to the service and well-being of the people of India
* **Administered by:** **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** (or seniormost available Supreme Court judge in CJI's absence)

### Conditions of President’s Office (Article 59)
1. **No Legislative Membership:** Cannot be a member of either House of Parliament or State Legislature (seat deemed vacated upon entering presidential office)
2. **No Office of Profit:** Prohibited from holding any other office of profit
3. **Official Residence:** Entitled to rent-free use of official residence (**Rashtrapati Bhavan**)
4. **Emoluments & Allowances:** Determined by Parliament; **cannot be diminished** during term of office
   * *Salary:* Increased to **₹5 lakh per month** (2018, w.e.f. Jan 1, 2016 via Finance Act 2018)
   * *Pension:* **50% of monthly salary**; office expenses up to ₹1,00,000/year for former President; spouse gets 50% family pension + ₹20,000/year office expenses
5. **Immunities & Privileges:**
   * **Personal Immunity:** Personal legal immunity for official acts
   * **Criminal Immunity:** **Complete immunity** from criminal proceedings during term (cannot be arrested/imprisoned)
   * **Civil Proceedings:** Permissible for personal acts after giving **2 months' prior written notice**

## TERM, IMPEACHMENT AND VACANCY

### Term of President’s Office (Article 56)
* **Tenure:** **5 years** from date of entering office
* **Resignation:** Addressed in writing to the **Vice-President**
* **Continuance:** Holds office beyond 5 years until successor assumes charge
* **Re-election:** Eligible for re-election for **any number of terms** (*US limit = max 2 terms*; in India, only Dr. Rajendra Prasad served 2 terms)

### Impeachment of President (Article 61)
* **Ground:** **‘Violation of the Constitution’** (phrase **not defined** in Constitution)
* **Nature:** **Quasi-judicial procedure** in Parliament
* **Procedure:**
  1. Can be initiated in **either House of Parliament**
  2. Charges signed by **1/4th members** of initiating House + **14 days' written notice** given to President
  3. Resolution passed by **2/3rd majority of TOTAL membership** of initiating House
  4. Sent to second House for investigation (President has right to appear & be represented)
  5. Resolution passed by **2/3rd majority of TOTAL membership** of second House → President stands removed from date of resolution passage
* **Composition Difference (Election vs Impeachment):**
  * **Nominated MPs** CAN participate in impeachment (though excluded from election)
  * **Elected MLAs of States & UTs (Delhi, Puducherry)** CANNOT participate in impeachment (though included in election)
* *Historical Fact:* **No President has been impeached so far**

### Vacancy in The President’s Office (Article 62)
* **5 Causes of Vacancy:**
  1. Expiry of 5-year tenure
  2. Resignation
  3. Removal by impeachment
  4. Death (*Dr. Zakir Husain in 1969 & Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed in 1977 died in office*)
  5. Disqualification / election declared void by Supreme Court
* **Filling Tenure Expiry Vacancy:** Election held before term expires; outgoing President continues till successor assumes charge to prevent 'interregnum' (Vice-President does NOT act as President)
* **Filling Unexpected Vacancy (Resignation, Removal, Death, Disqualification):**
  * Election must be held within **6 months** from vacancy date
  * Newly elected President serves full **5-year term**
  * **Vice-President** acts as President until new President is elected (Article 65)
  * **Absence/Illness:** Vice-President discharges functions until President resumes office
  * **If VP Office Vacant:** **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** (or seniormost SC judge) acts as President (*Example:* CJI M. Hidayatullah acted as President in July–Aug 1969 when VP V.V. Giri resigned to contest presidency)
  * Acting President enjoys all powers, immunities, and emoluments of President

## POWERS AND FUNCTIONS OF THE PRESIDENT

### 1. Executive Powers
* All executive actions of GoI formally taken in President's name
* Makes rules for authentication of orders & convenient transaction of Union Govt business
* Appoints & holds during pleasure: **Prime Minister & Ministers**, **Attorney General of India**
* Appoints: **CAG**, **Chief Election Commissioner & Election Commissioners**, **UPSC Chairman & Members**, **Governors**, **Finance Commission Chairman & Members**
* Seeks administrative info & legislative proposals from PM; can require PM to submit any minister's decision to Council of Ministers
* Appoints commissions for SCs, STs, OBCs & Inter-State Council
* Directly administers UTs via appointed Administrators
* Declares Scheduled Areas & has administrative powers over Scheduled/Tribal Areas

### 2. Legislative Powers
* Integral part of Parliament
* Summons/prorogues Parliament & dissolves Lok Sabha; summons **joint sitting** (presided by LS Speaker)
* Addresses Parliament at start of 1st session after general elections & 1st session each year
* Sends messages to Parliament on pending bills/other matters
* Appoints presiding officer: LS member as Speaker/Deputy Speaker pro tem when both offices vacant; RS member as Chairman/Deputy Chairman when both vacant
* **Nominations:**
  * **12 members to Rajya Sabha** (literature, science, art, social service)
  * *Discontinued:* 2 Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha (*104th Amendment Act, 2019*)
* Decides MP disqualification questions in consultation with **Election Commission**
* **Prior Recommendation Required for Bills:**
  * Consolidated Fund expenditure bill
  * State boundary alteration / new state creation bill (**Article 3**)
  * Money Bill (**Article 110**)
  * Tax affecting state interest / agricultural income definition / central surcharges (**Article 271**)
  * State trade restriction bill (prior sanction for introduction in state assembly, **Article 304**)
* **Action on Parliamentary Bills (Article 111):** Assent, Withhold assent, or Return (non-money bills) for reconsideration
* **Action on Reserved State Bills (Article 201):** Assent, Withhold assent, or Direct Governor to return (non-money bills) to state legislature
* Promulgates & withdraws **Ordinances (Article 123)** when Parliament in recess
* Lays reports before Parliament: CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission, etc.
* Makes regulations for peace, progress & good government of UTs (Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Ladakh; Puducherry when assembly suspended/dissolved)

### 3. Financial Powers
* Money Bills introduced in Parliament **ONLY with prior recommendation**
* Causes **Annual Financial Statement (Union Budget)** to be laid before Parliament (**Article 112**)
* Demand for grant made **ONLY on recommendation**
* Advances from **Contingency Fund of India** for unforeseen expenditure
* Constitutes **Finance Commission every 5 years** (**Article 280**)

### 4. Judicial Powers
* Appoints **Chief Justice of India** & SC/HC Judges
* **Seeks SC Advice (Article 143):** On question of law/fact (SC advice is advisory, **NOT binding**)
* **Pardoning Power (Article 72):** Grant pardon, reprieve, respite, remission, commutation for:
  1. Court martial sentences
  2. Offences against Union Law
  3. Death sentences (all cases)

### 5. Diplomatic Powers
* International treaties/agreements negotiated & concluded in President's name (subject to Parliamentary approval)
* Represents India in international forums; sends & receives ambassadors, high commissioners

### 6. Military Powers
* Supreme Commander of Defence Forces
* Appoints Army, Navy & Air Force Chiefs
* Declares war or concludes peace (subject to Parliamentary approval)

### 7. Emergency Powers
1. **National Emergency (Article 352):** War, external aggression, or armed rebellion → Modifies financial distribution, suspends FRs (except Arts 20 & 21)
2. **President’s Rule (Article 356 & 365):** Constitutional machinery failure / Central direction non-compliance → Assumes state govt powers & governor functions
3. **Financial Emergency (Article 360):** Financial stability/credit threat → Directs reduction of salaries of Union/State servants & SC/HC judges

## VETO POWER OF THE PRESIDENT
* **Article 111:** Action on Parliamentary Bills (Assent, Withhold, or Return)
* **Two-fold Object:** Prevent hasty/ill-considered legislation & unconstitutional legislation
* **4 Types of Vetoes in Modern States:**
  1. **Absolute Veto:** Withholding assent completely
  2. **Qualified Veto:** Overridden by higher majority (*USA President has this; Indian President does NOT*)
  3. **Suspensive Veto:** Overridden by ordinary majority
  4. **Pocket Veto:** Taking no action indefinitely
* **Indian President's Veto Powers (3 Types):**

### 1. Absolute Veto
* President withholds assent → Bill ends & fails to become Act
* Exercised in 2 cases: (a) Private members' bills, (b) Govt bills when cabinet resigns before assent & new cabinet advises withholding
* *Historical Examples:*
  * **1954:** Dr. Rajendra Prasad withheld assent to PEPSU Appropriation Bill (President's rule revoked before assent)
  * **1991:** R. Venkataraman withheld assent to Salary, Allowances & Pension of MPs (Amendment) Bill (passed without prior presidential recommendation on last day of LS)

### 2. Suspensive Veto
* President returns bill for reconsideration → Passed again by Parliament by **ordinary majority** → President **MUST give assent**
* *Exception:* **Money Bills cannot be returned** with suspensive veto (President gives assent or withholds; usually assented as introduced with prior permission)

### 3. Pocket Veto
* Neither ratifies, rejects, nor returns bill → Keeps bill pending indefinitely (*"pocket of Indian President is bigger than American President"* where US President must return in 10 days)
* *Historical Example:* **1986:** President Zail Singh exercised pocket veto on Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill (Rajiv Gandhi Govt); 1989 President R. Venkataraman sent it back; dropped by National Front Govt
* *No Veto on Amendment Bills:* **24th Amendment Act (1971)** made assent **obligatory** for Constitutional Amendment Bills

### Presidential Veto over State Legislation
* **Article 200:** Governor reserves state bill for President
* **Article 201:** President can: (a) Give assent, (b) Withhold assent, or (c) Direct Governor to return (non-money bill) for state reconsideration
* **No Override by State:** If state legislature passes bill again with/without amendment, President is **NOT bound to give assent** (state legislature cannot override presidential veto)
* **Pocket Veto on State Bills:** No time limit prescribed for President → Can exercise pocket veto on state bills

## ORDINANCE-MAKING POWER OF THE PRESIDENT
* **Article 123:** Promulgates ordinances during recess of Parliament (most important legislative power; temporary law)
* **4 Limitations:**
  1. **Recess Required:** Promulgated ONLY when both Houses or either House is not in session (cannot be issued when both Houses in session)
  2. **Immediate Action Necessity:** Satisfied that urgent circumstances exist
     * *Cooper Case (1970) & 44th Amendment (1978):* President's satisfaction is **justiciable** on grounds of mala fide (38th Amendment 1975 immunity deleted)
  3. **Co-extensive with Parliamentary Law:** Same subjects as Parliament + same constitutional limitations (cannot abridge Fundamental Rights)
  4. **Parliamentary Approval:** Laid before both Houses upon reassembly:
     * Ceases to operate **6 weeks from reassembly** (or earlier if disapproved by both Houses)
     * Max life of ordinance = **6 months and 6 weeks** (6 months max gap between sessions + 6 weeks)
* **Withdrawal & Nature:** Can be withdrawn by President anytime on Council of Ministers' advice; can be retrospective; can amend/repeal Parliamentary laws/taxes; **CANNOT amend Constitution**
* **D.C. Wadhwa Case (1986):** SC held successive re-promulgation of ordinances without legislative passage is unconstitutional (*"subversion of democratic legislative process"*)

## PARDONING POWER OF THE PRESIDENT
* **Article 72:** Grants pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions, commutations for:
  1. Court martial sentences
  2. Offences against Union Law
  3. Death sentences (all cases)
* **Objective:** Correct judicial errors & afford relief from unduly harsh sentences; independent executive power (not court of appeal)
* **5 Terminology Definitions:**
  1. **Pardon:** Completely absolves convict from all sentences, punishments & disqualifications
  2. **Commutation:** Substitution of one form of punishment for a **lighter form** (e.g., Death → Rigorous Imprisonment → Simple Imprisonment)
  3. **Remission:** **Reducing period** of sentence without changing character (e.g., 2 yrs Rigorous Imprisonment → 1 yr Rigorous Imprisonment)
  4. **Respite:** Awarding **lesser sentence** due to special fact (physical disability, pregnancy)
  5. **Reprieve:** **Temporary stay** of execution of sentence (especially death) to allow time to seek pardon
* **President (Art 72) vs Governor (Art 161) Comparison:**
  * **Court Martial:** President can pardon court martial sentences; Governor **cannot**
  * **Death Sentence:** President can **pardon death sentence**; Governor **cannot pardon death sentence** (Governor can only suspend, remit, or commute death sentence)
* **Judicial Principles (*Kehar Singh 1988 & Maru Ram 1980 Cases*):**
  * No right to oral hearing by petitioner
  * President can examine evidence afresh
  * Exercised on **advice of Union Cabinet**
  * No specific guidelines required
  * **Justiciable ONLY** if decision is irrelevant, irrational, mala fide, or discriminatory

## CONSTITUTIONAL POSITION OF THE PRESIDENT
* **System:** Parliamentary system → President = Nominal Executive (titular head, symbol of nation); Council of Ministers headed by PM = Real Executive
* **Constitutional Triad (Articles 53, 74, 75):**
  * **Article 53:** Executive power vested in President, exercised directly or via subordinate officers
  * **Article 74:** Council of Ministers with PM at head to **aid and advise** President who **‘shall’** act in accordance with advice
  * **Article 75:** Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Lok Sabha
* **Amendment Evolution of Advice:**
  * **42nd Amendment Act (1976):** Made ministerial advice **strictly binding** on President
  * **44th Amendment Act (1978):** President can require Council of Ministers to **reconsider advice once**; BUT reconsidered advice is **binding**
* **Situational Discretion (No Constitutional Discretion, but Situational Discretion):**
  1. Appointment of PM when no party has clear majority in Lok Sabha or PM dies in office without obvious successor
  2. Dismissal of Council of Ministers when it loses confidence of Lok Sabha and fails to prove majority
  3. Dissolution of Lok Sabha if Council of Ministers loses majority

## Veto Power of the President

| Bill Type | Central Legislation | State Legislation (Reserved) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ordinary Bills** | Can ratify, reject, or return | Can ratify, reject, or direct Governor to return (state override not binding) |
| **Money Bills** | Can ratify or reject (cannot return) | Can ratify or reject (cannot return) |
