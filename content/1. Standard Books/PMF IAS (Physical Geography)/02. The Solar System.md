**The Formation of the Solar System: Nuclear Disc Model (Neo-Laplacian model)**
*   **Nebula Collapse (~5–5.6 Bya)** ⇒ **Sun + Planets Formed (~4.6 Bya)** ⇒ **Earth Age = 4.543 Billion Years**

**Earlier Theories**
*   **Nebular Hypothesis (Kant & Laplace, 1796)**: Solar system born from a giant interstellar cloud called **nebula** (vast, swirling cloud of gas and dust). Drawback: based on erroneous scientific assumptions.
*   **Planetesimal Hypothesis (Chamberlain & Moulton, 1900)**: Wandering star approached Sun → cigar-shaped material separated from solar surface → star moved away → material continued to revolve and condensed into planets. Supported by **Sir James Jeans** & **Sir Harold Jeffrey**.
*   **Revised Nebular Hypothesis (Otto Schmidt & Carl Weizascar, 1950)**: Sun surrounded by solar nebula (mostly **hydrogen + helium** + dust) → particle friction & collision → flat disc-shaped cloud → planets formed via **accretion**.
*   **Big Bang Theory (Edwin Hubble, 1920)**: Provided expanding universe evidence. Core precursor to Solar system models.

---

**The Formation of The Sun**
*   **Supernova Shock Waves** → Nebular gravitational instability → **Gravitational Collapse** in on itself.
*   **Snowball Effect**: Gravity pulled dust/gas to centre → center became denser/hotter → increased gravity pulled more material.
*   **Protosun Formation**: **99.9% of material** collapsed to centre.
*   **Sun Birth**: High core temp triggered **nuclear fusion** ⇒ Solar radiation commenced.
*   **Protoplanetary Disc**: Remaining **0.1% of matter** orbited Sun, forming a flat disc where planets formed.

---

**The Formation of The Planets**
*   **Accretion Process**: Dust collisions inside protoplanetary disc → clumped into microscopic particles → grew into **planetesimals** (infant planets; up to a few km across).
*   **Protoplanets**: cooling disc → planetesimals accreted further → grew larger, sweeping up dust, other planetesimals, and protoplanets → grew into **planets**.
*   **Terrestrial Planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars)**: Hot, inner nebula → planetesimals composed of refractory **silicates + metals** → condensed into metal cores (**iron + nickel**).
*   **Gas & Ice Giants (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune)**: Cool, outer nebula → dominant **water ice** component.
*   **Asteroids**: Escapees of planetary gravitational pull, remaining scattered rocky debris.

---

**Iron Catastrophe and Planetary Differentiation**
*   **Earth Birth (~4.5 Bya)**: Uniform ball of hot rock → radioactive decay + primordial heat increased temp.
*   **Iron Catastrophe (~4.0 Bya / 500 million years post-formation)**: Temp reached melting point of iron (**1,538°C**).
*   **Planetary Differentiation**:
    *   Buoyant materials (**silicates, water, air**) floated close to exterior (crust/mantle).
    *   Heavy metals (**iron, nickel**) gravitated to centre → formed **early core**.

### **Components of The Solar System**
*   **Solar System Composition**: Sun, 8 planets, dwarf planets (**Pluto, Ceres, Eris**), satellites, asteroids, meteors, comets.
*   **Heliocentric Model (Nicolaus Copernicus)**: Mathematically predictive model placing Sun at center, Earth & planets revolving around it. Refuted Ptolemy's **geocentric model** (Earth at center).
*   **Spherical Earth**: Floated by **Pythagoras (~500 BC)** → validated by **Aristotle (340 BC)**.
*   **Strabo**: Wrote **Geographica** ("Geography"), describing historical regions, people, and places.
*   **Planetary Planes**: Most orbit close to Earth's orbital plane, the **ecliptic**. Comets and Kuiper belt objects orbit at greater angles.
*   **Orbit Shapes**: Planets follow **nearly circular** orbits; comets, asteroids, Kuiper belt objects follow **highly elliptical** orbits.

**Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion**
1.  **First Law (Law of Orbits)**: Orbit of a planet is an **ellipse** with the Sun at one of the two foci.
2.  **Second Law (Law of Equal Areas)**: Line joining planet and Sun sweeps out **equal areas during equal intervals of time** (planet moves faster near Sun, slower further away).
3.  **Third Law (Harmonic Law)**: Square of orbital period ($T^2$) is proportional to cube of semi-major axis ($a^3$) ($T^2 \propto a^3$).

*   **Mercury**: Closest to Sun (**0.4 AU**), temp **+427°C**, rotation **58 days**, revolution **87 days**, moons **0**, gravity **3.7 m/s²**.

*   **Jupiter**: Largest planet (**140,000 km diameter**), distance **5.2 AU**, rotation **9.9 hrs**, moons **79**, gravity **24.9 m/s²**.
*   **Saturn**: Density **less than water** (**0.7 g/cm³**), distance **9.6 AU**, rotation **10.7 hrs**, moons **82**, gravity **10.4 m/s²**.
*   **Uranus**: Axis tipped on its side, distance **19.2 AU**, rotation **17 hrs** (retrograde/clockwise), moons **27**, gravity **8.8 m/s²**.
*   **Neptune**: Strongest wind speed (**2,100 km/h**), distance **30 AU**, rotation **16 hrs**, moons **14**, gravity **11.1 m/s²**.

### **The Sun**
*   **Sun Factuals**: Age = **4.6 Billion Years** | Diameter = **1.39 Million km** | Temp = **6000°C** (surface), **16 million°C** (core) | Density = **1.41 times water** | Gravity = **274 m/s²** (28x Earth).

**The Sun’s Internal Structure and Atmosphere**
*   **Interior Structure (Inside Out)**: **Core** → **Radiative Zone** → **Convective Zone**.
*   **Atmosphere Structure (Inside Out)**: **Photosphere** → **Chromosphere** → **Corona**.

**Photosphere**
*   Bright, extremely uneven outer layer emitting most radiation; Temp = **6000°C**.

**Chromosphere**
*   Thin layer of burning gases above photosphere; cooler, Temp = **4,320°C**.

**Sunspot**
*   Temporary dark spots on photosphere; cooler by **500–1500°C** than surrounding area.
*   **Cause**: Concentrations of magnetic fields inhibiting convection, reducing surface temperature.
*   **Sunspot Cycle**: **11-year cycle** of peaks (**Solar Maximum**) and troughs (**Solar Minimum**).
*   **Structure**: Black center (**umbra**) + lighter surrounding region (**penumbra**).
*   **Climate Impact**: Sun is **1% cooler** when sunspot-free; may affect Earth's climates.

**Solar Wind**
*   High-speed stream of **plasma** (charged particles: electrons and protons) flowing from Sun's corona.
*   Velocity = up to **900 km/s** | Temperature = **1 million °C**.

**Plasma**
*   Fourth state of matter: **ionized gas** (atoms/molecules stripped of outer-shell electrons).
*   Examples: **Lightning, electric sparks, neon lights** (plasma lights).

**Aurora**
*   Natural light displays in polar skies (high latitudes) due to Earth’s magnetic field lines guiding solar wind.
*   **Cause**: Charged solar particles excite/ionize atmospheric gases ⇒ optical light emissions.
*   **Aurora Borealis** = Arctic/Northern Hemisphere | **Aurora Australis** = Antarctic/Southern Hemisphere.

**Solar Flares**
*   Gaseous eruptions from magnetic storms, appearing as very bright spots. Heat corona gas to **10–20 million °C**.

**Solar Prominence**
*   Eruptive gaseous loops held above Sun’s surface by strong magnetic fields; can last for months.

**Corona**
*   Sun’s outer atmosphere of plasma extending millions of km; most easily visible during a **total solar eclipse**.

### **Planets**

**Inner Planets**
*   **Terrestrial (Earth-like) Planets**: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (inner circle, inside asteroid belt).
*   **Composition**: Rocky crusts/mantles (**refractory silicates**) + metal cores (**iron + nickel**); high densities.
*   **Features**: atmospheres thick enough to generate weather (except Mercury); all have impact craters, rift valleys, and volcanoes.
*   **Venus Atmosphere**: Inhospitable to human life due to extreme, toxic **carbon dioxide** concentrations.

**Moon**
*   **Factuals**: Diameter = **1/4 of Earth** | Distance = **384,400 km** | **Tidally Locked** (rotational period matches orbital period).
*   **Apollo 11 (July 1969)**: First landing; only 12 astronauts have ever walked on surface.
*   **Water Discovery**: India's **Chandrayaan-1 (2009)** discovered lunar soil holds **0.1% water by weight** at polar regions.

**Advantages of Colonising The Moon**
*   Launching rocket base utilizing locally manufactured fuels to explore distant space.

**There are Several Disadvantages To The Moon as a Colony Site**
*   **Long Nights**: 350+ hour lunar nights block solar power (except at polar regions).
*   **Resource Depletion**: Highly depleted in **carbon** and volatiles (**nitrogen, hydrogen**).
*   **Health Hazards**: Low gravity weakens bones/muscles. No atmosphere leads to solar radiation, temperature extremes, and meteor impacts.
*   **Agriculture Barriers**: Crop growth is hindered by long nights, extreme temp ranges, solar flares, nitrogen-poor soil, and absence of insect pollinators.

**Outer Planets**
*   **Jovian (Jupiter-like) Planets / Gas Giants**: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
*   Hold **99% of mass** orbiting Sun.
*   **Composition**: Dominated by **hydrogen + helium**; lack a solid surface (but moons are solid).
*   **Ice Giants**: **Uranus & Neptune** are composed primarily of heavier ices (**water, ammonia, methane**).
*   **Common Traits**: Ring systems, magnetospheres, numerous moons, intense atmospheric weather.
*   **Winds**: **Neptune** has strongest wind speed (**2,100 km/h**), followed by **Saturn** (**1,800 km/h**).

**Why are The Inner Planets Rocky while The Outer Planets are Mostly in Gaseous Form?**
1.  **Proximal Warmth**: Terrestrial planets formed near parent star; too warm for surface gases to condense into solid particles.
2.  **Solar Wind Intensity**: Strongest solar winds near Sun stripped gases/dust from inner planets; outer planets preserved gases due to distant, weak winds.
3.  **Planetary Gravity**: Inner planets are smaller with lower gravity, unable to hold escaping gases.

**Saturn**
*   Density is **less than water** (< 1 g/cm³).
*   Rings composed of billions of ice particles and ice-covered rocks.
*   **Titan**: Second-largest moon in solar system; only moon with a substantial, **nitrogen-rich atmosphere**.

**Uranus**
*   Tipped on its side; axis of rotation lies nearly in the plane of its orbit (poles lie where other equators lie).

**Neptune**
*   Uranus and Neptune are "twins of the outer solar system", classified as **ice giants** to highlight water/ammonia/methane ice dominance over hydrogen/helium gas.

### **Other Solar System Objects**

**Asteroid Belt**
*   **Asteroids (Planetoids)**: Rock and metal debris with some ice, ranging from km-scale to microscopic.
*   **Cause of Non-coalescence**: Jupiter's strong gravitational interference blocked planet formation.
*   **Belt Location**: Circular orbit zone between Mars and Jupiter, spanning **2.3 to 3.3 AU**.

**Ceres**
*   Largest asteroid (**946 km diameter**), protoplanet, and **dwarf planet** (only asteroid with gravity sufficient to pull itself into spherical hydrostatic equilibrium).

**Kuiper Belt, Pluto, and Charon**
*   **Kuiper Belt**: Great ring of frozen icy debris extending from **30 to 50 AU** from Sun.
*   **Pluto (39 AU)**: Largest Kuiper belt object. Moon = **Charon** (largest). Reclassified as dwarf planet in 2006.
*   **IAU Definition of a Planet (2006)**:
    1.  Orbits the Sun.
    2.  Sufficient mass for hydrostatic equilibrium (nearly round shape).
    3.  Is not a satellite (moon) of another object.
    4.  **Has cleared its orbital neighbourhood of debris.**
*   **IAU Definition of a Dwarf Planet**: Meets planetary criteria but **has not cleared its orbital neighbourhood**.
*   **Key Dwarf Planets**: **Ceres** (asteroid belt), **Pluto** (Kuiper belt), **Eris** (Kuiper belt; diameter 2,326 km).

**Comets**
*   **Comet Composition**: Frozen gases (**water, ammonia, methane, carbon dioxide**) holding rocky and metallic minerals. highly elliptical orbits.
*   **Short-Period Comets**: Originate in **Kuiper Belt**; orbit periods < few hundred years.
*   **Long-Period Comets**: Originate in **Oort Cloud**; orbit periods of thousands of years.
*   **Oort Cloud**: Spherical shell of icy bodies surrounding solar system at **5,000 to 100,000 AU**.
*   **Anatomy**: Approaching Sun → solar wind/heating vaporizes gases (outgassing) → forms a bright visible atmosphere (**coma**) and a **tail** pointing away from Sun.
*   **Halley's Comet**: Periodic comet, orbital period of **76 years**. Last seen **1986**.

**Meteoroid, Meteor and Meteorite**
*   **Meteoroid**: Any solid debris floating in interplanetary space.
*   **Meteor (Shooting Star)**: Streak of light in sky. Occurs when meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere (**mesosphere, at ~200 km**) and burns due to high-speed friction.
*   **Meteorite**: Surviving space chunk that successfully reaches Earth's surface.
*   **Meteorite Crater**: Circular impact depression. Most prominent on geologically inactive bodies with thin atmospheres (**Moon, Mercury**).
*   **Largest Crater**: Arizona Crater, USA (**1,300 m deep**, formed over 10,000 years ago).

**Meteorite Craters in India**
*   **Lonar Lake (Maharashtra)**: **Ramsar Site**, 1.8 km diameter (Buldhana District).
*   **Dhala crater (Madhya Pradesh)**: 14 km diameter (Shivpuri District).
*   **Ramgarh crater (Rajasthan)**: 3.5 km potential impact crater (Kota Plateau).

### **What’s The Closest Star to The Sun?**
*   **Proxima Centauri (4.2 light-years)**: Red dwarf, closest star to the Sun. Part of the **Alpha Centauri system (4.37 lya)** (three stars: Alpha Centauri A, B, and Proxima Centauri).
*   **Star Drift**: Proxima Centauri has been closest for 32,000 years, will remain so for 33,000 years; after that, **Ross 248** will become closest.
*   **Alpha Centauri visibility**: Visible only in **Southern Hemisphere**.
*   **Barnard’s Star**: Second closest star, closest visible in **Northern Hemisphere**, but red dwarf (too dim for naked eye).
*   **Sirius (Dog Star)**: Brightest star in Earth’s night sky; closest star visible to the naked eye in Northern Hemisphere.

**How do Astronomers Measure The Distance to Stars?**
*   **Parallax Method**: Measure star's angle from one side of Earth's orbit, wait 6 months (Earth at opposite side), and measure angle change against distant reference stars to compute distance.

### **Heliopause - The Boundary?**
*   **Heliosphere**: Bubble-like space region created by outgoing solar wind pressure pushing against incoming interstellar medium pressure.
*   **Bow Shock / Wave**: Shock wave in interstellar medium created by the Sun moving relative to it.
*   **Heliosheath**: Slower boundary region where solar wind begins interacting with interstellar medium.
*   **Termination Shock**: Innermost Heliosheath boundary; where solar wind drops to subsonic speeds.
*   **Heliopause**: Outermost Heliosphere boundary; where interstellar pressure completely halts solar wind flow.
*   **Voyager Crossings**: Voyager 1 crossed termination shock at **94 AU**; Voyager 2 crossed at **84 AU**.

### **Distant Artificial Objects Exploring The Solar System**
*   Five artificial objects have escape velocity to leave Solar System, communicating via the global **NASA Deep Space Network (DSN)** (located in **California, Madrid, Canberra**):
    *   **Voyager 1 (Sep 1977)**: Passed heliopause in **2012** (first artificial object to leave Solar System). Explored Jupiter, Saturn, and Titan. Active at **~155 AU** (Jan 2022).
    *   **Voyager 2 (Aug 1977)**: Passed heliopause in **Dec 2018** (second object to leave). Explored all Jovian planets. Active at **~129 AU** (Jan 2022).
    *   **Pioneer 10 (1972)**: Studied asteroid belt, Jupiter/Saturn environment, solar wind. Flew past Saturn (1979). Contact lost **2003**. Distance **~120 AU**.
    *   **Pioneer 11 (1973)**: Same objectives. Flew past Saturn (1979). Contact lost **1995**. Distance **~90 AU**.
    *   **New Horizons (2006)**: Flew past Pluto in 2015. Exploring Kuiper Belt. Active at **~53 AU** (Jan 2022).
    *   **Juno (2011)**: Polar orbit of Jupiter (2016). Studied Jupiter's composition, gravity, and magnetic fields. Active.
