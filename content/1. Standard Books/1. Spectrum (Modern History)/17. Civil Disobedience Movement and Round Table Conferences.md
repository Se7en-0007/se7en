## The Run-Up To Civil Disobedience Movement

### Calcutta Session of Congress
* **December 1928**: **Nehru Report** approved, but younger elements led by **Jawaharlal Nehru, Subhas Chandra Bose, and Satyamurthy** dissatisfied with **Dominion Status** → demanded adoption of **Purna Swaraj** (complete independence) as the goal.
* **Older Leaders (Gandhi & Motilal Nehru)**: Wished to retain Dominion Status consensus → proposed a **two-year grace period** for the British government to accept the demand, later reduced to **one year** under pressure from the youth.
* **Congress Resolution**: If the British government did not accept a constitution based on Dominion Status by the end of **1929**, the Congress would:
  * Demand complete independence.
  * Launch a **Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM)** to attain it.

### Political Activity During 1929
* **Gandhi's Incessant Travel**: Toured the country to prepare the masses for direct action, organize village constructive work, and redress local grievances (modeled on the **1928 Bardoli Agitation**).
* **Foreign Cloth Boycott**: **CWC** organized a **Foreign Cloth Boycott Committee** → Gandhi initiated the aggressive boycott and public burning of foreign cloth in **March 1929** (Calcutta) → **Gandhi arrested** → triggered nationwide cloth bonfires.
* **Temperature-Raising Events**:
  * **Meerut Conspiracy Case** (March 1929).
  * **Central Legislative Assembly Bombing** by **Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt** (April 1929).
  * Minority **Labour Government** led by **Ramsay MacDonald** came to power in Britain (May 1929) → **Wedgewood Benn** became Secretary of State for India.

### Irwin’s Declaration (October 31, 1929)
* **Joint Effort**: British Labour government + Viceroy **Lord Irwin** issued an official communique in the *Indian Gazette*.
* **Objective**: "Restore faith in the ultimate purpose of British policy."
* **Core Provision**: Declared that it is implicit in the **Declaration of 1917** that the natural issue of India's constitutional progress is the attainment of **Dominion Status** (no timeframe specified; long-term projection).
* **Promise**: Proposed a **Round Table Conference (RTC)** after the Simon Commission submitted its report.

### Delhi Manifesto
* **November 2, 1929**: Conference of prominent national leaders issued the **'Delhi Manifesto'** containing conditions for attending the RTC:
  1. RTC purpose must not be *whether* or *when* Dominion Status would be reached, but to **formulate a constitution** to implement it (acting as a constituent assembly).
  2. Congress must have **majority representation** at the conference.
  3. General amnesty for political prisoners and a policy of conciliation.
* **Viceroy's Response**: **Lord Irwin** met Gandhi, Motilal Nehru, and other leaders in **December 1929** → explicitly refused to guarantee that the RTC was meant to draft a Dominion Status scheme → **Irwin rejected the Delhi Manifesto**.

## Lahore Congress and Purna Swaraj

* **Jawaharlal Nehru's Nomination as President**: Chosen for the **Lahore Session (December 1929)** due to **Gandhi’s backing** (overcoming 15 out of 18 PCC oppositions) → acknowledged the massive youth upsurge and the need to adopt complete independence.
* **Presidential Address**: Nehru declared himself a **socialist and a republican** → opposed feudal and industrial monarchs → called for a peaceful mass movement for liberation.
* **Lahore Decisions (December 1929)**:
  1. **Round Table Conference** to be boycotted.
  2. **Complete Independence (Purna Swaraj)** declared as the official goal of Congress.
  3. **CWC authorized** to launch civil disobedience, including the non-payment of taxes.
  4. All members of legislatures asked to **resign their seats**.
  5. **January 26, 1930** fixed as the first **Independence (Swarajya) Day** to be celebrated nationwide.
* **December 31, 1929**: At midnight, the newly adopted tricolour flag of freedom was hoisted by **Jawaharlal Nehru** on the banks of **River Ravi** amidst slogans of *Inquilab Zindabad*.

### January 26, 1930: The Independence Pledge
* **Public Meetings**: Mass gatherings held nationwide; local language pledges read out and the national flag hoisted.
* **Core Points of the Pledge (Drafted by Gandhi)**:
  * Inalienable right of the Indian people to freedom, fruits of labor, and basic necessities.
  * **Four-Fold Ruin**: British rule ruined India:
    1. **Economically**: High land revenue, destruction of village industries without substitutes, and manipulation of customs, currency, and exchange rates.
    2. **Politically**: Denied rights of free association, killed administrative and executive talent.
    3. **Culturally**: Education system severed Indians from their cultural moorings.
    4. **Spiritually**: Compulsory disarmament emasculated the population.
  * Submission to such rule is "a crime against man and God."
  * **Path Forward**: Prepare for complete independence by withdrawing voluntary association and preparing for civil disobedience (tax refusal).

## Civil Disobedience Movement - The Salt Satyagraha And Other Upsurges

### Gandhi’s Eleven Demands
* **Ultimatum**: Gandhi presented **11 demands** to the government, setting a deadline of **January 31, 1930**:
  * **Issues of General Interest**:
    1. Reduce army and civil service expenditure by **50%**.
    2. Introduce **total prohibition** of alcohol.
    3. Carry out reforms in the Criminal Investigation Department (**CID**).
    4. Amend the **Arms Act** to allow popular control of firearm licenses.
    5. Release **political prisoners**.
    6. Accept the **Postal Reservation Bill**.
  * **Specific Bourgeois Demands**:
    7. Reduce the rupee-sterling exchange ratio to **1s 4d**.
    8. Introduce **textile protection**.
    9. Reserve **coastal shipping** for Indian companies.
  * **Specific Peasant Demands**:
    10. Reduce land revenue by **50%**.
    11. Abolish the **salt tax** and the government’s salt monopoly.
* **Ultimatum Expired**: No positive response from the government → CWC invested Gandhi with full powers to launch the CDM.

### Why Salt was Chosen as The Important Theme
* **Universal Reach**: Outside of water, salt is the most heavily taxed article reaching the poorest, sickest, and most helpless millions → branded by Gandhi as "the most inhuman poll tax."
* **Psychological & Social Link**: Connected the ideal of Swaraj with a highly concrete, universal grievance of the rural poor without sparking class division (unlike a no-rent campaign).
* **Symbol of Identification**: Offered self-help income (like khadi) for the poor and allowed the urban population to symbolically identify with mass suffering.

### Dandi March (March 12–April 6, 1930)
* **Viceroy Informed**: Gandhi sent a plan of action to Lord Irwin on **March 2, 1930**.
* **The March**: Gandhi + **78 Sabarmati Ashram members** marched **240 miles** from Ahmedabad through Gujarat villages to the coastal village of **Dandi**.
* **Gandhi's Directives for Action**:
  * Start local salt satyagrahis wherever possible.
  * Picket foreign liquor and cloth shops.
  * Refuse taxes if the local organization is strong enough.
  * Lawyers to give up practice; public to boycott courts.
  * Government servants to resign.
  * **Strict Condition**: Faultless adherence to **truth and non-violence**.
  * Obey local leaders in the event of Gandhi’s arrest.
* **April 6, 1930**: Gandhi picked up a lump of salt at **Dandi**, ceremonially violating the salt law → signaled the nationwide launch of the Civil Disobedience Movement.

## Spread of Salt Law Disobedience

* **Defiance of Salt Laws**: Triggered across the length and breadth of India.
* **Key Events**:
  * **April 1930**: **Jawaharlal Nehru arrested** for salt law violation → sparked massive protests in Madras, Calcutta, and Karachi.
  * **May 4, 1930**: **Gandhi arrested** ahead of his planned raid on the **Dharasana Salt Works** → provoked massive strikes and industrial closures in Bombay, Delhi, Calcutta, and Sholapur.
* **CWC Post-Arrest Actions**: Sanctioned non-payment of revenue in ryotwari areas, no-chowkidara-tax campaigns in zamindari areas, and violation of forest laws in Central Provinces.

### Satyagraha At Different Places
* **Tamil Nadu**: Organized by **C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji)** → marched from **Thiruchirapalli to Vedaranniyam** on the Tanjore coast to break the salt law → widespread picketing of foreign cloth and anti-liquor campaigns → strike at **Choolai Mills** repressed by police → unemployed weavers attacked liquor shops at **Gudiyattam** → price-hit peasant riots at **Bodinayakanur**.
* **Malabar**: **K. Kelappan** (Vaikom Satyagraha hero) organized salt marches → **P. Krishna Pillai** (future Kerala Communist founder) heroically defended the national flag against police lathi-charge on Calicut beach (November 1930).
* **Andhra Region**: District salt marches in East and West Godavari, Krishna, and Guntur → military-style headquarters camps called **Sibirams** established → merchants contributed funds; **Kamma and Raju** cultivators defied repression (mass support was lower than in 1921–22).
* **Orissa**: Effective coastal satyagraha led by **Gopalbandhu Choudhuri** in Balasore, Cuttack, and Puri districts.
* **Assam**: Lower heights than 1921–22 due to Assamese-Bengali, Hindu-Muslim, and East Bengal peasant immigration tensions → successful student strike against the **Cunningham Circular** (which banned student political participation) in May 1930 → **Chandraprabha Saikiani** incited Kachari tribal villages to break forest laws (December 1930).
* **Bengal**: Bengal Congress split into **Subhas Chandra Bose** and **J.M. Sengupta** factions → alienated Calcutta *bhadralok* from rural masses → communal riots in **Dacca** and **Kishoreganj** (low Muslim participation) → *Yet* Bengal produced the largest number of arrests and highest rate of violence → Midnapur, Arambagh saw powerful salt and chowkidari struggles → **Surya Sen’s Chittagong group** raided two armouries and declared a provisional government.
* **Bihar**: Champaran and Saran first to break salt laws → Patna's **Nakhas Pond** chosen as salt site under **Ambika Kant Sinha** → landlocked limits shifted the satyagraha into a highly powerful **No-Chowkidara tax agitation** → administrative collapse in areas like **Barhee (Munger)** by November 1930 → Chhotanagpur tribal belt saw lower-class militancy: **Bonga Majhi and Somra Majhi** led socio-religious 'sanskritising' reforms (meat/liquor ban, khadi) → Santhals conducted illegal liquor distillation under Gandhi's banner → small landlords/tenants participated, though big zamindars remained loyal.
* **Peshawar**: Politicized by **Khan Abdul Gaffar Khan (Badshah Khan / Frontier Gandhi)** through social reforms → started first Pushto monthly ***Pukhtoon*** → organized non-violent volunteer brigade **"Khudai Khidmatgars" (Red-Shirts)** → mass demonstrations after Congress arrests (April 23, 1930) controlled Peshawar for over a week → **Garhwal Rifles soldiers** refused to fire on unarmed crowds → nervous British imposed martial law in a 92% Muslim province.
* **Sholapur**: Fiercest response to Gandhi's arrest → textile workers went on strike (May 7), burnt liquor shops, railway stations, municipal offices, police stations, and courts → established a **parallel government** (dislodged only by martial law after May 16).
* **Dharasana**: **Sarojini Naidu, Imam Sahib, and Manilal (Gandhi's son)** led a raid on the **Dharasana Salt Works** (May 21, 1930) → unarmed satyagrahis brutally beaten with steel-shod lathis (2 dead, 320 injured) → became a template for raids in Wadala (Bombay), Karnataka (Sanikatta), Andhra, Midnapore, Balasore, Puri, and Cuttack.
* **Gujarat**: High impact in Anand, Borsad, Nadiad (Kheda), Bardoli (Surat), and Jambusar (Bharuch) → determined **no-tax movement** (refusal of land revenue) → villagers crossed into princely states (Baroda) to camp in the open and evade police property confiscation.
* **Maharashtra, Karnataka, Central Provinces**: Defiance of forest laws (grazing and timber restrictions, public sale of illegally acquired forest produce).
* **United Provinces**: Organized a **no-revenue campaign** (for loyalist zamindars to refuse revenue to government) and **no-rent campaign** (for tenants to refuse rent to zamindars) → gathered force in October 1930, particularly in Agra and Rai Bareilly.
* **Manipur and Nagaland**: Young **Rani Gaidinliu** (13-year-old Naga spiritual leader following her cousin **Haipou Jadonang**) raised the banner of revolt → urged tax strikes → Jadonang was hanged by the British in 1931 for treason → Gaidinliu evaded capture until October 1932 → sentenced to life imprisonment (released in 1946 by the Interim Government).

### Forms of Mobilisation
* **Prabhat pheries** (early morning processions), **vanar senas** (children's monkey armies), **manjari senas** (girls' associations), **secret patrikas** (illegal cyclostyled news bulletins), and **magic lantern shows** to educate villagers.

### Views
* **Sarojini Naidu**: *"Gandhiji’s body is in jail but his soul is with you... You will be beaten but you must not resist, you must not even raise a hand to ward off blows."*
* **Web Miller** (American journalist on Dharasana): *"Although everyone knew that within a few minutes he would be beaten down, and perhaps killed, I could detect no signs of wavering or fear. They marched steadily with heads up..."*

## Impact of Agitation

1. **Trade Decline**: Imports of foreign cloth and other British manufactures fell drastically.
2. **Fiscal Loss**: British government suffered massive revenue deficits from the loss of liquor excise, land revenue, and stamp duties.
3. **Political Boycott**: Elections to the Legislative Assembly were largely boycotted.

---

## Extent of Mass Participation

* **Women**: Mass entry into the public sphere; picketed liquor shops, opium dens, and foreign cloth shops.
* **Students & Youth**: Played the most prominent part in the boycott and picketing operations.
* **Muslims**: Overall participation lower than 1920–22 due to calls by community leaders and British communal division; *exceptions*: NWFP (overwhelming), and active middle-class/working-class participation in Senhatta, Tripura, Gaibandha, Bagura, Noakhali, Dacca, and weaving groups in Bihar, Delhi, and Lucknow.
* **Merchants and Petty Traders**: Highly enthusiastic; traders’ associations enforced the boycott, especially in Tamil Nadu and Punjab.
* **Tribals**: Active participants in the Central Provinces, Maharashtra, and Karnataka.
* **Workers**: Went on strikes and participated in demonstrations in Bombay, Calcutta, Madras, and Sholapur.
* **Peasants**: Extremely active in the United Provinces, Bihar, and Gujarat.

---

## Government Response - Efforts for Truce

* **The Dilemma**: Government faced a classic "damned if you do, damned if you don't" situation (repression proved their brutality, while non-action was seen as a Congress victory).
* **Repression**: Issued emergency ordinances, gagged the press, banned civil disobedience groups, conducted brutal lathi-charges, fired on crowds, and jailed thousands of satyagrahis.
* **July 1930 Peace Efforts**: Lord Irwin suggested an RTC, reiterated Dominion Status, and allowed **Tej Bahadur Sapru and M.R. Jayakar** to mediate between Congress and the Government.
* **August 1930 Jail Meeting**: Nehrus transferred to Yerawada jail to meet Gandhi → nationalists demanded:
  1. Right of secession from Britain.
  2. Complete national government with control over defence and finance.
  3. An independent tribunal to settle Britain's financial claims.
  * **Result**: Peace talks broke down.

## Gandhi-Irwin Pact

* **January 25, 1931**: Gandhi and all CWC members released unconditionally → CWC authorized Gandhi to talk with the Viceroy.
* **March 5, 1931**: **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact)** signed in Delhi → placed Congress on an equal footing with the government.
* **Viceroy Agreed to (on behalf of Government)**:
  1. Immediate release of all political prisoners not convicted of violence.
  2. Remission of all fines not yet collected.
  3. Return of all lands not yet sold to third parties.
  4. Lenient treatment to government servants who had resigned.
  5. Right to make salt in coastal villages for personal consumption (not for sale).
  6. Right to peaceful, non-aggressive picketing.
  7. Withdrawal of emergency ordinances.
* **Viceroy Rejected**:
  * Public enquiry into police excesses.
  * Commutation of **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru's** death sentences to life imprisonment.
* **Gandhi Agreed to (on behalf of Congress)**:
  1. Suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement.
  2. Participate in the **Second Round Table Conference** to discuss the constitutional question around the three linchpins of:
     * Federation.
     * Indian responsibility.
     * Reservations and safeguards (defence, external affairs, minority positions, finance).

## Evaluation of Civil Disobedience Movement

### Was Gandhi-Irwin Pact A Retreat ?
* **Not a retreat** because:
  1. Mass movements are structurally short-lived; the masses have a limited capacity to make continuous sacrifices (unlike political activists).
  2. Clear signs of economic exhaustion had emerged after September 1930, particularly among shopkeepers and merchants.
* **Disappointments**:
  * **Youth**: Highly disappointed by the suspension of the movement and the failure to save Bhagat Singh.
  * **Gujarat Peasants**: Unsold lands were not restored immediately (happened only later under the 1937 Congress ministry).
* **Jubilation**: Broad masses were jubilant that the British state had been forced to treat their leader as an equal and sign a bilateral pact.

### Comparison to Non-Cooperation Movement
| Feature | Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) | Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stated Objective** | Vaguely worded Swaraj & remedy of specific wrongs. | **Complete Independence (Purna Swaraj)**. |
| **Tactics** | Non-cooperation with foreign institutions. | Active **violation of laws** from the very beginning. |
| **Intelligentsia** | High participation (lawyers, students giving up schools/courts). | Lower participation of lawyers and students in school boycotts. |
| **Muslims** | **High mass participation** (Khilafat link). | **Nowhere near** the earlier levels. |
| **Labour** | Coincided with major labor upsurges. | No major concurrent labor upsurges. |
| **Peasant & Business** | Moderate mobilization. | **Massive participation** of peasants and business groups. |
| **Imprisonment** | Around 30,000 satyagrahis jailed. | **Over 90,000** satyagrahis jailed (three times more). |
| **Organization** | Developing stage. | Congress was **organisationally far stronger**. |

### Views
* **M.K. Gandhi to Lord Irwin**: *"India is one vast prison-house. I repudiate this law."*
* **Ellen Wilkinson**: *"Gandhi was the best policeman the British had in India."*
* **Brailsford**: *"Dandi March is the ‘kindergarten stage of revolution’... based on the notion that King Emperor can be unseated by boiling sea-water in a kettle."*

## Karachi Congress Session (1931)

* **Special Session (March 29, 1931)**: Convened to endorse the Gandhi-Irwin Pact under the presidency of **Vallabhbhai Patel**.
* **Tragic Backdrop**: Six days earlier (**March 23, 1931**), **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru** were executed → Gandhi met with black flag protests by the **Punjab Naujawan Bharat Sabha** for failing to save them.

### Congress Resolutions At Karachi
* Dissociated from and disapproved of political violence, but admired the extreme bravery and sacrifice of the three martyrs.
* Endorsed the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact**.
* Reiterated the goal of **Purna Swaraj**.
* Adopted two historic resolutions: **Fundamental Rights** and **National Economic Programme** (spelling out what Swaraj would mean for the starving millions).

#### The Resolution on Fundamental Rights Guaranteed:
* Free speech, free press, and the right to form associations and assemble.
* **Universal adult franchise**.
* Equal legal rights irrespective of caste, creed, and sex.
* State neutrality in religious matters.
* Free and compulsory primary education.
* Protection of minority cultures, languages, and scripts.

#### The Resolution on National Economic Programme included:
* Substantial reduction in rent and revenue for peasants and landholders.
* Exemption from rent for uneconomic holdings.
* Relief from agricultural indebtedness and control of usury.
* Better working conditions: living wage, limited work hours, and protection of women workers.
* Right of workers and peasants to form unions.
* State ownership and control of key industries, mines, and transport.

## The Round Table Conferences

### First Round Table Conference
* **November 12, 1930–January 1931 (London)**: Opened by King George V, chaired by Ramsay MacDonald → first conference with British and Indians as equals.
* **Boycott**: Boycotted by the Congress and prominent business leaders.
* **Key Representatives**:
  * **Princely States**: Maharajas of Alwar, Baroda, Bhopal, Bikaner, Dholpur, J&K, Nawanagar, Patiala, Rewa, Chief Sahib of Sangli, Akbar Hydari, Mirza Ismail.
  * **Muslim League**: Aga Khan III, Maulana Mohammad Ali Jauhar, Muhammad Shafi, M.A. Jinnah, Zafarullah Khan, Fazlul Huq, Shafa’at Ahmad Khan.
  * **Hindu Mahasabha**: B.S. Moonje, M.R. Jayakar, Raja Narendra Nath.
  * **Sikhs**: Ujjal Singh, Sampuran Singh.
  * **Parsis**: Phiroze Sethna, Cowasji Jehangir, Homi Mody.
  * **Women**: Begum Jahanara Shahnawaz, Radhabai Subbarayan.
  * **Liberals**: Tej Bahadur Sapru, C.Y. Chintamani, V.S. Srinivasa Sastri, Chimanlal Setalvad.
  * **Depressed Classes**: **B.R. Ambedkar**, Rettamalai Srinivasan.
  * **Justice Party**: Arcot Ramasamy Mudaliar, Sir A.P. Patro.
  * **Labour**: N.M. Joshi, B. Shiva Rao.
  * **Indian Christians**: K.T. Paul → **Anglo-Indians**: Henry Gidney → **Europeans**: Sir Hubert Carr.
* **Outcome**: Broad agreement on a proposed Indian Federation with safeguards on defence and finance, but little concrete implementation. British realized no constitutional scheme was viable without Congress participation.

### Second Round Table Conference
* **September 7–December 1, 1931 (London)**:
* **Congress Representation**: **M.K. Gandhi** attended as the **sole representative** of the Congress, along with advisors A. Rangaswami Iyengar and Madan Mohan Malaviya.
* **Other Key Participants**: Same as the First RTC, plus **G.D. Birla**, **Purshottamdas Thakurdas**, **V.V. Giri** (Labour), and **Sarojini Naidu** (Women).
* **Reasons for Deadlock/Failure**:
  * **Viceroy Change**: **Lord Willingdon** replaced Lord Irwin in India.
  * **UK Government Shift**: Tory-dominated Conservative National Government replaced Labour; reactionary **Samuel Hoare** became Secretary of State.
  * **Conservative Backlash**: Winston Churchill objected to negotiating with the Congress on an equal basis; demanded firm rule.
  * **Representation Claims**: Gandhi claimed the Congress alone represented all political India against British imperialism. Other delegates (conservatives, loyalists, communalists) disputed this, and were utilized by the British to neutralize Gandhi.
  * **Minorities Deadlock**: Muslims, depressed classes, Christians, and Anglo-Indians demanded separate electorates, forming a joint **'Minorities' Pact'**.
    * Gandhi vehemently fought separate electorates for untouchables, stating they were Hindus and separate electorates would "ensure untouchables remained untouchables in perpetuity."
  * **Princes' Hesitation**: Federal enthusiasm declined once the Congress suspended the CDM.
* **Outcome**: Complete deadlock. Ramsay MacDonald ended the session by announcing:
  1. Setting up of two Muslim-majority provinces (**NWFP and Sindh**).
  2. Formation of an Indian Consultative Committee and three expert committees (finance, franchise, states).
  3. Threat of a unilateral **British Communal Award** if Indian delegates failed to agree.
  * Gandhi returned to India on December 28, 1931.

### Third Round Table Conference
* **November 17–December 24, 1932**: Not attended by the Congress or Gandhi; ignored by most major Indian leaders.
* **Attendees**: Representatives of some States (Hydari, Ismail, Krishnamachari), Ambedkar, Sapru, Jayakar, Iqbal, Zafarullah Khan.
* **Outcome**: Recommendations published in a **White Paper (March 1933)** → Joint Select Committee formulated a draft bill in February 1935 → enacted as the **Government of India Act, 1935** in July 1935.

## Civil Disobedience Resumed

* **Resumption**: December 29, 1931 → CWC decided to resume CDM due to RTC failure.

### During Truce Period (March–December 1931)
* Spirit of defiance kept alive:
  * **United Provinces**: Congress led rent-reduction and anti-eviction campaigns.
  * **NWFP**: Repression unleashed against the **Khudai Khidmatgars** agitating against brutal tax collection.
  * **Bengal**: Draconian ordinances and mass detentions; **Hijli Jail firing** on political prisoners (September 1931).

### Changed Government Attitude After Second RTC
* British officials determined to reverse the "Delhi Pact prestige" of Congress through three main considerations:
  1. Do not permit Gandhi to build up mass momentum again.
  2. Goodwill of Congress not required; prioritize confidence of loyalists and government functionaries.
  3. Halt national movement consolidation in rural areas.
* Viceroy Willingdon refused to meet Gandhi (December 31) → **Gandhi arrested on January 4, 1932**.

### Government Action
* Issued repressive ordinances establishing a civilian-controlled **'Civil Martial Law'**.
* Congress organizations banned at all levels; property confiscated, ashrams occupied, press gagged, and literature banned → harsh repression on women.

### Popular Response
* Angry, massive, but unstructured response → **80,000 satyagrahis jailed** in the first 4 months.
* Protest forms: Foreign cloth/liquor picketing, flag hoistings, non-payment of chowkidara tax, and a secret radio transmitter near Bombay.
* Coincided with popular upsurges in **Kashmir and Alwar** princely states.
* **Why it declined**: Lack of time to build tempo; masses unprepared.
* **Withdrawal**: Gandhi officially withdrew CDM in **April 1934**.

## Communal Award And Poona Pact

* **The Communal Award (August 16, 1932)**: Announced by British PM Ramsay MacDonald ( Lothian Committee findings).
  * **Core Provisions**:
    * Established separate electorates and reserved seats for minorities.
    * **Depressed classes** were granted minority status and allocated **78 reserved seats**.
    * Provided separate electorates for Muslims, Europeans, Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, depressed classes, and Marathas (Bombay).
    * Arrangement for depressed classes to last for 20 years.
    * Depressed classes received a **'double vote'** (one in general electorate, one in separate electorate).
* **Ambedkar's Stance**: Had consistently argued (to Simon Commission and 2nd RTC) that Dalits should be treated as a distinct, independent political minority separate from caste Hindus.
* **Congress Stand**: Opposed to separate electorates but decided "neither to accept nor reject" the Communal Award without minority consent.
* **Gandhi’s Response**: Saw the Award as a divide-and-rule attack on Indian unity.
  * Argued separate electorates would kill the struggle to eradicate untouchability and make untouchables perpetual social outcasts.
  * Demanded depressed classes be elected via joint electorate with wider/universal franchise, with no limit on reserved seats.
  * To press his demands, went on an **indefinite fast on September 20, 1932** (Yerawada Jail).

### Poona Pact
* **September 24, 1932**: Compromise hammered out by B.R. Ambedkar, M.C. Rajah, Madan Mohan Malaviya, and Gandhi.
* **Key Provisions**:
  * **Abandoned separate electorates** for the depressed classes.
  * **Increased reserved seats** in provincial legislatures from 71 to **147**.
  * Depressed classes granted **18% of the total seats** in the Central Legislature.
  * Accepted by the government as an amendment to the Communal Award.

### Impact of Poona Pact on Dalits
* **Critical Assessments**:
  * Enabled the continuation of the old Hindu social order; Dalit interests subordinated to caste organizations.
  * Left depressed classes politically leaderless by choosing cooperative candidates backed by caste Hindus over genuine representatives.
  * Denied recognition of Dalits as a distinct element, pre-empting proper constitutional safeguards.

### Joint Electorates and Its Impact on Depressed Classes
* **All India Scheduled Caste Federation (Working Committee)**: Argued that the joint electorate system under the 1935 Act deprived Dalits of electing true representatives, turning Dalit candidates into tools of the Hindu majority → demanded restoration of separate electorates.
* B.R. Ambedkar continued to denounce the Poona Pact until **1947**.

## Gandhi’s Harijan Campaign And Thoughts On Caste

* **Launch**: September 1932 (while in jail) → set up the **All India Anti-Untouchability League** and started the weekly ***Harijan*** in January 1933.
* **Harijan Tour (November 1933–July 1934)**: Started from Wardha (vowed not to return to Sabarmati until Swaraj won) → covered **20,000 km**, collected funds for the **Harijan Sevak Sangh**, and campaigned against untouchability.
* **Fasts**: May 8 and August 16, 1934 → threw nationalist ranks into emotional crises.
* **Opposition**: Orthodox Hindus held black flag protests, accused him of attacking Hinduism, and supported the government (which defeated the **Temple Entry Bill** in August 1934).
* **Core Themes of Campaign**:
  * Total eradication of untouchability; throw open temples.
  * Demanded caste Hindus do "penance" for historic oppression.
  * *"Hinduism dies if untouchability lives, untouchability has to die if Hinduism is to live."*
  * Campaign based on reason and humanism (scriptures sanctioning untouchability must be ignored).
  * Kept untouchability campaign separate from inter-dining and inter-marriage to avoid over-diversion.
  * Distinguished untouchability from the caste system (varnashram system was not sinful, but high/low discrimination was).
  * Favored internal reform by Dalits (education, hygiene, giving up beef/liquor).

### Impact of The Campaign
* Purified Hindu society and carried the nationalist message directly to Dalit agricultural laborers → increased their participation in national and peasant movements.

## Ideological Differences And Similarities Between Gandhi And Ambedkar

### Key Similarities and Differences
* **Symbolic Parallels**:
  * Foreign cloth burning (Gandhi) $\approx$ Manusmriti burning (Ambedkar).
  * Pinch of salt (Dandi) $\approx$ drop of water (Mahad tank).
* **Attainment of Freedom**:
  * **Gandhi**: Wrest from British authority via direct political action and struggle.
  * **Ambedkar**: Expected bestowing of freedom by the imperial rulers.
* **Parliamentary Democracy**:
  * **Gandhi**: Had little respect for parliamentary governance; feared elite-dominated mass democracy.
  * **Ambedkar**: Advocated a parliamentary system; believed mass democracy could pressure the state to advance oppressed groups.
* **Ideological Stance**:
  * **Gandhi**: Pragmatic, non-rigid except on non-violence.
  * **Ambedkar**: Rigorously structured, natural inclination for liberal ideology and institutional structures.
* **Indian Unity**:
  * **Gandhi**: Believed India was always a unified nation culturally prior to British rule.
  * **Ambedkar**: Viewed unity as a by-product of the uniform legal system introduced by the British.
* **Indian Villages (Gramraj)**:
  * **Gandhi**: *"Gramraj is Ramraj"* → village self-sufficiency represents real independence.
  * **Ambedkar**: Denounced villages as status-quoist dens of discrimination, hierarchy, and casteism.
* **Social Reform Force**:
  * Both opposed the use of state coercion/force for social integration, choosing proper education to inspire change.
* **Harijan Nomenclature**:
  * **Gandhi**: Coined the term 'Harijan' (people of God).
  * **Ambedkar**: Denounced it as a clever, patronizing scheme; left the Harijan Sevak Sangh.
* **Religion & State**:
  * **Gandhi**: Opposed separation of politics and religion, but held caste was a perverse degeneration separate from pure spirituality.
  * **Ambedkar**: Advocated strict separation of State and religion; left Hinduism in later life, denouncing it as an entity that cannot be reformed.
* **State Power**:
  * Both believed in limited sovereign state power.
  * **Gandhi**: Least governance is best governance.
  * **Ambedkar**: Legal sovereignty should be limited, ultimate sovereignty lies with the people.
* **Violence vs. Non-violence**:
  * **Gandhi**: Avowed opponent of violence; purity of means determines the end.
  * **Ambedkar**: Absolute non-violence as an end, relative violence as a means; ends justify means.
* **Machinery & Industry**:
  * **Gandhi**: Opposed heavy mechanization due to its dehumanizing and exploitative socio-economic impacts.
  * **Ambedkar**: Welcomed machinery and modern civilization; believed wrong social organization (not machinery) caused its evils → demanded "machinery and more machinery."
* **Transformation Method**:
  * Both strongly supported democratic, peaceful, and constitutional means of transformation; rejected violent revolution.
* **Mobilisation**:
  * **Gandhi**: Vernacular communication, emphasized morality and conscience over legal systems.
  * **Ambedkar**: English communication, relied heavily on law, constitutionality, and historical analysis.
