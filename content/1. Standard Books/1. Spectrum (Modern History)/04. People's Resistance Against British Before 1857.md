### People’s Resistance: Meaning
*   **"People"** = Peasants + artisans + tribals + active/dispossessed ruling classes + demobilised soldiers + religious leaders (Hindu and Muslim) fighting for self-interest.
*   **Urban Movements** (Artisans + shopkeepers + urban poor + urban gentry) → Combined resistance against municipal/tax policies:
    *   **Benares (1810)** → Against colonial house tax.
    *   **Surat (1814)** → Against salt duty.
    *   **Bareilly (1816)** → Against police and municipal taxes.
*   **Three Broad Forms (Bipan Chandra)**:
    1.  **Civil rebellions**
    2.  **Tribal uprisings**
    3.  **Peasant movements**
*   **Military Revolts**: Sporadic mutinies by Indian sepoys within Company's forces over service conditions.

---

### Genesis of People’s Resistance
*   **Pre-colonial Protests** → Redress existed for high revenue, corruption, or harsh official attitudes.
*   **Colonial Rule** → Annihilative impact + absolute lack of grievance redressal → EIC sole focus: revenue extraction.
*   **Colonial Judiciary & Law** → Safeguarded government interests and collaborators (**landlords, merchants, moneylenders**) → Left masses no choice but to resort to arms.
*   **Tribal Aggrievement** → Encroachment by mainland outsiders (*dikus*) into independent tribal polity → Violent defense of autonomy.

---

### Causative Factors for People’s Uprisings
*   **Colonial land revenue settlements** + new heavy taxes → Massive peasant evictions.
*   **Encroachment on tribal lands** → Rapid expansion of revenue administration → Complete loss of tribal rights over forests and agricultural land.
*   **Rural exploitation** → Shifting power to intermediary revenue collectors, tenants, and usurious moneylenders.
*   **Deindustrialisation** → British manufactured goods promoted + heavy duties on Indian exports → Devastation of indigenous handloom and handicraft industries.
*   **Pressure on land** → Ruined artisans/workers migrate to rural areas → Agriculture over-burdened.

---

### Civil Uprisings
*   **Concept** = Non-military uprisings led by deposed native rulers/descendants, former zamindars, landlords, **poligars** (South Indian village territory holders granted land by Nayakas for military service/tribute), ex-officials, or religious leaders.
*   **Mass Base** = Discontented/rack-rented peasants, unemployed artisans, and demobilised soldiers.

---

### Major Causes of Civil Uprisings
*   **Rapid agrarian/administrative changes** → Colonial land revenue system ruined traditional landholders.
*   **Loss of power & status** → Zamindars/poligars dispossessed of lands; traditional elites sidelined by EIC officials and a new class of merchants/moneylenders.
*   **Ruin of handlooms/handicrafts** → Traditional patrons (princes, chieftains, zamindars) vanished → Millions of artisans impoverished.
*   **Priestly class instigation** → Pundits, priests, maulvis dependent on traditional landed elite lost livelihood → Spread hatred against alien rule.
*   **Foreign character of British rule** → Contemptuous treatment of natives hurt local pride.

---

### General Characteristics of Civil Uprisings
*   **Semi-feudal Leadership** → Backward-looking, traditional in outlook; basic objective was restoration of earlier forms of rule and social relations.
*   **Localized** → Triggered by local grievances with localized consequences; lacked broad-based national integration.

### Important Civil Uprisings

#### **Sanyasi Revolt (1763–1800)**
*   **Causes**: Famine of **1770** + harsh British economic exploitation → Peasant evictions → Joint revolt of Sanyasis, dispossessed zamindars, disbanded soldiers, and rural poor.
*   **Actions**: Raided Company factories, plundered treasuries, clashed with EIC forces.
*   **Resolution**: Subdued after prolonged military action by **Warren Hastings**.
*   **Key Feature**: Commendable Hindu-Muslim unity; also termed **Fakir Rebellion**.
*   **Leaders**: **Majnum Shah** (or **Majnu Shah**), **Chirag Ali**, **Musa Shah**, **Bhawani Pathak**, and **Debi Chaudhurani** (marking early female resistance).
*   **Literary Anchors**: Based on this revolt are Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's semi-historical novels ***Anandamath*** and ***Devi Chaudhurani***.

#### **Revolt in Midnapore and Dhalbhum (1766–74)**
*   **Cause**: Introduction of the new land revenue system (**1772**) by Governor Vansittart → Dispossessed nearly 3,000 peaceful zamindars/talukdars who sided with ryots.
*   **Leaders**: **Damodar Singh** and **Jagannath Dhal**.

#### **Revolt of Moamarias (1769–99)**
*   **Nature**: Potent low-caste peasant rebellion in Assam following teachings of **Aniruddhadeva**; challenged Ahom kings' authority.
*   **Chronology**: Moamaria rebellion weakened Ahom state → In 1792, King **Krishnanarayan** of Darrang revolted with *burkandazes* (demobilised Muslim mercenaries) → Ahom ruler forced to seek British help → Assam fell to Burmese invasion, then to British rule.
*   **Affected Regions**: **Bhatiapar** (rebel HQ), **Rangpur**, and **Jorhat**.

#### **Civil Uprisings in Gorakhpur, Basti and Bahraich (1781)**
*   **Cause**: Warren Hastings involved British officer **Major Alexander Hannay** as *izaradar* (revenue farmer) in **1778** to raise funds for Maratha/Mysore wars → Hannay secured Gorakhpur and Bahraich *izara* for Rs 22 lakh.
*   **Consequence**: Hannay's extreme extortion/oppression → Flourihsing region panicked → Zamindars & cultivators rose in 1781 → Hannay's subordinates killed/besieged → Rebellion suppressed, but Hannay dismissed and *izara* removed.

#### **Revolt of Raja of Vizianagaram (1794)**
*   **Cause**: **1758 treaty** between EIC and **Ananda Gajapatiraju** promised to oust French from Northern Circars; EIC broke terms post-victory.
*   **Trigger**: EIC demanded Rs 3 lakh tribute from **Vizayaramaraju** (Raja) and ordered him to disband his troops.
*   **Consequence**: Raja and subjects rebelled → Raja killed at **Battle of Padmanabham (1794)** → Vizianagaram annexed; later handed to his son at reduced tribute.

#### **Revolt of Dhundia in Bednur (1799–1800)**
*   **Leader**: **Dhundia Wagh** (Maratha chief, converted to Islam by Tipu, released after fall of Seringapatam).
*   **Outcome**: Organized anti-British force → Defeated in Aug 1799 → Fled to Maratha territory to lead rebel coalition. Killed in battle against **Arthur Wellesley** (Sept 1800).

#### **Resistance of Kerala Varma Pazhassi Raja (1797; 1800–05)**
*   **Leader**: **Kerala Varma Pazhassi Raja** (**Pyche Raja** / **Kerala Simham**), de facto head of Kottayam (Malabar).
*   **1st Phase (1793–97)**: British ignored Kottayam independence (violating 1790 treaty), appointed **Vira Varma** (uncle) as Raja → Exorbitant peasant taxation → Mass peasant resistance under Pyche Raja using guerrilla warfare → Peace treaty in **1797**.
*   **2nd Phase (1800–05)**: Dispute over Wayanad annexation → Pyche Raja raised large force of Nairs, Mappilas, and Pathans (unemployed soldiers of Tipu) → Killed in gunfight at **Mavila Todu** (Nov 1805).

#### **Civil Rebellion in Awadh (1799)**
*   **Trigger**: **Wazir Ali Khan** (4th Nawab of Awadh) deposed by British, replaced by uncle **Saadat Ali Khan II** and pensioned to Benares.
*   **Massacre of Benares**: In Jan 1799, Wazir Ali assassinated British Resident **George Frederick Cherry**, his guards, and other Europeans.
*   **Outcome**: Wazir Ali raised several thousand men → Defeated by **Gen. Erskine** → Fled to Butwal; granted asylum by Jaipur Raja → Extradited on condition of no execution/fetters → Confined at Fort William, Calcutta.

#### **Uprisings in Ganjam and Gumsur (1800, 1835–37)**
*   **1st Phase (1800)**: Zamindar **Strikara Bhanj** rebelled in Ganjam, joined by **Jlani Deo** and **Jagannath Deo** (captured 1804). **Dhananjaya Bhanj** (Strikara's son) rebelled in 1815. Strikara reappointed as zamindar in compromise (1819–30).
*   **2nd Phase (1835–37)**: **Dhananjaya Bhanj** rebelled again over revenue arrears → British forces occupied Gumsur → Dhananjaya died (Dec 1835). Commissioner **George Russell** suppressed the ongoing resistance by Feb 1837; arrested leader **Doora Bisayi** and forfeited Gumsur zamindari.

#### **Uprisings in Palamau (1800–02)**
*   **Cause**: Landlordism and feudal crisis → Chero chief **Bhukhan Singh** rebelled in 1800.
*   **Outcome**: Suppressed by **Col. Jones** after a two-year campaign; Bhukhan Singh executed/died in 1802.

#### **Poligars’ Revolt (1795–1805)**
*   **Cause**: Nawab of Arcot gave revenue control of Tinneveli and Carnatic to EIC in **1781** → Poligars resented loss of independent sovereign rights.
*   **1st Phase (1795–99)**: **Veerapandiya Kattabomman** (Poligar of Panjalankurichi) led taxation revolt → Betrayed by **Ettappan** (Raja of Pudukottai) → Kattabomman and associate **Subramania Pillai** hanged; estates confiscated.
*   **2nd Phase (1801–05)**: Escaped rebels led by **Oomathurai** (Kattabomman's brother) joined **Marudu Pandian** ("Marudus" of Sivaganga). Suppressed by Oct 1801; Panjalankurichi fort razed.
*   **North Arcot (1803–05)**: Poligars of Chittur and Chandragiri revolted against EIC's suppression of hereditary **kaval** (watch/village police) fees. Suppressed by Feb 1805.

#### **Uprisings in Haryana Region (1803-1810)**
*   **Cause**: Treaty of Surji-Arjungaon (**1803**) transferred Haryana to EIC.
*   **Resistances**:
    *   Sikh chiefs of Ambala, Karnal, and Thanesar.
    *   Muslim Bhatti Rajputs under **Zabita Khan** (Sirsa/Rania) and **Khan Bahadur Khan** (Fatehabad) → Defeated by **Col. Adams** (1809).
    *   Jats & Ranghars of Rohtak/Bhiwani → Subdued by **Gardiner** (1810) using heavy artillery to breach Bhiwani walls; Hansi fort fortified.

#### **Diwan Velu Thampi’s Revolt (1808–09)**
*   **Cause**: Heavy subsidy arrears from Wellesley’s 1805 Subsidiary Alliance with Travancore + Resident's interference.
*   **Kundara Proclamation (1809)**: Prime Minister (**Dalawa**) **Velu Thampi** called for armed struggle against British → Large-scale Nair troop uprising.
*   **Outcome**: EIC launched major campaign; the Maharaja defected to EIC; Velu Thampi committed suicide to avoid capture.

#### **Disturbances in Bundelkhand (1808–12)**
*   **Context**: Bundela chiefs resisted EIC from ~150 forts post-Second Maratha War.
*   **Key Resistances**:
    *   **Lakshaman Dawa** (Killadar of Ajaygarh Fort, surrendered Feb 1809).
    *   **Darya Singh** (Killadar of Kalanjar, suppressed Jan 1812).
    *   **Gopal Singh** (military adventurer; waged 4-year guerrilla war).
*   **Resolution**: Chieftains bound down by contractual obligations called **Ikarnamahs**.

#### **Parlakimedi Outbreak (1813–34)**
*   **Conflict**: Raja **Narayan Deo** of Parlakimedi resisted Ganjam acquisition. EIC sent **Col. Peach** (1768) who installed **Gajapathi Deo** (son), but Narayan and brothers continued revolting.
*   **Resolution**: Commissioner **George Russell** pacified the region by 1834.

#### **Kutch or Cutch Rebellion (1816–32)**
*   **Cause**: Power struggle between Raja **Bharamal II** (who raised Arab/African troops to expel EIC in 1819) and pro-British chieftains.
*   **Outcome**: Bharamal II deposed in favor of infant son; British resident ruled via regency council. Conciliatory policy adopted after Burma War reverses emboldened chiefs.

#### **Rising at Bareilly (1816)**
*   **Trigger**: Imposition of municipal police tax + tax collectors injuring a local woman.
*   **Religious Turn**: Venerated **Mufti Muhammad Aiwaz** petitioned; armed Muslims from Pilibhit, Shahjahanpur, and Rampur rose in "jehad" to defend faith.
*   **Outcome**: Insurgents murdered son of judge Leycester. Suppressed by military force; 300+ rebels killed.

#### **Upsurge in Hathras (1817)**
*   **Leader**: **Dayaram** (talukdar of several villages with base in Hathras Fort—a "second Bharatpur").
*   **Cause**: EIC hiked land revenues → Dayaram defaulted and harbored government fugitives.
*   **Outcome**: EIC besieged Hathras in Feb 1817; Dayaram escaped but submitted later for a pension. **Bhagwant Singh** (Raja of Mursan) also dismantled his fort and submitted.

#### **Paika Rebellion (1817)**
*   **Context**: **Paiks** (hereditary landed militia of Odisha, holding rent-free land tenures) ruined by EIC's 1803 conquest of Khurda.
*   **Grievances**: Extortionate land revenue, salt tax/price rise, cowrie currency abolition, silver tax-payment rule.
*   **Outbreak**: **Bakshi Jagabandhu Bidyadhar** (dispossessed of ancestral Killa Rorang estate in 1814) led Paiks, supported by last Raja of Khurda (**Mukunda Deva**) and Khonds of Gumsur.
*   **Consequence**: Paika rebels resorted to guerrilla tactics; Puri temple priests hanged for sheltering Jagabandhu. Suppressed by 1818; Jagabandhu surrendered in 1825.
*   **Reforms Obtained**: Arrears remitted, assessments reduced, default sales suspended, fixed tenures introduced.

#### **Waghera Rising (1818–20)**
*   **Cause**: Extortions of pro-British Gaekwad of Baroda.
*   **Outcome**: **Waghera chiefs** of Okha Mandal launched armed incursions; peace treaty signed in Nov 1820.

#### **Ahom Revolt (1828)**
*   **Cause**: British broke pledge to withdraw from Assam after First Burma War (1824–26).
*   **Outcome**: Rebellion under Ahom prince **Gomdhar Konwar**, **Dhanjay Borgohain**, and **Jairam Khargharia Phukan**. EIC pacified them by restoring Upper Assam to Maharaja **Purandar Singh Narendra**.

#### **Surat Salt Agitations (1840s)**
*   **1844**: Local attacks on Europeans forced the withdrawal of salt duty hike (from 50 paise to Re 1).
*   **1848**: Mass boycott and passive resistance forced withdrawal of Bengal Standard Weights and Measures.

#### **Kolhapur and Savantvadi Revolts**
*   **Kolhapur (1844)**: Disbandment of **Gadkaris** (hereditary military class in Maratha forts) → Gadkaris captured Samangarh and Bhudargarh forts. Suppressed.
*   **Savantvadi (1844–59)**: Native revolts against deposition of their ruler. Led by **Phond Savant**, **Subana Nikam**, **Daji Lakshman**, and **Har Savant Dingnekar**.

#### **Wahabi Movement**
*   **Inspiration**: Islamic revivalist movement founded by **Syed Ahmed of Rai Bareilly** (inspired by **Abdul Wahab** of Arabia and **Shah Waliullah** of Delhi); advocated return to pure Islam.
*   **Structure**: Secret code + vice-regents (**Khalifas**) + base at **Sithana** (NW frontier) + HQ at **Patna**.
*   **Target Shift**: Originally declared *jihad* against Sikh Kingdom; post-1849 EIC annexation of Punjab, EIC became sole target. Weakened by 1860s military campaigns.

#### **Kuka Movement**
*   **Founders**: **Bhagat Jawahar Mal** (**Sian Saheb**, 1840) and **Baba Ram Singh** (Namdhari Sikh sect).
*   **Aims**:
    *   *Social*: Abolition of caste, widow remarriage, women stepping out of seclusion, ban on meat/alcohol/drugs.
    *   *Political*: Oust British and restore Sikh rule. Propagated **Swadeshi** (hand-woven clothes) and **non-cooperation** (boycott of British laws/goods/education).
*   **Fate**: Ram Singh deported to Rangoon in **1872**.

### Peasant Movements With Religious Overtones

#### **Narkelberia Uprising (1831)**
*   **Leader**: **Mir Nithar Ali** (**Titu Mir**) inspired Muslim tenants of West Bengal.
*   **Target**: Hindu landlords (imposing beard-tax on Faraizis) and British indigo planters. Merged into Wahabi Movement; first armed peasant uprising.

#### **The Pagal Panthis (1825–35)**
*   **Profile**: Semi-religious group of Hajong and Garo tribes in Mymensingh (Bengal) founded by **Karam Shah**.
*   **Action**: Led by Karam Shah’s son **Tipu** against oppressive zamindars; refused rent above equitable limits. Suppressed violently.

#### **Faraizi Revolt (1838–57)**
*   **Founders**: Sect of **Haji Shariatullah** of Faridpur (East Bengal).
*   **Leader**: **Muhsinuddin Ahmad** (**Dudu Miyan**, 1819–62) organized tenants to expel British. Later merged with Wahabis.

#### **Moplah Uprisings (1836–1854)**
*   **Cause**: Revenue hikes, field size reductions, and official oppression of Moplah peasants in Malabar.
*   **Outcome**: 22 unsuccessful uprisings before 1854.

#### **Peasants’ Role in The 1857 Revolt**
*   Active in western Uttar Pradesh; joined local feudal leaders.
*   **Post-Revolt**: Plight worsened as British restored lands/powers to **taluqdars** (Awadh) and ignored the **1859 Bengal Rent Act**; additional cesses levied on peasants as punishment.

### Tribal Revolts
*   **Characteristics**: Most frequent militant, and violent of all pre-1857 uprisings.

---

### Different Causes for Mainland and North-Eastern Tribal Revolts

| Mainland Tribal Revolts | Frontier Tribal Revolts (North-East) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Land & Forest loss**: British land settlements destroyed joint ownership; shifting cultivation restricted to supply EIC with railway/ship timber. | **Political Autonomy**: Fought for political independence/autonomy; did not suffer forest/land loss. |
| **Outsiders**: Influx of *dikus* (traders, moneylenders, police) who exploited tribals. | **Cross-Border Ties**: Cultural links across borders; colonization came much later; struggles lasted longer. |
| **Religion**: Christian missionary work seen as an EIC threat to traditional customs. | **De-sanskritisation**: Meiteis under **Churchand Maharaja** (1891–1941) revolted against neo-Vaishnavite Brahmins. |

---

### Characteristics of Tribal Revolts
*   **Solidarity**: Strong ethnic and tribal identity ties.
*   **Selective Violence**: Targeted moneylenders/traders; local poor village professionals left unharmed.
*   **Anti-Foreign Laws**: Resisted EIC land and forest regulations.
*   **Messiah Leadership**: Led by messiah-like figures promising liberation from *dikus*.
*   **Outdated Arms**: traditional bows and arrows proved no match for modern British weaponry.

### Important Tribal Movements of The Mainland

#### **The Pahariyas (1778)**
*   **Profile**: Rajmahal hill folk practicing shifting cultivation; raided plains villages for subsistence and power.
*   **Conflict**: EIC expanded settled agriculture and cleared forests → Pahariya raids increased → EIC launched brutal hunts in 1770s.
*   **Outcome**: 1778 rebellion led by Raja **Jagganath** → EIC adopted pacification policy (1780s), giving annual allowances to chiefs.

#### **The Revolt Led by Tilka Manjhi (1784–85)**
*   **Leader**: **Tilka Manjhi** (real name **Jabra Paharia**), Santhal chief.
*   **Action**: Sultanganj/Tarai guerrilla encounters; forming joint front with Paharia Sardars to take Ramgarh Camp (1778).
*   **Outcome**: Shot Rajmahal magistrate **Augustus Cleveland** (1784). Hanged in **1785**; revered as first Adivasi armed insurgent.

#### **Jungle Mahal Revolt or The Chuar Uprising (1766–1816)**
*   **Chuars** = Bhumij tribals (Mundari stock) of Midnapur, Bankura, and Purulia; village *paiks* (guards) hired by jungle zamindars under head *sardars*.
*   **Phases**:
    *   **1768**: Ghatsila zamindar **Jagannath Singh** (Dhalbhum King) rose with Chuars against revenue hikes; EIC capitulated.
    *   **1771**: Chuar sardars **Shyam Ganjan** (Dhadka), **Subla Singh** (Kaliapal), and **Dubraj** rebelled.
    *   **1798**: Significant revolt under **Durjan** (or **Durjol**) **Singh** (dispossessed zamindar of Raipur) when EIC Permanent Settlement and police rules replaced native *paiks* with professional police. Other leaders: **Madhab Singh**, **Raja Mohan Singh**, and **Lachman Singh**.

#### **Tamar Revolt (1798)**
*   **Cause**: Alien EIC administrative systems → Mundas and chiefs joined **Bholanath Sahay** against *dikus*. Suppressed by **Lt. Cooper**.

#### **Kol Mutiny (1831)**
*   **Cause**: Large-scale land transfer from Kol headmen to Hindu, Sikh, Muslim farmers/moneylenders + EIC judicial encroachment.
*   **Outcome**: Led by **Buddho Bhagat**; killed ~1,000 outsiders. Suppressed after heavy military operations.

#### **Ho and Munda Uprisings (1820–37)**
*   **Raja of Parahat** led Ho tribals against EIC occupation of Singhbhum (submitted 1827).
*   **1831**: Ho & Mundas rebelled against farming revenue policy. Ho resistance continued till 1837.

#### **The Santhal Rebellions (1833; 1855–56)**
*   **"Hoe vs. Plough"** = Nomadic Pahariyas (hoe) vs. settled Santhals (plough).
*   **Damin-i-koh (1832-33)** = Rajmahal foothills demarcated for settled Santhals.
*   **Grievances**: 1793 Permanent Settlement taxes heavy → Santhals indebted to *diku* moneylenders → Zamindars seized Damin lands.
*   **The Hul (Liberation)**: Proclaimed end of EIC rule under **Sidhu** and **Kanhu Murmu**; used **sal branches** for secret communication.
*   **Women's role**: Sisters **Phulo** and **Jhano Murmu** raided enemy camps, killing soldiers before dying.
*   **Result**: Suppressed in 1856 using elephants and village burnings; Murmu brothers killed. **Santhal Pargana** created with special laws.

### Some Tribal Movements after 1857

#### **Bokta Rising, Sardari Larai, or Mukti Larai Movement (1858–95)**
*   **Context**: Chotanagpur. Aimed at regaining age-old land rights. Turned anti-European in the 1890s due to perceived collusion with landlords.

#### **Birsa Munda Revolt (1899–1900)**
*   **The Ulgulan (Great Tumult)**: South of Ranchi; aimed at Munda Raj & independence; rebelled against zamindari tenures, usury, and **begar** (forced labor).
*   **Leader**: **Birsa Munda** (arrested 1895, jailed for 2 years).
*   **Outcome**: Waged armed struggle against *dikus* (missionaries, landlords, police). Birsa captured in 1900, died of illness in jail.
*   **Reforms**: *Begar* abolished; Mundas' Khuntkatti system recognized under **Tenancy Act of 1903**; **Chotanagpur Tenancy Act (1908)** passed.

#### **Tana Bhagat Movement (1914–15)**
*   **Leaders**: **Jatra Oraon** (Ranchi, 1914) and **Sibu Oraon** (1919).
*   **Profile**: Oraon and Munda tribes practicing nonviolent, Sanskritisation-based satyagraha against *begar*, rent hikes, and missionaries. Joined INC in 1921.

#### **Devi Movement (1922–23)**
*   **Type**: South Gujarat/Surat social movement commanding clean habits and abstinence from meat/liquor. Targeted exploitative landlords/liquor traders; integrated with Non-Cooperation.

#### **Khond Uprisings (1837–56)**
*   **Leader**: **Chakra Bisoi** (young Raja) led Khonds of hilly tracts from Odisha to Andhra.
*   **Cause**: Opposed EIC suppression of human sacrifice (**mariah**), new taxes, and zamindari intrusion. (Later 1914 Orissa rebellion for autonomous government).

#### **Koya Revolts**
*   **Phases**: 1803, 1840, 1845, 1858, 1861, 1862; under **Tomma Sora** (1879–80); under **Raja Anantayyar** (1886) in Eastern Godavari.
*   **Cause**: Police/moneylender oppression, forest rights denial.

#### **Bhil Revolts**
*   **Western Ghats (1817–19, 1825, 1831, 1846)**: Against EIC rule over famine and misgovernment.
*   **South Rajasthan (1913)**: Under **Govind Guru** for Bhil Raj.

#### **Koli Risings (1829, 1839, 1844–48)**
*   Western Ghats; EIC rule caused large-scale unemployment and dismantling of traditional forts.

#### **Ramosi Risings (1822–29, 1839–41)**
*   **Cause**: Ramosis (Western Ghats hill tribes) lost livelihood after British annexed Maratha lands.
*   **Phases**: Under **Chittur Singh** (1822); under **Umaji Naik** and **Bapu Trimbakji Sawant** (1825–29 Poona); in 1839 over deposition of Raja Pratap Singh of Satara.

### Tribal Movements of The North-East

#### **Khasi Uprising (1829–33)**
*   **Cause**: EIC occupied Jaintia/Garo hills, planned road through Brahmaputra Valley to Sylhet → Influx of plains laborers and Englishmen.
*   **Outcome**: Khasis, Garos, Khamptis, and Singphos revolted under **Tirath Singh** (Nunklow ruler). Suppressed by military in 1833.

#### **Singphos Rebellion**
*   **1830**: Assam uprising quelled; **1839** revolt killed EIC political agent.
*   **1843**: Chief **Nirang Phidu** attacked British garrison.

#### **North-East Frontier Tribal Movements: Year, Region, Major Causes**

*   **Movements Before 1857**:
    *   **Ahoms’ Revolt (1828–33)**: Assam; against non-fulfilment of EIC pledge to withdraw after Burmese War. Led by **Gomdhar Konwar**; kingdom partitioned.
    *   **Khasis’ Revolt (1830s)**: Garo/Jaintia Hills under **Tirath Singh** against military occupation.
    *   **Singphos’ Rebellion (1830s)**: Assam; culminated in 1839 agent murder.
*   **Movements After 1857**:
    *   **Kukis’ Revolt (1917–19)**: Manipur; against WWI labor recruitment.
    *   **Tripura Revolts**: Under **Parikshit Jamatia** (1863), Reangs’ revolt under **Ratnamani** (1942–43), and **Bharti Singh** (1920s) against house taxes.
    *   **Zeliangsong Movement (1920s)**: Manipur; Zemi, Liangmei, Rongmei tribes; against British failure to protect from Kuki violence.
    *   **Naga Movement (1905–31)**: Manipur under **Jadonang** for Naga Raj.
    *   **Heraka Cult (1930s)**: Manipur under **Gaidinliu** (Kabui Naga Association formed 1946).

### Sepoy Mutinies

#### **Causes**
1.  **Discrimination**: Extreme bias in pay, allowances, and promotion.
2.  **Mistreatment**: Physical/verbal abuse of sepoys by British officers.
3.  **Bhatta Refusal**: EIC refused foreign service allowance (*bhatta*) while fighting in remote regions (e.g., Sindh).
4.  **Caste/Religious Objections**:
    *   **Lord Canning’s General Service Enlistment Act (1856)**: Obliged recruits to serve anywhere, including overseas (crossing sea = loss of caste).
    *   **Vellore (1806)**: Turban replaced by leather cockade.
    *   **Barrackpore (1824)**: Refusal to cross sea to Burma.

#### **Important Mutinies**
*   **Bengal (1764)**
*   **Vellore Mutiny (1806)** → Unfurled Mysore ruler's flag.
*   **47th Native Infantry Unit (1824)** (Barrackpore).
*   **Grenadier Company (1825)** (Assam).
*   **Sholapur (1838)**.
*   **Regiment Mutinies**: **34th N.I. (1844)**, **22nd N.I. (1849)**, **66th N.I. (1850)**, and **37th N.I. (1852)**.

### Weaknesses Of People’s Uprisings
*   **Localized & Sporadic**: Lacked integration, occurred at different times, driven by distinct local grievances.
*   **Backward-Looking Leadership**: Led by semi-feudal elements with traditional outllook; lacked progressive alternatives to existing social set-up.
*   **No National Impulse**: Shared common oppressive conditions, but had no unified "national" consciousness.
*   **Obsolete Weapons**: Traditional fighters with bows/arrows/swords easily crushed by modern British arms, military strategy, and chicanery.
