* **Late 19th Century Industrialisation** ⇒ Entry of modern industry (railways, coal, cotton, jute) → rise of modern Indian working class.
* **Exploitation** ⇒ Low wages + long hours + hazardous conditions + child labour + no basic amenities.
* **Colonial Constraint** ⇒ Working class faced dual antagonism: **imperialist political rule** + **economic exploitation** (foreign and native capitalists) → intertwined with national political struggle.

---

## Early Efforts

* **Moderates' Indifference** ⇒ Indifferent to labour struggles due to:
  * Distinguishing between Indian-owned and British-owned enterprises.
  * Believing labour laws would reduce the competitive advantage of native industries.
  * Fearing class divisions within the national movement.
  * **Factory Acts of 1881 and 1891** ⇒ Opposed by Moderates for the above reasons.
* **Early Character** ⇒ Philanthropic, isolated, sporadic, aimed at specific local grievances rather than systemic change.
* **Key Milestones & Contributions**:
  * **1870 (Sasipada Banerjea)** ⇒ Started a workingmen’s club and published the newspaper ***Bharat Shramjeevi***.
  * **1878 (Sorabjee Shapoorji Bengalee)** ⇒ Attempted to introduce a bill in the Bombay Legislative Council for better working conditions.
  * **1880 (Narayan Meghajee Lokhande)** ⇒ Started ***Deenbandhu*** newspaper; established the **Bombay Mill and Millhands Association** (first labour organisation).
  * **1899 (GIP Railway Strike)** ⇒ First railway strike occurred; extensively supported by Tilak's ***Kesari*** and ***Maharatta***.
  * **Nationalist Pro-labour Advocates** ⇒ **Bipin Chandra Pal** and **G. Subramania Aiyar** demanded worker welfare reforms.

---

## During Swadeshi Upsurge

* **Political Shift** ⇒ Workers shifted focus to wider political issues.
* **Strikes Organised** ⇒ Ashwini Coomar Banerjee, Prabhat Kumar Roy Chaudhuri, Premtosh Bose, and Apurba Kumar Ghosh led strikes in:
  * Government press.
  * Railways.
  * Jute industry.
* **Trade Union Formation** ⇒ Early unsuccessful attempts made to build permanent trade unions.
* **Tuticorin & Tirunelvelli Strikes** ⇒ Led by **Subramaniya Siva** and **Chidambaram Pillai** → arrested by administration.
* **Biggest Strike** ⇒ Historic mass strike organised in Bombay following **Tilak's arrest and trial (1908)**.

---

## During The First World War And After

* **Wartime Squeeze** ⇒ Exports surge + soaring prices + massive industrial profiteering vs. stagnant low wages → acute worker discontent.
* **Gandhian Mobilisation** ⇒ Shift to a broad-based national movement → emphasis on mobilising workers/peasants for national struggle → created strong impetus for unionisation.
* **International Influences** ⇒ Russian Revolution (1917) + formation of Comintern (1919) + establishment of International Labour Organisation (ILO, 1919) → added a global socialist dimension.

---

## The AITUC

* **All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC)** ⇒ Founded on **October 31, 1920**.
  * **First President** ⇒ **Lala Lajpat Rai** (linked capitalism with imperialism: *"imperialism and militarism are the twin children of capitalism"*).
  * **First General Secretary** ⇒ **Dewan Chaman Lal**.
* **INC Support**:
  * **Gaya Session (1922)** ⇒ Welcomed AITUC's formation; appointed a committee to assist it.
  * **C.R. Das** ⇒ Presided over 3rd and 4th sessions of AITUC; advocated integrating workers and peasants into Congress to prevent isolation.
* **Prominent Associated Leaders** ⇒ Jawaharlal Nehru, Subhas Chandra Bose, C.F. Andrews, J.M. Sengupta, S. Satyamurthy, V.V. Giri, Sarojini Naidu.
* **Early Ideology** ⇒ Guided by British Labour Party social democracy + Gandhian philosophy of non-violence, trusteeship, and class-collaboration.
* **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (1918)** ⇒ Founded by **Mahatma Gandhi**; secured 27.5% wage hike via protest (later increased to 35% by arbitrator's award).

---

## The Trade Union Act, 1926

* **Legal Recognition** ⇒ Recognised trade unions as legal associations.
* **Registration & Regulation** ⇒ Prescribed specific conditions for registration and management.
* **Immunity** ⇒ Granted **civil and criminal immunity** from prosecution for legitimate union activities.
* **Political Restrictions** ⇒ Placed strict limitations on political activities and funding.

---

## Late 1920s

* **Communist Surge** ⇒ Militant and revolutionary shift led by growing communist influence.
* **1928 Bombay Textile Mill Strike** ⇒ Six-month-long strike led by **Girni Kamgar Union** (Lal Bawta) → unprecedented nationwide industrial unrest.
* **Communist Crystallisation** ⇒ Emergence of leaders like **S.A. Dange**, **Muzaffar Ahmed**, **P.C. Joshi**, and **Sohan Singh Joshi**.
* **Legislative Repression** ⇒ Government countered extremist influence with two measures:
  * **Public Safety Ordinance (1929)**.
  * **Trade Disputes Act (TDA), 1929**.
* **Trade Disputes Act, 1929 Provisions**:
  * Compulsory appointment of **Courts of Inquiry** and **Consultation Boards** to settle disputes.
  * Made strikes in public utilities (posts, railways, water, electricity) illegal without **one month advance notice**.
  * Banned coercive, purely political, and sympathetic strikes.

---

## Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929)

* **Crackdown** ⇒ In March 1929, British government arrested **31 prominent labour leaders**.
* **Trial** ⇒ Lasted three-and-a-half years.
* **Key Convicts** ⇒ Muzaffar Ahmed, S.A. Dange, S.V. Joglekar, Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley, Shaukat Usmani.
* **Consequence** ⇒ Generated immense global publicity, but severely weakened the Indian working-class movement by decapitating its leadership.
* **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930)** ⇒ Active participation of workers, followed by a subsequent dip in trade union activity.
* **1931 Split** ⇒ Ideological division; corporatist faction under **N.M. Joshi** broke away to form the **All India Trade Union Federation (AITUF)**.
* **1935 Left Unity** ⇒ Communists rejoined AITUC → formed a Left Front with Congress Socialists and leftist nationalists (Nehru and Bose).

---

## Under Congress Ministries

* **1937 Elections** ⇒ AITUC supported Congress candidates.
* **Policy Boost** ⇒ Congress provincial ministries (1937–39) adopted a highly sympathetic attitude → passed several pro-labour welfare legislations → major fillip to trade unionism.

---

## During And After The Second World War

* **War Opposition** ⇒ Workers initially opposed the imperial war effort.
* **1941 Shift ("People's War")** ⇒ Russia's entry into Allies → Communists rebranded the conflict as a **"People's War"** and supported it.
* **Quit India Movement (1942)** ⇒ Communists dissociated themselves from QIM; advocated for industrial peace to aid the war effort.
* **Post-War Upsurge (1945–47)** ⇒ Resurgence of active working-class militancy:
  * **1945 (Dock Workers)** ⇒ Bombay and Calcutta dock workers refused to load supply ships bound for warring Allied troops in Indonesia.
  * **1946 (RIN Mutiny Support)** ⇒ Massive workers' strikes in support of the Royal Indian Navy Ratings.
  * **1946–47 (General Strikes)** ⇒ Widespread strikes by post, telegraph, and railway workers on the eve of independence.

---

## After Independence

* **Fragmentation** ⇒ Working-class trade unions split and aligned with diverse, competing political ideologies and party lines.
