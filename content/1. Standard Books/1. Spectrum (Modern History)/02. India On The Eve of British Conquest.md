*   **First half of 18th century** → Decline and disintegration of the Mughal Empire (decline initiated during **Aurangzeb's** reign, 1658–1707).
*   **Muhammad Shah** (1719–48) → Incompetent rule → Irreversible decay of imperial fortunes → Emergence of independent states (**Hyderabad**, **Bengal**, **Awadh**, **Punjab**) & Maratha bid for power.

## Challenges Before The Mughals

### External Challenges
*   **Neglected NW Borders** → Later Mughals failed to protect frontiers → Vulnerability to NW invasions.
*   **Nadir Shah (Persian Emperor)**:
    *   **1738–39** → Invasion of India; captured Lahore.
    *   **Feb 13, 1739** → **Battle of Karnal** → Nadir Shah defeated Mughal army.
    *   **Consequences**:
        *   **Muhammad Shah** captured; Delhi looted & devastated.
        *   Official treasury & rich nobles plundered of **70 crore rupees**, the **Peacock Throne**, and the **Kohinoor diamond**.
        *   Mughal territory west of Indus (including **Kabul**) annexed to Persian Empire → India highly vulnerable to future land-based NW attacks.
*   **Ahmad Shah Abdali (Ahmad Shah Durrani)**:
    *   **1747** → Elected successor of Nadir Shah.
    *   **1748–1767** → Invaded India **8 times**.
    *   **1751–52** → Mughals ceded **Punjab** to Abdali to buy peace.
    *   **1757** → Captured Delhi → Recognized **Alamgir II** as emperor; Rohilla chief **Najib-ud-Daula** appointed **Mir Bakhshi** (Abdali’s personal "supreme agent").
    *   **1758** → Maratha chief **Raghunath Rao** expelled Najib-ud-Daula & captured Punjab.
    *   **1759** → Abdali returned to India to take revenge.
    *   **1761** → **Third Battle of Panipat** → Abdali routed the Marathas.
    *   **1767** → Last Afghan invasion.

### Why Many Empire-Shaking Battles At Panipat ?
*   **First Battle (1526)**: Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi → Ended Delhi Sultanate → Laid **Mughal Empire** foundation.
*   **Second Battle (1556)**: Akbar vs Hemu → Decided continuation of Mughal rule.
*   **Third Battle (1761)**: Marathas vs Ahmad Shah Abdali → Ended Maratha ambition of ruling India.
*   **Why Panipat was a favourite battlefield**:
    *   **Strategic Location**: Direct gateway to Delhi from NW passes (Khyber Pass); avoids risky terrain of Rajasthan deserts or dense northern forests.
    *   **Proximity to Delhi**: Short distance allowed Delhi rulers to transport troops, weapons, and food supplies easily while keeping Delhi insulated from combat.
    *   **Flat Terrain**: Flat ground highly suitable for cavalry movements (the primary mode of 18th-century warfare).
    *   **Grand Trunk Road**: Built by **Sher Shah Suri** (1540–45) → High accessibility for moving massive armies.
    *   **Short Monsoons**: Facilitated longer, uninterrupted military campaigning.
    *   **Local Military Industry**: Skilled local artisans/smiths in Haryana easily replenished warfare materials for both sides.

### Weak Rulers After Aurangzeb ~ An Internal Challenge
*   **Bahadur Shah I (Ruled 1707–12)**:
    *   63-year-old **Prince Mu'azzam** (eldest son of Aurangzeb) secured throne after a 2-year succession war by killing brothers **Azam** and **Kam Bakhsh**.
    *   **Khafi Khan** nickname = **'Shahi-Bekhabar'** (Heedless King).
    *   **Policy**: Pacific policy with Marathas, Rajputs, and Jats.
    *   **Actions**: Released Maratha prince **Shahu**; confirmed Rajput chiefs in their ancestral states; waged campaign against Sikh leader **Banda Bahadur** in Punjab.
*   **Jahandar Shah (Ruled 1712–13)**:
    *   Secured throne with backing of prime minister **Zulfikar Khan**.
    *   **Reforms**: Introduced **izara** system (revenue farming) to fix failing finances; abolished **Jaziya**.
*   **Farrukhsiyar (Ruled 1713–1719)**:
    *   Assassinated Jahandar Shah with aid of Sayyid brothers—**Abdulla Khan** and **Hussain Ali** (known as the **'King Makers'**).
    *   **Actions**: Abolished **Jaziya** and pilgrimage tax; granted **1717 Farmans** (valuable trade privileges) to British EIC.
    *   **Death (1719)**: Sayyid brothers allied with Peshwa **Balaji Vishwanath** → Dethroned, blinded, and murdered Farrukhsiyar (first time in Mughal history an emperor was killed by his own nobles).
*   **Rafi-ud-Darajat (Ruled Feb. 28–June 6, 1719)**:
    *   Reigned for the shortest period among all Mughals.
*   **Rafi-ud-Daula (Ruled June 6–Sep. 17, 1719)**:
    *   Placed by Sayyid brothers with title **Shah Jahan II**; died as an opium addict.
*   **Muhammad Shah (Ruled 1719–48)**:
    *   Original name **Raushan Akhtar**; nicknamed **'Rangeela'** due to his luxurious, pleasure-loving lifestyle.
    *   **Actions**: Eliminated Sayyid Brothers with help of **Nizam-ul-Mulk**.
    *   **Losses**:
        *   **1724** → Nizam-ul-Mulk resigned as wazir and founded independent **Hyderabad**.
        *   **1737** → Peshwa **Baji Rao I** invaded Delhi with only 500 horsemen.
        *   **1739** → Defeated by Nadir Shah at **Karnal**; lost NW territory west of Indus.
*   **Ahmad Shah Bahadur (Ruled 1748–1754)**:
    *   Incompetent ruler; administration fell to Queen Mother **Udham Bai** (title **Qibla-i-Alam**) and her paramour **Javid Khan** (a notorious eunuch).
*   **Alamgir II (Ruled 1754–59)**:
    *   Son of Jahandar Shah.
    *   **Key Events**: Abdali captured Delhi (**Jan 1757**); **Battle of Plassey** fought (**June 1757**). Assassinated by wazir.
*   **Shah Jahan III (Ruled 1759–60)**:
    *   Title given to **Muhi-ul-millat**; deposed by Maratha intervention.
*   **Shah Alam II (Ruled Oct. 1760–July 1788; Oct. 1788–Nov. 1806)**:
    *   Coincided with two epoch-defining battles: **Third Battle of Panipat** (1761) and **Battle of Buxar** (1764).
    *   **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765)**: Taken under British EIC protection at Allahabad; issued imperial farman granting EIC perpetual **Diwani** (revenue collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.
    *   **End**: Taken to Delhi by Marathas in **1772**; EIC captured Delhi in **1803** after defeating **Daulat Rao Scindia** → Mughal emperor reduced to an EIC pensioner.
*   **Akbar Shah II (Ruled 1806–37)**:
    *   Conferred title of **'Raja'** on **Rammohan Roy**.
    *   **1835** → EIC stopped issuing coins in the Mughal emperor's name; discontinued calling itself subject of the Mughal.
*   **Bahadur Shah II (Ruled 1837–57)**:
    *   **Bahadur Shah Zafar** (last Mughal emperor).
    *   Revolt of 1857 declared him Emperor of India; captured by British and exiled to **Rangoon** (died 1862).
    *   **Legal end of Mughal Empire**: **November 1, 1858** (Queen Victoria's Proclamation).

## Causes Of Decline Of The Mughal Empire

### Scholarly Debates
*   **Mughal-centric (Empire-related) view**: Internal causes within the structure, policies of Aurangzeb, and weakness of central rulers.
*   **Region-related view**: Decay due to local instability, peasant revolts, and rising autonomy of regions.

### Shifting Allegiance Of Zamindars
*   **Two classes shared imperial power**: Zamindars (hereditary land owners with private troops, e.g, rais, rajas, thakurs, deshmukhs) and Nobles.
*   **Aurangzeb's reign** → Marked increase in zamindari power.
*   **Impact**: Zamindars encouraged local/regional loyalties → Assisted ambitious nobles in exploiting imperial weakness to carve out autonomous regional kingdoms.

### Jagirdari Crisis
*   Mughal rule = "rule of the nobility" (nobles managed jagirs, mansabs, and subas).
*   **Nobility Divisiveness**: Fragmented on lines of religion, homeland, and tribe (Irani, Turani, Afghan, Hindustani groups) → Bitter factional contests for power under later Mughals.
*   **Bejagiri (Shortage of Jagirs)**:
    *   Sharp increase in amirs/mansabdars vs. limited land for distribution.
    *   Aurangzeb's solution = Artificially inflated recorded income of jagirs.
    *   **Consequence**: Amirs squeezed peasantry to extract the inflated paper revenues → Peasantry & Amirs both deeply alienated from the State.

### Rise Of Regional Aspirations
*   **Aurangzeb's policies** → Spurred rebellions by regional groups (**Jats**, **Sikhs**, **Marathas**).
*   **Rajput Alienation**: Suppression of Rajputs by Aurangzeb & Bahadur Shah I → Permanent trust deficit → Rajputs refused to ally for imperial defense.
*   **Maratha Expansion**: Gradual transformation of goals from Maharashtra home territory → Northern expansion; by **1740**, Marathas controlled Gujarat, Malwa, Bundelkhand; collected **chauth** and **sardeshmukhi** across the subcontinent.

### Economic And Administrative Problems
*   Imperial wars + courtly luxury + decline in khalisa (crown) land → Massive state deficits (expenditure > income).
*   Stagnant medieval economy → Lack of scientific and technological innovations to improve productivity.
*   European coastal trade grew but did not enrich the Mughal treasury.
*   Centralized administration collapsed under the weight of vast territories, poor communications, and incompetent rulers.

## Rise Of Regional States

### Three Main Categories
1.  **Successor States**: Mughal provinces that achieved hereditary autonomy but retained symbolic ties & acknowledged Mughal sovereignty. *Examples*: **Awadh**, **Bengal**, **Hyderabad**.
2.  **Independent Kingdoms**: Rose due to the disintegration of central control over provinces. *Examples*: **Mysore**, **Rajput States**.
3.  **The New States**: Set up by rebel groups against the Mughal Empire. *Examples*: **Maratha**, **Sikh**, **Jat** states.

### Survey Of Regional Kingdoms
*   **Hyderabad**:
    *   Founder: **Kilich Khan** (**Nizam-ul-Mulk**), established Asaf-Jah house.
    *   **Origin**: Independent Deccan state idea first conceived by **Zulfikar Khan** (died 1713).
    *   **Battle of Shakr-Kheda (1724)**: Kilich Khan defeated & killed Mughal viceroy Mubariz Khan.
    *   **1725** → Became viceroy; assumed title **Asaf-Jah**.
*   **Awadh**:
    *   Founder: **Saadat Khan** (**Burhan-ul-Mulk**), Shia.
    *   **Origin**: Gained high mansab by conspiring against Sayyid brothers; later exiled from court and established Awadh.
    *   **End**: Committed suicide under pressure of Nadir Shah's exorbitant ransom demands; succeeded by **Safdar Jang**.
*   **Bengal**:
    *   Founder: **Murshid Kuli Khan** (made Bengal prosperous).
    *   Succession: Succeeded by **Shuja-ud-din** (1727).
    *   **1740**: **Alivardi Khan** (deputy governor of Bihar) killed successor **Sarfaraz Khan** at **Gheria** → Seized power; became virtually independent by paying nominal annual tribute to Delhi.
*   **The Rajputs**:
    *   **1708**: Ajit Singh, Jai Singh II, and Durgadas Rathor formed alliance against Bahadur Shah I → Forced a Mughal retreat; alliance eventually fractured.
*   **Mysore**:
    *   Strategic location at Eastern-Western Ghats junction; ruled by **Wodeyars**.
    *   **Haider Ali** (modernized army with French aid) and son **Tipu Sultan** usurped power → Embroiled in continuous wars with British.
*   **Kerala**:
    *   Founder: **Martanda Varma**, capital **Travancore** (borders extended from Kanyakumari to Cochin).
    *   **Actions**: Organized army on Western model; modernized state administration.
*   **The Jats**:
    *   **1669 Mathura Revolt**: Agriculturist Jats led by **Gokula** rebelled against Aurangzeb’s oppressive policies (suppressed).
    *   Bharatpur Kingdom founded by **Churaman** and **Badan Singh**.
    *   Zenith under **Suraj Mal**: Provided efficient administration; extended territory from Ganga (East) to Chambal (South), including Subahs of Agra, Mathura, Meerut, Aligarh.
    *   **Decline (Post-1763)**: Suraj Mal's death → State fragmented under petty zamindars living by plunder.
*   **The Sikhs**:
    *   **1675**: Aurangzeb executed 9th Guru **Tegh Bahadur** for defending Kashmiri Pandits.
    *   **Guru Gobind Singh**: Transformed Sikhs into a militant sect to defend faith.
    *   **Banda Bahadur**: Led Sikhs in open warfare after 1708; defeated & killed by Mughals.
    *   **12 Misls (Confederacies)**: Formed post-Nadir Shah/Abdali invasions to rule Punjab.
    *   **Ranjit Singh** (son of Mahan Singh, Sukarchakiya misl): Established strong unified kingdom; conquered **Lahore (1799)** and **Amritsar (1802)**.
    *   **Diplomacy**:
        *   **Treaty of Amritsar**: Recognized British supremacy over Cis-Sutlej territories.
        *   **Tripartite Treaty (1838)**: Signed with British and Shah Shuja to grant British troops passage through Punjab to place Shah Shuja on Kabul throne.
    *   **Decline (Post-1839)**: Ranjit Singh's death → Succession chaos → British annexed Punjab.
*   **The Marathas**:
    *   Uprooted Mughal power in Malwa and Gujarat under capable Peshwas.
    *   **1761 Third Battle of Panipat** → Halted Maratha bid to inherit Mughal rule. Recovered rapidly to offer the toughest challenge to British EIC.
*   **Rohilakhand and Farukhabad**:
    *   Afghan migration to North India in mid-18th century due to economic and political chaos in Afghanistan.
    *   **Rohilakhand**: Independent principality (Himalayan foothills from Kumaon to Ganga) set up by **Ali Muhammad Khan** post-Nadir Shah invasion. Rohillas clashed with Jats, Awadh rulers, Marathas, and British.
    *   **Farrukhabad**: Independent East of Delhi territory founded by **Mohammad Khan Bangash** under Farrukhsiyar/Muhammad Shah.

### Nature And Limitations Of Regional States
*   **Imperial Ties**: Continued to recognize Mughal Emperor as paramount umbrella (even Marathas and Sikhs sought imperial legitimacy).
*   **Polity**: Regional and collaborative; functioned with alliances of local zamindars, merchants, and nobles.
*   **Critical Drawbacks**:
    *   Failed to develop a sound administrative, financial, or military structure.
    *   Scientific & technological backwardness (stagnation in core knowledge).
    *   Continuous inter-state wars (no state was strong enough to replace the Mughals on an all-India scale).
    *   Agrarian crisis intensified while surplus contenders multiplied.

## Socio-Economic Conditions

*   **Land of Contrasts**: Extreme luxury of elite alongside extreme poverty of common populace (bare subsistence level).
*   *Note*: Masse conditions were generally better in the 18th century than 100 years later under British rule.

### Agriculture
*   Technically backward but intensively cultivated by hardworking peasantry.
*   Peasants forced to pay exorbitant dues to state, zamindars, jagirdars, and revenue farmers (which worsened under subsequent British rule).

### Trade And Industry
*   Self-sufficient in agricultural and handicraft products → Low imports of foreign goods vs. massive global demand for Indian products.
*   **Balanced Trade**: Heavy exports meant trade was balanced by import of silver and gold → India known as a **"sink of precious metals"**.
*   **Items of Import**:
    *   **Persian Gulf**: Pearls, raw silk, wool, dates, dried fruits, rose water.
    *   **Arabia**: Coffee, gold, drugs, honey.
    *   **China**: Tea, sugar, porcelain, silk.
    *   **Tibet**: Gold, musk, woollen cloth.
    *   **Africa**: Ivory, drugs.
    *   **Europe**: Woollen cloth, copper, iron, lead, paper.
*   **Items of Export**: Cotton textiles, raw silk, silk fabrics, hardware, indigo, saltpetre, opium, rice, wheat, sugar, pepper, other spices, precious stones, drugs.
*   **Textile Industry Centres**:
    *   Bengal (muslins of **Dacca**, **Murshidabad**), Bihar (**Patna**), Gujarat (**Surat**, **Ahmedabad**, **Broach**), MP (**Chanderi**, **Burhanpur**), UP (**Jaunpur**, **Varanasi**, **Lucknow**, **Agra**), Punjab (**Multan**, **Lahore**), Coromandel/South (**Masulipatnam**, **Aurangabad**, **Chicacole**, **Vizag**, **Bangalore**, **Coimbatore**, **Madurai**).
    *   **Kashmir** was the primary centre of woollen manufactures.
*   **Ship-building Industry**:
    *   Leaders: **Maharashtra**, **Andhra**, **Bengal**.
    *   Kerala Coast: Calicut & Quilon flourished. **Zamorin** utilized Muslim **Kunjali Maraikkars** for naval forces.
    *   **Shivaji’s navy** defended the West Coast against Portuguese.
    *   European companies routinely purchased Indian-made ships for their superior build quality.

### Status Of Education
*   Traditional and stagnant; confined to literature, law, religion, philosophy, and logic.
*   **Critical Gap**: Totally excluded physical and natural sciences, technology, and geography; original thought discouraged by heavy reliance on ancient text reproduction.
*   **Elementary Education**: Quite widespread.
    *   **Pathshalas** for Hindus; **Maktabs** for Muslims (confined to reading, writing, and basic arithmetic). Female presence rare.
*   **Institutes of Higher Learning**:
    *   **Chatuspathis** or **Tols** (Bihar & Bengal) → Sanskrit learning. Key centres: **Kasi** (Varanasi), **Tirhut** (Mithila), **Nadia**, **Utkala**.
    *   **Madrasahs** → Persian (court language) and Arabic higher learning. Key centre: **Azimabad** (Patna).

### Societal Set-Up
*   **Many Castes, Many Sects**: Traditional, highly fragmented.
    *   **Hindus**: Family patriarchal; caste central. Sub-castes fixed social status. Caste panchayats strictly enforced norms. Profession mainly tied to caste, with fluid exceptions.
    *   **Muslims**: Deeply divided by race, status, and faction (Irani, Afghan, Turani, Hindustani nobles). **Sharif** (elite nobles, priests, officers) treated **Ajlaf** (lower-class) Muslims with the same disdain as high-caste Hindus treated lower castes.
*   **Position of Women in Society**: Patriarchal system.
    *   Upper-class women confined to homes; lower-class women supplemented income by working in fields.
    *   Prevalent social evils: **purdah**, **sati**, **child marriage**, **polygamy**, and miserable plight of widows.
    *   Dowry rampant in **Bengal** and **Rajputana**.
    *   Reform attempts: **Raja Sawai Jai Singh** of Amber and Maratha General **Prashuram Bhau** tried and failed to promote widow remarriage.
*   **Menace of Slavery**:
    *   Economic distress, famines, and poverty forced parents to sell offspring.
    *   Domestic female slaves kept by higher classes of Rajputs, Khatris, Kayasthas.
    *   *Status difference*: Indian slaves treated better than European counterparts (hereditary servants, offspring were considered free citizens).
    *   **European impact**: Greatly expanded slave trade. EIC and other European companies bought slaves from Bengal, Assam, Bihar to sell in Western markets. Abyssinian slaves sold at Surat, Madras, Calcutta.

### Developments In Art, Architecture and Culture
*   Mughal decline forced artists to migrate to regional courts for patronage (**Hyderabad, Lucknow, Jaipur, Murshidabad, Patna, Kashmir**).
*   **Architecture**:
    *   **Bara Imambara (1784)**: Built by Nawab **Asaf-ud-Daula** at Lucknow.
    *   Pink City of **Jaipur** and **5 astronomical observatories** (Delhi, Jaipur, Benares, Mathura, Ujjain): Built by **Sawai Jai Singh** of Amber. He also compiled the astronomical timetable **Jij Muhammad-shahi**.
    *   **Padmanabhapuram Palace** (Kerala): Notable for architecture and mural paintings.
*   **Painting**: **Rajputana** and **Kangra** schools came into prominence.
*   **Literature & Poetry**:
    *   **Urdu**: Rapid growth in North India. Prominent poets: **Mir**, **Sauda**, **Nazir**, and **Mirza Ghalib** (19th century).
    *   Malayalam: Patronized by Travancore rulers; noted poet **Kalakkathu Kunchan Nambiar**.
    *   Tamil: Enriched by **sittar poetry**. Best exponent: **Tayumanavar** (protested caste system and temple abuses).
    *   Punjabi: Romantic epic **Heer Ranjha** composed by **Warris Shah**.
    *   Sindhi: **Risalo** (poetry collection) composed by **Shah Abdul Latif**.
