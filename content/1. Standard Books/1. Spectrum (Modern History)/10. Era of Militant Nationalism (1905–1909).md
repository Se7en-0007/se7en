## Growth Of Militant Nationalism
- **Emergence** → Radical trend of militant nationalist approach (1890s) → Concrete shape (by 1905) + revolutionary wing.

## Why Militant Nationalism Grew
- **Recognition of the True Nature of British Rule** → Disillusionment with unfulfilled demands → Need for effective political action + mass belief in Indian self-governance.
  - **Economic miseries (1890s)** → Severe famines (1896–1900: **90 lakh deaths**) + Bubonic plague (Deccan) + Deccan riots → Exploitative colonial character exposed.
  - **Repressive measures**:
    - **1892** → Indian Councils Act rejected as unsatisfactory by nationalists.
    - **1897** → **Natu brothers** deported without trial; **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** imprisoned for sedition.
    - **1898** → Repressive laws amplified: IPC Section 124A (sedition) + Section 153A.
    - **1899** → Indian representation in **Calcutta Corporation** reduced.
    - **1904** → **Official Secrets Act** curbed press freedom.
    - **1904** → **Indian Universities Act** imposed greater state control (universities viewed as "factories producing revolutionaries").
- **Growth of Confidence and Self-Respect**:
  - Faith in self-effort (**Tilak**, **Aurobindo**, **Bipin Chandra Pal**) → Rely on character/capacity of Indian masses → Masses ready to sacrifice for freedom.
- **Growth of Education** → Increased awareness + rising unemployment/underemployment → Highlighted colonial economic underdeveloped state → Simmering discontent.
- **International Influences** → Demolished myth of European invincibility:
  - **Japan** (post-1868) → Rapid industrialization without foreign help.
  - **1896** → Ethiopian victory over the Italian army.
  - **1899–1902** → British reverses in **Boer Wars**.
  - **1905** → Japan's victory over Russia.
  - **Global movements** → Ireland, Russia, Egypt, Turkey, Persia, China → United masses can challenge mighty empires.
- **Reaction to Increasing Westernisation** → Threat to Indian national identity → Intellectual/moral inspiration drawn from Indian heritage:
  - **Vivekananda**, **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee**, **Swami Dayananda Saraswati** (Political message: **"India for the Indians"**) → Exploded western superiority myth.
- **Dissatisfaction with Achievements of Moderates** → Younger elements rejected Moderate **"Three Ps" (Prayer, Petition, Protest)** → Termed as **"political mendicancy"**.
- **Reactionary Policies of Curzon** → Curzon's 7-year rule (missions, commissions, omissions) → Refused to recognize India as a nation + insulted intelligentsia ("letting off of gas").
  - **Repressive Acts**: Calcutta Corporation Act (1899), Official Secrets Act (1904), Indian Universities Act (1904), and **Partition of Bengal (1905)**.
- **Existence of a Militant School of Thought**:
  - Key Thinkers: **Raj Narain Bose**, **Ashwini Kumar Dutta**, **Aurobindo Ghosh**, **Bipin Chandra Pal** (Bengal); **Vishnu Shastri Chiplunkar**, **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (Maharashtra); **Lala Lajpat Rai** (Punjab).
  - **Basic Tenets**:
    - Hatred for foreign rule → Indians must work out their own salvation.
    - **Swaraj** = Goal of national movement.
    - Direct political action + belief in mass capacity + personal sacrifice.
- **Emergence of a Trained Leadership** → Channelized mass potential → Released during the anti-partition Swadeshi agitation.

## The Swadeshi And Boycott Movement
- **Genesis** → Anti-partition movement opposing the division of Bengal.

## Partition of Bengal To Divide People
- **Timeline** → Public announcement: **December 1903** → Implemented: **October 16, 1905**.
- **Official Reason** → Bengal (population **78 million**, 1/4th of British India) too large to administer; partition would develop Assam.
- **Real Motive** → Weaken Bengal (nerve center of Indian nationalism) → Subdivide Bengalis under two administrations:
  - **Language Division** → Bengalis reduced to a minority in Bengal proper (17 million Bengalis vs 37 million Hindi/Oriya speakers).
  - **Religion Division**:
    - **Western Bengal** → Hindu majority (42 million out of 54 million).
    - **Eastern Bengal & Assam** → Muslim majority (18 million out of 31 million; capital at **Dacca**).
  - **Divide & Rule** → Curzon wooed Muslim communalists (promising Dacca would restore old glory of Muslim rule) to counter Congress.
  - **Risley (Home Secretary, 1904)**: *"Bengal united is a power. Bengal divided will pull in several different ways..."*

## Anti-Partition Campaign Under Moderates (1903–05)
- **Leadership** → **Surendranath Banerjea**, **K.K. Mitra**, **Prithwishchandra Ray**.
- **Methods** → Petitions, public meetings, memoranda, pamphlets, and press propaganda:
  - Key Papers: ***Hitabadi***, ***Sanjibani***, ***Bengalee***.
- **Outcome**:
  - **July 1905** → Partition announced despite public opposition.
  - **August 7, 1905** → **Boycott Resolution** passed at Calcutta Townhall → Formal proclamation of **Swadeshi Movement**.
  - **October 16, 1905** (Partition Day) → Day of mourning:
    - Fasting, bathing in Ganga, barefoot processions singing ***Vande Mataram***.
    - ***Amar Sonar Bangla*** composed by **Rabindranath Tagore** sung on streets.
    - **Rakshabandhan** observed (proposed by **Tagore** and **Ramendrasundar Trivedi**) → Rakhis tied as symbol of Bengal's unity.
    - Huge gatherings addressed by **Surendranath Banerjea** and **Ananda Mohan Bose** → **Rs 50,000** raised.
- **Spread Outside Bengal**:
  - Poona & Bombay → **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**
  - Punjab → **Lala Lajpat Rai** & **Ajit Singh**
  - Delhi → **Syed Haider Raza**
  - Madras → **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai**

## The Congress Position
- **1905 Benaras Session** (**G.K. Gokhale** presiding):
  - Condemned Bengal partition + Curzon's policies.
  - Supported Anti-Partition and Swadeshi Movement in Bengal.
- **Ideological Rift**:
  - **Extremists** (Tilak, Lajpat Rai, B.C. Pal, Aurobindo) → Expand movement nationwide + go beyond boycott to full political mass struggle for Swaraj.
  - **Moderates** → Opposed extending movement outside Bengal; opposed full-scale passive resistance.
- **1906 Calcutta Session** (**Dadabhai Naoroji** presiding):
  - Congress goal defined: **"self-government or swaraj like the United Kingdom or the colonies"** (Australia/Canada).
  - Resolutions passed: **Swadeshi**, **Boycott**, **National Education**, **Swaraj** (word used for the first time, left un-spelled, enabling multiple interpretations).
- **1907 Surat Session** → Dispute over pace/techniques reached deadlock → **Surat Split** → Damaged Swadeshi Movement.

## The Movement Under Extremist Leadership
- **Dominance (post-1905)** → Caused by:
  1. Failure of Moderate-led movement to yield results.
  2. Divisive government tactics embittering nationalists.
  3. Brutal government suppression (student corporal punishment, ban on *Vande Mataram*, press restrictions, arrests, deportations).

## The Extremist Programme
- **Call for Passive Resistance** → Boycott of government schools, colleges, services, courts, legislative councils, municipalities, and titles.
- **Aurobindo Ghosh**: Make administration impossible by organized refusal to cooperate.
- **Central Focus** → India’s independence from foreign rule. *"Political freedom is the lifebreath of a nation"* (Aurobindo). Goal achieved through self-sacrifice.

## New Forms of Struggle and Impact
- **Boycott of Foreign Goods** → Public burning of foreign cloth; boycott of foreign salt/sugar; priests refused marriages with foreign items; washermen refused foreign clothes.
- **Public Meetings and Processions** → Major vehicle for mass mobilization.
- **Corps of Volunteers ('Samitis')**:
  - **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti** (founded by **Ashwini Kumar Dutta** in Barisal) → Powerful mass mobilization.
  - **Swadeshi Sangam** (Tirunelveli, Tamil Nadu; **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai**, **Subramania Siva**, lawyers) → Generated political consciousness.
  - *Functions*: Magic lantern lectures, swadeshi songs, physical training, famine/epidemic relief, schools, swadeshi craft training, arbitration courts.
- **Traditional Popular Festivals and Melas** → Spread political messages:
  - **Tilak's Ganapati & Shivaji festivals** used in Western India & Bengal; traditional folk theatres (Yatras) in Bengal.
- **Emphasis Given to Self-Reliance (Atma Shakti)** → Village social/economic regeneration, anti-caste campaigns, campaign against dowry, early marriage, and alcohol.
- **Programme of Swadeshi or National Education**:
  - Under government threat to stop grants/scholarships for student agitators → National schools founded.
  - **Raja Subodh Mullick** contributed **Rs 100,000** for a national university.
  - **August 15, 1906** → **National Council of Education** established to organize a literary, scientific, and technical system on national lines via vernacular medium.
  - **Bengal National College** → First Principal: **Aurobindo Ghosh**; First President: **Rashbehari Ghosh** (inspired by Tagore's Shantiniketan, 1901).
  - **Satishchandra Mukherjee** → Pioneered national education via **Bhagabat Chatuspathi (1895)**, ***Dawn*** newspaper (since 1897), and **Dawn Society (1902)**.
- **Swadeshi or Indigenous Enterprises**:
  - Textile mills, soap/match factories, tanneries, banks, insurance companies, shops.
  - **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company** (founded by **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai** at Tuticorin) → Challenged British India Steam Navigation Company.
  - Exhibitions & Stores: **Swadeshi Bhandar** (Tagore, 1897), **Indian Stores** (Jogeshchandra Chaudhuri, 1901), **Lakshmir Bhandar** (Sarala Debi, 1903).
  - **Bengal Chemicals** factory founded by **Prafullachandra Ray** (1893).
  - ***Desher Katha*** (1904) → Published by **Sakharam Ganesh Deuskar** (Aurobindo's associate) → Promoted swadeshi, warned against colonial "hypnotic conquest of the mind", sold **15,000+ copies** before 1910 ban.
- **Impact in the Cultural Sphere**:
  - Patriotic Songs: Tagore, Rajnikant Sen, Dwijendralal Ray, Mukunda Das, Syed Abu Muhammad.
  - In Tamil Nadu → **Subramania Bharati** wrote ***Sudesha Geetham***.
  - Painting → **Abanindranath Tagore** broke Victorian naturalism, took inspiration from Ajanta, Mughal, Rajput art.
  - **Nandalal Bose** → First scholarship recipient from **Indian Society of Oriental Art** (founded 1907).
  - Science → Original research by **Jagdish Chandra Bose** and **Prafullachandra Ray**.

## Extent of Mass Participation
- **Students**:
  - Led picketing of shops selling foreign goods (Bengal, Poona, Guntur, Madras, Salem).
  - *Repression*: Disaffiliation of schools, grant cuts, expulsion, fines, arrests, physical beatings, disqualified for government jobs/scholarships.
- **Women**: Urban middle-class women actively joined processions and picketing.
- **Stand of Muslims**:
  - Some participation: Barrister Abdul Rasul, Liaqat Hussain, Guznavi, **Maulana Azad** (joined revolutionary group).
  - Majority of upper/middle-class Muslims stayed away; led by **Nawab Salimullah of Dacca**, they supported partition to get a Muslim-majority East Bengal.
  - **December 30, 1906** → **All-India Muslim League** propped up as an anti-Congress front.
  - Hindu-centric rhetoric/festivals used by Extremist leaders tended to alienate Muslims.
- **Labour Unrest and Trade Unions**:
  - **September 1905** → Walkout by 250 Bengali clerks at **Burn Company** (Howrah).
  - **July 1906** → **East Indian Railway** strike → Formation of **Railwaymen's Union**.
  - **1906–1908** → Frequent strikes in jute mills.
  - Tuticorin/Tirunelveli → Cotton mill strike led by Subramania Siva and Chidambaram Pillai.
  - Rawalpindi → Arsenal & railway strike led by Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh.
  - Subsidence: Summer of 1908 under strict government action.
- **Social Base Expansion**: Included certain zamindars, students, women, lower-middle classes in towns, and urban labor (Muslim peasantry remained largely alienated).

## All India Aspect
- Local struggles outside Bengal organized under **Tilak**'s leadership → Viewed as a crucial opportunity to build a nationwide mass movement against British rule.

## Annulment Of Partition
- **1911** → Partition annulled to curb revolutionary terrorism.
- **Delhi** became the new capital (sop to Muslims, but failed to please them).
- **Territorial adjustment** → Bihar and Orissa separated from Bengal; Assam made a separate province.

## Evaluation Of The Swadeshi Movement

### The Movement Fizzles Out
- **By 1908**, open phase ended due to:
  1. Severe government repression.
  2. Lack of effective organization or party structure to channel mass energy.
  3. Failure to institutionalize techniques (non-cooperation, passive resistance, jail-filling).
  4. Movement became leaderless: most leaders arrested/deported by 1908; Aurobindo and B.C. Pal retired; Lajpat Rai went abroad.
  5. Internal squabbles + **Surat Split (1907)**.
  6. Narrow social base: confined to upper/middle classes and zamindars; failed to mobilize the peasantry.
  7. Exhaustion: mass-based high pitch unsustainable over long periods.

### Differences between Moderates and Extremists

| Feature | Moderates | Extremists |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Social Base** | Zamindars & upper-middle classes in towns. | Educated middle & lower-middle classes in towns. |
| **Ideological Base** | Western liberal thought & European history. | Indian history, heritage, & Hindu traditional symbols. |
| **Providential Mission**| Believed in England's providential mission in India. | Rejected the providential mission theory as an illusion. |
| **Anglo-Indian Ties** | British connection crucial for social/cultural progress. | British connection causes systematic economic exploitation. |
| **Loyalty** | Professed loyalty to the British Crown. | British Crown unworthy of Indian loyalty. |
| **Role of Masses** | Masses not ready; limit movement to intelligentsia. | Immense faith in mass capacity to fight and sacrifice. |
| **Demands** | Constitutional reforms & Indianisation of services. | **Swaraj** as the sole remedy for Indian ills. |
| **Methods** | Strictly constitutional methods (**Three Ps**). | Extra-constitutional (Boycott, Passive Resistance). |

### Movement a Turning Point
- **Leap Forward** → Mass participation (students, women, workers, urban population).
- **Ideological Genesis** → Bounded all future trends: conservative moderation, political extremism, revolutionary militancy, and early socialism.
- **Scope Expansion** → Encompassed art, literature, science, and indigenous industry.
- **Institutional Impact** → Undermined hegemony of colonial ideas/institutions.

### Moderate Methods Give Way to Extremist Modes
- Petitions/speeches proved obsolete; Moderates failed to recruit the younger generation or establish roots among the masses.
- **Extremist Limitations**:
  - Lacked consistent ideology (goals ranged from Tilak's self-government to Aurobindo's complete independence).
  - **Social Reactionaries/Revivalists**: Obscurantist undertones alienated Muslims.
    - Tilak's opposition to **Age of Consent Bill** (raising girls' marriage age from 10 to 12).
    - Use of Shivaji/Ganapati festivals, anti-cow killing campaigns.
    - Pal and Aurobindo's rhetoric of a "Hindu nation".
    - Promoted unhealthy mixing of politics and religion → Growth of communalism.

## The Surat Split
- **Timeline** → **December 1907** (coincided with rise of revolutionary terrorism).

### Run-up to Surat
- **December 1905 (Benaras Session)** → **Gokhale** presided:
  - Extremists wanted to extend Swadeshi/Boycott nationwide and boycott government association (courts, services, councils).
  - Moderates opposed; minor resolution passed as compromise.
- **December 1906 (Calcutta Session)** → **Dadabhai Naoroji** presided:
  - Extremists proposed Tilak or Lajpat Rai as president; Moderates bypassed them with the widely respected Naoroji.
  - Goal defined as **"swarajya or self-government"** (undefined, subject to dual interpretation).
  - Resolutions passed on Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education.
- **1907 Showdown** → Extremists initiated passive resistance; Moderates, sensing upcoming council reforms, wanted to tone down the Calcutta resolutions.
  - **Extremist View** → Freedom battle had begun; Moderates were a drag on progress.
  - **Moderate View** → Extremists' radical agitation would provoke ruthless British state repression and alienate the British Liberal Party.
  - Both failed to realize: Reforms were designed to isolate Extremists, and Moderates needed Extremists as their shield.

### Split Takes Place
- **1907 Session Location**:
  - Extremists wanted **Nagpur** (Central Provinces) with Tilak or Lajpat Rai as president + reaffirmation of Calcutta resolutions.
  - Moderates shifted venue to **Surat** (Bombay Presidency, Tilak's home province, making him ineligible for presidency) → Proposed **Rashbehari Ghosh** as president → Dropped Swadeshi, Boycott, and National Education resolutions.
- **Outcome** → Split occurred; Moderates controlled Congress, committing it strictly to constitutional methods within the Empire.

## Government Repression
- **Repressive Legislation (1907–1911)**:
  1. **Seditious Meetings Act, 1907**
  2. **Criminal Law Amendment Act, 1908**
  3. **Indian Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act, 1908**
  4. **Explosive Substances Act, 1908**
  5. **Indian Press Act, 1910**
- **Sedition Case against Tilak (1909)**:
  - Prosecuted for articles in ***Kesari*** (1908) defending Muzaffarpur bomb throwers and calling for Swaraj.
  - Sentenced to **6 years' transportation** + **Rs 1,000 fine** → Sent to **Mandalay Jail** (Burma).
  - Historic Courtroom 46 (Bombay High Court) Statement: *"There are higher powers that rule the destiny of men and nations and it may be the will of providence that the cause which I represent may prosper more by my suffering than my remaining free."*
- **Leadership Void** → Aurobindo & B.C. Pal retired; Lajpat Rai went abroad; Moderates left with no popular support. National movement declined until Tilak's release in 1914.

## The Government Strategy
- **John Morley's Strategy** → **"Rallying them" / "Carrot and Stick"** Policy:
  1. **Repression** → Target Extremists mildly to scare Moderates.
  2. **Conciliation** → Offer concessions (Morley-Minto reforms) to Moderates to isolate them from Extremists.
  3. **Suppression** → Suppress isolated Extremists with full force.
  4. **Ignore** → Ignore Moderates once Extremists are crushed.
- **Result** → Surat split proved the success of the British policy.

## Morley-Minto Reforms Of 1909

### Background
- **October 1906 (Simla Deputation)** → **Agha Khan** led Muslim elites to Viceroy Minto, demanding separate electorates.
- **December 30, 1906** → Nawab Salimullah, Mohsin-ul-Mulk, Waqar-ul-Mulk floated **Muslim League** to preach imperial loyalty and counter Congress.
- **G.K. Gokhale** met Secretary of State Morley in England to lobby for colonial self-government.

### The Reforms (Indian Councils Act of 1909)
- **Elective Principle** recognized for non-official members (indirect election).
- **Separate Electorates for Muslims** established → Most detrimental step.
- **Council Expansion**:
  - Imperial Legislative Council: **69 members** (37 officials, 32 non-officials {5 nominated, 27 elected}).
  - **27 Elected Seats Breakdown** → **8 Muslim separate electorates**, 4 British capitalists, 2 landlords, 13 general electorates.
- **Favored Muslim Treatment** → Seat representation in excess of population; lower income qualification for Muslim voters than Hindus.
- **Expanded Powers** → Members could ask questions/supplementaries, pass resolutions (non-binding), vote separate budget items (cannot vote on total budget).
- **Executive Council** → One Indian appointed to Viceroy's Executive Council → **Satyendra Sinha** (1909).

### Evaluation
- **No Real Power** → John Morley declared colonial self-government/parliamentary system was unsuitable for India.
- **Divisive Objective** → Designed to divide nationalist ranks and isolate Extremists via separate electorates.
- **Indirect Election** → System was too indirect ("infiltration of legislators through a number of sieves").
- **No Responsibility** → Led to irresponsible criticism; Gokhale used council debates constructively to demand universal primary education.
- **Outcome** → Delivered a "shadow rather than substance" → **"Benevolent Despotism"** instead of self-government.
