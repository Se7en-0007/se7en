## **The Portuguese In India: The Quest For And Discovery Of A Sea Route To India**
- **Decline of Roman Empire (7th Century)** → **Arab domination** in **Egypt & Persia** → **Direct Europe-India contact ↓** → **Access to Indian commodities ↓** (spices, calicoes, silk, precious stones)
- **1453: Fall of Constantinople** to **Ottoman Turks** → land & Red Sea trade routes under **Islamic monopoly** → high transit revenues → Europeans forced to find direct sea route
- **15th-Century Europe Factors**:
  - **Renaissance spirit** → call for exploration
  - **Advances in ship-building & navigation**
  - **Economic prosperity** → demand for oriental luxury goods ↑
  - **Agricultural growth** → expanded cultivation, improved plough, crop rotation → meat supply ↑ → spices needed for preservation/cooking
  - **Venice & Genoa** too small to counter Turks → North Europeans aided with money/men, Genoese with ships/technical skills
  - **1454: Pope Nicholas V Bull** → conferred Prince Henry the Navigator right to navigate "sea to distant shores of Orient" to fight Islam and spread Christianity
  - **1494: Treaty of Tordesillas** → Portugal and Spain divided non-Christian world:
    - Imaginary line 1,300 miles west of Cape Verde Islands
    - **East of line = Portugal** | **West of line = Spain**
  - **1487: Bartholomew Dias** → rounded **Cape of Good Hope** (Africa) → proved sea route existed

### **From Trading To Ruling**

#### **Vasco Da Gama**
- **May 1498: Arrival at Calicut** (3 ships led by Gujarati pilot **Abdul Majid**)
- **Zamorin (Samuthiri)**: Hindu ruler of Calicut → gave friendly reception (Calicut = major entrepot)
- **Arab traders**: Apprehensive, resisted Portuguese presence
- **Indian Ocean Trade System Shift**: Old system (Indians, Arabs, Africans, Chinese, Javanese) acted on tacit rules of profit, no single dominant power → **Portuguese changed this** → sought absolute monopoly by excluding competitors
- **Vasco Da Gama returns**: Sold cargo in Portugal at huge profit (pepper purchased directly was 1/10th the cost through Muslim middlemen)
- **1500: Pedro Alvarez Cabral** voyage:
  - Established factory at Calicut
  - Portuguese factory attacked by locals → several Portuguese killed
  - Retaliation: Cabral seized Arab merchant ships, killed crew, confiscated cargo, bombarded Calicut
  - Made treaties with local rulers of **Cochin** & **Cannanore**
- **1501: Vasco Da Gama's second voyage**:
  - Combined commercial greed with ferocious hostility to Arab shipping
  - Complete rupture with Zamorin
  - Established trading factory at **Cannanore**
  - **Calicut, Cannanore, Cochin** became key trade centres → fortified under pretext of protection

#### **Francisco De Almeida**
- **1505: Appointed first Governor** (3-year term) to protect trade & destroy Muslim commerce
- **Directives**: Seize **Aden, Ormuz, Malacca**; build forts at **Anjadiva, Cochin, Cannanore, Kilwa**
- **Egypt/Venice opposition**: Venice encouraged Mameluke Sultan of Egypt to raise fleet in Red Sea to stop Portuguese
- **1507 Battle of Diu**: Portuguese fleet defeated by combined **Egyptian & Gujarat navies**; Almeida's son killed
- **1508 Retaliation**: Almeida totally crushed the combined navies
- **Policy**: **Blue Water Policy** (Cartaze system) = Master the Indian Ocean rather than building land fortresses

#### **Alfonso de Albuquerque**
- **Real founder of Portuguese power in the East**
- **Strategic choke-points secured**: Bases overlooking entrances to Indian Ocean (East Africa, Red Sea, Ormuz, Malabar, Malacca)
- **Stranglehold methods**:
  - Introduced **permit system (cartaze)** for other ships
  - Controlled major ship-building centres (timber scarce in Gulf/Red Sea, aiding monopoly)
- **1510: Captured Goa** from **Sultan of Bijapur** (first bit of Indian territory under Europeans since Alexander the Great)
- **Domestic/Social policies**:
  - **Abolished sati** in Goa
  - Encouraged Portuguese men to marry local women (**casados**) → established permanent settlement of village landlords, artisans, traders
  - Introduced new crops: **Tobacco**, **cashew nut**, improved varieties of **coconut** (for coir rigging & cordage)
- **Drawback**: Bitter persecution of Muslims

#### **Nino da Cunha**
- **Nov 1529: Assumed office**
- **1530: Shifted HQ from Cochin to Goa**
- **Conflict in Gujarat**:
  - **Bahadur Shah of Gujarat** ceded **Bassein** (1534) and promised base in **Diu** in exchange for help against Mughal Humayun
  - Humayun withdrew (1536) → relations soured → Bahadur Shah wanted to build a wall of partition → Portuguese opposed
  - **1537: Bahadur Shah invited to Portuguese ship & killed** during negotiations
- **Bengal Expansion**: Settled Portuguese nationals with **Hooghly** as HQ

#### **Favourable Conditions For Portuguese**
- **Political division**: Northern India divided; Bahmani Kingdom in Deccan breaking up
- **Naval vacuum**: No Indian power had a navy worth its name or prioritized naval strength
- **Chinese limits**: Imperial decree restricted Chinese naval reach
- **Arab trade lacks organization/unity** compared to Portuguese state backing
- **Technological edge**: Cannon-mounted Portuguese ships

## **Portuguese State**
- **Estado Português da India**: Large element of Indian history
- **Territorial reach**:
  - 60 miles coast around Goa
  - West Coast: Narrow tract from Mumbai to Daman & Diu
  - South: Seaport fortresses (Mangalore, Cannanore, Cochin, Calicut)
  - East Coast: San Thome (Chennai) and Nagapattinam (TN)
  - Bengal: Wealthy settlement at Hooghly
- **Geopolitical role**: Balanced power between Vijayanagara and Deccan Sultans, Deccanis and Mughals, Mughals and Marathas (treaties signed with Deccan Sultans in 1570)
- **Historical footprint**: First Europeans to arrive (1498), last to leave (**1961: recaptured by India**)

### **Portuguese Administration In India**
- **Viceroy (3-year term)**: Head of administration, assisted by secretary and later a council
- **Vedor da Fazenda**: Responsible for revenues, cargoes, fleet dispatch (second in importance)
- **Captains & Factors**: Run individual fortresses from Africa to China (frequently used power for personal gains)

### **Religious Policy Of The Portuguese**
- **Antagonism**: Extreme zeal to promote Christianity and persecute Moors/Arabs/Muslims
- **Hindus**: Tolerant initially → turned to persecution after introduction of **Goa Inquisition**
- **Mughal Interaction (Akbar)**:
  - Akbar invited priests to theological debates; Jesuits hoped to convert the court
  - **1st Jesuit Mission (1580-1583)**: Rodolfo Aquaviva and Antonio Monserrate at Fatehpur Sikri
  - **2nd Jesuit Mission (1590-1592)**: Ended without conversion
  - **3rd Jesuit Mission (1595)**: Permanent presence at Lahore; led by Father Jerome Xavier and Emanuel Pinheiro
  - **Jahangir's reign**: Renewed Jesuit favors in 1606; built Agra church and held public baptisms in 1608

## **Portuguese Lose Favour With The Mughals**
- **1608: William Hawkins (Hector)** arrived at Surat with letter from James I to Jahangir
- **Mughal favor**: Hawkins received favorably in 1609, conversed in Turki, appointed **Mansabdar of 400** (Rs 30,000 salary), married daughter of Armenian Christian Mubarak Shah
- **Portuguese counter-moves**: Instigated intrigues, blocked English ships at Surat → Hawkins left court in 1611
- **Nov 1612 Battle off Surat**: English ship **Dragon** (under **Captain Best**) and **Osiander** defeated Portuguese fleet → Jahangir highly impressed by English naval power
- **1613 Portuguese Piracy**: Captured Mughal ships, plundered cargo, imprisoned Muslims → Enraged Jahangir ordered Surat governor Muqarrab Khan to seek compensation
- **Loss of court favor**: Completely lost during Shah Jahan's reign

### **Capture Of Hooghly**
- **Background**: Settled down at Hooghly under 1579 imperial farman → trade migrated from Satgaon
- **Portuguese excesses**:
  - Monopolised salt manufacture
  - Enforced duties on tobacco
  - Built custom house
  - Started **slave trade** (seizing/purchasing Hindu and Muslim children to convert to Christianity)
  - Seized two slave girls of **Mumtaz Mahal**
- **Mughal Retaliation (1632)**:
  - Shah Jahan ordered Bengal Governor **Qasim Khan** to besiege Hooghly
  - 3-month siege → Portuguese fled; 1,000 Mughals dead, 400 Portuguese prisoners taken to Agra (offered Islam or slavery)

---

## **Decline Of The Portuguese**
- **Loss of commercial influence by 18th century** (many turned to piracy at Hooghly)
- **Key Factors for Decline**:
  - **Rise of powerful dynasties**: Egypt, Persia, and North India
  - **Immediate neighbors**: Rise of turbulent **Marathas** (captured Salsette & Bassein in 1739; invaded Goa in 1683)
  - **Religious intolerance**: Inquisition and forced conversions alienated Hindus & Muslims
  - **Dishonest trade practices**: Arrogance, piracy, and violent plunder
  - **Discovery of Brazil**: Diverted Portuguese colonization to the West
  - **Union of Spain & Portugal (1580–81)**: Dragged Portugal into Spain’s wars with England and Holland → broke trade monopoly
  - **Loss of sea route monopoly**: Dutch and English mastered ocean navigation
  - **Goa decline**: Lost port importance after the fall of Vijayanagara empire; Brazil became economic center

---

## **Significance Of The Portuguese**
- **Emergence of Naval Power**: First foreign power to dominate India via the sea, asserting monopoly through force (cannons on ships)
- **Military Innovation**:
  - Introduced body armour, matchlock men, and ship-landed guns in Malabar
  - Influenced Mughal field guns and "artillery of the stirrup"
  - Introduced **drilling groups of infantry** on the Spanish model (1630s) → adopted by French/English, later Marathas/Sikhs
- **Shipbuilding & Seafaring**:
  - Heavily constructed multi-decked ships to ride out Atlantic gales (adapted by Indian shipbuilders)
  - Castled prow and stern to launch/repel boarding parties
  - Systematic state organisation: royal arsenals, dockyards, regular pilots, mapping
- **Cultural influence**:
  - Missionaries as patrons of painting, carving, sculpting, and music
  - Goa became center of silversmith/goldsmith arts (filigree, fretted foliage)
  - Architectural simplicity in churches with rich woodwork interiors

## **The Dutch**
- **1596: Cornelis de Houtman** → first Dutchman to reach Sumatra and Bantam
- **1602: United East India Company of Netherlands (VOC)** established by States-General charter
  - Empowered to: Wage war, conclude treaties, acquire territory, build fortresses

### **Dutch Settlements**
- **1605: First factory at Masulipatnam** (Andhra)
- **Nagapatam**: Captured from Portuguese, became main South Indian stronghold
- **Key Factories**:
  - **Coromandel Coast / South**: Pulicat (1609), Karaikal (1645), Nagapatam (1658)
  - **West**: Surat (1616)
  - **East/Bengal/Bihar**: Bimlipatam (1641), Chinsura (1653), Baranagar, Kasimbazar, Balasore, Patna, Cochin (1663)
- **Redistributive/Carrying Trade**:
  - **Indigo** (Yamuna Valley / Central India)
  - **Textiles & Silk** (Bengal, Gujarat, Coromandel)
  - **Saltpetre** (Bihar)
  - **Opium & Rice** (Ganga Valley)

### **Anglo-Dutch Rivalry**
- **Commercial conflict** → escalated to warfare
- **1623 Amboyna Massacre**: Dutch massacred 10 Englishmen and 9 Japanese in Indonesia
- **1667 Compromise**: British withdrew all claims on Indonesia | Dutch retired from India to focus on Indonesian spice trade (black pepper & spices monopoly)

### **Decline Of The Dutch in India**
- Shifting attention strictly to Malay Archipelago (Spice Islands)
- **Third Anglo-Dutch War (1672-74)**: Dutch captured 3 English ships in Bay of Bengal, cutting communications
- **Retaliation**: English defeated Dutch in **Battle of Hooghly (Battle of Bidara) in November 1759** → crushed Dutch ambitions in India
- **Objective shift**: Pure trade focus over empire building; Indonesia was vastly more profitable

## **The English**

### **Charter Of Queen Elizabeth I**
- **Catalysts**: Francis Drake’s world voyage (1580) & defeat of Spanish Armada (1588) → fueled English enterprise
- **1599: "Merchant Adventurers"** formed a company
- **Dec 31, 1600: Queen Elizabeth I Charter** → granted 15-year exclusive trade monopoly to the **'Governor and Company of Merchants of London trading into the East Indies'**
- **Focus Shift**: Dutch dominated Spice Islands → English turned to India for textiles

### **Progress Of The English Company**

#### **Foothold In West And South**
- **1609: Captain Hawkins** arrived at Jahangir's court (left unsuccessful in 1611 due to Portuguese opposition)
- **1611: Began trade at Masulipatnam** (first southern factory set up there in 1616)
- **1612: Captain Thomas Best** defeated Portuguese fleet off Surat
- **1613: Permanent factory established at Surat** under Thomas Aldworth by imperial permission
- **1615-1619: Sir Thomas Roe** (James I Ambassador) secured privileges to build factories at **Agra, Ahmedabad, and Broach**
- **Acquisition of Bombay**:
  - Gained by **King Charles II** in 1662 as Portuguese dowry (marriage to Catherine of Braganza)
  - Leased to EIC in 1668 for **annual rent of 10 pounds**
  - **1687**: Seat of Western Presidency shifted from Surat to Bombay (HQ)
- **Settlement of Rivals**: Portuguese resolved by Bombay dowry peace; Dutch resolved by 1667 compromise
- **1632 Golden Farman**: Sultan of Golconda granted free trade in Golconda ports for **500 pagodas/year**
- **1639: Francis Day** obtained lease of **Madras** from Chandragiri ruler:
  - Built fortified factory **Fort St. George**
  - Replaced Masulipatnam as headquarters in South India
- **Eastern expansion**: Factories at **Hariharpur (Mahanadi delta)** & **Balasore (Odisha)** in 1633

#### **Foothold In Bengal**
- **1651: Shah Shuja (Bengal Subahdar)** permitted English to trade in Bengal for fixed **Rs 3,000/year** (exempt from all duties)
- **Factories**: Hooghly (1651), Kasimbazar, Patna, Rajmahal
- **Conflict with Mughals**:
  - Customs officials obstructed business with toll demands
  - EIC sought fortified base at Hooghly to use force
  - **1682**: William Hedges (Governor) appealed to Shaista Khan (Bengal Governor) → no result
  - **Oct 1686: Hooghly sacked** by Mughals → English retaliated by capturing Thana fort, raiding Hijli, storming Balasore
  - English forced to leave Hooghly for unhealthy mouth of Ganga
- **Sutanuti Settlement**:
  - Agent **Job Charnock** negotiated return; signed treaty in **Feb 1690**; returned to **Sutanuti** in August 1690
  - **Feb 10, 1691**: Imperial farman permitted trade in Bengal for Rs 3,000/year
  - **1696 Fortification**: Pretext of rebellion by zamindar **Sobha Singh** (Bardhaman)
  - **1698**: Bought Zamindari of **Sutanuti, Gobindapur, Kalikata** for **Rs 1,200**
  - **1700: Fort William** named; became seat of Eastern Presidency (Calcutta) with **Sir Charles Eyre** as first president

#### **Farrukhsiyar’s Farmans**
- **1715 John Surman Mission** → secured 3 farmans in **1717** from Emperor **Farrukhsiyar** (called the **Magna Carta of the Company**):
  - **Bengal**: Imports/exports exempt from customs duties for Rs 3,000/year; Company authorized to issue **dastaks** (passes) for transport of goods; permitted to rent more land around Calcutta
  - **Hyderabad**: Retained freedom from duties; paid prevailing rent only for Madras
  - **Surat**: Exempt from all duties for annual payment of **Rs 10,000**
  - **Currency**: Bombay-minted coins declared legal tender throughout the Mughal Empire

#### **Merging Of Two English Companies**
- **1688 English Revolution** → Whig opposition to EIC monopoly
- **New Rival Company** formed → sent **Sir William Norris** to Aurangzeb (1701-1702) → failed
- **1708 Amalgamation**: Crown/Parliament pressure forced merger into **'United Company of Merchants of England Trading to the East Indies'** (retained this structure till 1873)

## **The French**

### **Foundation Of French Centres In India**
- **Late entry**: Last Europeans to arrive for trade
- **1664: Colbert** (minister to Louis XIV) founded **Compagnie des Indes Orientales** (French East India Company)
  - Under deep royal interest; granted **50-year trade monopoly** in Indian/Pacific Oceans, perpetual concession of Madagascar (exploration failed)
- **1667: Francois Caron** led expedition → set up first factory at **Surat**
- **1669: Mercara** (Persian) established factory at **Masulipatnam** (patent from Sultan of Golconda)
- **1673**: Obtained permission from Shaista Khan to establish township at **Chandernagore** (near Calcutta)

#### **Pondicherry—Nerve Centre Of French Power In India**
- **1673**: **Sher Khan Lodi** (Bijapur governor) granted settlement site to **Francois Martin** (Masulipatnam director)
- **1674**: **Pondicherry founded**; Francois Martin became French Governor, developing it into their main Indian stronghold
- **Other factories**: Mahe, Karaikal, Balasore, Kasimbazar

#### **Early Setbacks To The French East India Company**
- **1693 Dutch Capture**: Dutch captured Pondicherry due to Anglo-French War (Revolution of 1688 alliance)
- **1697 Treaty of Ryswick**: Restored Pondicherry to French (garrison held till 1699) → Francois Martin revived its prosperity
- **War of Spanish Succession**: Forced abandonment of factories at Surat, Masulipatnam, Bantam (early 18th Century)
- **Dec 31, 1706**: Death of Francois Martin (major setback)

#### **Reorganisation Of The French Company**
- **1720**: Reorganised as **'Perpetual Company of the Indies'** → revived fortunes
- **1720–1742 Stewardship**: Governors **Lenoir** and **Dumas** consolidated power
- **Territorial backing**: Retained southern Indian Ocean islands of **Mauritius** and **Reunion**

### **The Anglo-French Struggle For Supremacy: The Carnatic Wars**
- **Context**: Shift from trade to political ambition; mirrored traditional European rivalry (Austrian War of Succession & Seven Years' War)
- **Political vacuum in South India (1740)**:
  - Nizam Asaf Jah of Hyderabad old and occupied with Marathas
  - Coromandel Coast (Carnatic) lacked a central authority
  - Declining Hyderabad allowed British/French adventures
  - Marathas of Tanjore intervened frequently

#### **First Carnatic War (1740–48)**
- **Background**: Extension of War of Austrian Succession in Europe
- **Immediate Cause**: English navy under **Barnet** seized French ships to provoke hostilities → French Governor **Dupleix** retaliated, capturing **Madras (1746)** with Mauritius fleet under **Admiral La Bourdonnais**
- **Result**: **Treaty of Aix-La-Chapelle (1748)** → Madras returned to English; French received territories in North America
- **Significance**:
  - **Battle of St. Thome (1746)**: Small French force under **Captain Paradise** defeated larger army of Anwaruddin (Nawab of Carnatic under Mahfuz Khan) on River Adyar
  - **Key Lesson**: Disciplined European troops can easily defeat much larger Indian armies; highlighted critical role of naval power

#### **Second Carnatic War (1749–54)**
- **Immediate Cause**: Death of Hyderabad Nizam-ul-Mulk (1748) and release of Chanda Sahib by Marathas
- **Dynastic Conflicts**:
  - **Hyderabad**: Nasir Jung (English backed) vs Muzaffar Jung (French backed)
  - **Carnatic**: Anwaruddin Khan (English backed) vs Chanda Sahib (French backed)
- **Course of War**:
  - Combined French/Muzaffar/Chanda forces killed Anwaruddin at **Battle of Ambur (1749)**
  - Muzaffar Jung became Deccan subahdar → appointed **Dupleix governor** of all territories south of Krishna River
  - French army under **Bussy** stationed at Hyderabad; territories near Pondicherry & Orissa coast ceded to French
  - **Clive’s Diversion (1751)**: Robert Clive proposed attack on Carnatic capital **Arcot** to relieve French pressure on Trichinopoly (where Muhammad Ali was holed up)
  - **Capture & Siege of Arcot**: Clive captured Arcot with 210 men → Chanda Sahib diverted 4,000 men to lay siege for 53 days, but failed to recapture it
  - **French defeat**: Allied forces (Mysore, Tanjore, Maratha Morari Rao) aided English → June 1752: Chanda Sahib surrendered and was executed by Muhammad Ali
- **Result**: French directors recalled Dupleix (1754) due to heavy financial losses; **Godeheu** succeeded him, signing a treaty of non-interference and mutual territorial retention
- **Implications**: Indian rulers became dependent on European support (Muhammad Ali & Salabat Jang became client states)

#### **Third Carnatic War (1758–63)**
- **Background**: Seven Years’ War (1756–63) in Europe
- **Course of War**:
  - 1758: French Count de Lally captured English Fort St. David & Vizianagaram
  - English fleet under Admiral D’Ache inflicted losses on French
  - **Battle of Wandiwash (Jan 22, 1760)**: Decisive battle in Tamil Nadu → English **General Eyre Coote** totally routed French under Count de Lally; took Bussy prisoner
  - Jan 16, 1761: Count de Lally surrendered Pondicherry after 8-month defense; Jinji & Mahe fell
- **Result**: **Treaty of Paris (1763)** → restored factories to French but **extinguished all French political influence** in India (confined to enclaves and trade only)
- **Significance**: English established absolute European supremacy in India (having defeated Dutch in 1759 & French in 1760); ready to conquer the subcontinent

### **Causes For The English Success And The French Failure**
- **Company Structure**: EIC was private (enthusiasm, instant decisions) | French company was State-owned (bureaucratic delays, rigid control)
- **Naval Power**: Superior English navy cut off the French sea link with France
- **Strategic Bases**: English held 3 major ports (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras) | French had only Pondicherry
- **Financial Strategy**: French subordinated trade to territorial conquest (starving company of funds) | British kept commercial interests supreme, ensuring constant wartime funding (aided by Bengal resources)
- **Leadership**: English had multiple top-tier commanders (Coote, Lawrence, Clive) | French relied solely on Dupleix

### **About the Goods In Trade Initially**
- **Textiles**: Huge demand for **cotton longcloth** (35-50m), **salempores** (staple cotton), **morees** (superior cotton), painted/printed cloths, silks, and dyes
- **Spices**: Indian textiles traded in Malacca/Java for spices to bring to Europe
- **Slavery Link**: French ships traded European goods to Asia for textiles/cowries → exchanged in West Africa for slaves → slaves sent to French colonies in Americas → sugar brought back to France (completed the triangular circle)
- **Complex Indian Economy**: Europeans entered an already advanced, intricate network of Indian weavers, brokers, exporters, and shipowners

## **Why The English Succeeded Against Other European Powers**

### **Structure And Nature Of The Trading Companies**
- **EIC**: Private enterprise run by annually elected Board of Directors; active shareholders (votes bought/sold via shares)
- **Rivals (French/Portuguese)**: State-owned, feudalistic; monarchs held majority shares (e.g., >60% in French company); directors nominated by state; state-guaranteed dividends bred shareholder apathy (French company had no meetings between 1725-1765; managed as state dept)

### **Naval Superiority**
- British Royal Navy was the largest and technologically most advanced (victories at Spanish Armada 1588, Trafalgar 1805)
- Fast ship movements and naval tactics defeated Portuguese and French in India

### **Industrial Revolution**
- Started in England (early 18th Century) with spinning jenny, steam engine, power loom → massive increase in textile & metal production
- Arrived much later in other European nations, establishing English industrial hegemony

### **Military Skill And Discipline**
- Disciplined, well-trained soldiers
- Commanders were strategists using innovative warfare tactics and advanced military technology (infantry drilling)

### **Stable Government**
- Stable monarchy/parliament post-1688 Glorious Revolution
- France suffered 1789 Revolution and Napoleonic Wars; Spain/Dutch suffered 80-years war; Italy united very late (1861)

### **Lesser Zeal For Religion**
- British prioritized trade over conversion (less zealous than Spain, Portugal, or Dutch), making their rule far more acceptable to Indian subjects

### **Use Of Debt Market**
- Established **Bank of England** (world's first central bank) to sell government debt on the promise of decent returns after victory
- Enabled massive military spending; rival France went bankrupt due to outdated financial systems
