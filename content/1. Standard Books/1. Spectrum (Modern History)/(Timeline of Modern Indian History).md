**1498 :** Vasco da Gama reached **Calicut**, establishing a direct maritime route between Europe and India and beginning the Portuguese phase of European expansion in India.

**1505 :** **Francisco de Almeida** became the first Portuguese governor/viceroy in India and developed the policy of establishing naval supremacy, known as the **Blue Water Policy**.

**1510 :** **Albuquerque captured Goa** from the Bijapur Sultanate, making it the headquarters of Portuguese power in India.

**1600 :** The **English East India Company** was established by a royal charter granted by Queen Elizabeth I, giving it a monopoly over English trade with the East.

**1602 :** The **Dutch East India Company (VOC)** was established.

**1608 :** The English established their first factory at **Surat**.

**1612 :** The English defeated the Portuguese in the **Battle of Swally**, strengthening their position on India's western coast.

**1613 :** The English established a permanent factory at **Surat**.

**1615–1619 :** **Sir Thomas Roe** stayed at the Mughal court of Jahangir as England's ambassador and secured important trading privileges for the East India Company.

**1639 :** The English obtained land at **Madras** from the Nayak ruler and established **Fort St. George**.

**1661 :** **Bombay** was transferred to England by Portugal as part of the marriage treaty between **Charles II of England and Catherine of Braganza**.

**1668 :** Charles II transferred Bombay to the **East India Company** on an annual rent, making it an important English commercial and naval base.

**1690 :** **Job Charnock** established the English settlement at **Calcutta**.

**1698 :** The Company acquired the **zamindari rights of the villages of Sutanuti, Kalikata and Govindpur**, laying the foundation for British Calcutta.

**1707 :** **Aurangzeb died**, marking the beginning of the rapid political decline of the Mughal Empire and the rise of regional powers.

**1717 :** Mughal emperor **Farrukhsiyar** granted the East India Company important trading privileges through a farman, including duty-free trade in Bengal in return for an annual payment.

**1740–1748 :** The **First Carnatic War** was fought between the British and French in the context of the **War of Austrian Succession** in Europe.

**1746 :** The French captured **Madras** under **La Bourdonnais**.

**1748 :** The **Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle** ended the First Carnatic War and restored Madras to the British.

**1749–1754 :** The **Second Carnatic War** was fought mainly over succession disputes in Hyderabad and the Carnatic, providing the French and British opportunities to intervene in Indian politics.

**1751 :** **Robert Clive captured Arcot**, dramatically improving the British position in the Carnatic.

**1754 :** The Second Carnatic War ended with the **Treaty of Pondicherry**; British influence increased while French political ambitions in southern India declined.

**1756 :** **Siraj-ud-daulah**, the Nawab of Bengal, captured Calcutta after conflicts with the East India Company.

**1757 :** The **Battle of Plassey** was fought on **23 June**; Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-daulah with the help of conspirators including **Mir Jafar**, establishing Company political influence in Bengal.

**1757 :** **Mir Jafar** was installed as Nawab of Bengal, demonstrating the Company's ability to determine the succession of Indian rulers.

**1760 :** **Mir Qasim** replaced Mir Jafar as Nawab of Bengal after the latter became increasingly inconvenient to the Company.

**1763 :** Mir Qasim's conflict with the Company intensified; he abolished internal duties in an attempt to create equality between Indian and Company traders.

**1764 :** The **Battle of Buxar** was fought between the Company and the combined forces of **Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-daulah and Shah Alam II**; the Company achieved a decisive victory.

**1765 :** The **Treaty of Allahabad** was concluded; the Mughal emperor **Shah Alam II granted Diwani rights** of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the East India Company.

**1765 :** The Company introduced the **Dual Government in Bengal**, under which the Company controlled revenue while the Nawab formally retained responsibility for administration.

**1770 :** The **Great Bengal Famine** caused massive loss of life; Company revenue demands and administrative failures aggravated the crisis.

**1772 :** **Warren Hastings ended the Dual Government** and brought Bengal's administration more directly under Company control.

**1773 :** The **Regulating Act** was passed by the British Parliament; it created the office of **Governor-General of Bengal**and attempted to regulate Company administration.

**1774 :** The **Supreme Court at Calcutta** was established; **Warren Hastings** became the first Governor-General of Bengal.

**1784 :** **Pitt's India Act** established a system of dual control, giving the British government greater supervision over the Company's political affairs through the **Board of Control**.

**1793 :** **Cornwallis Code** consolidated administrative and judicial reforms; Cornwallis also strengthened the separation between revenue administration and judicial functions.

**1793 :** The **Permanent Settlement** was introduced in Bengal, fixing land revenue permanently with zamindars and recognizing them as hereditary proprietors subject to revenue payment.

**1798 :** **Lord Wellesley** became Governor-General and adopted an aggressive policy of territorial expansion.

**1798 :** The **Subsidiary Alliance system** was systematically developed by Wellesley; Indian rulers accepting it had to maintain British troops in their territory and surrender control over external relations.

**1799 :** The **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War** ended with the death of **Tipu Sultan** at Seringapatam; Mysore was defeated and British dominance in southern India was strengthened.

**1802 :** The **Treaty of Bassein** was signed between the British and **Peshwa Baji Rao II**, bringing the Peshwa under the Subsidiary Alliance and triggering the Second Anglo-Maratha War.

**1803 :** The British defeated major Maratha forces during the **Second Anglo-Maratha War** and captured Delhi, bringing the Mughal emperor under British protection.

**1813 :** The **Charter Act of 1813** ended the Company's trade monopoly in India, except for tea and trade with China, and formally permitted Christian missionaries to operate in British India.

**1817–1818 :** The **Third Anglo-Maratha War** ended Maratha political supremacy; the Peshwa was deposed and British paramountcy was established over most of India.

**1828 :** **Lord William Bentinck** became Governor-General and initiated several important social, administrative and economic reforms.

**1829 :** **Sati was abolished** through Regulation XVII of Bengal under Bentinck's administration, making the practice legally punishable.

**1833 :** The **Charter Act of 1833** ended the Company's commercial activities completely and transformed it into an administrative body; the Governor-General of Bengal became the **Governor-General of India**.

**1835 :** **English education** was strongly promoted following **Macaulay's Minute on Education**; English became the principal medium for higher education supported by the colonial government.

**1835 :** The **Government declared the use of English for higher official and educational purposes**, accelerating the growth of an English-educated Indian middle class.

**1839 :** **Maharaja Ranjit Singh died**, weakening the Sikh Empire and contributing to internal instability in Punjab.

**1843 :** **Sind was annexed** by the British under **Charles Napier**.

**1845–1846 :** The **First Anglo-Sikh War** ended in British victory; the **Treaty of Lahore** reduced Sikh power and increased British influence in Punjab.

**1848 :** **Lord Dalhousie** became Governor-General and pursued an aggressive policy of territorial consolidation.

**1848 :** The **Doctrine of Lapse** was systematically applied by Dalhousie; states without a natural male heir could be annexed by the British if the adoption of an heir was not recognized.

**1848 :** **Satara was annexed** under the Doctrine of Lapse.

**1849 :** The **Second Anglo-Sikh War** ended in British victory and the **annexation of Punjab**.

**1852 :** The **Second Anglo-Burmese War** resulted in the British annexation of Lower Burma's Pegu province.

**1853 :** The first passenger **railway line in India** opened between **Bombay and Thane**, covering approximately 34 km.

**1853 :** The **Charter Act of 1853** separated the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council and introduced an open competition principle for recruitment to the civil services.

**1854 :** **Wood's Despatch** laid the foundation of a systematic modern education system in India and recommended universities, teacher training and a hierarchical education structure.

**1855–1856 :** The **Santhal rebellion** occurred against zamindars, moneylenders and colonial authorities in the Rajmahal region.

**1856 :** **Awadh was annexed** by the British on the grounds of alleged maladministration, creating considerable resentment among the taluqdars, soldiers and population.

**1856 :** The **General Service Enlistment Act** required certain new recruits to accept overseas service, contributing to discontent among sections of the Bengal Army.

**1857 :** The **Revolt of 1857** began at **Meerut on 10 May** after sepoys rebelled against their officers and marched towards Delhi.

**1857 :** The rebels captured **Delhi** and proclaimed **Bahadur Shah II** as the symbolic emperor of India.

**1857 :** **Mangal Pandey** had earlier attacked British officers at Barrackpore on **29 March**, becoming an important symbol of the growing sepoy discontent.

**1857 :** **Kanpur** became a major centre of rebellion under **Nana Sahib**, with **Tantia Tope** playing an important military role.

**1857 :** **Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi** emerged as one of the principal leaders of the revolt in central India.

**1857 :** **Begum Hazrat Mahal** led resistance in Awadh and proclaimed her son **Birjis Qadr** as ruler.

**1857 :** **Kunwar Singh** led the revolt in Bihar.

**1857 :** The British gradually recaptured Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow and other centres, suppressing the revolt through military force.

**1858 :** **Bahadur Shah II was captured and exiled to Rangoon**, effectively ending the Mughal dynasty.

**1858 :** The **Government of India Act of 1858** ended the rule of the East India Company and transferred sovereignty over India directly to the **British Crown**.

**1858 :** The office of **Secretary of State for India** was created in Britain, assisted by an India Council.

**1858 :** **Queen Victoria's Proclamation** promised non-interference in religious matters, equal treatment under law and respect for treaties with Indian rulers.

**1861 :** The **Indian Councils Act of 1861** restored legislative councils and introduced limited Indian participation in legislation.

**1861 :** The **Indian High Courts Act** led to the establishment of High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras.

**1869 :** **Mahatma Gandhi was born** on 2 October at Porbandar.

**1872 :** The first **all-India census** was conducted, though the first synchronous census across India was later conducted in 1881.

**1875 :** **Arya Samaj** was founded by **Swami Dayanand Saraswati** at Bombay.

**1875 :** **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** founded the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh**, which later developed into Aligarh Muslim University.

**1876 :** **Indian Association** was founded by **Surendranath Banerjee and Anand Mohan Bose**, seeking wider political participation and national political unity.

**1877 :** **Queen Victoria was proclaimed Empress of India** at the Delhi Durbar.

**1878 :** The **Vernacular Press Act** restricted the freedom of Indian-language newspapers.

**1878 :** The **Arms Act** imposed restrictions on Indians possessing arms, with discriminatory exemptions.

**1882 :** The **Hunter Commission** examined education, particularly primary education, and emphasized greater government attention to elementary education.

**1883 :** The **Ilbert Bill controversy** exposed racial discrimination in colonial judicial administration when European opposition resisted the prospect of Indian judges exercising jurisdiction over Europeans.

**1885 :** The **Indian National Congress was founded** at Bombay, with **W.C. Bonnerjee** as its first president; **A.O. Hume**played a major role in organizing its formation.

**1885 :** The first session of the **Indian National Congress** was held at Bombay with 72 delegates.

**1886 :** The second Congress session was held at Calcutta under **Dadabhai Naoroji**.

**1889 :** **Jawaharlal Nehru was born** at Allahabad.

**1892 :** The **Indian Councils Act of 1892** expanded legislative councils and introduced limited indirect election while retaining official dominance.

**1893 :** **Swami Vivekananda addressed the World Parliament of Religions at Chicago**, gaining international recognition for Indian philosophical traditions.

**1897 :** **Subhas Chandra Bose was born** at Cuttack.

**1897 :** **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** was arrested in the context of growing nationalist agitation and later became an important leader of assertive nationalism.

**1904 :** The **Indian Universities Act** increased government control over universities under Lord Curzon.

**1905 :** **Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal** on 16 October, officially for administrative reasons but widely perceived as an attempt to weaken Bengali nationalism.

**1905 :** The **Swadeshi Movement** began in response to the Partition of Bengal, promoting boycott of foreign goods, indigenous industry and national education.

**1906 :** The **All-India Muslim League** was founded at **Dhaka**, initially seeking to protect Muslim political interests.

**1906 :** **Dadabhai Naoroji** declared **Swaraj** as the goal of the Indian National Congress at its Calcutta session.

**1907 :** The Congress split at **Surat** between the Moderates and Extremists.

**1908 :** **Khudiram Bose** was executed for his involvement in revolutionary activities.

**1908 :** **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** was sentenced to six years' imprisonment for sedition.

**1909 :** The **Morley-Minto Reforms**, through the **Indian Councils Act of 1909**, introduced **separate electorates for Muslims**, institutionalizing communal representation.

**1911 :** The **Partition of Bengal was annulled**.

**1911 :** The British government announced the **transfer of India's capital from Calcutta to Delhi**.

**1913 :** **Ghadar Party** was founded in North America by Indian expatriates, advocating armed struggle against British rule.

**1915 :** **Mahatma Gandhi returned to India from South Africa**.

**1916 :** The **Home Rule Movement** was launched by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant**, demanding self-government for India.

**1916 :** The **Lucknow Pact** brought the Congress and Muslim League together on constitutional demands; Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims.

**1917 :** Gandhi led the **Champaran Satyagraha** against the exploitation of indigo cultivators, marking his first major satyagraha in India.

**1918 :** Gandhi led the **Ahmedabad Mill Strike**, using a hunger strike and non-violent pressure to support textile workers.

**1918 :** Gandhi led the **Kheda Satyagraha**, demanding suspension of land revenue collection because of crop failure.

**1919 :** The **Rowlatt Act** extended wartime emergency powers and allowed detention without trial, provoking widespread opposition.

**1919 :** On **13 April**, British troops under **General Dyer** fired upon an unarmed gathering at **Jallianwala Bagh**, killing hundreds and causing nationwide outrage.

**1919 :** The **Government of India Act of 1919** introduced **dyarchy in the provinces** and expanded legislative councils.

**1919 :** The **Khilafat Movement** began to mobilize Indian Muslims around the issue of the Ottoman Caliphate.

**1920 :** Gandhi launched the **Non-Cooperation Movement**, calling for boycott of government institutions, foreign cloth, titles and colonial institutions.

**1920 :** The Congress adopted a new organizational structure based on **linguistic provinces** and broadened its membership.

**1921 :** The Non-Cooperation Movement developed into a major mass movement involving students, lawyers, workers, peasants and sections of the middle class.

**1922 :** The **Chauri Chaura incident** occurred on 5 February, when protesters killed police personnel after a confrontation with the police.

**1922 :** Gandhi **withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement**, arguing that the movement had become vulnerable to violence.

**1923 :** **Swaraj Party** was founded by **C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru** to enter legislative councils and obstruct colonial governance from within.

**1925 :** The **Communist Party of India** was formally organized at Kanpur.

**1927 :** The British appointed the **Simon Commission** to review constitutional reforms; it contained **no Indian member**.

**1928 :** The **Simon Commission arrived in India**, provoking nationwide protests under the slogan **"Simon Go Back."**

**1928 :** **Lala Lajpat Rai** was injured during a protest against the Simon Commission and subsequently died.

**1928 :** The **Nehru Report**, chaired by Motilal Nehru, proposed dominion status and constitutional reforms for India.

**1929 :** The **Lahore Congress** under **Jawaharlal Nehru** declared **Purna Swaraj** as the Congress objective.

**1930 :** **26 January** was observed as the first **Independence Day** by the Congress.

**1930 :** Gandhi launched the **Civil Disobedience Movement** through the **Dandi March**, beginning on 12 March and culminating in the violation of the salt law at Dandi on 6 April.

**1930 :** The **First Round Table Conference** was held in London without Congress participation.

**1931 :** Gandhi and **Lord Irwin** signed the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact**, leading to the suspension of the Civil Disobedience Movement.

**1931 :** Gandhi attended the **Second Round Table Conference** in London as the sole official Congress representative.

**1932 :** The British Prime Minister **Ramsay MacDonald announced the Communal Award**, providing separate electorates to several communities, including the Depressed Classes.

**1932 :** Gandhi opposed separate electorates for the Depressed Classes and began a fast in Yerwada Jail.

**1932 :** The **Poona Pact** was signed between Gandhi and **B.R. Ambedkar**, replacing separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with reserved seats within joint electorates.

**1935 :** The **Government of India Act of 1935** introduced provincial autonomy, proposed an all-India federation and significantly expanded the electorate.

**1937 :** Provincial elections were held under the Government of India Act of 1935.

**1937 :** The **Congress formed ministries in several provinces**, providing Indian leaders with practical experience of provincial governance.

**1939 :** **World War II began**; the British government declared India at war without consulting Indian political leaders.

**1939 :** Congress ministries **resigned** in protest against India's automatic involvement in the war.

**1940 :** The **Muslim League adopted the Lahore Resolution**, calling for independent states in Muslim-majority areas, later associated with the demand for Pakistan.

**1940 :** Gandhi launched the **Individual Satyagraha** to assert the right to free expression against the war effort; **Vinoba Bhave** was the first individual satyagrahi.

**1942 :** The British government sent the **Cripps Mission** to India with proposals for post-war constitutional arrangements; Congress rejected the offer as inadequate.

**1942 :** On **8 August**, the Congress passed the **Quit India Resolution** at Bombay, demanding immediate British withdrawal.

**1942 :** Gandhi gave the call of **"Do or Die."**

**1942 :** On **9 August**, Gandhi and other major Congress leaders were arrested, triggering widespread spontaneous protests.

**1942–1944 :** The **Quit India Movement** spread across India through strikes, demonstrations, underground networks and attacks on colonial infrastructure.

**1943 :** **Subhas Chandra Bose** took leadership of the **Indian National Army (INA)** and established the **Provisional Government of Free India**.

**1945 :** World War II ended in Europe and Asia, leaving Britain economically weakened and increasingly unable to maintain its empire.

**1945 :** The **Simla Conference** was convened by Lord Wavell to discuss the formation of a new Executive Council but failed because of disagreements between Congress and the Muslim League.

**1945–1946 :** The **INA trials** at the Red Fort generated widespread public sympathy for INA soldiers and intensified anti-British sentiment.

**1946 :** The **Royal Indian Navy Mutiny** began in Bombay and spread to other naval establishments; it demonstrated growing unrest within the armed forces.

**1946 :** The **Cabinet Mission** arrived in India to negotiate the transfer of power and proposed a constitutional framework based on a united India with a weak central government and grouped provinces.

**1946 :** The **Cabinet Mission Plan** was accepted initially by both Congress and the Muslim League, but disagreements over its interpretation eventually led to its collapse.

**1946 :** The Muslim League called for **Direct Action Day on 16 August**, leading to severe communal violence beginning in Calcutta.

**1946 :** The **Interim Government** was formed under **Jawaharlal Nehru**, with the Muslim League later joining it.

**1946 :** The **Constituent Assembly** met for the first time on **9 December**.

**1947 :** **Lord Mountbatten** became the last Viceroy of India.

**1947 :** On **3 June**, the **Mountbatten Plan** announced the partition of British India and the creation of two dominions.

**1947 :** The **Indian Independence Act** was passed by the British Parliament on **18 July**, legally providing for the creation of India and Pakistan.

**1947 :** **Pakistan became independent on 14 August**.

**1947 :** **India became independent on 15 August**, ending approximately two centuries of British political dominance.

**1947 :** **Jawaharlal Nehru** became the first Prime Minister of independent India and delivered his famous **"Tryst with Destiny"** speech.

**1947 :** The **Partition of India** resulted in the creation of India and Pakistan and triggered one of the largest and most violent migrations in modern history.
