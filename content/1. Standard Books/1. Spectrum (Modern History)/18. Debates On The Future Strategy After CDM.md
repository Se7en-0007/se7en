*   **CDM Withdrawal (1934) → Two-Stage Strategy Debate**:
    *   **First Stage (1934–35)**: Action plan during the immediate non-mass struggle phase.
    *   **Second Stage (1937)**: Question of office acceptance post-provincial elections under the **Government of India Act, 1935**.

---

## The First Stage Debate

*   **Three Perspectives on Immediate Course of Action**:
    1.  **Constructive Work**: Follow pure **Gandhian lines** (grassroots social work).
    2.  **Constitutional Struggle & Council Entry**: Participate in **Central Legislature elections (Nov 1934)**.
        *   *Advocates*: **M.A. Ansari, Asaf Ali, Bhulabhai Desai, S. Satyamurthy, B.C. Roy**.
        *   *Arguments*:
            *   Political apathy phase → Council work keeps up popular interest and morale.
            *   Elections/councils $\neq$ faith in constitutionalism; serves as a political front.
            *   Builds Congress prestige/confidence; strong presence in councils $=$ equivalent to the movement.
    3.  **Leftist Trend (Resumption of Mass Struggle)**:
        *   *Advocate*: **Jawaharlal Nehru** (supported by a strong left wing).
        *   *Arguments*:
            *   Critical of both constructive work and council entry → sidetracks political mass action and diverts focus from anti-colonial struggle.
            *   Favored immediate resumption/continuation of **non-constitutionalist mass struggle**.
            *   *Justification*: Continued **economic crisis** $+$ readiness of masses to fight $=$ revolutionary situation.

---

## Nehru’s Vision

*   **Core Goal**: Abolition of capitalism $+$ establishment of **socialism** (both in India and globally).
*   **Satyagraha Withdrawal & Council Entry** = "Spiritual defeat", "surrender of ideals", and "retreat from revolutionary to reformist mentality".
*   **Proposed Alternative Strategy**:
    *   Revise vested interests in favor of the masses → take up economic/class demands of peasants and workers against landlords and capitalists.
    *   Organize masses via class organizations: **Kisan Sabhas** and **Trade Unions**.
    *   **Class affiliation**: Formally affiliate these class organizations with the Congress to radicalize its policies.
    *   *Premise*: No genuine anti-imperialist struggle is possible without incorporating class struggle.

---

## Nehru’s Opposition To Struggle-Truce-Struggle Strategy

*   **Struggle-Truce-Struggle (S-T-S) Strategy** (Gandhi & Majority View):
    *   Mass struggle phase (**Struggle**) → Reprieve/Re-cooperating phase (**Truce**) → Next mass struggle (**Struggle**).
    *   *Logic*: Masses cannot sacrifice indefinitely; truce allows them to recoup strength and gives the government a chance to respond.
*   **Nehru’s Critique & Alternative**:
    *   *Critique*: Post-**Lahore Congress (1929)** and **Purna Swaraj** call, the movement must maintain continuous confrontation with imperialism without any constitutionalist interlude.
    *   *Real power* $\neq$ "two annas and four annas" (incremental reforms).
    *   *Proposed Strategy*: **Struggle-Victory (S-V) Strategy** → continuous direct action until the colonial state is overthrown.

---

## Finally, Yes To Council Entry

*   **Fear of Surat-like Split** (expected by nationalists, hoped for by British) → Avoided via **Gandhi's conciliation**.
*   **Gandhi's Compromise**: Acceded to basic demand of council entry.
    *   *Rationale*: Council work serves as an outlet for patriotic energies of those unable to offer satyagraha or do constructive work, provided they do not get sucked into constitutionalism/self-serving politics.
*   **May 1934 (Patna AICC Meeting)**: Formed the **Congress Parliamentary Board** to run elections under Congress control.
*   **October 1934**: **Gandhi announced resignation** from Congress due to fundamental differences:
    *   Intelligentsia favored parliamentary politics.
    *   Sections alienated by emphasis on the spinning wheel ("second lung of the nation").
    *   Socialists (Nehru) differed ideologically but remained in Congress.
        *   *Socialist Rationale*: Anti-imperialist unity around Congress (the only mass organization) is indispensable; better to gradually **radicalize Congress** from within than get isolated.
*   **November 1934 Central Assembly Elections Outcome**: Congress captured **45 out of 75 seats** reserved for Indians → Right-wing vindication.

## Government Of India Act, 1935

*   **Origin**: Discussions of the **Third Round Table Conference (Nov 1932)** held without Congress participation. Passed by British Parliament in **August 1935**.

### Main Features

1.  **An All India Federation**:
    *   *Composition*: British Indian provinces $+$ Chief Commissioner's provinces $+$ Indian (Princely) States.
    *   *Conditions for formation*:
        *   (i) States holding at least **52 seats** in the Council of States must agree to join.
        *   (ii) Aggregate population of joining states must be **50% of total princely state population**.
    *   *Outcome*: Conditions not met → **Federation never formed**. Central government operated under **Government of India Act, 1919** provisions until **1946**.

2.  **Federal Level: Executive**:
    *   **Governor General (GG)** = Pivot of the entire Constitution.
    *   **Division of Subjects**:
        *   **Reserved Subjects** (Foreign Affairs, Defence, Tribal Areas, Ecclesiastical Affairs): Administered solely by GG on advice of non-responsible **Executive Councillors**.
        *   **Transferred Subjects** (All other subjects): Administered by GG on advice of elected **Ministers** responsible to the legislature (required to resign on loss of confidence).
    *   **Special Powers**: GG could act in "individual judgment" using special responsibilities for India's security/tranquility.

3.  **Federal Level: Legislature**:
    *   **Bicameral System**:
        *   **Council of States (Upper House)**: 260 members. Partly **directly elected** from British provinces, partly (**40%**) nominated by princes. Permanent body; **1/3rd retire every third year**.
        *   **Federal Assembly (Lower House)**: 375 members. Partly **indirectly elected** from British provinces, partly (**1/3rd**) nominated by princes. Duration: **5 years**.
    *   *Anomalous Franchise*: Election to Upper House was direct; election to Lower House was indirect.
    *   **Legislative Lists**: Three lists established → **Federal, Provincial, Concurrent**.
    *   **Powers & Financial Restrictions**:
        *   No-confidence vote: Only movable in Federal Assembly (not Council of States).
        *   Electorates: Religion-based and class-based separate electorates further extended.
        *   **80% of budget was non-votable**.
        *   GG residuary powers: Could restore cuts in grants, certify rejected bills, issue ordinances, and exercise veto.

4.  **Provincial Autonomy**:
    *   **Dyarchy abolished** in provinces; replaced with provincial autonomy.
    *   Provinces granted independent legal identity and freed from "superintendence, direction" of Secretary of State and GG → derived legal authority **directly from the British Crown**.
    *   Given independent financial powers and resources (could borrow money on own security).

5.  **Provincial Level: Executive**:
    *   **Governor** = Crown's nominee and representative in the province.
    *   **Special Powers**: Discretionary powers over minorities, civil servants' rights, law and order, British business interests, partially excluded areas, and princely states.
    *   **Emergency Authority**: Governor could take over and indefinitely run provincial administration.

6.  **Provincial Level: Legislature**:
    *   Separate electorates based on **Communal Award** operationalized.
    *   **Direct Election**: All members directly elected; franchise extended (women got voting rights on same basis as men).
    *   **Council of Ministers**: Headed by a **Premier**; administered all provincial subjects; answerable to and removable by legislature.
    *   **Financial & Veto Limits**: **40% of provincial budget non-votable**. Governor could refuse assent, promulgate ordinances, and enact **Governor’s Acts**.

---

## Evaluation of The Act

*   **Structural Brakes**: Numerous 'safeguards' and 'special responsibilities' of GG/Governors paralyzed real autonomy.
*   **Limited Franchise**: Enfranchised only **14% of British Indian population**.
*   **Divisive Design**: Extended communal electorates promoted separatist tendencies → eventually culminated in **Partition**.
*   **Rigid Framework**: Provided a rigid constitution with no scope for internal growth; right of amendment reserved solely with the **British Parliament**.

---

## The Long-Term British Strategy

*   **Tactical Shift**: Suppression (short-term) → Reforms (long-term) to weaken the national movement and integrate it into colonial/constitutional structures.
*   **Key Goals of the Strategy**:
    *   Revive the political standing of **constitutionalist liberals and moderates** who lost support during CDM.
    *   Convince Congressmen that extra-legal struggle is ineffective.
    *   **Co-optation**: "Taste of power" would make Congressmen reluctant to return to politics of sacrifice.
    *   **Divide and Rule**: Create right-left dissensions in Congress → placate right-wing via concessions, crush left-wing via police measures.
    *   **Provincialization of Congress**: Create powerful provincial leaders as autonomous centers of power → weaken central Congress leadership.

---

## Nationalists’ Response

*   **Unanimous Rejection**: Congress condemned and rejected the 1935 Act root and branch.
*   **Counter-Demand**: Demanded convening of a **Constituent Assembly** elected on adult franchise to frame independent India's constitution.
*   **Dissenting Organizations**: **Hindu Mahasabha** and **National Liberal Federation** declared themselves in favor of working the Act at both central and provincial levels.

---

## The Second Stage Debate

*   **Context**: Early 1937 provincial assembly elections announced during a non-mass struggle phase.
*   **Core Question**: If Congress wins a majority in a province, should it form a ministry ("Office Acceptance")?

---

## Divided Opinion

*   **Opponents of Office Acceptance (Leftists)**:
    *   *Leaders*: **Jawaharlal Nehru, Subhas Bose**, Congress socialists, and communists.
    *   *Arguments*:
        *   Accepting office → assumption of responsibility without real power → compromises the total rejection of the Act.
        *   Sidetracks issues of freedom, social/economic justice → erodes the revolutionary character of the movement.
    *   *Counter-Strategy*: Enter councils solely to **create deadlocks** and render the Act unworkable (older Swarajist tactic).
    *   *Long-Term Strategy*: Integrate class organizations (peasants/workers) into Congress to direct it toward socialism and prepare for next mass struggle.
*   **Proponents of Office Acceptance (Rightists)**:
    *   *Arguments*:
        *   Equally committed to fighting the Act, but legislative work is a necessary short-term tactic when mass struggle is not possible.
        *   Administrative arena must not be left open to pro-government reactionary forces.
        *   Limited powers of provincial ministries could still be utilized to promote constructive work.

---

## Gandhi’s Position

*   **Evolution**: Initially opposed office acceptance in CWC meetings, but by early 1936 favored giving Congress ministries a trial.
*   **Congress Compromise (Lucknow 1936 & Faizpur 1937 Sessions)**:
    *   Decided to contest elections but postponed the final decision on office acceptance until after the polls.
    *   *Official line*: "Not to submit to this constitution or cooperate with it, but to combat it both inside and outside the legislatures so that it can be ended."

---

## Congress Manifesto for Elections

*   **Core Reaffirmation**: Total rejection of 1935 Act.
*   **Key Promises**:
    *   Release of political prisoners.
    *   Removal of gender and caste-based disabilities.
    *   Radical transformation of the agrarian system.
    *   Substantial reduction of land rent and revenue.
    *   Scaling down of rural debts and provision of cheap credit.
    *   Right to form trade unions and to strike.
*   *Note*: **Gandhi did not attend a single election meeting**.

---

## Congress’ Performance

*   **Enfranchisement Scale**: First election with expanded franchise → **30.1 million eligible** (14% of population), including **4.25 million women**.
*   **Turnout**: **15.5 million actually voted**, including **917,000 women**.
*   **Election Results**:
    *   Congress won **716 out of 1,161 seats** contested (total provincial assembly seats: 1,585).
    *   Won absolute majority in **6 provinces**: Madras, CP, Bihar, Orissa, UP, Bombay (near majority).
    *   Emerged as largest party in **Bengal, Assam, and NWFP** (subsequently formed ministries there too via coalitions).
    *   No majority only in: **Punjab** and **Sindh**.
*   **Strategic Outcome**: Prestige of Congress rose exponentially; **Nehru reconciled** to the dominant S-T-S strategy of the party.
