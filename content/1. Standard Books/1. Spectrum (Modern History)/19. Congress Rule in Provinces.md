### Ministries Formed
*   **Core Provinces** → **Bombay, Madras, Central Provinces, Orissa, United Provinces, Bihar**
*   **Later Coalition Additions** → **NWFP**, **Assam**

### Gandhi’s Advice
*   **Office Status** = **"Crowns of thorns"** accepted solely to hasten the nationalist goal.
*   **Manner of Use** → Hold offices **lightly, not tightly**; exploit them in ways **unanticipated/unintended by the British**.
*   **Core Objective** → Demonstrate that the Congress can govern with **minimal reliance on police and military forces**.

### Work Under Congress Ministries
*   **Duration** = **28 months** (1937–1939).
*   **Impact** → Released suppressed mass energy + elevated the **prestige and administrative authority of the Congress**.
*   **Limitations** → Unable to alter the **basic imperialist character** of the colonial administration or execute radical systemic changes.

### Civil Liberties
*   **Positive Achievements**:
    *   **Emergency power laws** → Repealed.
    *   **Bans lifted** → Revoked on illegal organizations (e.g., **Hindustan Seva Dal**, **Youth Leagues**), books, and journals.
    *   **Press restrictions** → Abolished; blacklisted newspapers reinstated.
    *   **Arms & licenses** → Confiscated weapons and revoked licenses restored.
    *   **Police powers** → Curbed; **CID surveillance** of political figures halted.
    *   **Political prisoners** → Released; internment and deportation orders cancelled.
    *   **Bombay Land Restoration** → Confiscated lands (seized during Civil Disobedience Movement) returned to original owners.
    *   **Official pensions** → Restored to government servants dismissed/suspended during the CDM.
*   **Blemishes / Repressive Slides**:
    *   **Yusuf Meherally** (Socialist leader) → Arrested in Madras for inflammatory speeches (later released).
    *   **S.S. Batliwala** (Socialist) → Arrested in Madras; sentenced to **6 months** for a seditious address.
    *   **K.M. Munshi** (Bombay Home Minister) → Deployed the **CID** to monitor leftists and communists.

### Agrarian Reforms
*   **Constraints Hindering Complete Zamindari Abolition**:
    1.  **Inadequate legislative powers** under the 1935 constitutional framework.
    2.  **Inadequate finance** → Imperial government appropriated the lion's share of provincial revenues.
    3.  **Class adjustment strategy** → Imperative to conciliate and neutralize landlord/zamindar factions.
    4.  **Time limits** → Structural logic of Congress was confrontation with colonialism, not prolonged administration.
    5.  **War shadow** (by 1938).
    6.  **Reactionary Second Chambers** (Legislative Councils) in **UP, Bihar, Bombay, Madras, Assam** → Dominated by wealthy landlords and capitalists whose assent was required for bills.
    7.  **Overly complex land tenure system**.
*   **Key Enacted Reforms**:
    *   **Legislation** passed regarding debt relief, land tenures, forest grazing fee reductions, rent arrears, and tenancy protections.
    *   **Primary beneficiaries** = **Statutory and occupancy tenants**.
    *   **Unreached groups** = **Sub-tenants** (minimal gains) & **agricultural laborers** (remained unbenefited due to lack of mobilization).

### Attitude Towards Labour
*   **Underlying Policy** → Promote worker welfare while enforcing **industrial peace** to limit active strikes.
*   **Mechanism** → Urged compulsory arbitration before established conciliation machinery prior to launching strikes.
*   **Law & Order Intervention** → Treated militant labor agitation as a public peace issue; enforced **Section 144** and arrested leaders.
*   **Bombay Trades Disputes Act, 1938** → Seen as an anti-labour measure; generated severe leftist criticism as Congress drew closer to industrial capitalists.
*   **Internal Response**:
    *   **Nehru** → Dissatisfied with provincial repressive actions but defended the ministries in public to prevent political division.
    *   **Gandhi** → Opposed violent methods but advocated popular political education; warned against excessive resort to colonial administrative machinery.

### Social Welfare Reforms
*   **Prohibition** → Enforced in selected zones.
*   **Harijan Upliftment** → Facilitated temple entry, access to public water/facilities, educational scholarships, and increased police/civil service quotas.
*   **Education** → Reconstructed primary, technical, and higher tiers; prioritized public health and sanitation.
*   **Khadi Support** → Promoted through financial subsidies.
*   **Prison reforms** → Undertaken to humanize jails.
*   **Indigenous Enterprise** → Extended administrative patronage to local industries.
*   **National Planning Committee** → Formed in **1938** under Congress President **Subhas Chandra Bose** to draft industrial policies.

### Extra-Parliamentary Mass Activity of Congress
*   **Mass literacy campaigns** → Implemented widely.
*   **Alternate dispute channels** → Created popular Panchayats and Congress police stations.
*   **Congress Grievance Committees** → Collected and presented mass petitions to local officials.
*   **States Peoples’ Movements** → Built close organizational linkages to struggle for democracy in Princely States.

### Evaluation
*   **Internal Decay (by 1939)** → Factionalism, opportunism, and hunger for office began to surface among local Congressmen.
*   **Key Historical Significance**:
    *   **Social Reform Premise** → Proved that complete Indian independence is prerequisite to deep social/agrarian transformation.
    *   **State Power Strategy** → Demonstrated that nationalist movements can use state apparatuses to gain leverage without being co-opted.
    *   **Communal Security** → Ministries successfully suppressed communal friction.
    *   **Imperial Bureaucracy** → Severely demoralized and weakened the authority of the colonial civil services.
    *   **Hostile Neutralization** → Co-opted and neutralized conservative landlord elements.
    *   **Self-Rule Demonstration** → Allowed the Indian public to preview democratic administration.
    *   **Rule Competency Myth** → Demolished the imperialist claim that Indians were administrative novices unfit for self-rule.
*   **Resignation and Post-1939 Dilemmas**:
    *   **October 1939 Resignation** → Triggered by Britain unilaterally dragging India into **World War II** without provincial consultation.
    *   **Princely States Dilemma** → Hard choice over whether to officially support the democratic **Praja Mandal** movements.
    *   **Pirpur Committee (1938)**:
        *   Formed by the **All India Muslim League** (led by Raja of Pirpur) to report on alleged atrocities.
        *   **Core Allegations** → Suppression of Urdu (in favor of Hindi), denial of political representation, economic oppression, and interference with Muslim religious practices.
        *   **Real-world impact** → Fueled communal estrangement and built the foundations for the two-nation theory.
