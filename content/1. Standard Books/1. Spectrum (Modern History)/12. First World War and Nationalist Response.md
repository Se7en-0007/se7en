### **First World War and Nationalist Response**
*   **First World War (1914–1919)**: **Allies** (**Britain**, **France**, **Russia**, **USA**, **Italy**, **Japan**) vs. **Central Powers** (**Germany**, **Austria-Hungary**, **Turkey**).
*   **Three-Fold Nationalist Response**:
    *   **Moderates** → Supported British empire as a matter of duty.
    *   **Extremists** (including **Tilak**, released **June 1914**) → Supported war efforts believing loyalty would be repaid with **self-government**.
    *   **Revolutionaries** → Saw war as opportunity to launch armed crusade to liberate India.
*   **Revolutionary Calculus**: War → British troops drained (**white soldiers $\downarrow$ 15,000** at one point) + potential financial/military aid from **Germany** & **Turkey** → Favourable window for armed revolt.
*   **Operational Arenas**: **Ghadr Party** (North America), **Berlin Committee** (Europe), and military mutinies (e.g., **Singapore Mutiny**).

### **Home Rule League Movement**
*   **Concept**: Indian response to WWI, structured on the lines of the **Irish Home Rule League**.
*   **Key Pioneers**: **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**, **Annie Besant**, **G.S. Khaparde**, **Sir S. Subramania Iyer**, **Joseph Baptista**, and **Muhammad Ali Jinnah**.
*   **Core Objective**: **Self-government / Home Rule** for all of India within the **British Commonwealth** via year-round political mobilization (contrasting the Congress's annual sessions).
*   **The Split Launch**: **Two separate leagues** formed due to friction between individual supporters of Tilak and Besant, though they strictly coordinated areas of operation.

---

### **Factors Leading to The Movement**
*   **Need for Popular Pressure** → Standard consensus that concessions require active mass agitation.
*   **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) Disillusionment** → Moderates realized the reforms offered no real representative power.
*   **Wartime Economic Burden** → High taxation + hyperinflation → Severe mass distress → Public ready for active protest.
*   **Demystification of White Superiority** → Intra-imperialist slaughter in Europe exposed the myth of racial/moral dominance.
*   **Tilak’s Strategic Realignment**: Released **June 1914** → Made conciliatory gestures to government (pledged loyalty, sought administrative reform, not overthrow) + reassured Moderates (admitted violence retarded political progress) → Ready to lead constitutional agitation.
*   **Annie Besant’s Initiative**: Irish theosophist (in India since **1896**) decided to expand her sphere from theosophy to active political mobilization for Home Rule.

---

### **The Leagues**
*   **Precursor Steps**:
    *   **1914 Congress Session**: Failed to achieve Moderate-Extremist rapprochement.
    *   **Early 1915**: Besant launched unilateral press campaign via ***New India*** and ***Commonweal***.
    *   **1915 Congress Session**: Extremists readmitted to INC; Congress agreed to educative propaganda but stalled Besant's league proposal.
    *   **Besant's Ultimatum**: If Congress failed to implement propaganda commits, she would set up her own league.
*   **Tilak’s League**:
    *   **Founded**: **April 1916** (first meeting at **Belgaum**).
    *   **Headquarters**: **Poona**.
    *   **Jurisdiction**: Restricted to **Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city)**, **Karnataka**, **Central Provinces**, and **Berar**.
    *   **Structure**: Highly cohesive, organized into **6 branches**.
    *   **Primary Demands**: **Swarajya**, formation of **linguistic states**, and **education in the vernacular**.
*   **Besant’s League**:
    *   **Founded**: **September 1916**.
    *   **Headquarters**: **Madras**.
    *   **Jurisdiction**: Covered **rest of India (including Bombay city)**.
    *   **Structure**: Loosely organized, composed of **200 branches**.
    *   **Key Executives**: **George Arundale** (Organising Secretary), **B.W. Wadia**, and **C.P. Ramaswamy Aiyar**.

---

### **The Home Rule League Programme**
*   **Objective**: Popularize the concept of **Home Rule = Self-Government** among the common masses.
*   **Geographic Expansion**: Attracted hitherto politically dormant regions of **Gujarat** and **Sindh**.
*   **Tactics**: Political discussions, public libraries, political classes for students, pamphlets, posters, illustrated postcards, plays, religious songs, fund collections, and local government participation.
*   **External Catalyst**: **Russian Revolution (1917)** infused fresh anti-imperialist zeal into the campaign.
*   **Prominent Adherents**: **Motilal Nehru**, **Jawaharlal Nehru**, **Bhulabhai Desai**, **Chittaranjan Das**, **K.M. Munshi**, **B. Chakravarti**, **Saifuddin Kitchlew**, **Madan Mohan Malaviya**, **Muhammad Ali Jinnah** (headed Bombay division), **Tej Bahadur Sapru**, and **Lala Lajpat Rai**.
*   **Partial Servants of India Society Enrolment** → Members joined despite Gokhale's traditional caution.
*   **Non-Participants**: **Anglo-Indians**, major **Muslim** blocks, and **non-Brahmins of the South** → Feared Home Rule would result in high-caste Hindu majority domination.

---

### **Government Attitude**
*   **Repressive Measures**: Government banned students from attending political meetings (especially in **Madras**).
*   **Sanctions on Tilak**: Banned from entering **Punjab** and **Delhi**; high court case initiated (later rescinded).
*   **June 1917 Arrests**: Internment of **Annie Besant**, **B.P. Wadia**, and **George Arundale**.
*   **Nationalist Outrage**:
    *   **Sir S. Subramania Aiyar** renounced his **knighthood**.
    *   Tilak advocated **passive resistance**.
*   **Montagu’s Critique**: *"Shiva ...cut his wife into fifty-two pieces only to discover that he had fifty-two wives. This is what happens to the Government of India when it interns Mrs Besant."*
*   **De-escalation**: Besant released in **September 1917**.

---

### **Why The Agitation Faded Out by 1919**
*   **Organizational Fragility** → Lack of centralized, disciplined structure.
*   **Communal Disturbances** → **1917–1918 communal riots** fractured mass unity.
*   **Moderate Pacification** → **Montagu’s August 1917 statement** (promising self-government as a goal) + Besant’s release satisfied Moderate leaders.
*   **Ideological Rifts** → Extremist calls for **passive resistance** alienated Moderates after **September 1918**.
*   **Reform Division** → Announcement of **Montagu-Chelmsford reforms (July 1918)** split nationalist ranks; Besant vacillated over response and passive resistance.
*   **Tilak’s Absence**: Departed to England (**September 1918**) for libel case against **Valentine Chirol** (author of ***Indian Unrest***, which branded Tilak as the father of Indian unrest) → Movement left leaderless.
*   **The Gandhian Transition** → **Gandhi’s** novel satyagraha style captured public imagination, sidelining the Home Rule movement.
*   **1920 Merger**: **Gandhi** accepted Presidentship of All India Home Rule League → Renamed it **Swarajya Sabha** → Merged into the **Indian National Congress** within a year.

---

### **Positive Gains**
*   **Elite → Masses**: Permanently shifted the focus of national struggle from educated elites to the common masses.
*   **Town-Country Link**: Established essential organizational channels between rural and urban centers.
*   **New Cadres**: Created a highly motivated, politically conscious generation of nationalists.
*   **Political Training**: Laid the mass psychological foundation for **Gandhian-style mass satyagraha**.
*   **Policy Impact** → Directly pressured Britain into issuing the **August 1917 Declaration** and formulating the **Montford reforms**.
*   **Congress Revitalization** → Facilitated the reunion of Moderates and Extremists at the **Lucknow Congress (1916)**.

### **Lucknow Session Of The Indian National Congress (1916)**

#### **Readmission of Extremists To Congress**
*   **President**: **Ambika Charan Majumdar** (Moderate).
*   **Reunion Factors**:
    *   Old factional controversies had lost relevance over time.
    *   Realization that the **1907 Surat Split** had induced political paralysis.
    *   Vigorous mediation by **Annie Besant** and **Tilak** (Tilak declared support for administrative reform, not overthrow; denounced violence).
    *   Death of **Pherozeshah Mehta** (the chief Moderate opponent of Extremist readmission) cleared obstacles.

#### **Lucknow Pact Between Congress and Muslim League**
*   **Concept**: Coming together of Congress and Muslim League to present joint constitutional demands.
*   **Factors Behind the League’s Anti-Imperialist Shift**:
    *   **Balkan Wars (1912–13)** & **Italo-Turkish War (1911)** → Britain's refusal to assist **Turkey** (ruled by the **Khalifa**, religio-political head of global Muslims) angered Indian Muslims.
    *   **Annulment of Partition of Bengal (1911)** → Disillusioned pro-partition elite Muslims.
    *   **Aligarh University Dispute** → British refused to grant the university affiliation rights over colleges nationwide.
    *   **Rise of the Young Party** → Shift from Aligarh school's loyalist politics to bold nationalism (**1912 Calcutta session**: committed to self-government provided it safeguarded Muslim interests).
    *   **Wartime Repression** → Suppression of Maulana Azad's ***Al Hilal*** and Mohammad Ali's ***Comrade*** + internment of **Ali brothers (Mohammad & Shaukat)**, **Maulana Azad**, and **Hasrat Mohani** → Ignited intense anti-imperialist sentiments.

#### **The Nature of The Pact**
*   **Constitutional Compromise**: League agreed to present joint demands; **Congress accepted the League's position on separate electorates** (until a community demanded joint electorates).
*   **Legislative Quotas**: Muslims granted fixed seat proportions at central and provincial levels.
*   **Joint Demands to the Government**:
    1. Early British declaration promising **self-government** at an early date.
    2. Expansion of central and provincial legislative councils with an **elected majority** and enhanced powers.
    3. Term of the Legislative Council fixed at **5 years**.
    4. **Secretary of State for India** to be paid from the **British Treasury** (not from Indian revenues).
    5. **Half the members** of the Viceroy’s and Governors' Executive Councils to be **Indians**.
*   **A.C. Majumdar’s View**: *"After nearly ten years of painful separation... both wings of Indian Nationalist Party have come to realise the fact that united they stand, but divided they fall."*

#### **Critical Comments**
*   **Constitutional Deadlock Risk**: Executive not responsible to the legislature (legislature could not remove the executive, yet controlled the budget) → Designed for deadlock.
*   **Morley-Minto Extension**: Demands were merely an expanded version of the **1909 Morley-Minto Reforms**.
*   **Institutionalization of Two-Nation Theory**: Acceptance of separate electorates meant Congress and League united as separate political entities, reinforcing communal identity rather than integrating.
*   **Leaders' Pact, Not Masses' Union** → Completely bypassed communal integration at the grassroots/mass level.
*   **Desperate Conciliation**: Represented a serious, albeit flawed, attempt by Congress to allay minority fears of majority rule.

### **Montagu’s Statement Of August 1917**
*   **The August Declaration (August 20, 1917)**: Declared by Secretary of State **Edwin Samuel Montagu** in the British House of Commons.
*   **Core Objective**: *"increasing participation of Indians in every branch of administration and gradual development of self-governing institutions with a view to the progressive realisation of responsible government in India as an integral part of the British Empire."*
*   **Significance**:
    *   **Self-government** became official British policy → Demands could no longer be labeled "seditious" (reversing Morley's 1909 stand).
    *   **"Responsible government"** concept implied British rulers were answerable to elected representatives in India, not just London.
*   **The Catch**: British had no intention of actual transfer of power to elected Indian majorities → Concocted the concept of **"dyarchy"** to divide executive authority and maintain ultimate control.

---

### **Indian Objections**
*   **No Specific Time Frame**: Absence of a concrete timeline for self-government.
*   **Unilateral Decision Power**: British retained sole authority to judge the nature and timing of India’s advance towards responsible government → Highly resented by Indian nationalists.
