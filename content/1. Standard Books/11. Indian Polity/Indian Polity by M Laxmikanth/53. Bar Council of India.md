## ESTABLISHMENT
* **Statutory Status:** Statutory (non-constitutional) autonomous body established under **Advocates Act, 1961**
* **Historical Origins:**
  * **1951:** Government of India appointed **All India Bar Committee** under **Justice S.R. Das** (Das Committee) to examine re-organisation of Bar and legal profession
  * **1953:** Das Committee submitted report → Recommended establishment of an **All India Bar Council** and **State Bar Councils (SBCs)**
  * **1958:** **Law Commission of India** (**14th Report** on Reform of Judicial Administration) endorsed Das Committee recommendations
  * **1961:** Parliament enacted **Advocates Act, 1961** → Created Bar Council of India (BCI) and State Bar Councils (SBCs)
* **Administrative Ministry:** Functions under **Department of Legal Affairs**, Union **Ministry of Law and Justice**

---

## COMPOSITION
* **Member Types:** Consists of elected members + ex-officio members
* **Elected Members:** **1 member** elected by each State Bar Council from amongst its members
* **Ex-Officio Members (2):**
  1. **Attorney-General of India**
  2. **Solicitor-General of India**
* **Leadership (Chairman & Vice-Chairman):**
  * Elected by BCI from amongst its members
  * **Tenure:** **2 years**
* **Term of Elected Members:** Co-terminus with their term as a member of respective State Bar Council
* **Committees of BCI:**
  1. Disciplinary Committee (one or more)
  2. Legal Aid Committee (one or more)
  3. Executive Committee
  4. Legal Education Committee
  5. Other Committees (as necessary)

---

## FUNCTIONS
* **15 Statutory Functions (Advocates Act, 1961):**
  1. **Professional Ethics:** Lay down standards of professional conduct & etiquette for advocates
  2. **Disciplinary Procedure:** Lay down procedure for disciplinary committees of BCI and SBCs
  3. **Rights & Privileges:** Safeguard rights, privileges and interests of advocates
  4. **Law Reform:** Promote and support law reform
  5. **SBC Disputes:** Deal with and dispose of matters referred by a State Bar Council
  6. **Supervision:** Exercise general supervision and control over State Bar Councils
  7. **Legal Education Standards:** Promote legal education & lay down standards in consultation with Universities and SBCs
  8. **University Recognition & Inspection:** Recognise law degrees for advocate enrolment; visit and inspect universities
  9. **Seminars & Journals:** Conduct seminars, organize legal talks by eminent jurists, publish legal journals/papers
  10. **Legal Aid:** Organise legal aid to the poor
  11. **Foreign Qualifications:** Recognise foreign law qualifications on reciprocal basis for admission as advocate
  12. **Fund Management:** Manage and invest BCI funds
  13. **Elections:** Provide for election of its members
  14. **Conferred Functions:** Perform all other functions conferred under Advocates Act, 1961
  15. **Incidental Powers:** Do all things necessary for discharging above functions
* **Funds Established by BCI:**
  * Financial assistance for welfare schemes (indigent/disabled advocates)
  * Legal aid or advice
  * Establishing law libraries
  * *Sources:* Grants, donations, gifts and benefactions
* **International Affiliation:** Empowered to become member of international legal bodies (**International Bar Association**, **International Legal Aid Association**)

## STATE BAR COUNCILS
* **Statutory Basis:** Envisaged under Advocates Act, 1961 for each state, or a common SBC for 2+ states / state and UT
* **Total SBCs:** Currently **24 State Bar Councils** across India

### 1. Composition
* **Elected Members (Based on Electorate Size):**
  * Electorate ≤ **5,000 advocates:** **15 members**
  * Electorate **5,000 to 10,000 advocates:** **20 members**
  * Electorate > **10,000 advocates:** **25 members**
  * *Electoral System:* Proportional representation by single transferable vote
  * *Qualification Constraint:* At least **1/2 (50%)** of elected members must have **≥10 years standing** on state roll
* **Ex-Officio Members:**
  * **Advocate-General of the State** (or Advocate-General of each state for common SBC)
  * **Delhi SBC Exception:** **Additional Solicitor-General of India** is ex-officio member
* **Leadership:** Chairman and Vice-Chairman elected by Council from amongst its members
* **Tenure:** **5 years** (*BCI can extend term by **6 months** if elections not held before expiry*)
* **Committees:** Disciplinary, Legal Aid, Executive, Enrolment and Other Committees

### 2. Functions
1. Admit persons as advocates on state roll & maintain roll
2. Entertain and determine cases of misconduct against advocates
3. Safeguard rights, privileges and interests of advocates
4. Promote Bar Associations for implementing advocate welfare schemes
5. Promote and support law reform
6. Conduct seminars, talks, publish legal journals/papers
7. Organise legal aid to the poor
8. Manage and invest SBC funds
9. Provide for election of its members
10. Visit and inspect universities per BCI directions
11. Perform other statutory functions under Advocates Act, 1961
* **SBC Funds:** Financial assistance for indigent/disabled advocates, legal aid/advice, law libraries

## TYPES OF ADVOCATES

### Categories Under Advocates Act, 1961
* **2 Primary Categories:** **Senior Advocates** and **Other Advocates**
* **Designation Authority:** Supreme Court or High Courts designate an advocate as Senior Advocate with their consent based on ability, standing at Bar, or special knowledge/experience in law

### Categories Entitled to Practise in Supreme Court (3 Types)

#### 1. Senior Advocate
* **Definition:** Designated under Advocates Act, 1961 / Supreme Court Rules, 2013, or borne on senior advocate roll prior to 1961 Act
* **Inclusion:** Retired Chief Justices and Judges of High Courts are also considered for designation
* **Procedural Constraint:** **NOT entitled** to appear in Supreme Court **without an Advocate-on-Record**
* **Permanent Committee for Designation (SC):**
  * **Chairperson:** Chief Justice of India
  * **Members:** 2 seniormost Supreme Court Judges + Attorney General for India + 1 Bar member nominated by Committee

#### 2. Advocate-on-Record (AOR)
* **Definition:** Entitled under **Supreme Court Rules, 2013** to **act as well as plead** for a party in Supreme Court
* **Exclusive Right:** No advocate other than an AOR is entitled to file appearance or act for a party in Supreme Court
* **Evolution:**
  * **1950:** Supreme Court inherited Federal Court / Privy Council system of **“Agents”**
  * **1954:** System modified → **“Advocates-on-Record”** replaced “Agents” (advocates with 7 years standing registered)
  * **1959:** **Advocate-on-Record Examination** introduced
* **Exam Eligibility:** Conducted periodically by SC Registry; candidate must undergo **1 year continuous training** under an AOR of **≥10 years standing**, commencing after completion of **4 years** from enrolment date (total **≥5 years** post-enrolment)

#### 3. Other Advocates
* **Definition:** Advocates whose names are entered on roll of any State Bar Council
* **SC Practice Restriction:** Cannot appear, plead and address Supreme Court in any matter **unless instructed by an Advocate-on-Record** or permitted by Court
