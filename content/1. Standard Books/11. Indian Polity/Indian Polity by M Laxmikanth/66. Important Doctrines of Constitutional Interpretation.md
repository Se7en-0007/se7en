## DOCTRINE OF SEVERABILITY

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Alternative Name:** Also known as **doctrine of separability**
* **Purpose:** Devised by Supreme Court to resolve validity of laws declared unconstitutional
* **Core Rule:** Invalid portion separable from valid portion → **ONLY invalid portion declared void**, rest remains valid & operative
* **Inseparable Exception:** Valid and invalid provisions inextricably linked → **Entire law declared void**

---

### Basis of The Doctrine
* **Constitutional Anchor:** **Article 13**
  * **Article 13(1):** Pre-constitution laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights → Void **to the extent of inconsistency**
  * **Article 13(2):** Post-constitution laws abridging Fundamental Rights → Void **to the extent of contravention**
* **SC Principle:** Answer depends on whether valid part can be separated from invalid part upon considering provisions of the Act

---

### Propositions of The Doctrine
* **7 SC Rules on Separability:**
  1. **Legislative Intention:** Determining factor is whether legislature would have enacted valid part alone had it known rest was invalid (determined via legislative history, object, title, and preamble)
  2. **Inextricably Mixed vs. Distinct:** Inextricably mixed → Entire Act void; Distinct & complete independent code → Valid part upheld
  3. **Single Scheme:** Valid provisions distinct but part of single overall scheme → Entire Act fails
  4. **Truncated Statute:** Valid parts remaining after expunging invalid ones so thin/truncated as to alter original substance → Rejected in entirety
  5. **Substance over Form:** Separability depends on substance and overall examination of Act, NOT whether enacted in same or different section
  6. **Judicial Legislation Bar:** If enforcing remaining statute requires judicial alterations/modifications → Entire Act struck down as void
  7. **Aids to Construction:** Legitimate to examine legislative history, object, title, and preamble to ascertain separability intent

---

### Important Cases

#### Important Cases Relating to The Doctrine of Severability

| Sl. No. | Case (Year) | Supreme Court Judgement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.** | **A.K. Gopalan vs. State of Madras (1950)** | Struck down **Section 14 of Preventive Detention Act (1950)** for violating **Article 22**; upheld rest of Act as valid and effective |
| **2.** | **State of Bombay vs. F.N. Balsara (1951)** | Declared **8 sections of Bombay Prohibition Act (1949)** ultra-vires; held void provisions did not affect validity of entire Act |
| **3.** | **R.M.D. Chamarbaugwalla vs. Union of India (1957)** | Struck down gambling-related provisions of **Prize Competitions Act (1955)** under **Section 2(d)** while upholding skill-based competitions under **Article 19(1)(g)** |
| **4.** | **Minerva Mills vs. Union of India (1980)** | Struck down **Sections 4 and 55 of 42nd Amendment Act (1976)** as beyond amending power; declared rest of Act valid |
| **5.** | **Kihoto Hollohan vs. Zachillhu (1992)** (*Defection Case*) | Declared **Paragraph 7 of Tenth Schedule** (*inserted by 52nd Amendment 1985*) unconstitutional for violating **Article 368(2)**; upheld rest of Tenth Schedule |

## DOCTRINE OF WAIVER

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Definition:** Voluntary and intentional relinquishment/abandonment of a known legal right, advantage, claim, or privilege with full knowledge
* **USA vs. India Comparison:**
  * **USA:** Doctrine of waiver **applies** to fundamental rights (*American citizen can waive fundamental rights*)
  * **India:** Doctrine of waiver **DOES NOT apply** to Fundamental Rights (*citizen cannot waive fundamental rights; **doctrine of non-waiver***)

---

### Position In India
* **6 SC Observations on Non-Applicability:**
  1. Fundamental Rights are mandatory obligations on State to achieve Preamble objectives; citizen cannot relieve State of this constitutional duty
  2. American judicial doctrine of waiver cannot be imported into Indian Constitution
  3. Enacted as a matter of **public policy** and constitutional policy, not merely for private individual benefit
  4. Examples (**Articles 15(1), 20, 21**): Citizen cannot permit State to discriminate or invite conviction by waiving protections
  5. No distinction in India between rights for individual benefit vs. public interest/policy
  6. Sacred duty of SC in a nascent democracy to safeguard Fundamental Rights against self-relinquishment

---

### Important Cases

#### Important Cases Relating to The Doctrine of Waiver

| Sl. No. | Case (Year) | Supreme Court Judgement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.** | **Behram Khurshid Pesikaka vs. State of Bombay (1954)** | Held accused in criminal prosecution cannot waive fundamental rights to get convicted |
| **2.** | **Basheshar Nath vs. Commissioner of Income Tax (1958)** | Rejected contention that voluntary agreement to pay tax waived fundamental right under **Article 14**; affirmed FRs cannot be waived |
| **3.** | **Olga Tellis vs. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985)** (*Pavement Dwellers Case*) | Held person cannot waive fundamental rights by any act; **no estoppel against the Constitution** |
| **4.** | **Nar Singh Pal vs. Union of India (2000)** | Reiterated fundamental rights cannot be bartered away, compromised, or subject to estoppel |

## DOCTRINE OF ECLIPSE

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Origin:** Emerged from prospective nature of **Article 13(1)** (*pre-constitution laws*)
* **Core Principle:** Pre-constitutional law inconsistent with Fundamental Right is **NOT a nullity or void ab initio**; becomes dormant/inoperative (*eclipsed/overshadowed*)
* **Status of Eclipsed Law:**
  1. Continues to exist for past transactions
  2. Enforceable for rights/liabilities accrued before Jan 26, 1950
  3. Operative against **non-citizens** who do not enjoy certain Fundamental Rights
* **Removal of Eclipse:** Constitutional amendment removing inconsistency removes shadow → Law becomes constitutional and enforceable again without fresh re-enactment

---

### Formulation of The Doctrine
* **Enunciating Case:** ***Bhikaji Narain Dhakras vs. State of M.P. (1955)***
* **Causal Chain:** **Sec 43 Motor Vehicles Act (1939)** amended by CP & Berar Act 1947 (*state transport monopoly*) → Eclipsed on Jan 26, 1950 by **Article 19(1)(g)** → **1st Amendment Act (1951)** amended **Article 19(6)** permitting state monopoly → Shadow removed → Sec 43 validated automatically

---

### Application to Post-Constitutional Laws
* ***Deep Chand Case (1959) & Mahendra Lal Jain Case (1962):*** Held doctrine of eclipse applies **ONLY to pre-constitution laws (Art 13(1))**, NOT post-constitution laws (**Art 13(2)** = still-born/dead from inception)
* ***Ambica Mills Case (1974) Shift:*** Overruled absolute nullity stand; applied doctrine of eclipse to post-constitution laws violating **Article 19** regarding **non-citizens** (*Bombay Labour Welfare Fund Act 1953 valid for non-citizens*)
* ***Dulare Lodh Case (1984):*** Applied doctrine of eclipse to post-constitution law even against citizens

## DOCTRINE OF TERRITORIAL NEXUS

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Constitutional Basis:** **Article 245**
  * **Parliament:** Can make laws for whole/any part of India + **extra-territorial legislation** (*applies to Indian citizens & property worldwide*)
  * **State Legislature:** Can make laws ONLY for whole or part of State (*no extra-territorial power generally*)
* **Exception Rule:** State extra-territorial law is **valid IF real and sufficient nexus** exists between State and subject-matter/object of law
* **2 Essential Elements:**
  1. Nexus must be **real and not illusory**
  2. Liability imposed must be **relevant to that nexus**

---

### Formulation of The Doctrine
* **Pre-Constitution Cases:** Applied by Federal Court in ***Raleigh (1944)*** & ***Wadia (1949)*** and Privy Council in ***Wallace (1948)*** (*income-tax laws*)
* **Post-Constitution Tax Cases:**
  * ***R.M.D. Chamarbaugwalla (1957):*** Upheld tax on gambling where newspaper printed in Bangalore had major circulation & prize competitions in Bombay
  * ***TISCO Case (1958):*** Applied territorial nexus to sales-tax law
  * ***NTPC Case (2002):*** Reaffirmed extra-territorial State law sustainable ONLY on real and sufficient territorial nexus

---

### Application to Non-Tax Laws
* ***Charusila Dasi Case (1959):*** Upheld Bihar trust law governing religious trust situated in Bihar even though part of trust property lay in West Bengal
* ***Shrikant Case (1994):*** Upheld **Gujarat Agricultural Land Ceiling Act (1960)** taking into account land held outside Gujarat to determine state land ceiling

---

### Exceptions to The Doctrine
* **2 Scenarios where Territorial Nexus does NOT apply:**
  1. Approval of inter-state transport schemes under Central Motor Vehicles Act (***Khazan Singh Case 1973*** - UP nationalised routes extending into Rajasthan)
  2. State carrying on trade/business under **Article 298** (*not restricted to state boundaries*)

---

## DOCTRINE OF PITH AND SUBSTANCE

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Constitutional Basis:** **Article 246** (Division of powers across Union, State, and Concurrent Lists in **Seventh Schedule**)
* **Core Rule:** Examine true nature and character (*pith and substance*) of law as an organic whole
* **Validation Principle:** If true substance falls within enacting legislature's competence → Law is **valid even if it incidentally encroaches** on subjects assigned to another legislature
* **Assessment Criteria:** (a) Enactment as a whole, (b) Main objects, (c) Scope and effect of provisions

---

### Rationale of The Doctrine
* Originates from Privy Council Canadian/Australian federal appeals
* Recognises that in elaborate constitutional enumeration of fields, **some overlapping of legislative fields is inevitable**

---

### Principles of The Doctrine
* **Benchmark Case:** ***Prafulla Kumar Mukherjee vs. Bank of Commerce (1947)*** (*Bengal Money Lenders Act 1946 vs. central "promissory notes"*)
* **3 Principles Laid Down:**
  1. Overlapping powers inevitable; determine true nature/character and which list it belongs to in substance
  2. Degree/extent of invasion into another list examined to determine true pith and substance
  3. Where lists conflict, Union List priority does NOT prevent State from passing law that merely *incidentally affects* a Union item

---

### Important Cases

#### Important Cases Relating to The Doctrine of Pith and Substance

| Sl. No. | Case (Year) | Supreme Court Judgement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.** | **State of Bombay vs. F.N. Balsara (1951)** | Upheld **Bombay Prohibition Act (1950)** despite incidental encroachment on import/export of liquor (Union List) |
| **2.** | **D.N. Banerji vs. P.R. Mukherjee (1952)** | Upheld Central **Industrial Disputes Act** applying to municipalities despite "local government" being State List item |
| **3.** | **State of Rajasthan vs. G. Chawla (1958)** | Upheld State Act restricting sound amplifiers under "public health" despite incidental touch on "broadcasting/communication" (Union List) |
| **4.** | **State of Gujarat vs. Shantilal Mangaldas (1969)** | Held doctrine applies to legislative competence, NOT for determining fundamental rights infringement |
| **5.** | **M. Ismail Faruqui vs. Union of India (1994)** | Upheld **Acquisition of Certain Area at Ayodhya Act (1993)** as 'acquisition of property' (Concurrent List), not 'public order' (State List) |
| **6.** | **Zameer Ahmed Latifur Rehman Sheikh vs. State of Maharashtra (2010)** | Upheld **MCOCA (1999)** against challenge of encroaching on Union List items |

## DOCTRINE OF COLOURABLE LEGISLATION

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Maxims:** *“Quando aliquid prohibetur ex directo, prohibetur et per obliquum”* (**“You cannot do indirectly, what you cannot do directly”**)
* **Core Definition:** Legislature lacks competence on a subject, but clothes/disguises the law in a form that appears within competence → Law is a **colourable device and void**
* **Trigger:** Absence of legislative power under Seventh Schedule entries OR express constitutional prohibitions (e.g. Part III Fundamental Rights)

---

### Propositions of The Doctrine
* **6 SC Propositions:**
  1. Transgression of constitutional limits may be direct/patent OR disguised/covert/indirect (*colourable*)
  2. Form/outward appearance is immaterial; **substance of the Act** determines validity
  3. Enquiry must determine true nature, character, object, and effect of challenged legislation
  4. **Motive/Bona fides/Mala fides is IRRELEVANT**; question is strictly one of **legislative competency/power**
  5. Courts must look beyond language to obvious purpose and effect
  6. Whole scheme of related Acts/Bills can be examined together to uncover disguised evasion

---

### Doctrine of Fraud on The Constitution
* Covert non-compliance with constitutional limitations = **“Fraud on the Constitution”** (*epigrammatic expression for covert unconstitutionality*)

---

### Doctrine of Fraud on Legislative Power
* **Fraud on Legislative Power:** Legislature possesses power but pretends to exercise it without actual execution (*mere pretence of law*)
* **Fraud on the Constitution:** Legislature **lacks power** due to constitutional prohibition but enacts law under pretence of power

---

### Important Cases

#### Important Cases Relating to The Doctrine of Colourable Legislation

| Sl. No. | Case (Year) | Supreme Court Judgement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.** | **State of Bihar vs. Kameshwar Singh (1952)** | Invalidated **Bihar Land Reforms Act (1950)**; held compensation provisions were a mere pretence/colourable device |
| **2.** | **K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo vs. State of Orissa (1953)** | Upheld **Orissa Agricultural Income Tax (Amendment) Act (1950)** as within State competence (*not colourable*) |
| **3.** | **K.T. Moopil Nair vs. State of Kerala (1960)** | Declared **Travancore-Cochin Land Tax Act (1955)** void for violating **Articles 14 & 19(1)(f)** (*confiscatory in nature*) |
| **4.** | **M.R. Balaji vs. State of Mysore (1962)** | Declared executive order reserving **68% seats** in educational institutions void as a **fraud on Article 15(4)** |

## DOCTRINE OF IMPLIED POWERS

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Alternative Name:** **Doctrine of implication**
* **Maxim:** *“Cuicunque aliquid conceditur, conceditur et id sine quo res ipsa esse non potest”* (**Whoever grants a thing is deemed also to grant that without which the grant itself would be of no effect**)
* **Definition:** Un-enumerated power assumed to exist because indispensable/necessary to carry out an express grant/duty

---

### Scope of The Doctrine
* Invoked when explicit statutory duty/power cannot be performed/exercised at all without assuming existence of an auxiliary or subsidiary power (*general impossibility rendering statute dead-letter*)

---

### Application of The Doctrine
* ***Gopal Chandra Misra Case (1978):*** High Court Judge has implied power under **Article 217** to revoke resignation before effective date
* ***Rupa Ashok Hurra Case (2002):*** SC has inherent/implied power under **Article 137** to entertain **Curative Petitions** to prevent gross miscarriage of justice
* ***Raja Ram Pal Case (2007):*** Parliament has implied power under **Article 105** to expel members for contempt
* ***Salil Sabhlok Case (2013):*** Governor’s power to appoint SPSC members (**Article 316**) implies power to lay down appointment procedures

---

### Doctrine of Implied Prohibition
* **Maxim:** *“Expressio unius est exclusio alterius”* (**Express mention of one thing implies exclusion of another**)
* **Application:** Applied in **USA & Australia** (*residuary powers left to States → implied prohibition on Centre*); **NOT applicable in India** (*powers of Union and States fully enumerated; residuary powers vested in Parliament*)

---

## DOCTRINE OF INCIDENTAL AND ANCILLARY POWERS

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* Express legislative power in Seventh Schedule includes all incidental/ancillary powers necessary for effective execution
* **Global Terminology:** **“Necessary and Proper Powers”** (USA); **“Powers Incidental to Execution”** (Australia)
* **Wider Construction Examples:**
  * Power to levy tax → Includes power to check tax evasion
  * Power to collect rent → Includes power to remit rent
* **Limitations:** Cannot extend to confiscation of goods OR comprehend subjects explicitly listed as separate entries elsewhere (e.g. Entry 34 "betting" != Entry 62 "tax on betting")

---

### Rationale of The Doctrine
* Relieves statute from unconstitutionality where law in pith and substance is within competence and incidentally trenches on another field

---

### Important Cases

#### Important Cases Relating to The Doctrine of Incidental and Ancillary Powers

| Sl. No. | Case (Year) | Supreme Court Judgement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.** | **Chaturbhuj vs. Union of India (1959)** | Held power to impose tax includes power to raise revenue via licence fees |
| **2.** | **Rai Ramkrishna vs. State of Bihar (1963)** | Held power to legislate includes power to enact retrospective validating laws |
| **3.** | **Pathumma vs. State of Kerala (1978)** | Held agricultural debt relief power includes restoring property sold in decree execution |
| **4.** | **State of Haryana vs. Sant Lal (1993)** | Held sales tax levy power cannot be stretched to cover mere transporters of goods |
| **5.** | **Godfrey Phillips India Ltd. vs. State of U.P. (2005)** | Held tax power includes all events concerning that tax unless listed elsewhere |

## DOCTRINE OF PRECEDENT

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Alternative Name:** **Doctrine of stare decisis**
* **Maxim:** *“Stare decisis et non quieta movere”* (**“To stand by decisions and not to disturb what is settled”**)
* **Core Rule:** Lower courts strictly bound by decisions of higher courts to ensure judicial consistency and certainty

---

### Basis of The Doctrine
* **Constitutional Anchor:** **Article 141** (*Law declared by Supreme Court binding on all courts within India*)
* **SC Ruling:** High Court order refusing to follow SC decision or reviving set-aside High Court ruling is a **nullity** and judicial impropriety

---

### Rationale of The Doctrine
* Hierarchical judicial system requires lower tiers to accept higher tier rulings loyally
* Consistency creates public confidence; prevents judicial anarchy

---

## DOCTRINE OF OCCUPIED FIELD

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Alternative Name:** **Doctrine of covered field**
* **Core Rule:** Parliament enacts exhaustive law occupying a field in **Concurrent List (List III)** → State Legislature barred from making law in that occupied field
* **Constitutional Origin:** **Article 254** (*Repugnancy*)

---

### Doctrine of Repugnancy
* **Article 254(1):** Parliamentary law prevails over State law in Concurrent List to extent of repugnancy
* **3 Repugnancy Principles:** (1) Direct conflict, (2) Intended exhaustive code, (3) Occupied field
* **Article 254(2) Exception:** State law on Concurrent List receiving **President's Assent** prevails in that State (*Parliament can subsequently override it*)

---

### Repugnancy Outside The Concurrent List
* Overlapping between List II and List I/III resolved via **non-obstante clause in Article 246(1)** (*Union power predominates*)

---

## DOCTRINE OF PROSPECTIVE OVERRULING

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Origin:** American doctrine; introduced in India in ***Golak Nath Case (1967)***
* **Core Rule:** Overruling of old precedent operates **ONLY from future date**; past transactions/actions remain valid and undisturbed

---

### Formulation of The Doctrine
* Reconciles Blackstonian theory (*Judges discover law*) with pragmatic need to correct judicial error without causing past chaos

---

### Basis of The Doctrine
* Constitutional support derived from **Articles 32, 141, and 142** (*wide powers to declare law and do complete justice*)

---

### Propositions of The Doctrine
* **3 Rules Laid Down in Golak Nath:**
  1. Invoked ONLY in constitutional matters
  2. Applied ONLY by Supreme Court
  3. Scope of retroactive restriction left to SC discretion

---

### Rationale of The Doctrine
* Avoids reopening settled issues, prevents multiplicity of proceedings, and maintains legal stability

---

### Criticism of The Doctrine
* Objections (judicial legislation, obiter, lack of benefit to appellant) rejected by SC as surmountable in interest of justice

---

### Continuation of The Doctrine
* Upheld in ***Kesavananda Bharati (1973)***, ***Indra Sawhney / Mandal Case (1992)*** (postponed operation for 5 years), and ***Karunakar Case (1993)***; extended to ordinary statutes

---

## DOCTRINE OF HARMONIOUS CONSTRUCTION

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Alternative Name:** **Rule of avoidance of conflict**
* **Core Rule:** In case of conflict between constitutional provisions, interpret them harmoniously so effect is given to both and neither is rendered nugatory/dead letter

---

### Application of The Doctrine
* **6 Major Areas of Application:**
  1. FRs vs. DPSPs (*Kesavananda Bharati 1973 - complementary tools for social revolution*)
  2. Between different Fundamental Rights
  3. FRs vs. Legislative Privileges
  4. FRs vs. Amendment Procedure
  5. FRs vs. Other constitutional parts
  6. Between entries in Seventh Schedule lists

---

### Important Cases

#### Important Cases Relating to The Doctrine of Harmonious Construction

| Sl. No. | Case (Year) | Supreme Court Judgement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.** | **Sri Venkataramana Devaru vs. State of Mysore (1957)** | Reconciled denomination temple management under **Article 26(b)** with social reform under **Article 25(2)(b)** |
| **2.** | **M.S.M. Sharma vs. Krishna Sinha (1958)** | Read speech freedom under **Article 19(1)(a)** subject to legislative privileges under **Article 194(3)** |
| **3.** | **O.N. Mohindroo vs. Bar Council of Delhi (1968)** | Parliament exclusively regulates SC/HC practitioners; rest falls in Concurrent List |
| **4.** | **D.A.V. College vs. State of Punjab (1971)** | State cannot prescribe medium of instruction interfering with Union higher education powers |
| **5.** | **Kesavananda Bharati vs. State of Kerala (1973)** | Reconciled 'law' in **Article 13(2)** to mean ordinary laws, excluding **Article 368** amendments |

## DOCTRINE OF LIBERAL INTERPRETATION

### Meaning of The Doctrine
* **Core Rule:** Constitution is a living, organic instrument and machinery for making laws; must be interpreted broadly, liberally, and in widest amplitude (*not narrow or pedantic*)
* Applies strictly to general words in Seventh Schedule entries to comprehend subsidiary/ancillary matters

---

### Doctrine of Literal Interpretation
* **Alternative Name:** **Strict construction / positivist construction**
* **Core Rule:** Expound provisions in plain, ordinary, natural, and grammatical meaning; no reading into provisions based on unexpressed "spirit"

---

### Doctrine of Purposive Interpretation
* **Core Rule:** Object-oriented approach ascertaining true context, purpose, and intent of Constitution-makers (guided by Preamble & Constituent Assembly Debates)

---

### Doctrine of Creative Interpretation
* **Core Rule:** Innovative judicial interpretation evolving new concepts/procedures to meet changing societal needs
* **Key Manifestations:**
  * ***Golak Nath (1967):*** Introduced American Doctrine of Prospective Overruling
  * ***Kesavananda Bharati (1973):*** Innovated **Doctrine of Basic Structure**
