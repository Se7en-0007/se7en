## ROMESH THAPPAR CASE (1950)
* **Popular Name:** Cross Roads case
* **Related Issue:** Freedom of the press (**Article 19(1)(a)**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held freedom of speech & expression includes freedom of propagation and circulation
  * Declared Madras Government order banning entry/circulation of weekly journal *Cross Roads* under Sec 9(1-A) of Madras Maintenance of Public Order Act, 1949 as unconstitutional and void
  * Ruled restrictions on speech can be imposed ONLY on grounds specified in **Article 19(2)** (*ordinary breaches of public order not grounds for restriction*)
* **Impact:** Led to **1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951** → Added **“public order”** as a reasonable restriction under Article 19(2)

---

## A.K. GOPALAN CASE (1950)
* **Popular Name:** Preventive Detention case
* **Related Issue:** Procedure established by law (**Articles 21 & 22**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Invalidated Sec 14 of Preventive Detention Act, 1950 (violated Article 22)
  * Adopted **textualist / narrow interpretation** of Article 21
  * Held ‘personal liberty’ = freedom of physical body (*freedom from physical restraint/detention*)
  * Ruled Article 21 protects ONLY against arbitrary **executive action**, NOT arbitrary **legislative action**
  * Declared ‘procedure established by law’ != American ‘due process of law’ (*‘law’ = state-made law, not natural justice*)
* **Impact:** Held field for 3 decades (1950–1978); reached logical end in *A.D.M. Jabalpur case (1976)*; overruled in *Maneka Gandhi case (1978)*

---

## CHAMPAKAM DORAIRAJAN CASE (1951)
* **Related Issue:** Communal reservation in educational admissions (**Articles 15, 29 & 46**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Affirmed Madras High Court verdict; struck down Madras Government ‘Communal GO’ providing proportionate community reservation in medical & engineering colleges
  * Held Communal GO based on religion, race, and caste violated Articles 15(1) & 29(2)
  * Declared **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs) cannot override Fundamental Rights (FRs)** (*DPSPs run as subsidiary to FRs*)
* **Impact:** Led to **1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951** → Inserted **Article 15(4)** enabling special provisions for SEBCs, SCs, and STs

---

## SHANKARI PRASAD CASE (1951)
* **Related Issue:** Parliament’s power to amend the Constitution (**Articles 13 & 368**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held Parliament’s amending power under Article 368 includes power to amend Part III (Fundamental Rights)
  * Separated constituent power (Art 368) from ordinary legislative power
  * Declared word ‘law’ in Article 13(2) refers ONLY to ordinary law, NOT constitutional amendment law
  * Upheld validity of **1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951** (Articles 31A & 31B)
* **Impact:** Reaffirmed in *Sajjan Singh case (1964)*; overruled in *Golak Nath case (1967)*

---

## BERUBARI UNION CASE (1960)
* **Related Issue:** Cession of Indian territory to a foreign state (**Articles 3, 368 & First Schedule**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Ruled Article 3 power to diminish area of a state covers ONLY internal adjustment inter-se of Indian Union territories, NOT cession to a foreign country
  * Held Indian territory can be ceded ONLY via constitutional amendment under Article 368 altering First Schedule
  * Declared **Preamble is NOT a part of the Constitution**
* **Impact:** Led to **9th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1960** → Transferred Berubari Union (WB) to Pakistan per 1958 Indo-Pak (Nehru-Noon) Agreement

---

## K.M. NANAVATI CASE (1961)
* **Related Issue:** Jury System of trial (**Sec 302 IPC**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Affirmed Bombay High Court verdict convicting Commander K.M. Nanavati under Sec 302 IPC (life imprisonment) for murder of wife’s paramour
* **Impact:** Exposed severe flaws in working of Jury System → Led to gradual abolition; **Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973** completely removed Jury Trial system in India

---

## I.C. GOLAK NATH CASE (1967)
* **Related Issue:** Parliament’s power to amend the Constitution (**Articles 13 & 368**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Overruled *Shankari Prasad (1951)* and *Sajjan Singh (1964)*
  * Held Article 368 cannot be used to abridge/take away Fundamental Rights
  * Declared constitutional amendment act IS a ‘law’ within Article 13(2)
  * Applied **doctrine of prospective overruling** (1st, 4th, 17th Amendments remained valid for past, but future amendments restricted)
* **Impact:** Led to **24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971** → Provided Parliament power to amend any part of Constitution including FRs (inserted Art 13(4) & Art 368(3))

---

## KESAVANANDA BHARATI CASE (1973)
* **Popular Name:** Fundamental Rights case
* **Related Issue:** Parliament’s power to amend the Constitution & Basic Structure (**Articles 13 & 368**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Overruled *Golak Nath case (1967)*
  * Held Parliament can amend any provision of Constitution including FRs under Article 368, EXCEPT the **‘Basic Structure’** of the Constitution
  * Upheld validity of 24th Amendment Act (1971) and 25th Amendment Act (1971) (partly)
* **Impact:** Emergence of **Doctrine of Basic Structure** as a limitation on Parliament’s amending power; led to 42nd Amendment Act (1976) attempting to grant unlimited amending power

---

## INDIRA NEHRU GANDHI CASE (1975)
* **Popular Name:** Election case
* **Related Issue:** Basic structure of the Constitution (**Article 329A - Repealed**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Reaffirmed applicability of Basic Structure doctrine
  * Struck down Clause (4) of Article 329A (inserted by 39th Amendment Act, 1975) keeping PM & Speaker election disputes outside court jurisdiction
  * Held judicial review of election disputes is part of basic structure
* **Impact:** Led to **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** inserting Clauses (4) & (5) in Article 368 to dilute basic structure doctrine

---

## A.D.M. JABALPUR CASE (1976)
* **Popular Name:** Habeas Corpus case
* **Related Issue:** Right to life and personal liberty during Emergency (**Articles 21 & 359**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held Article 21 sole repository of right to life/liberty; Presidential Order under Article 359 suspending Art 21 bars writ petition for habeas corpus under Art 226
  * Upheld constitutional validity of Sec 16A(9) of MISA, 1971
* **Impact:** Flawed judgment → **44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978** amended Article 359 so enforcement of **Articles 20 & 21 CANNOT be suspended** during Emergency

---

## MANEKA GANDHI CASE (1978)
* **Popular Name:** Personal Liberty case
* **Related Issue:** Procedure established by law (**Article 21, Passport Act 1967**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Overruled *A.K. Gopalan case (1950)*
  * Established **‘Golden Triangle’ rule** → Articles 14, 19, and 21 are mutually inclusive
  * Expanded ‘personal liberty’ to widest amplitude (includes right to live with human dignity)
  * Declared ‘procedure established by law’ must be **just, fair, and reasonable** (conforming to principles of natural justice, not arbitrary/oppressive)
* **Impact:** Introduced American concept of **‘due process of law’** into Indian jurisprudence; expanded scope of Article 21 dramatically

---

## BACHAN SINGH CASE (1980)
* **Related Issue:** Constitutional validity of death penalty (**Articles 19 & 21, Sec 302 IPC**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Ruled death penalty in Sec 302 IPC is NOT unconstitutional (does not violate Art 19 or 21)
  * Held death penalty must be an exception, confined ONLY to **‘rarest of rare cases’** when alternative option is foreclosed
* **Impact:** Laid down **‘rarest of rare cases’ doctrine** for capital punishment

---

## MINERVA MILLS CASE (1980)
* **Related Issue:** Basic structure of the Constitution (**Articles 31C & 368**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Invalidated Clauses (4) & (5) of Article 368 and 42nd Amendment expansion of Article 31C
  * Held limited amending power of Parliament, judicial review, and harmony/balance between FRs and DPSPs are basic features of Constitution
* **Impact:** Nullified 42nd Amendment's attempt to confer unlimited constituent power on Parliament

---

## WAMAN RAO CASE (1980)
* **Related Issue:** Judicial review of Ninth Schedule (**Article 368 & Ninth Schedule**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held constitutional amendments adding laws to Ninth Schedule ON OR AFTER **April 24, 1973** (*Kesavananda verdict date*) open to challenge on Basic Structure test
  * Additions made prior to April 24, 1973 remain valid and immune

---

## SHAH BANO CASE (1985)
* **Related Issue:** Maintenance of a divorced Muslim wife (**Sec 125 CrPC**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Ruled Muslim husband liable to pay maintenance to divorced wife beyond iddat period under Sec 125 CrPC
  * Declared Sec 125 CrPC is secular and prevails over personal laws
  * Emphasized that Uniform Civil Code (UCC) promotes national integration
* **Impact:** Led to **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986** (*overturned verdict by restricting maintenance to iddat*; validity upheld in *Danial Latifi case 2001* extending maintenance beyond iddat)

---

## D.C. WADHWA CASE (1986)
* **Related Issue:** Ordinance-making power of the Governor (**Article 213**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Pointed out Bihar Governor re-promulgated 256 ordinances between 1967–1981 (kept alive 1–14 years)
  * Declared re-promulgation without legislative passage as **subversion of democratic process** and **fraud on the Constitution**
* **Impact:** Exposed executive misuse of ordinance route

---

## M.C. MEHTA CASE (1986)
* **Popular Name:** Oleum Gas Leak case
* **Related Issue:** SC power to award compensation & Environmental Law (**Articles 21 & 32**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held Article 32 remedial power includes awarding compensation for gross violations of Article 21
  * Replaced strict liability with **principle of absolute liability (no-fault liability)** for hazardous industries
* **Impact:** Expanded Environmental Law ambit in India

---

## KIHOTO HOLLOHAN CASE (1992)
* **Popular Name:** Defection case
* **Related Issue:** Validity of Anti-Defection Law (**Tenth Schedule & Article 368**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Declared Para 7 of Tenth Schedule unconstitutional for violating Art 368(2) (*lacked ratification by 50% state legislatures*)
  * Upheld rest of Tenth Schedule; held Speaker/Chairman acts as **tribunal** subject to **judicial review** under Arts 136, 226, 227
* **Impact:** Brought Speaker’s decisions under Tenth Schedule within judicial review ambit

---

## INDRA SAWHNEY CASE (1992)
* **Popular Name:** Mandal case
* **Related Issue:** Reservation for OBCs in government jobs (**Article 16**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Upheld 27% job reservation for OBCs under Art 16(4) with conditions:
    1. Exclusion of **creamy layer**
    2. **NO reservation in promotions**
    3. Overall reservation cap = **50%**
    4. Carry-forward backlog subject to 50% cap
  * Invalidated 10% executive reservation for economically backward upper castes
* **Impact:** Led to Ram Nandan Committee (creamy layer), statutory NCBC setup, and **76th, 77th, 81st, 82nd, 85th Amendment Acts**

---

## MOHINI JAIN CASE (1992)
* **Popular Name:** Capitation Fee case
* **Related Issue:** Right to education (**Articles 21 & 41**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Ruled Right to Education is a Fundamental Right under Article 21 at ALL levels (*including higher/professional education*)
  * Declared capitation fees illegal (“selling education”)
* **Impact:** Paved way for *Unni Krishnan case (1993)*

---

## UNNI KRISHNAN CASE (1993)
* **Related Issue:** Right to education (**Articles 21 & 45**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Modified *Mohini Jain*: Right to free education is FR under Article 21 ONLY for children up to **14 years of age**
  * Beyond 14 years, obligation depends on state economic capacity
* **Impact:** Led to **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** (Article 21A) & **Right to Education (RTE) Act, 2009**

---

## SUPREME COURT ADVOCATES-ON-RECORD ASSOCIATION CASE (1993)
* **Popular Name:** Second Judges case
* **Related Issue:** Appointment of Supreme Court and High Court Judges (**Articles 124 & 217**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Overruled *S.P. Gupta / First Judges case (1981)*
  * Changed ‘consultation’ to **‘concurrence’**
  * Advice tendered by CJI (after consulting 2 senior-most colleagues) binding on President
  * Senior-most SC judge to be appointed CJI
* **Impact:** Formulated the **Collegium System** for judicial appointments

---

## S.R. BOMMAI CASE (1994)
* **Related Issue:** President’s Rule (**Article 356**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held Article 356 proclamation subject to **judicial review**
  * Floor test in Assembly is sole test for majority
  * Declared **secularism is part of basic structure** (*anti-secular state govt acts ground for 356*)
  * Court can restore dissolved Assembly/Ministry if proclamation held invalid
* **Impact:** Curbed arbitrary Centre dismissal of State Governments

---

## VISHAKA CASE (1997)
* **Related Issue:** Sexual harassment of women at workplace (**Articles 15 & 21**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Declared workplace sexual harassment violates Articles 15 & 21
  * Laid down legally binding **‘Vishaka Guidelines’** for employers until legislation enacted
* **Impact:** Filled legislative vacuum → Led to **Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013**

---

## VINEET NARAIN CASE (1997)
* **Popular Name:** Jain Hawala case
* **Related Issue:** Autonomy and functioning of CBI & CVC
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Directed conferring statutory status on CVC
  * Directed autonomy measures for CBI and Enforcement Directorate (ED)
  * Struck down ‘Single Directive’ requiring prior Govt permission to investigate Joint Secretary+ officers
* **Impact:** Led to **CVC Act, 2003** conferring statutory status on CVC

---

## ASSOCIATION FOR DEMOCRATIC REFORMS CASE (2002)
* **Popular Name:** Poll Reforms case
* **Related Issue:** Criminalisation of politics (**Article 19(1)(a)**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Ruled voter’s right to know candidate antecedents (criminal background, assets, liabilities, education) is FR under Article 19(1)(a)
* **Impact:** ECI issued 2003 order mandating candidate nomination affidavits; false info made electoral offence

---

## T.M.A. PAI FOUNDATION CASE (2002)
* **Related Issue:** Rights of minority educational institutions (**Articles 29 & 30**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Minority status determined **state-wise** (not nationally)
  * Right to establish/administer professional colleges covered under Article 30(1)
  * State cannot regulate admissions in unaided minority institutions except minimum eligibility
* **Impact:** Led to **93rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2005** (inserted Article 15(5) for SEBC/SC/ST reservation in private education except Art 30(1) minority institutions)

---

## NAVEEN JINDAL CASE (2004)
* **Related Issue:** Right to fly the national flag (**Article 19(1)(a)**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held flying national flag freely with respect and dignity is FR under Article 19(1)(a) (*non-commercial usage*)

---

## PRAKASH SINGH CASE (2006)
* **Related Issue:** Police reforms
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Issued 7 binding directives: State Security Commission, DGP 2-year tenure from UPSC panel, 2-year tenure for operational officers, separation of investigation & law/order, Police Establishment Board, Police Complaints Authority, National Security Commission
* **Impact:** Initiated structural police reforms across Centre & States

---

## M. NAGARAJ CASE (2006)
* **Related Issue:** Reservation in promotions for SCs and STs (**Articles 16(4A), 16(4B) & 335**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Upheld constitutional validity of 77th, 81st, 82nd, and 85th Amendments
  * Mandated State must collect quantifiable data showing backwardness, inadequacy of representation, and overall administrative efficiency (Art 335) subject to 50% cap & creamy layer

---

## I.R. COELHO CASE (2007)
* **Popular Name:** Ninth Schedule case
* **Related Issue:** Judicial review of Ninth Schedule (**Article 31B & Ninth Schedule**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Reaffirmed *Waman Rao (1980)*
  * Held laws inserted into Ninth Schedule ON OR AFTER **April 24, 1973** open to challenge on ground of damaging/destroying basic structure

---

## ARUNA RAMACHANDRA SHANBAUG CASE (2011)
* **Related Issue:** Euthanasia (**Article 21**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Declared active euthanasia and assisted death illegal
  * Permitted **passive euthanasia** subject to High Court approval and 3-doctor medical board panel

---

## PEOPLE’S UNION FOR CIVIL LIBERTIES CASE (2013)
* **Popular Name:** NOTA case
* **Related Issue:** Electoral reforms (**Articles 14, 19 & 21, RPA 1951**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Struck down Rules 41(2),(3) & 49-O of Conduct of Election Rules 1961 as violating secrecy of voting under Art 19(1)(a)
  * Directed ECI to provide **None of the Above (NOTA)** option on EVMs/ballots
* **Impact:** ECI introduced NOTA button on EVMs starting late 2013 Assembly elections

---

## LILY THOMAS CASE (2013)
* **Related Issue:** Criminalisation of politics (**Articles 102 & 191, Sec 8(4) RPA 1951**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Struck down **Section 8(4) of RPA, 1951** as unconstitutional
  * Convicted MPs/MLAs lose House membership **immediately from date of conviction** (*removed 3-month appeal window*)
* **Impact:** Led to immediate disqualification of convicted legislators

---

## T.S.R. SUBRAMANIAN CASE (2013)
* **Related Issue:** Civil service reforms
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Directed setup of Civil Service Boards, fixed minimum tenure for civil servants, and mandated written orders (*civil servants not to act on oral instructions*)
* **Impact:** Led to 2014 amendment of IAS, IPS, and IFoS Cadre Rules

---

## NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY CASE (2014)
* **Popular Name:** NALSA case
* **Related Issue:** Rights of transgender persons (**Articles 14 & 21**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Declared Transgenders / Hijras / Eunuchs as **‘third gender’**
  * Upheld right to self-identified gender identity under Article 21
  * Directed Centre/States to treat transgenders as SEBCs for educational & public employment reservation
* **Impact:** Led to **Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019**

---

## SHREYA SINGHAL CASE (2015)
* **Related Issue:** Restrictions on online speech (**Article 19(1)(a), Sec 66A IT Act 2000**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Struck down **Section 66A of Information Technology Act, 2000** in entirety
  * Held Sec 66A vague, over-broad, and violative of freedom of speech under Art 19(1)(a) (*not saved by 19(2)*)

---

## SUPREME COURT ADVOCATES-ON-RECORD ASSOCIATION CASE (2015)
* **Popular Name:** Fourth Judges case / NJAC case
* **Related Issue:** Appointment of Judges & Basic Structure (**Articles 124 & 217, 99th Amendment**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Declared **99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014** and **NJAC Act, 2014** unconstitutional and void
  * Held judicial independence is basic feature of Constitution
* **Impact:** Restored earlier **Collegium System** of judicial appointments

---

## SHAYARA BANO CASE (2017)
* **Popular Name:** Triple Talaq case
* **Related Issue:** Divorce in Muslim community (**Article 14, Shariat Act 1937**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Declared instant Triple Talaq (*‘talaq-e-biddat’*) unconstitutional as manifestly arbitrary under Article 14
  * Struck down Sec 2 of Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937 to that extent
* **Impact:** Led to **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019** (Triple Talaq Act)

---

## K.S. PUTTASWAMY CASE (2017)
* **Popular Name:** Right to Privacy case
* **Related Issue:** Fundamental Right to Privacy (**Article 21**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Overruled *M.P. Sharma (1954)* and *Kharak Singh (1962)*
  * Unanimously declared **Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right** under Article 21 & Part III
  * Formulated **3-fold test for privacy restriction:** (1) Legality, (2) Need (legitimate state aim), (3) Proportionality
* **Impact:** Influenced *Sabarimala, Joseph Shine, Navtej Johar cases*; upheld Aadhaar Act in *Puttaswamy-II (2018)* with modifications; led to Aadhaar Amendment Act, 2019

---

## INDIAN YOUNG LAWYERS ASSOCIATION CASE (2018)
* **Popular Name:** Sabarimala case
* **Related Issue:** Women’s entry into Sabarimala temple (**Articles 14, 21 & 25**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Allowed women of all ages to enter Sabarimala temple and worship Lord Ayyappa
  * Declared 10–50 age entry ban violative of Articles 14, 21, and 25
  * Struck down Rule 3(b) of Kerala Hindu Places of Public Worship Rules, 1965
* **Impact:** Referred to 9-judge larger bench in late 2019 review

---

## JOSEPH SHINE CASE (2018)
* **Related Issue:** Decriminalisation of adultery (**Sec 497 IPC, Sec 198(2) CrPC, Articles 14, 15 & 21**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Decriminalised adultery; struck down **Section 497 IPC** and **Sec 198(2) CrPC** as unconstitutional (violative of Arts 14, 15, 21)
* **Impact:** Adultery ceased to be a criminal offence (remains civil ground for divorce)

---

## NAVTEJ SINGH JOHAR CASE (2018)
* **Related Issue:** Decriminalisation of homosexuality (**Sec 377 IPC, Articles 14, 15, 19 & 21**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Overruled *Suresh Kumar Koushal case (2013)*
  * Decriminalised consensual adult same-sex acts by partly striking down **Section 377 IPC**
  * Held Sec 377 violated Articles 14, 15, 19(1)(a), and 21
* **Impact:** Consensual LGBT sexual conduct permitted; Sec 377 continues to govern non-consensual acts, minor offences, and bestiality

---

## M. SIDDIQ CASE (2019)
* **Popular Name:** Ayodhya case
* **Related Issue:** Ram Janmabhoomi-Babri Masjid land dispute
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Awarded entire 2.77 acres disputed land to deity Ram Lalla Virajman
  * Directed Centre to frame scheme establishing trust (**Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Teerth Kshetra**) for temple construction
  * Directed allotment of alternative 5 acres land to UP Sunni Central Waqf Board in Ayodhya for mosque
* **Impact:** Ended century-old dispute; overturned 2010 Allahabad High Court 3-way split verdict

---

## ANURADHA BHASIN CASE (2020)
* **Related Issue:** Suspension of internet services (**Article 19, Telecom Suspension Rules 2017**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Held freedom of speech/expression & trade over internet protected under **Articles 19(1)(a) & 19(1)(g)**
  * Declared indefinite internet suspension impermissible; must adhere to test of proportionality and temporary duration
* **Impact:** Established right to access internet/information as protected under Article 19

---

## RAMBABU SINGH THAKUR CASE (2020)
* **Related Issue:** Criminalisation of politics
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Made it mandatory for political parties to upload detailed criminal background of candidates on website, social media, and newspapers
  * Required parties to state specific reasons for selection over candidates without criminal antecedents
* **Impact:** ECI released new **Form C-7** for political parties compliance

---

## INTERNET AND MOBILE ASSOCIATION OF INDIA CASE (2020)
* **Related Issue:** Ban on crypto currency trading (**Article 19(1)(g)**)
* **Supreme Court Judgement:**
  * Struck down RBI circular banning banks from virtual currency (VC) trading as illegal, disproportionate, and violative of Article 19(1)(g)
* **Impact:** Removed ban on cryptocurrency trading in India
