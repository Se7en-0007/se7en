## INTRODUCTION
* **Constitutional Status:** Non-statutory advisory body (*not created by Constitution or Act of Parliament*)
* **Establishment Authority:** Established by order of **Central Government** (Department of Legal Affairs, Ministry of Law and Justice) from time to time for a fixed tenure (*usually 3 years*)
* **Core Function:** Recommends legislative measures for purpose of consolidation and codification of laws
* **Nature of Recommendations:** **Not binding** on the government

---

## HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
* **British Regime:** 4 Law Commissions established in 19th century → Significant contribution to Indian Statute Book (*adapted English laws to Indian conditions*)
* **Enacted Products:** Indian Penal Code (IPC 1860), Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC), Civil Procedure Code (CPC), Indian Contract Act, Indian Evidence Act, Transfer of Property Act

## COMMISSIONS CONSTITUTED SO FAR
* **Post-Independence Mandate:** Constitution (Fundamental Rights & DPSPs) gave new direction to law reform
* **Article 372:** Pre-Constitution laws remain in force until amended or repealed
* **First Law Commission of Independent India (1955):**
  * Established in **1955** for a 3-year term
  * Chairman = **M.C. Setalvad** (then Attorney-General of India)
* **Subsequent Commissions:** **22 Law Commissions** constituted till date, each with a 3-year tenure

---

## COMPOSITION
* **Flexible Structure:** Composition not fixed; varies from Commission to Commission
* **Core Components:**
  1. **Chairman:** Serving/retired Judge of Supreme Court or High Court, or legal expert / jurist / law professor
  2. **Full-Time Members:** Serving/retired SC or HC Judges, legal experts, or law professors
  3. **Member-Secretary:** Indian Legal Service (ILS) officer holding rank of Additional Secretary or Secretary to GOI
  4. **Part-Time Members:** Appointed from amongst eminent members of Bar, academic scholars, or legal branch specialists
* **Staff:** Regular research personnel (~1 dozen) + secretarial staff

---

## FUNCTIONS
1. **Repeal Obsolete Laws:** Identify laws no longer needed/relevant for immediate repeal
2. **Implement DPSPs:** Examine existing laws in light of Directive Principles of State Policy & Preamble objectives → Suggest reforms & necessary legislation
3. **Government References:** Consider & convey views on law and judicial administration matters specifically referred by Central Govt
4. **Foreign Research Assistance:** Consider requests for providing research to foreign countries as referred by Govt
5. **Serve the Poor:** Harness law and legal process in service of the poor
6. **Revise Central Acts:** Simplify Central Acts of general importance & remove anomalies, ambiguities and inequities

---

## WORKING
* **8-Step Report Process:**
  1. Initiation of projects in Commission meetings
  2. Priority discussion, topic identification, & member assignment
  3. Methodology adoption for research & data collection
  4. Preparation of **Working Paper** outlining problem & suggested reforms
  5. Public & stakeholder circulation of Working Paper for reactions/suggestions
  6. Response evaluation & information organization
  7. Detailed scrutiny & report finalisation in full Commission meetings
  8. Submission of final report to **Ministry of Law and Justice**
* **Parliamentary Laying:** Ministry of Law & Justice considers reports in consultation with administrative ministries → Lays before **Parliament** from time to time
* **Total Reports:** **277 reports** submitted till date

---

## ROLE
1. Provides specialized expert recommendations on legal matters referred by Government
2. Undertakes research & law review (**suo-motu** or on reference) for legal reforms & new enactments
3. Recommends judicial system reforms for eliminating procedural delays, speedy disposal and reducing litigation cost
4. Contributes progressively to codification and development of Indian law
