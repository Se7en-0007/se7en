## INTRODUCTION
* **Integrated Judicial System:** Single system of courts enforcing both Central and State laws (*adopted from GOI Act 1935*; *vs USA double system: federal courts for federal law, state courts for state law*)
* **Inauguration:** **January 28, 1950** (succeeded **Federal Court of India** under GOI Act 1935 & replaced British **Privy Council** as highest court of appeal)
* **Constitutional Location:** Enshrined in **Part V** of the Constitution (**Articles 124 to 147**)

---

## COMPOSITION AND APPOINTMENT
* **Present Strength:** **34 judges** (1 Chief Justice + 33 other judges)
* **Original Strength (1950):** **8 judges** (1 Chief Justice + 7 other judges)
* **Parliamentary Increase:** Empowered to alter judge strength via legislation

### Appointment of Judges
* **Appointing Authority:** **President**
* **Chief Justice of India (CJI):** Appointed by President after consultation with SC & HC judges deemed necessary
* **Other Judges:** Appointed by President after **mandatory consultation with CJI** + other SC & HC judges

### Controversy over Consultation
* **First Judges Case (1981) (*S.P. Gupta Case*):** Consultation = exchange of views, NOT concurrence → Executive primacy
* **Second Judges Case (1993) (*SC Advocates-on-Record Association Case*):** Reversed 1981 ruling → Consultation = **concurrence**; CJI advice binding; CJI consults **2 seniormost colleagues**
* **Third Judges Case (1998) (*Re-Presidential Reference*):** Consultation = **plurality of judges** → CJI + **collegium of 4 seniormost SC judges**; if 2 judges give adverse opinion, CJI shall NOT send recommendation
* **99th Constitutional Amendment Act (2014) & NJAC Act (2014):** Replaced collegium system with **National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC)**
* **Fourth Judges Case (2015) (*SC Advocates-on-Record Association Case*):** SC declared 99th Amendment & NJAC Act **unconstitutional and void** (infringed judicial independence) → **Collegium system revived**

### Appointment of Chief Justice
* **1950–1973 Convention:** Seniormost SC judge appointed CJI
* **Convention Violations:**
  * **1973:** **A.N. Ray** appointed CJI superseding 3 senior judges (*J.M. Shelat, K.S. Hegde, A.N. Grover*, who resigned)
  * **1977:** **M.U. Beg** appointed CJI superseding **H.R. Khanna** (who resigned)
* **Second Judges Case (1993) Ruling:** Discretion curtailed → **Seniormost judge of SC** must alone be appointed CJI

## QUALIFICATIONS, OATH AND SALARIES

### Qualifications of Judges
1. Citizen of India
2. Must fulfill **ANY ONE** of the following:
   * Judge of a High Court (or HCs in succession) for **5 years**
   * Advocate of a High Court (or HCs in succession) for **10 years**
   * **Distinguished jurist** in the opinion of the President
* **Minimum Age:** **No minimum age** prescribed in the Constitution

### Oath or Affirmation
* **Administered by:** **President** (or person appointed by President)
* **Swears/Affirms to:** True faith & allegiance to Constitution; uphold sovereignty & integrity of India; perform duties without fear or favour, affection or ill-will; uphold Constitution and laws

### Salaries and Allowances
* Determined by **Parliament**; charged on **Consolidated Fund of India** (cannot be varied to disadvantage except during Financial Emergency)
* **Revision (2018):** CJI salary = **₹2.80 lakh per month**; Judge salary = **₹2.50 lakh per month**
* **Pension:** **50% of last drawn salary** as monthly pension + sumptuary allowance, free residence, car, medical, telephone

## TENURE AND REMOVAL

### Tenure of Judges
* **No fixed tenure** in Constitution; 3 provisions:
  1. Holds office until attaining **65 years of age** (age questions determined by authority provided by Parliament)
  2. Resigns by writing to **President**
  3. Removed by **President** on recommendation of Parliament

### Removal of Judges
* **Order:** Issued by **President** after Parliamentary address presented in **same session**
* **Majority Required:** Passed in each House by **Special Majority** (majority of total membership + ≥2/3 members present and voting)
* **Grounds:** **Proved misbehaviour** or **incapacity**
* **Judges Enquiry Act (1968) Procedure:**
  1. Removal motion signed by **100 members in Lok Sabha** OR **50 members in Rajya Sabha** submitted to Speaker/Chairman
  2. Speaker/Chairman may admit or refuse
  3. If admitted, **3-member committee** constituted: (a) CJI or SC judge, (b) Chief Justice of a High Court, (c) Distinguished jurist
  4. If committee finds judge guilty, House considers motion
  5. Motion passed in both Houses by Special Majority → Address sent to President → President issues removal order
* **Historical Fact:** **No Supreme Court judge has been impeached so far** (*V. Ramaswami 1991–1993 faced proceedings; committee found guilty, but motion defeated in Lok Sabha due to Congress abstention*)

## ACTING, ADHOC AND RETIRED JUDGES

### Acting Chief Justice
* Appointed by **President** when CJI office is vacant, CJI is temporarily absent, or CJI is unable to perform duties

### Ad hoc Judge
* **Trigger:** Lack of quorum of permanent judges to hold/continue SC session
* **Appointment:** CJI appoints a **High Court judge** (qualified for SC) as ad hoc judge for temporary period
* **Prerequisites:** Consultation with CJ of concerned High Court + **previous consent of President**
* **Status:** Enjoys all jurisdiction, powers and privileges of an SC judge (priority duty over HC duties)

### Retired Judge
* **Appointment:** CJI can request a retired SC judge or retired HC judge (qualified for SC) to act as SC judge temporarily
* **Prerequisites:** **Previous consent of President** + **consent of retired judge**
* **Status:** Entitled to allowances determined by President; enjoys all jurisdiction, powers and privileges of an SC judge (not otherwise deemed an SC judge)

## SEAT AND PROCEDURE

### Seat of Supreme Court
* **Declared Seat:** **Delhi**
* **Relocation Power:** CJI authorised to appoint other place(s) as seat **with approval of President** (optional provision; non-justiciable/no court direction can be issued)

### Procedure of the Court
* SC makes rules regulating practice & procedure with **President's approval**
* **Bench Size:** Constitutional cases & Article 143 references decided by **Bench of at least 5 judges**; other cases decided by single judges / division benches
* **Decisions:** Delivered in open court; by **majority vote** (dissenting opinions permitted)

## INDEPENDENCE OF SUPREME COURT
* **Constitutional Safeguards:**
  1. **Mode of Appointment:** Executive discretion curtailed by judicial consultation (collegium)
  2. **Security of Tenure:** Removed by President ONLY on constitutional grounds/procedure (does not hold office during pleasure)
  3. **Fixed Service Conditions:** Salaries/allowances non-diminishable during term (except Financial Emergency)
  4. **Expenses Charged on Consolidated Fund:** Non-votable by Parliament
  5. **Conduct of Judges Cannot be Discussed:** Prohibited in Parliament/State Legislatures except during impeachment motion
  6. **Ban on Practice After Retirement:** Prohibited from pleading/acting in any court/authority in India
  7. **Power to Punish for Contempt:** Can punish any person for contempt (dignity & authority protected)
  8. **Freedom to Appoint Staff:** CJI appoints officers/servants without executive interference
  9. **Jurisdiction Cannot be Curtailed:** Parliament can extend but CANNOT curtail guaranteed jurisdiction
  10. **Separation from Executive:** Article 50 Directive Principle separates judiciary from executive

## JURISDICTION AND POWERS OF SUPREME COURT
* *Sir Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar:* *"The Supreme Court of India has more powers than any other Supreme Court in any part of the world."*

### 1. Original Jurisdiction
* **Scope:** Federal court disputes involving legal rights:
  * (a) Centre vs 1 or more States
  * (b) Centre + State(s) vs 1 or more States
  * (c) 2 or more States *inter se*
* **Nature:** **Exclusive and Original**
* **Exclusions from Original Jurisdiction:**
  * Pre-constitution treaties, agreements, covenants, sanads
  * Inter-state river water disputes (**Article 262**)
  * Matters referred to Finance Commission
  * Adjustment of expenses/pensions between Centre and States
  * Commercial suits by state against Centre
* *First Suit:* **1961** (West Bengal vs Centre challenging Coal Bearing Areas Act 1957; dismissed)

### 2. Writ Jurisdiction
* **Guarantor of Fundamental Rights:** Issues writs (*Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari*)
* **Nature:** **Original** (aggrieved citizen can move directly) but **NOT exclusive** (concurrent with High Courts under **Article 226**)
* **SC vs HC Writ Scope:**
  * **SC (Article 32):** Writs ONLY for Fundamental Rights (narrower scope); CANNOT refuse writ remedy (Art 32 is an FR)
  * **HC (Article 226):** Writs for FRs AND other legal purposes (wider scope); discretionary remedy

### 3. Appellate Jurisdiction
* **(a) Constitutional Matters:** Appeal lies if High Court certifies case involves substantial question of law requiring constitutional interpretation
* **(b) Civil Matters:** Appeal lies if High Court certifies case involves substantial question of general importance needing SC decision (*30th CAA 1972 removed ₹20,000 monetary limit*)
* **(c) Criminal Matters:** Appeal lies if High Court:
  * Reverses acquittal and sentences accused to death
  * Takes case from subordinate court and sentences accused to death
  * Certifies case is fit for appeal
  * *1970 Parliamentary Expansion:* Appeal allowed if HC reversed acquittal and sentenced to **life imprisonment or ≥10 years**
* **(d) Appeal by Special Leave (Article 136):**
  * Plenary discretionary power to grant special leave from any judgment/order of any court/tribunal in India (**except Military Tribunal / Court Martial**)
  * Covers constitutional, civil, criminal, income-tax, labour, revenue cases; interlocutory or final orders

### 4. Advisory Jurisdiction (Article 143)
* **Presidential References:**
  * (a) On question of law/fact of public importance → SC **may tender or refuse** opinion
  * (b) On dispute arising from pre-constitution treaty/agreement → SC **MUST tender** opinion
* **Binding Character:** **Advisory only** (NOT binding on President)
* *Notable References:* Kerala Education Bill (1958), Berubari Union (1960), Special Courts Bill (1978), Ayodhya / Rama Janma Bhumi (1993), Consultation Process / 3rd Judges Case (1998), 2G Spectrum auctioning (2012)

### 5. A Court of Record
* **Two Powers:**
  1. Judgments, proceedings and acts recorded for perpetual memory and evidentiary value (recognized as legal precedents)
  2. Power to punish for contempt of itself (extended in 1991 to contempt of High Courts, subordinate courts and tribunals)
* **Contempt of Courts Act (1971) (*H.N. Sanyal Committee 1963*):**
  * **Civil Contempt:** Wilful disobedience to judgment/order/writ or breach of undertaking
  * **Criminal Contempt:** Publication/act that scandalises/lowers authority, prejudices/interferes with judicial proceedings, or obstructs administration of justice
  * *Exemptions:* Innocent publication, fair/accurate report of proceedings, fair criticism
  * *Penalty:* Simple imprisonment up to **6 months** OR fine up to **₹2,000** OR both; limitation period = **1 year**

### 6. Power of Judicial Review
* Power to examine constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders of Central and State governments; declare ultra vires / null & void if violative

### 7. Review Jurisdiction
* Power to review its own judgment/order (petition filed within **30 days** before same judge/bench)
* **Grounds:** Discovery of new evidence, error apparent on face of record, or sufficient reason
* **Curative Petition:** SC can reconsider final judgment post-review dismissal on limited grounds (violation of natural justice, gross miscarriage of justice, bias)

### 8. Constitutional Interpretation
* Ultimate interpreter of Constitution; applies doctrines (*Pith and Substance, Severability, Eclipse, Colorable Legislation, Harmonious Construction*)

### 9. Other Powers
* Decides disputes regarding election of **President and Vice-President** (**Article 71**)
* Inquires into conduct of **UPSC Chairman & members** on Presidential reference (**Article 317**; advice binding)
* Withdraws or transfers cases pending before High Courts
* Law declared by SC binding on all courts in India (**Article 141**)
* Decrees/orders enforceable throughout India (**Article 142**)

## INDIAN AND AMERICAN SUPREME COURTS COMPARED

### Comparing Indian and American Supreme Courts

| Feature / Aspect | Indian Supreme Court | American Supreme Court |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Original Jurisdiction** | Confined to federal cases only | Covers federal cases + naval, maritime, ambassadors, etc. |
| **2. Appellate Jurisdiction** | Covers constitutional, civil and criminal cases | Confined to constitutional cases only |
| **3. Special Leave** | Wide plenary discretion (**Article 136**) | No such plenary power |
| **4. Advisory Jurisdiction** | Has advisory jurisdiction (**Article 143**) | Has no advisory jurisdiction |
| **5. Judicial Review Scope** | Limited scope (**‘Procedure established by law’**) | Very wide scope (**‘Due process of law’**) |
| **6. Power Enlargement** | Jurisdiction can be enlarged by Parliament | Jurisdiction limited to constitutional grant |
| **7. Control over State Courts** | Judicial superintendence over state High Courts (Integrated System) | No such control over state courts (Double/Separated System) |
