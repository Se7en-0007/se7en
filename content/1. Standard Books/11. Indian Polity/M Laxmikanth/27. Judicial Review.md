## INTRODUCTION
* **Origin & Development:** Originated and developed in **USA**
  * Propounded in ***Marbury vs. Madison (1803)*** by **John Marshall** (Chief Justice, US Supreme Court)
* **Indian Position:** Constitution explicitly confers power on judiciary (**Supreme Court & High Courts**)
* **Basic Structure Element:** SC declared judicial review as **Basic Feature of Constitution** → Cannot be curtailed/excluded even by constitutional amendment

---

## MEANING OF JUDICIAL REVIEW
* **Definition:** Power of judiciary to examine constitutionality of legislative enactments & executive orders of Central and State governments
  * Unconstitutional / Ultra Vires finding → Declared **illegal, unconstitutional and null & void** (cannot be enforced)
* **3 Categories (Justice Syed Shah Mohamed Quadri):**
  1. Judicial review of **constitutional amendments**
  2. Judicial review of **legislation** of Parliament, State Legislatures & subordinate legislation
  3. Judicial review of **administrative action** of Union, State and state authorities
* **Major Cases Exercising Judicial Review:**
  * ***Golaknath Case (1967)***
  * ***Bank Nationalisation Case (1970)***
  * ***Privy Purses Abolition Case (1970)***
  * ***Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)***
  * ***Minerva Mills Case (1980)***
  * ***NJAC Act & 99th CAA Declaration (2015):*** Declared 99th Amendment Act (2014) & NJAC Act (2014) unconstitutional and void

---

## IMPORTANCE OF JUDICIAL REVIEW
* **3 Core Reasons:**
  1. Uphold principle of **supremacy of the Constitution**
  2. Maintain **federal equilibrium** (balance between Centre and States)
  3. Protect **Fundamental Rights** of citizens
* **Key Observations by Supreme Court:**
  * **Justice Khanna (*Kesavananda Bharati, 1973*):** Judicial review is a basic feature
  * **Justice Bhagwati (*State of Rajasthan v. UOI, 1977*):** Constitution is *supreme lex*; SC is ultimate interpreter determining authority limits
  * **Chief Justice Chandrachud (*Minerva Mills, 1980*):** Rights without remedies are *"as writ in water"*; controlled constitution becomes uncontrolled without judicial review
  * **Chief Justice Ahmadi (*L. Chandra Kumar, 1997*):** Judges entrusted to maintain constitutional balance of power
  * **Justice Ramaswami (*S.S. Bola, 1997*):** Weapon to protect FRs and maintain federal balance

## CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISIONS FOR JUDICIAL REVIEW
* **Term Mention:** Phrase **‘Judicial Review’ is nowhere explicitly used** in Constitution, but power is conferred via explicit articles:
  1. **Article 13:** Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights = null and void
  2. **Article 32:** Guarantees right to move SC for FR enforcement (issues writs)
  3. **Article 131:** Original jurisdiction of SC in inter-governmental (Centre–State / Inter-State) disputes
  4. **Article 132:** Appellate jurisdiction of SC in constitutional cases
  5. **Article 133:** Appellate jurisdiction of SC in civil cases
  6. **Article 134:** Appellate jurisdiction of SC in criminal cases
  7. **Article 134A:** Certificate for appeal to SC from High Courts (*added by **44th Amendment Act, 1978***)
  8. **Article 135:** SC exercises powers/jurisdiction of Federal Court under pre-constitution law
  9. **Article 136:** SC authorised to grant special leave to appeal from any court/tribunal (except military)
  10. **Article 143:** Presidential reference to SC for advisory opinion
  11. **Article 226:** High Courts power to issue writs for FRs and other purposes
  12. **Article 227:** High Courts power of superintendence over all courts & tribunals in territorial jurisdiction
  13. **Article 245:** Territorial extent of laws made by Parliament and State Legislatures
  14. **Article 246:** Subject matter of laws (Union, State, Concurrent Lists)
  15. **Articles 251 & 254:** Conflict between Central and State laws → Central law prevails, State law void
  16. **Article 372:** Continuance in force of pre-constitution laws

## SCOPE OF JUDICIAL REVIEW
* **3 Grounds of Constitutional Challenge:**
  1. Infringes **Fundamental Rights** (Part III)
  2. Outside **competence** of framing authority
  3. Repugnant to **constitutional provisions**
* **India vs USA Comparison:**
  * **USA:** Operates on **‘Due process of law’** → Wide scope; protection on both substantive grounds (unlawful) AND procedural grounds (unreasonable) → US SC called *‘third chamber’* / *‘super-legislature’*
  * **India:** Operates on **‘Procedure established by law’** → Narrower scope; examines substantive question (authority competence/power), NOT reasonableness, suitability, or policy implications
* **Constitutional Synthesis:** India combines **American principle of judicial supremacy** and **British principle of parliamentary supremacy**

## JUDICIAL REVIEW OF THE NINTH SCHEDULE
* **Origin & Purpose:** **Article 31B** saves laws in **Ninth Schedule** from FR challenge → Added by **1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951** (originally 13 acts; currently **282 actual acts** / entry number up to 284)
* **Evolution of Judicial Stance:**
  * ***Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973):*** Ninth Schedule laws open to challenge if violative of Basic Structure
  * ***Waman Rao Case (1980):*** Clarified cut-off date → Laws added to Ninth Schedule **after April 24, 1973** (date of *Kesavananda Bharati* judgment) open to basic structure challenge
  * ***I.R. Coelho Case (2007):*** Reaffirmed no blanket immunity; judicial review = **Basic Feature**
* **Key Principles in *I.R. Coelho* Judgment (2007):**
  1. **Rights Test & Direct Impact:** Form of amendment irrelevant; consequence & direct impact on Part III determines validity
  2. **Golden Triangle Touchstone:** Laws added post-April 24, 1973 tested on basic structure as reflected in **Article 21 read with Articles 14 and 19**
  3. **No Blanket Protection:** Protection subject to constitutional adjudication examining infraction nature & extent
  4. **"Rights Test" vs "Essence of Right Test":** "Rights test" applicable when entire chapter/FRs abridged (*distinguished from M. Nagaraj case 2006*)
  5. **Prior Upheld Laws Exempt:** Laws previously upheld by SC cannot be challenged again; BUT laws declared violative and subsequently placed in Ninth Schedule post-April 24, 1973 are open to basic structure challenge
  6. **Past Transactions Protected:** Actions taken & transactions finalized prior to judgment remain valid
