## INTRODUCTION
* **Location:** Enshrined in **Part VI** of the Constitution (**Articles 153 to 167**) dealing with the State Executive
* **State Executive Composition:** Governor, Chief Minister, Council of Ministers and Advocate General of the State (*no office of Vice-Governor*)
* **Dual Role:**
  1. Chief executive head of state = **Nominal / Titular / Constitutional Head** (*real executive = Council of Ministers headed by CM*)
  2. **Agent of the Central Government**
* **7th Constitutional Amendment Act (1956):** Facilitated appointment of the **same person as Governor for 2 or more states**

---

## APPOINTMENT OF GOVERNOR
* **Mode:** Neither directly nor indirectly elected → Appointed by **President** by warrant under his hand and seal
* **Constitutional Position:** Nominee of Central Government
  * ***Hargovind Pant Case (1979) SC Ruling:*** Office of Governor is an **independent constitutional office** (NOT an employment under Central Govt, not subordinate to or under control of Central Govt)
* **Reasons for Nominative System over Direct Election (Constituent Assembly Choice):**
  1. Direct election incompatible with parliamentary system
  2. Conflict probability between Governor and Chief Minister
  3. Nominal head status makes elaborate election spending/machinery unnecessary
  4. Personal issue contestation not in national interest
  5. Elected Governor would belong to a party → Loss of neutral/impartial stance
  6. Separatist tendencies threatening political stability and national unity
  7. Presidential nomination retains Central control over states
  8. Leadership conflict during general elections
  9. CM would nominate a second-rate ruling party candidate
  * *Model Adopted:* Dropped **American Model** (directly elected) → Accepted **Canadian Model** (appointed by Centre/Governor-General)
* **Conventions / Recommendations (Sarkaria Commission):**
  * Person should be an **outsider** (from outside the state)
  * Detached figure, not connected with local state politics
  * Person not involved in active politics in recent past
  * Politician from Central ruling party should not be Governor in state ruled by opposition party
  * Procedure of consultation with state CM should be prescribed in Constitution (**Article 155**)

## QUALIFICATIONS, OATH AND CONDITIONS

### Qualifications for Appointment
1. Citizen of India
2. Completed **35 years of age**

### Oath or Affirmation (Article 159)
* **Swears/Affirms to:**
  1. Faithfully execute the office
  2. Preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law
  3. Devote to the service and well-being of the people of the state
* **Administered by:** **Chief Justice of concerned High Court** (or seniormost available HC judge)

### Conditions of Governor’s Office (Article 158)
1. **No Legislative Membership:** Cannot be a member of either House of Parliament or State Legislature (seat deemed vacated upon entering office)
2. **No Office of Profit:** Prohibited from holding any other office of profit
3. **Official Residence:** Entitled to rent-free use of official residence (**Raj Bhavan**)
4. **Emoluments & Allowances:** Determined by Parliament; **cannot be diminished** during term
   * *Salary:* Increased to **₹3.50 lakh per month** (*Finance Act 2018, w.e.f. Jan 1, 2016 amending 1982 Act*)
   * *Dual Governorship:* Emoluments shared between states in proportion determined by President
5. **Immunities & Privileges:**
   * **Official Acts:** Personal immunity from legal liability for official acts
   * **Criminal Proceedings:** **Complete immunity** from criminal proceedings during term (cannot be arrested or imprisoned)
   * **Civil Proceedings:** Permissible for personal acts after giving **2 months' prior written notice**

## TERM OF GOVERNOR’S OFFICE (ARTICLE 156)
* **Tenure:** **5 years** from date of entering office
* **Doctrine of Pleasure:** Holds office during **pleasure of the President** (can be removed by President at any time)
  * ***Surya Narain Case (1981) SC Ruling:*** Pleasure of President is **not justiciable**; Governor has no security of tenure or fixed term
* **Resignation:** Addressed in writing to the **President**
* **Transfer & Reappointment:** President can transfer Governor to another state for unexpired term or reappoint
* **Continuance:** Holds office beyond 5 years until successor assumes charge (prevents interregnum)
* **Contingency Provisions (Article 160):** President makes provisions for unexpected contingencies (e.g., Chief Justice of state High Court temporarily appointed as acting Governor)

## POWERS AND FUNCTIONS OF GOVERNOR
* Analogous to President (Executive, Legislative, Financial, Judicial) → **No Diplomatic, Military, or Emergency Powers**

### 1. Executive Powers
1. All executive actions formally taken in Governor's name
2. Makes rules for authentication of orders & convenient transaction of state government business
3. Appoints & holds during pleasure: **Chief Minister & State Ministers**
   * *Tribal Welfare Minister:* Mandated in **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha** (*Bihar excluded by 94th CAA 2006*)
1. Appoints **Advocate General of the State** (determines remuneration; holds office during pleasure)
2. Appoints **State Election Commissioner** (service conditions fixed by Governor; removed ONLY like High Court judge)
3. Appoints **Chairman & Members of State Public Service Commission (SPSC)** (removed **ONLY by President**, not Governor)
4. Seeks administration & legislative info from CM; requires CM to submit single-minister decisions to Council of Ministers
5. Recommends imposition of **President's Rule (Article 356)**; acts as agent of President during President's Rule
6. Acts as **Chancellor of State Universities**; appoints Vice-Chancellors

### 2. Legislative Powers
1. Summons, prorogues state legislature & dissolves State Legislative Assembly
2. Addresses state legislature at start of 1st session after general elections & 1st session each year; sends messages on pending bills
3. Appoints presiding officer pro tem: Assembly member as Speaker/Deputy Speaker pro tem when both vacant; Council member as Chairman/Deputy Chairman pro tem when both vacant
4. **Nominations:**
   * **1/6th members of State Legislative Council** (literature, science, art, cooperative movement, social service)
   * *Discontinued:* 1 Anglo-Indian to State Assembly (*104th CAA 2019*)
5. Decides member disqualification questions in consultation with **Election Commission**
6. **Action on Passed Bills (Article 200):** Assent, Withhold assent, Return (non-money bills) for reconsideration, or **Reserve for President's consideration**
   * *Mandatory Reservation:* Where bill endangers constitutional position of **State High Court**
   * *Soli Sorabji Categories for Reservation:* Ultra vires, opposed to DPSPs, against larger national interest, grave national importance, compulsory acquisition of property (**Article 31A**)
7. **Ordinance-Making Power (Article 213):** Promulgates ordinances during recess of state legislature
   * Must be approved by legislature within **6 weeks from reassembly**
   * ***D.C. Wadhwa Case (1986) SC Ruling:*** Successive re-promulgation of ordinances without legislative passage is unconstitutional
1. Lays reports before state legislature: State Finance Commission, SPSC and CAG reports

### 3. Financial Powers
1. Ensures **Annual Financial Statement (State Budget)** is laid before state legislature
2. Money Bills introduced **ONLY with prior recommendation**
3. Demand for grant made **ONLY on recommendation**
4. Advances from **Contingency Fund of the State** for unforeseen expenditure
5. Constitutes **State Finance Commission every 5 years** to review panchayat and municipality finances

### 4. Judicial Powers
1. **Pardoning Power (Article 161):** Grants pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions, suspensions, commutations for state law offences
   * *Comparison with President:* Governor **CANNOT pardon a death sentence** (can only suspend, remit, or commute); Governor **CANNOT pardon court-martial sentences**
2. Consulted by President during appointment of **State High Court Judges**
3. Appoints, posts and promotes **District Judges** in consultation with High Court
4. Appoints persons to judicial service of state (other than district judges) in consultation with High Court and SPSC

## COMPARISONS

### Comparing Veto Powers of President and Governor
* **Ordinary Bills:**
  * Both can assent, withhold, or return for reconsideration (**Suspensive Veto**)
  * Governor can also reserve bill for President → Governor has **no further role**
  * When reserved, President can assent, withhold, or return (state legislature must reconsider within **6 months**; President **NOT bound to assent** if passed again)
* **Money Bills:**
  * Both can assent or withhold (neither can return money bills)
  * Governor can reserve for President → President can assent or withhold (cannot return)

### Comparing Ordinance-Making Powers of President and Governor
* **Recess Requirement:** President when Parliament in recess; Governor when State Legislature in recess
* **Expiry:** **6 weeks** from reassembly (or earlier disapproval resolution)
* **Presidential Instructions Requirement:** Governor **CANNOT** issue ordinance without Presidential instructions in 3 cases:
  1. Bill containing same provisions would have required prior sanction of President
  2. Deemed necessary to reserve bill containing same provisions for President
  3. State Act containing same provisions would have been invalid without President's assent

### Comparing Pardoning Powers of President and Governor
* **Offences:** President for Central law offences; Governor for State law offences
* **Death Sentence:** **President alone** can pardon death sentence; Governor **CANNOT pardon death sentence** (can only suspend, remit, or commute)
* **Court Martial:** President possesses court martial pardon powers; Governor possesses **no court martial powers**

## CONSTITUTIONAL POSITION OF GOVERNOR
* **System:** Parliamentary System → Governor = Nominal Executive; Council of Ministers headed by CM = Real Executive
* **Constitutional Triad (Articles 154, 163, 164):**
  * **Article 154:** Executive power of state vested in Governor
  * **Article 163:** Council of Ministers with CM to **aid and advise** Governor (except in matters where Governor acts in discretion)
  * **Article 164:** Ministers appointed by Governor, hold office during pleasure
* **Difference from President (Advice):** **42nd CAA 1976** made ministerial advice strictly binding on President, but **NO such explicit provision** was made for Governor
* **Governor's Discretion Decision Final:** If question arises whether a matter falls in Governor's discretion, **Governor's decision is final** and non-challengeable

### Types of Discretionary Powers

#### 1. Constitutional Discretion (Express Provisions)
1. Reservation of a bill for President's consideration
2. Recommendation for imposition of President’s Rule (**Article 356**)
3. Exercising functions as Administrator of adjoining UT (additional charge)
4. Determining royalty amount payable to autonomous Tribal District Council by Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram (**6th Schedule**)

#### 2. Situational Discretion (Implicit Circumstances)
1. Appointment of CM when no party has clear majority or CM dies suddenly with no obvious successor
2. Dismissal of Council of Ministers when it loses assembly confidence
3. Dissolution of State Legislative Assembly if Council of Ministers loses majority

#### 3. Special Responsibilities (Presidential Directions)
1. **Maharashtra:** Separate development boards for Vidarbha and Marathwada
2. **Gujarat:** Separate development boards for Saurashtra and Kutch
3. **Nagaland:** Law and order (Naga Hills–Tuensang Area)
4. **Assam:** Administration of tribal areas
5. **Manipur:** Administration of hill areas
6. **Sikkim:** Peace and socio-economic advancement
7. **Arunachal Pradesh:** Law and order
8. **Karnataka:** Separate development board for Hyderabad-Karnataka region (*98th CAA 2012*)

## ISSUES IN THE GOVERNOR’S FUNCTIONING
* **Sarkaria Commission (1983–88) Identified Issues:**
  1. Selection and choice of Chief Minister in hung assembly
  2. Testing majority support (floor test vs governor lists/verification)
  3. Criteria for dismissal of Chief Minister
  4. Dissolution of Legislative Assembly on losing majority CM's advice
  5. Reservation of State Bills for President's consideration
  6. Discretion in nominations to Legislative Council
  7. Exercise of discretion as Chancellor of State Universities
