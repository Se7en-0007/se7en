## INTRODUCTION
* **Constitutional Status:** Independent office provided under **Article 148** of the Constitution of India
* **Head of Department:** Head of **Indian Audit and Accounts Department** (*created during British rule in **1753***)
* **Guardian of Public Purse:** Controls entire financial system of country at both levels (Centre and States)
* **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar’s View:** Declared CAG as **“the most important Officer under the Constitution of India”**
* **Bulwarks of Democracy:** One of the four bulwarks of democratic system in India (*along with Supreme Court, Election Commission and UPSC*)

---

## APPOINTMENT AND TERM
* **Appointing Authority:** Appointed by **President of India** by warrant under his hand and seal
* **Oath or Affirmation:** Administered by **President** (true faith & allegiance to Constitution; uphold sovereignty & integrity; perform duties without fear or favour; uphold Constitution & laws)
* **Tenure:** **6 years** or until attaining **65 years of age** (whichever is earlier)
* **Resignation:** Addressed in writing to **President**
* **Removal Procedure:** Removed by **President** on same grounds and in same manner as a **Judge of Supreme Court**
  * *Procedure:* Resolution passed by both Houses of Parliament with **Special Majority** on grounds of **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**

---

## INDEPENDENCE
* **Constitutional Safeguards:**
  1. **Security of Tenure:** Removed by President ONLY per constitutional procedure (*does NOT hold office during pleasure of President*)
  2. **Ineligibility for Further Office:** Ineligible for further office under GOI or any State after demitting office
  3. **Salary & Service Conditions:** Determined by Parliament; salary equals **Judge of Supreme Court** (**₹2.50 lakh per month** in 2018)
  4. **Non-Diminishable Service Rights:** Salary, rights in respect of leave, pension, or retirement age cannot be varied to disadvantage post-appointment
  5. **Consultation for Department Rules:** Service conditions of IA&AD staff & administrative powers prescribed by President after **consulting CAG**
  6. **Charged Expenditure:** Administrative expenses (including salaries, allowances, pensions) charged on **Consolidated Fund of India** (*non-votable by Parliament*)

## DUTIES AND POWERS
* **Enabling Constitutional Article:** **Article 149** authorises Parliament to prescribe duties and powers → Enacted **CAG’s (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971**
* **Key Duties and Functions:**
  1. **Consolidated Fund Audits:** Audits all expenditure from Consolidated Fund of India, each State and UT having Legislative Assembly
  2. **Contingency Fund & Public Account Audits:** Audits all transactions relating to Contingency Fund and Public Account of Centre and States
  3. **Subsidiary Accounts Audits:** Audits trading, manufacturing, profit & loss accounts, balance sheets of all departments
  4. **Substantially Financed Bodies:** Audits receipts/expenditure of bodies/authorities substantially financed from Central/State revenues
  5. **Grants & Loans Audits:** Audits accounts of bodies receiving grants/loans for specific purposes from Centre or States
  6. **Receipts Audit:** Audits all receipts of Centre and States (assessment, collection, proper allocation rules)
  7. **Stores & Stock Audit:** Audits accounts of all stores and stock in government offices/departments
  8. **Government Companies Audit:** Audits accounts of Government Companies under Companies Act
  9. **Corporations Audit:** Audits corporations whose statutes provide for audit by CAG
  10. **Presidential / Gubernatorial Reference:** Audits accounts of any authority (e.g. local bodies) when requested by President/Governor
  11. **Form of Accounts Advice (Article 150):** Advises President on form in which accounts of Centre and States shall be kept
  12. **Audit Reports Submission (Article 151):** Submits audit reports of Centre to **President** (placed before Parliament) and of States to **Governor** (placed before State Legislature)
  13. **Net Proceeds Certification (Article 279):** Ascertains and certifies net proceeds of any tax/duty (*certificate is final*; net proceeds = gross tax minus collection cost)
  14. **Guide, Friend and Philosopher:** Acts as guide, friend and philosopher to **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** of Parliament
  15. **Compilation & Maintenance of Accounts:** Compiles and maintains State Govt accounts (*relieved of Central Govt accounting duties in **1976** due to departmentalisation of accounts / separation of accounts from audit*)
* **Auditing Powers:**
  1. Inspect any office/department subject to audit
  2. Examine all transactions and question person-in-charge
  3. Call for any records, papers and documents
  4. Decide extent and manner of audit

## ROLE
* **Core Function:** Secure accountability of Executive (Council of Ministers) to Parliament in financial administration
* **3 Audit Reports Submitted to President:**
  1. Audit report on **Appropriation Accounts** (*compares actual expenditure with sanctioned expenditure*)
  2. Audit report on **Finance Accounts** (*shows annual receipts and disbursements*)
  3. Audit report on **Public Undertakings**
  * *Process:* Reports laid before Parliament → Examined by **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** → PAC reports findings to Parliament
* **Legal and Regulatory Audit vs. Propriety Audit:**
  * **Legal & Regulatory Audit (Obligatory):** Checks whether money disbursed was legally available, applied to authorised purpose and conformed to governing authority
  * **Propriety Audit (Discretionary):** Looks into "wisdom, faithfulness and economy" of expenditure; comments on wastefulness and extravagance
* **Performance Audit (2006 Ministry of Finance Memorandum):** Comprehensive appraisal of economy, efficiency and effectiveness in receipt and application of public funds
* **Scope Differences:**
  * CAG has full freedom in framing audit codes/manuals for **Expenditure**, but requires **Executive Government approval** for rules governing receipts, stores and stock audits
* **Limitations on Auditing Role:**
  * **Secret Service Expenditure:** CAG cannot call for particulars of expenditure incurred by executive agencies; must accept certificate from competent administrative authority
  * **Comptroller vs Auditor General:** In practice, CAG of India acts **ONLY as Auditor-General, NOT as Comptroller** (*has no control over money drawal from Consolidated Fund; cheques issued without prior CAG authority* vs **British CAG** who possesses full Comptroller powers where no money can be drawn without approval)

## CAG AND CORPORATIONS
* **Relationship with Public Corporations (3 Categories):**
  1. **Direct & Total Audit by CAG:** Air India, Indian Airlines, ONGC, Damodar Valley Corporation, Oil & Natural Gas Commission, etc.
  2. **Supplementary Audit by CAG:** Audited by private professional auditors appointed by Central Govt in consultation with CAG; CAG conducts supplementary audit (*e.g. Central Warehousing Corporation, Industrial Finance Corporation*)
  3. **Total Subject to Private Audit (CAG Excluded):** Audited exclusively by private professional auditors; submit annual reports directly to Parliament (*e.g. LIC, RBI, SBI, Food Corporation of India*)
* **Government Companies Audit:** Audited by private auditors appointed by Govt on advice of CAG; CAG can undertake **supplementary audit** or **test audit**

## APPLEBY’S CRITICISM
* **Critic = Paul H. Appleby** (American scholar of Public Administration; reports: *Public Administration in India (1953)* & *Re-examination of India’s Administrative System (1956)*)
* **Core Recommendation:** Recommended abolition of CAG office or relieving CAG of audit responsibilities
* **Key Points of Criticism:**
  1. Function of CAG is largely a **colonial inheritance**
  2. CAG is primary cause of **paralysing unwillingness to decide and act** (repressive and negative influence)
  3. Parliament has exaggerated notion of auditing importance
  4. Audit is a **pedestrian function** with narrow perspective; auditors do not know good administration
  5. Deputy Secretary in department knows more about departmental problems than CAG and entire staff

## CHALLENGES
* **Identified by:** **Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2nd ARC, 2005–2009)** in Report on *Strengthening Financial Management Systems*
* **Key Challenges & Impediments to External Audit Effectiveness:**
  1. **PAC Examination Pendency:** PAC examines only **15–20 paras** out of 1000–1500 paras in CAG reports annually; non-discussed paras get only formal Action Taken Notes
  2. **State Pendency:** Huge backlog in State PACs (**10 to 20 years old** pending paras)
  3. **Unattended Inspection Reports:** Thousands of reports lying unattended in departments without accountability
  4. **Post-Facto Nature & Time Gap:** Substantial lag between irregularity and reporting; audit is retrospective (*post-facto*)
  5. **Lack of Ground Verification:** Findings based exclusively on paper records without physical verification
  6. **Fault-Finding & Negative Focus:** Unduly negative, ignores practical constraints, acts as dampener against risk-taking/initiative
  7. **Lack of Constructive Approach:** Focuses on scattered observations rather than macro-level departmental view; does not credit good performance
  8. **Adversarial Relationship:** Auditor-auditee relation viewed as "policing" rather than management aid
  9. **Deficient Media & Public Interface:** Inspection reports not in public domain; poor civil society interface
  10. **Absence of Internal Audit Synergy:** Inadequate coordination between external and internal audit
  11. **NGO Audit Gap:** Rare audit of grants and loans to NGOs
