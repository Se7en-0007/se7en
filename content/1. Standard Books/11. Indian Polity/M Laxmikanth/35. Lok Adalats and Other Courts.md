## NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY (NALSA)
* **Constitutional Mandate:**
  * **Article 39A:** Free legal aid to poor & weaker sections; ensures justice for all
  * **Articles 14 & 22(1):** Obligatory for State to ensure equality before law & promote justice on equal opportunity basis
* **Enabling Act:** **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** (enacted by Parliament; came into force **9th November 1995**)
  * *Objective:* Establish nationwide uniform network for free & competent legal services to weaker sections
* **National Legal Services Authority (NALSA):** Constituted under 1987 Act to monitor/evaluate legal aid programmes & lay down policies
* **Hierarchy of Legal Services Authorities:**
  * **Central Level:** NALSA
  * **Supreme Court Level:** Supreme Court Legal Services Committee
  * **State Level:** State Legal Services Authority
  * **High Court Level:** High Court Legal Services Committee
  * **District Level:** District Legal Services Authority
  * **Taluk Level:** Taluk Legal Services Committee
* **Core Functions of Legal Services Authorities:**
  1. Provide free and competent legal services to eligible persons
  2. Organise Lok Adalats for amicable settlement of disputes
  3. Organise legal awareness camps in rural areas
* **Components of Free Legal Services:**
  1. Payment of court fees, process fees and all other charges
  2. Providing service of lawyers in legal proceedings
  3. Obtaining and supplying certified copies of orders/documents
  4. Preparation of appeal, paper book (including printing & translation)
* **Eligible Persons for Free Legal Services:**
  1. Women and children
  2. Members of SC/ST
  3. Industrial workmen
  4. Victims of mass disasters, violence, flood, drought, earthquake, industrial disasters
  5. Disabled persons
  6. Persons in custody
  7. Victims of human trafficking or beggar
  8. Persons with annual income ≤ **₹1 lakh** (limit is ≤ **₹5 lakh** for SC Legal Services Committee)

## LOK ADALATS

### Meaning
* **Concept:** Forum where pending court cases or pre-litigation disputes are compromised/settled amicably (*'Lok Adalat' = 'People's Court'*)
* **Foundation:** Ancient Indian adjudicating system based on **Gandhian principles**; key component of **Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)**
* **Approach:** Negotiation, conciliation, persuasive & human approach → No victors/vanquished, no rancour, informal, cheap, expeditious

### Features
* **First Post-Independence Camp:** Organised in **Gujarat in 1982** (voluntary/conciliatory agency without statutory backing)
* **Statutory Status:** Granted statutory status under **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**
* **Organisation:** Organised by NALSA, State/District Legal Services Authorities, SC/HC/Taluk Legal Services Committees
* **Composition:** Judicial officer (Chairman) + Lawyer (Advocate) + Social worker (Members)
* **Jurisdiction:**
  1. Any case pending before any court
  2. Any dispute falling within court jurisdiction at pre-litigation stage
  * *Exclusion:* **Non-compoundable offences** fall OUTSIDE Lok Adalat jurisdiction
* **Reference Criteria:**
  * *Pending Cases:* Both parties agree, OR one party applies, OR court satisfied matter appropriate
  * *Pre-Litigation:* Referred on application of any one party
* **Award & Finality:**
  * Deemed to be a **decree of a Civil Court** / order of any court
  * **Final and binding** on all parties
  * **No appeal** lies to any court against Lok Adalat award

### Types
1. **National Lok Adalats:** Held on single day at regular intervals across country (from SC to Taluk level); monthly focus on specific subject matters since 2015
2. **State Lok Adalats (Regular Lok Adalats):**
   * **Continuous Lok Adalat:** Sits continuously for set number of days
   * **Daily Lok Adalat:** Organised on daily basis
   * **Mobile Lok Adalat:** Multi-utility van travelling to different areas for petty cases & legal awareness
   * **Mega Lok Adalat:** Organised state-wide on a single day in all courts
3. **Permanent Lok Adalats:** Statutory body for public utility services

### Benefits
* No court fee (refunded if pending case settled)
* Procedural flexibility & speedy trial (no strict application of CPC or Evidence Act)
* Direct interaction between parties and judge through counsel
* Non-appealable binding decree → Prevents settlement delays
* **ADR Advantages (Law Commission):** Less expensive, less time-consuming, technicality-free, confidential discussions, win-win grievance redressal restoring relationships

## PERMANENT LOK ADALATS
* **Establishment:** Inserted by **2002 Amendment** to Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 for **Public Utility Services**
* **Composition:** Chairman (serving/retired District Judge or higher rank) + **2 members** having experience in public utility services
* **Covered Public Utility Services:** Passenger/goods transport (air, road, water); postal, telegraph, telephone; power, light, water supply; public conservancy/sanitation; hospitals/dispensaries; insurance
* **Pecuniary Jurisdiction:** Up to **₹1 Crore** (*increased from ₹10 lakh in 2015*)
* **Exclusion:** Non-compoundable offences
* **Pre-Litigation Mandatory Procedure:**
  * Party can apply before dispute brought to any court
  * Once application made, no party can invoke court jurisdiction in same dispute
  * PLA formulates terms of settlement → If agreement reached, passes award; if parties fail to agree, PLA **decides dispute on merits**
* **Award Status:** Final, binding on all parties, passed by **majority**

## FAMILY COURTS
* **Enabling Act:** **Family Courts Act, 1984**
* **Reasons for Setup:** Traditional civil courts used rigid adversarial approach → Specialized, speedy, conciliatory mechanism required

### Objectives
1. Specialized court exclusively for family matters (expertise + expeditious disposal)
2. Institute conciliation mechanism for family disputes
3. Inexpensive remedy
4. Flexibility and informal atmosphere

### Features
1. Established by State Governments in consultation with High Courts
2. **Mandatory Setup:** Mandatory in every city or town with population **exceeding 1 million (10 lakh)**
3. Optional setup in other areas by State Governments
4. **Exclusive Jurisdiction:**
   * Matrimonial relief (nullity, judicial separation, divorce, conjugal rights, marriage validity/status)
   * Property of spouses
   * Legitimacy of any person
   * Maintenance, guardianship and custody of infants/minors/dependants
1. Conciliation mandatory prior to trial
2. Rules of evidence relaxed; simplified procedure

## GRAM NYAYALAYAS
* **Enabling Act:** **Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008** (based on **114th Report of Law Commission of India**)
* **Objectives:** Constitutional mandate under **Article 39A** (justice at doorstep for rural, poor & disadvantaged)

### Features
1. **Court Rank:** Court of **Judicial Magistrate of First Class**
2. **Presiding Officer:** **Nyayadhikari** (appointed by State Govt in consultation with High Court)
   * *Status:* Strictly judicial officers drawing same salary & powers as First Class Magistrates
3. **Establishment Level:** Established for every **Panchayat at intermediate level** (or group of contiguous Panchayats)
4. **Mobile Nature:** Mobile court exercising both **Civil and Criminal** jurisdiction; seat at intermediate Panchayat headquarters, travels to villages
5. **Jurisdiction:** Tries criminal offences, civil suits, claims/disputes specified in First and Second Schedules of the Act
6. **Procedure:** Guided by natural justice principles; allows plea bargaining in criminal cases
7. **Appeals:**
   * *Criminal Cases:* Appeal lies to **Sessions Court** (disposed within 6 months)
   * *Civil Cases:* Appeal lies to **District Court** (disposed within 6 months)
8. **Establishment Status:** Setting up by State Governments is **optional / non-mandatory**

## COMMERCIAL COURTS
* **Enabling Act:** **Commercial Courts Act, 2015** (based on **253rd Report of Law Commission of India**)
* **Objectives:** Speedy disposal of high-value commercial disputes; improve investor confidence & international image of Indian justice delivery system

### Features
1. **Commercial Disputes Defined:** Broadly covers ordinary transactions of merchants, bankers, financiers, traders (mercantile documents, joint ventures, partnerships, IPR, insurance)
2. **Four-Tier Structure:**
   * **Commercial Courts:** Established at District level by State Govt (or at District Judge level in HCs with ordinary original civil jurisdiction)
   * **Commercial Appellate Courts:** Designated at District Judge level for courts below district judge rank
   * **Commercial Division:** Constituted in High Courts having ordinary original civil jurisdiction by Chief Justice
   * **Commercial Appellate Division:** Constituted in all High Courts by Chief Justice to hear appeals
3. **Pecuniary Jurisdiction:** Specified value not less than **₹3 lakh** (*reduced from ₹1 Crore by 2018 Amendment*)
4. **Compulsory Pre-Institution Mediation:** Mandatory **Pre-Institution Mediation and Settlement Mechanism** before filing suit (unless urgent interim relief sought); Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 authorities empowered for mediation
