## INTRODUCTION
* **Unique character:** Constitution of India is unique in spirit and contents; borrows from almost every world constitution but is highly distinguished
* **Major shifts:** Substantive changes made by **7th, 42nd, 44th, 73rd, 74th, 97th and 101st Amendments**
* **42nd Amendment Act (1976):** Known as **"Mini-Constitution"** due to massive changes
* **Kesavananda Bharati case (1973):** Supreme Court ruled that Article 368's constituent power does not permit altering the **"basic structure"** of the Constitution

## SALIENT FEATURES OF THE CONSTITUTION

### 1. Lengthiest Written Constitution
* **Classification:** Written (e.g., **USA**) vs Unwritten (e.g., **UK**)
* **Record length:** India's Constitution is the lengthiest of all written constitutions in the world
* **Factual comparison (Size growth):**
  * **1949 (Original):** Preamble + **395 Articles** (22 Parts) + **8 Schedules**
  * **Present:** Preamble + **~470 Articles** (25 Parts) + **12 Schedules**
  * **Net changes since 1951:** Deleted ~20 Articles & 1 Part (VII); added ~95 Articles, 4 Parts (**IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA**) and 4 Schedules (**9, 10, 11, 12**)
* **4 Contributory Factors to elephantine size:**
  1. **Geographical:** Country's vastness and extreme diversity
  2. **Historical:** Massive influence/size of the **Government of India Act of 1935**
  3. **Single Constitution:** Governs both Centre and States
  4. **Legal dominance:** Dominance of legal luminaries in the Constituent Assembly
* **Jammu & Kashmir Integration:** 
  * Till 2019, J&K had its own constitution under **Article 370**
  * **2019 Order ("The Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019"):** Superseded 1954 Order; extended all Indian constitutional provisions to J&K
  * **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019:** Bifurcated state into UT of Jammu & Kashmir and UT of Ladakh

### 2. Drawn from Various Sources
* **Ambedkar's claim:** Framed after **"ransacking all the known Constitutions of the World"**
* **Core structure origins:**
  * **Structural part:** **Government of India Act of 1935** (about 250 provisions included)
  * **Philosophical part:** **Fundamental Rights** (from **USA**) + **DPSP** (from **Ireland**)
  * **Political part:** Cabinet government & Executive-Legislative relations (from **Britain**)
  * *Other countries:* **Canada, Australia, Germany, USSR (now Russia), France, South Africa, Japan**

### 3. Blend of Rigidity and Flexibility
* **Classification:**
  * **Rigid:** Requires special amendment procedure (e.g., **USA**)
  * **Flexible:** Amended like ordinary laws (e.g., **UK**)
* **Synthesis:** Indian Constitution is neither purely rigid nor flexible, but a hybrid
* **Article 368 Amendment Types:**
  1. **Special Majority:** 2/3 of members present and voting + absolute majority (50%+) of total membership of each House
  2. **Special Majority + State Ratification:** Special majority of Parliament + ratification by half of total states
* **Simple Majority:** Some provisions can be amended by simple majority in normal legislative process (expressly excluded from Article 368)

### 4. Federal System with Unitary Bias
* **Federal Features:** Two governments, division of powers, written Constitution, supremacy of Constitution, rigidity of Constitution, independent judiciary, bicameralism
* **Unitary/Non-federal Features:** Strong Centre, single Constitution, single citizenship, flexibility of Constitution, integrated judiciary, appointment of state Governor by Centre, All-India Services, emergency provisions
* **Article 1:** India is a **"Union of States"** → implies:
  1. Indian federation is not the result of an agreement among states
  2. No state has the right to secede
* **Scholarly descriptions:**
  * **Quasi-federal:** **K.C. Wheare**
  * **Bargaining federalism:** **Morris Jones**
  * **Co-operative federalism:** **Granville Austin**
  * **Federation with a centralising tendency:** **Sir Ivor Jennings**

### 5. Parliamentary Form of Government
* **Choice:** Opted for British Parliamentary system (cooperation & coordination between executive and legislative organs) over American Presidential system (strict separation of powers)
* **Alternative names:** **Westminster Model**, Responsible Government, Cabinet Government
* **Key Features in India:**
  1. Nominal & Real executives (**President** vs **Prime Minister**)
  2. Majority party rule
  3. Collective responsibility of Executive to Legislature
  4. Membership of ministers in the legislature (non-legislators must get elected within 6 months)
  5. Leadership of PM (Centre) or CM (State)
  6. Dissolution of Lower House (Lok Sabha or Legislative Assemblies)
* **British vs. Indian Parliamentary System:**
  * Indian Parliament is **not a sovereign body** (unlike British Parliament)
  * Indian State has an **elected head** (Republic) vs British **hereditary head** (Monarchy)
* **Prime Ministerial Government:** Term used by political scientists due to dominant role of PM

### 6. Synthesis of Parliamentary Sovereignty and Judicial Supremacy
* **Origins:** Parliamentary Sovereignty (**Britain**) vs Judicial Supremacy (**USA**)
* **Synthesis in India:** Properly balanced
  * **Supreme Court:** Can declare Parliamentary laws unconstitutional via **judicial review**
  * **Parliament:** Can amend major portions of the Constitution via constituent power under Article 368
* **Scope difference:** Judicial review in India is narrower than in the US because of **"Procedure established by law"** (**Article 21**) vs US **"Due process of law"**

### 7. Integrated and Independent Judiciary
* **Integrated Hierarchy:** **Supreme Court (top)** → **High Courts (state level)** → **Subordinate Courts (district & lower courts)**
  * Single court system enforces both Central and State laws (unlike USA where federal and state judiciaries are separate)
* **Supreme Court roles:** Federal court, highest court of appeal, guarantor of Fundamental Rights, guardian of the Constitution
* **Independence Safeguards:**
  * Security of tenure for judges
  * Fixed service conditions
  * Expenses charged on **Consolidated Fund of India**
  * Prohibition of debate on judge conduct in legislatures
  * Ban on practice after retirement
  * Contempt power to punish
  * Separation of judiciary from executive (**Article 50**)

### 8. Fundamental Rights
* **Part III** guarantees **6 Fundamental Rights**:
  1. **Right to Equality** (Articles 14–18)
  2. **Right to Freedom** (Articles 19–22)
  3. **Right against Exploitation** (Articles 23–24)
  4. **Right to Freedom of Religion** (Articles 25–28)
  5. **Cultural and Educational Rights** (Articles 29–30)
  6. **Right to Constitutional Remedies** (Article 32)
* **Right to Property (Article 31):** Deleted by **44th Amendment Act (1978)**; made a legal right under **Article 300-A** (Part XII)
* **Objective:** Promotes political democracy; checks executive tyranny and legislative overreach
* **Nature:**
  * **Justiciable:** Enforceable by courts; aggrieved citizens can go directly to SC for writ issuance (**Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto**)
  * **Not absolute:** Subject to reasonable restrictions
  * **Not sacrosanct:** Can be curtailed/repealed by Parliament via amendment
  * **Suspension:** Suspended during National Emergency except **Articles 20 and 21**

### 9. Directive Principles of State Policy
* **novel feature:** Described by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** as a novel feature; located in **Part IV**
* **Classification:** **Socialistic**, **Gandhian** and **Liberal-Intellectual**
* **Objective:** Promote social & economic democracy; establish a **"Welfare State"**
* **Nature:** Non-justiciable (not legally enforceable by courts), but declared fundamental in governance; imposes a moral obligation on state
* **Minerva Mills case (1980):** Supreme Court held that the Constitution is built on the bedrock of balance between **Fundamental Rights** and **Directive Principles**

### 10. Fundamental Duties
* **Addition:** Not in original constitution; added by **42nd Amendment Act (1976)** during internal emergency on recommendation of **Swaran Singh Committee**
* **Part IVA (Article 51-A):** Vests **11 duties** (10 added in 1976; 11th added by **86th Amendment Act of 2002**)
* **Nature:** Non-justiciable civic reminders (respect flag/anthem, protect sovereignty/unity, preserve culture)

### 11. A Secular State
* **Concept:** No official state religion
* **Positive Secularism:** Gives equal respect and protection to all religions (unlike negative Western concept of complete separation of church and state)
* **Communal representation:** Abolished; temporary legislative seat reservation kept only for SCs/STs
* **Secular Provisions:**
  * **Preamble:** "Secular" added by **42nd Amendment (1976)**; guarantees liberty of belief, faith and worship
  * **Article 14:** Equality before law
  * **Article 15:** No discrimination based on religion
  * **Article 16:** Equality of public employment opportunity
  * **Article 25:** Freedom of conscience; right to profess, practice, propagate
  * **Article 26:** Right to manage religious affairs
  * **Article 27:** No taxation for promoting any religion
  * **Article 28:** No religious instruction in state-run educational institutions
  * **Article 29:** Right to conserve distinct language, script, or culture
  * **Article 30:** Right of minorities to establish/administer educational institutions
  * **Article 44:** Secure a **Uniform Civil Code**

### 12. Universal Adult Franchise
* **Elections basis:** Right to vote for LS and Assemblies without discrimination
* **Voting Age reduction:** Reduced from **21 to 18 years** by **61st Amendment Act of 1988** (effective **1989**)
* **Significance:** Bold experiment; broadens democracy, elevates self-respect, enforces equality, protects minorities, offers hope to weaker sections

### 13. Single Citizenship
* **Polity vs Citizenship:** Federal dual-polity but only **single Indian citizenship** (unlike dual citizenship of **USA** where citizens hold state + federal rights and duties)
* **Goal:** Unity and integration (though communal riots, caste wars and conflicts indicate integration remains incomplete)

### 14. Independent Bodies
* **Bulwarks of Indian democracy:**
  1. **Election Commission (EC):** Free/fair elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President, Vice-President
  2. **Comptroller and Auditor-General (CAG):** Audits central/state accounts; guardian of public purse
  3. **Union Public Service Commission (UPSC):** Recruitment exams for All-India and central services; advises President
  4. **State Public Service Commissions (SPSC):** State-level exams; advises Governor
* **Safeguards:** Security of tenure, fixed service conditions, expenses charged on Consolidated Fund

### 15. Emergency Provisions
* **Objective:** Safeguard sovereignty, unity, integrity, security of country, democratic political system and Constitution
* **3 Categories (President's declaration):**
  1. **National Emergency (Article 352):** War, external aggression, or armed rebellion (armed rebellion replaced 'internal disturbance' via **44th Amendment in 1978**)
  2. **State Emergency / President's Rule (Article 356 & 365):** Constitutional machinery failure in states / non-compliance with Centre's directives
  3. **Financial Emergency (Article 360):** Threat to financial stability/credit of India
* **Federal-to-Unitary shift:** Centre gains absolute power; federal structure transitions to unitary without formal amendment

### 16. Three-tier Government
* **Uniqueness:** Vests a third local level of government, unknown in other major federal constitutions
* **73rd Amendment Act (1992):** Constitutionalized **Panchayats** (rural) → Added **Part IX** and **Schedule 11** (village, intermediate and district levels)
* **74th Amendment Act (1992):** Constitutionalized **Municipalities** (urban) → Added **Part IX-A** and **Schedule 12** (nagar panchayat, municipal council and municipal corporation)

### 17. Co-operative Societies
* **97th Amendment Act (2011):** Provided constitutional status and protection
* **3 Major changes:**
  1. Form co-operatives made a Fundamental Right (**Article 19**)
  2. Promotion of co-operatives added as DPSP (**Article 43-B**)
  3. Added **Part IX-B** ("The Co-operative Societies", **Articles 243-ZH to 243-ZT**)

## CRITICISM OF THE CONSTITUTION

### 1. A Borrowed Constitution
* **Charges:** "Bag of borrowings", "hotch-potch", "patchwork", "blind copy"
* **Ambedkar's defense:** Main provisions must look similar in all world constitutions framed so late; changes were variations to remove faults and accommodate India's specific needs

### 2. A Carbon Copy of the 1935 Act
* **Charges:** Textual plagiarism of Government of India Act of 1935
  * **N. Srinivasan:** Close copy in language and substance
  * **Sir Ivor Jennings:** Copied textually in many provisions
  * **P.R. Deshmukh:** Simply the 1935 Act with adult franchise added
* **Ambedkar's defense:** No apologies; nobody holds patent rights in constitutional ideas; expressed regret only that borrowed provisions relate mostly to details of administration

### 3. Un-Indian or Anti-Indian
* **Charges:** Foreign nature makes it unworkable; doesn't reflect political traditions/spirit
  * **K. Hanumanthaiya:** Music of an English band instead of Veena or Sitar
  * **Lokanath Misra:** "Slavish imitation of the west... slavish surrender"
  * **Lakshminarayan Sahu:** No manifest relation to India's fundamental spirit; bound to break down

### 4. An Un-Gandhian Constitution
* **Charges:** Lacks philosophy of Mahatma Gandhi; should have built from village/district panchayats upward
  * **K. Hanumanthaiya:** Exactly what Gandhi did not want/envisage
  * **T. Prakasam:** Attributed to Ambedkar's non-participation in Gandhian movement and antagonism to Gandhian ideas

### 5. Elephantine Size
* **Charges:** Too bulky, detailed and contains redundant elements
  * **Sir Ivor Jennings:** Too long, complicated and poorly-selected borrowings
  * **H.V. Kamath:** Assembly's elephant emblem is in consonance with our bulkiest constitution

### 6. Paradise of the Lawyers
* **Charges:** Legalistic, complex language, padding and verbiage promote litigation
  * **Sir Ivor Jennings:** Known as a "lawyer’s paradise"
  * **H.K. Maheswari:** Promotes litigious nature, less truth, opens vast legal avenues
  * **P.R. Deshmukh:** Ponderous manual rather than a life-giving socio-political document; excessive verbiage

## Schedules of The Constitution

* **First Schedule:**
  1. Names of States and their territorial jurisdiction
  2. Names of Union Territories and their extent
  * *Articles Covered:* 1 and 4
* **Second Schedule:** Emoluments, allowances, privileges, etc., of:
  1. President of India
  2. Governors of States
  3. Speaker & Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha
  4. Chairman & Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha
  5. Speaker & Deputy Speaker of State Legislative Assemblies
  6. Chairman & Deputy Chairman of State Legislative Councils
  7. Judges of Supreme Court
  8. Judges of High Courts
  9. Comptroller and Auditor-General of India
  * *Articles Covered:* 59, 65, 75, 97, 125, 148, 158, 164, 186 and 221
* **Third Schedule:** Forms of Oaths or Affirmations for:
  1. Union ministers
  2. Candidates for Parliamentary elections
  3. Members of Parliament (MPs)
  4. Supreme Court Judges
  5. Comptroller and Auditor-General of India
  6. State ministers
  7. Candidates for state legislature elections
  8. Members of state legislature (MLAs/MLCs)
  9. High Court Judges
  * *Articles Covered:* 75, 84, 99, 124, 148, 164, 173, 188 and 219
* **Fourth Schedule:** Allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha to States and UTs (Articles 4 and 80)
* **Fifth Schedule:** Provisions relating to administration and control of scheduled areas & scheduled tribes (Article 244)
* **Sixth Schedule:** Provisions relating to administration of tribal areas in **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram** (Articles 244 and 275)
* **Seventh Schedule:** Division of powers between Union and States via 3 Lists (Article 246):
  * **Union List (List I):** **98 subjects** (originally 97)
  * **State List (List II):** **59 subjects** (originally 66)
  * **Concurrent List (List III):** **52 subjects** (originally 47)
* **Eighth Schedule:** Languages recognized by the Constitution (originally 14, presently **22**):
  * Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri (Dongri), Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santhali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu
  * *Amendments:*
    * **Sindhi** added by **21st Amendment (1967)**
    * **Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali** added by **71st Amendment (1992)**
    * **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali** added by **92nd Amendment (2003)**
    * **Oriya** renamed **Odia** by **96th Amendment (2011)**
  * *Articles Covered:* 344 and 351
* **Ninth Schedule:** Acts & Regulations of state legislatures dealing with land reforms/abolition of zamindari and of Parliament dealing with other matters (originally 13, presently **282**)
  * *Amendments:* Added by **1st Amendment (1951)** to protect from judicial review on FR violation
  * *Judicial Review:* SC ruled that laws added after **April 24, 1973** (Kesavananda Bharati judgment) are open to judicial review
  * *Article Covered:* 31-B
* **Tenth Schedule:** Provisions on disqualification of members (Parliament/State Legislatures) on ground of defection (**Anti-defection Law**)
  * *Amendments:* Added by **52nd Amendment Act of 1985**
  * *Articles Covered:* 102 and 191
* **Eleventh Schedule:** Powers, authority and responsibilities of **Panchayats** (contains **29 matters**)
  * *Amendments:* Added by **73rd Amendment Act of 1992**
  * *Article Covered:* 243-G
* **Twelfth Schedule:** Powers, authority and responsibilities of **Municipalities** (contains **18 matters**)
  * *Amendments:* Added by **74th Amendment Act of 1992**
  * *Article Covered:* 243-W

| **1. Government of India Act of 1935** | Federal Scheme, Office of Governor, Judiciary, Public Service Commissions, Emergency provisions, Advisory Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court, administrative details |
