## ESTABLISHMENT
* **Constitutional Status:** Statutory (non-constitutional) body established in **1993** under **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**
* **Role:** Watchdog of human rights in the country (rights relating to life, liberty, equality and dignity guaranteed by Constitution or embodied in International Covenants)
* **International Covenants Covered:**
  * **ICCPR** (International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights)
  * **ICESCR** (International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights)
  * *Timeline:* Adopted by UN General Assembly **Dec 16, 1966**; acceded by India **April 10, 1979**
* **3 Specific Objectives:**
  1. Strengthen institutional arrangements to address human rights issues in a focused manner
  2. Look into allegations of excesses independently of government
  3. Complement and strengthen existing human rights protection efforts

---

## COMPOSITION
* **Multi-Member Body:** **1 Chairperson + 5 Full-Time Members + 7 Ex-Officio Members**
* **Chairperson:** Retired **Chief Justice of India** OR a **Judge of the Supreme Court**
* **5 Full-Time Members:**
  1. Serving or retired Judge of the Supreme Court
  2. Serving or retired Chief Justice of a High Court
  3. 3 persons having knowledge/practical experience in human rights (*at least 1 must be a woman*)
* **7 Ex-Officio Members (Chairpersons/Commissioners of):**
  1. National Commission for Minorities
  2. National Commission for SCs
  3. National Commission for STs
  4. National Commission for Women
  5. National Commission for BCs
  6. National Commission for Protection of Child Rights
  7. Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities
* **Selection Committee (6 Members):** Appointed by **President** on recommendations of:
  1. **Prime Minister** (Head)
  2. **Speaker of Lok Sabha**
  3. **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha**
  4. **Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha**
  5. **Leader of Opposition in Rajya Sabha**
  6. **Union Home Minister**
  * *CJI Consultation:* Appointment of sitting SC Judge or sitting High Court CJ requires **prior consultation with Chief Justice of India**
* **Tenure:** **3 years** or until attaining **70 years of age** (whichever is earlier); **eligible for re-appointment**
* **Post-Tenure Restriction:** Ineligible for further employment under Central or State Government
* **Removal by President:**
  * *Direct Removal Grounds:* Adjudged insolvent, paid employment outside duties, infirmity of mind/body, declared unsound mind, convicted & sentenced to imprisonment
  * *Proved Misbehaviour / Incapacity:* Matter referred to **Supreme Court** for inquiry → If SC upholds removal, President removes
* **Service Conditions:** Determined by **Central Government** (*cannot be varied to disadvantage post-appointment*)

## FUNCTIONS
1. **Inquire Complaints:** Inquire into human rights violation or negligence by public servant (**suo motu**, on petition, or court order)
2. **Court Intervention:** Intervene in court proceedings involving human rights violation allegations
3. **Jail Visits:** Visit jails/detention centers to study living conditions of inmates and recommend remedial measures
4. **Review Safeguards:** Review constitutional/legal human rights safeguards and recommend effective implementation
5. **Anti-Terrorism Review:** Review factors inhibiting human rights (including terrorism) and recommend remedial measures
6. **Treaty Studies:** Study human rights treaties and international instruments for effective implementation
7. **Research:** Undertake and promote research in human rights field
8. **Literacy & Awareness:** Spread human rights literacy and promote awareness of available safeguards
9. **NGO Encouragement:** Encourage efforts of non-governmental organisations (NGOs) working in human rights field
10. **Promotional Functions:** Conduct camp sittings, open hearings and awareness initiatives

## WORKING
* **Headquarters:** **Delhi** (empowered to establish offices elsewhere in India)
* **Procedural Powers:** Vested with powers of a **civil court**; regulates its own procedure; proceedings have judicial character
* **Information Seeking:** Can call for reports/info from Central/State Governments or subordinate authorities
* **Investigating Staff:** Has its own nucleus of investigating staff; can utilize services of any Central/State officer or agency
* **Limitation Period:** Cannot inquire into any matter after expiry of **1 year** from date of alleged violation (*1-year bar*)
* **4 Action Steps Post-Inquiry:**
  1. Recommend compensation/damages to victim
  2. Recommend prosecution or departmental action against guilty public servant
  3. Recommend immediate interim relief to victim
  4. Approach Supreme Court or concerned High Court for directions, orders, or writs

---

## ROLE
* **Recommendatory Nature:** Recommendations **NOT binding** on government/authority (*no power to punish violators or directly award monetary relief*)
* **Action Taken Response:** Concerned government/authority **must inform Commission within 1 month** of action taken on recommendations
* **Moral & Material Authority:** Justice V.S. Malimath noted: Govt cannot ignore recommendations; enjoys immense material authority
* **Armed Forces Exception:**
  * Limited jurisdiction over armed forces (*Navy, Army, Air Force, Union armed forces*)
  * Seeks report from Central Govt → Makes recommendations → Central Govt must inform action taken within **3 months**
* **Annual & Special Reports:** Submitted to Central and concerned State Governments → Laid before respective legislatures with memorandum of action taken and reasons for non-acceptance

---

## PROBLEMS
* **1. Administrative Constraints:**
  * Paris Principles mandate adequate funding and staff for independence
  * Handles **~1 lakh complaints annually** → Requires administrative/financial devolution to Chairperson
* **2. Financial Constraints:**
  * Receives grants-in-aid from Ministry of Home Affairs
  * Lacks financial autonomy in specific areas (e.g. vehicle procurement delegation)
* **3. Manpower Constraints:**
  * Never operated at full sanctioned strength; relies on retired government officers as contract consultants
  * Shortage of experienced investigating staff affects spot enquiries (custodial deaths, torture, fake encounters)
  * Shortage in **Law Division** hinders processing/disposal of complaints
  * **2017 Supreme Court Intervention:** Expressed concern over manpower shortage and directed GOI to remedy concerns at the earliest
