## INTRODUCTION
* **Concept:** System of rural local self-government in India; established by State Acts to build democracy at grassroot level & entrust rural development
* **Constitutional Entry:** **Seventh Schedule, State List (Entry 5)** = ‘Local Government’
* **Constitutionalisation:** Constitutionalised through **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992**

---

## EVOLUTION OF PANCHAYATI RAJ

### Balwantrai Mehta Committee
* **Setup:** Appointed **January 1957** by GOI to examine Community Development Programme (1952) & National Extension Service (1953); Chairman = **Balwantrai G. Mehta**; submitted report **November 1957**
* **Core Concept:** Scheme of **‘democratic decentralisation’** (became known as Panchayati Raj)
* **Key Recommendations:**
  1. **3-Tier System:** Gram Panchayat at village level (directly elected), Panchayat Samiti at block level (indirectly elected), Zila Parishad at district level (indirectly elected); organically linked
  2. **Role Division:** Panchayat Samiti = **executive body**; Zila Parishad = **advisory, coordinating & supervisory body**
  3. **Collector's Role:** **District Collector** as Chairman of Zila Parishad
  4. All planning and development activities entrusted to PR bodies; genuine transfer of power & adequate resources
* **Acceptance & Rollout:**
  * Accepted by **National Development Council (NDC) in January 1958** (allowed state flexibility)
  * **First State:** **Rajasthan** inaugurated system on **October 2, 1959** in **Nagaur district** (by PM Jawaharlal Nehru)
  * **Second State:** **Andhra Pradesh** (1959)
* **State Patterns / Differences:**
  * *Tiers:* 3-tier (Rajasthan), 2-tier (Tamil Nadu), 4-tier (West Bengal)
  * *Power Center:* Panchayat Samiti powerful (Rajasthan–AP pattern, block = unit); Zila Parishad powerful (Maharashtra–Gujarat pattern, district = unit)
  * *Nyaya Panchayats:* Judicial panchayats created in some states for petty civil/criminal cases

### Study Teams and Committees (1960–1976)
*(Listed in Table below)*

### Ashok Mehta Committee
* **Setup:** Appointed **December 1977** by Janata Government; submitted report **August 1978** with **132 recommendations**
* **Key Recommendations:**
  1. **2-Tier System:** **Zila Parishad** at district level + **Mandal Panchayat** (group of villages with population 15,000 to 20,000) (*replacing 3-tier system*)
  2. **District Unit:** District as first point for decentralisation; Zila Parishad as executive body for district planning
  3. **Political Parties:** Official participation of political parties at all levels
  4. **Taxation & Finance:** Compulsory taxation powers; regular **social audit** by district agency & committee of legislators
  5. **Supersession Limit:** Elections within **6 months** if superseded
  6. **Nyaya Panchayats:** Kept separate from development panchayats; presided by qualified judge
  7. **Elections:** Conducted by Chief Electoral Officer in consultation with CEC
  8. **Minister for PR:** Minister for Panchayati Raj in State Council of Ministers
  9. **Reservations & Status:** Population-based SC/ST reservations; **constitutional recognition** to PRIs
* **Outcome:** Janata Govt collapsed before action at Centre; Karnataka, West Bengal & Andhra Pradesh adopted key recommendations

### G.V.K. Rao Committee
* **Setup:** Appointed **1985** by Planning Commission under chairmanship of **G.V.K. Rao**
* **Core Finding:** Bureaucratisation of development administration divorced from PRIs → Created **‘grass without roots’**
* **Key Recommendations:**
  1. **Zila Parishad as Pivot:** Zila Parishad principal body for managing district planning & development
  2. **District Development Commissioner (DDC):** Post created to act as CEO of Zila Parishad & in-charge of district development departments
  3. Regular elections (found overdue in 11 states)
* **Comparison with Other Planning Committees:**
  * *G.V.K. Rao Committee:* Reduced developmental role of Collector; assigned major role to PRIs
  * *Dantwala Committee (1978) & Hanumantha Rao Committee (1984):* Advocated Collector-centric district planning body

### L.M. Singhvi Committee
* **Setup:** Appointed **1986** by Rajiv Gandhi Govt on ‘Revitalisation of PRIs for Democracy and Development’
* **Key Recommendations:**
  1. **Constitutional Recognition:** New chapter in Constitution for protection & preservation
  2. **Gram Sabha:** Embodiment of direct democracy
  3. **Nyaya Panchayats:** Established for a cluster of villages
  4. Reorganisation of villages to make Gram Panchayats viable + more financial resources
  5. Judicial tribunals in each state for PR election/dissolution disputes

### Thungon Committee
* **Setup:** Constituted **1988** as sub-committee of Parliamentary Consultative Committee under **P.K. Thungon**
* **Key Recommendations:**
  1. Constitutional recognition; 3-tier system with Zila Parishad as pivot
  2. Fixed **5-year tenure**; max supersession = **6 months**
  3. State-level planning & coordination committee chaired by Planning Minister with ZP Presidents as members
  4. Detailed constitutional list of subjects; population-based SC/ST/women reservations
  5. State Finance Commission; Collector as CEO of Zila Parishad

### Gadgil Committee
* **Setup:** Constituted **1988** by Congress party under chairmanship of **V.N. Gadgil** (Committee on Policy and Programmes)
* **Key Recommendations:**
  1. Constitutional status; 3-tier system; 5-year fixed term; direct elections at all 3 levels
  2. Reservation for SCs, STs and women
  3. Preparation & implementation of socio-economic plans (constitutional list of subjects)
  4. Taxation powers + **State Finance Commission** + **State Election Commission**
* **Significance:** Gadgil Committee recommendations formed the **direct basis** for drafting the constitutional amendment bill

## CONSTITUTIONALISATION

### Legislative History
1. **Rajiv Gandhi Government (July 1989):** Introduced **64th Constitutional Amendment Bill** in Lok Sabha; passed by LS (Aug 1989), **rejected by Rajya Sabha** (opposed as weakening federalism/centralising)
2. **V.P. Singh Government (Sept 1990):** CMs Conference (June 1990) approved fresh bill; introduced in LS (Sept 1990); **lapsed** due to government fall
3. **Narasimha Rao Government (Sept 1991):** Introduced modified bill in Lok Sabha
   * Passed Lok Sabha: **22 December 1992**
   * Passed Rajya Sabha: **23 December 1992**
   * Approved by **17 State Assemblies**
   * Presidential Assent: **20 April 1993**
   * Came into Force: **24 April 1993** (*enacted as **73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992***)

## 73RD CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT ACT OF 1992

### Significance of the Act
* **Constitutional Additions:** Added **Part-IX** (*‘The Panchayats’*, **Articles 243 to 243-O**) and **Eleventh Schedule** (**29 functional items**, **Article 243-G**)
* **DPSP Realisation:** Gives practical shape to **Article 40** (*Directive Principles of State Policy*)
* **Democratic Transformation:** Transforms representative democracy into **participatory democracy** at grassroot level
* **Justiciable Obligation:** Brings PRIs under justiciable part of Constitution; states under constitutional mandate to adopt

### Salient Features

#### 1. Gram Sabha (Article 243A)
* Foundation of PR system; body of all persons **registered in electoral rolls** of village within panchayat area
* Exercises powers & performs functions as State Legislature determines

#### 2. Three-Tier System (Article 243B)
* Village, intermediate and district levels throughout the country
* *Exemption:* State having population **not exceeding 20 lakh** may NOT constitute intermediate level

#### 3. Election of Members and Chairpersons (Article 243C)
* **Members:** All members at all 3 levels elected **directly** by the people
* **Chairpersons:**
  * *Intermediate & District Levels:* Elected **indirectly** (by & from amongst elected members)
  * *Village Level:* Elected in manner determined by **State Legislature**
* **Voting Right:** Chairpersons and members (directly or indirectly elected) have voting rights in meetings

#### 4. Reservation of Seats (Article 243D)
* **SCs and STs:** Reserved in proportion to population at all 3 levels (both members & chairperson offices)
  * *Arunachal Pradesh Exception:* **83rd CAA (2000)** exempted Arunachal Pradesh from SC reservation (fully indigenous tribal population)
  * *Tenure Limit:* Ceases per **Article 334** period (currently **80 years, till 2030**)
* **Women:** Not less than **1/3rd of total seats and chairperson offices** reserved for women (including SC/ST women)
* **Backward Classes:** State Legislature authorized to reserve seats/chairperson offices for OBCs

#### 5. Duration of Panchayats (Article 243E)
* **Term:** **5 years** from date of first meeting
* **Dissolution & Reconstitution:** Elections must be completed before 5-year expiry OR within **6 months** of premature dissolution
  * *Remainder Rule:* Reconstituted panchayat continues **ONLY for remainder of period**
  * *Six-Month Rule:* If remainder period is **less than 6 months**, no election required for remainder period

#### 6. Disqualifications (Article 243F)
* Disqualified under any state election law or State Legislature law
* **Minimum Age:** **21 years** (cannot be disqualified for being < 25 if attained 21 years)
* Decided by authority determined by State Legislature

#### 7. State Election Commission (Article 243K)
* **Appointment:** **State Election Commissioner** appointed by **Governor**
* **Removal:** Removed **ONLY like a High Court Judge** (by President on parliamentary address); service conditions not varied to disadvantage

#### 8. Powers and Functions (Article 243G)
* State Legislature endows powers/authority for self-government, including 29 matters of **Eleventh Schedule** (preparation of plans & implementation of schemes for economic development and social justice)

#### 9. Finances (Article 243H)
* State Legislature may authorise tax levy/collection/appropriation, assign state taxes, provide grants-in-aid from Consolidated Fund of State and create panchayat funds

#### 10. State Finance Commission (Article 243-I)
* Constituted every **5 years** by **Governor** to review financial position & recommend:
  1. Distribution of net tax proceeds between State and Panchayats & allocation among tiers
  2. Determination of taxes/duties assigned to Panchayats
  3. Grants-in-aid from Consolidated Fund of State
* Report placed before State Legislature; **Central Finance Commission** augments State Consolidated Fund based on SFC recommendations

#### 11. Audit of Accounts (Article 243J)
* State Legislature provides for maintenance and auditing of accounts

#### 12. Application to Union Territories (Article 243L)
* Applies to UTs; President may specify exceptions/modifications

#### 13. Exempted States and Areas (Article 243M)
* **Exempted States:** **Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram**
* **Exempted Areas:** Scheduled areas & tribal areas; Hill areas of Manipur (with district councils); Darjeeling district of West Bengal (Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council)
* *Parliamentary Extension:* Parliament empowered to extend to Scheduled/Tribal areas → Enacted **PESA Act 1996**

#### 14. Continuance of Existing Laws and Panchayats (Article 243N)
* State laws to align within **1 year** (by **24 April 1994**); existing panchayats continue till term expiry unless dissolved sooner

#### 15. Bar to Interference by Courts in Electoral Matters (Article 243-O)
* Delimitation & seat allocation non-questionable in courts; elections challenged **ONLY via election petition** before designated state authority

## ELEVENTH SCHEDULE (ARTICLE 243G)
* **29 Functional Items:**
  1. Agriculture, including agricultural extension
  2. Land improvement, land reforms implementation, land consolidation, soil conservation
  3. Minor irrigation, water management, watershed development
  4. Animal husbandry, dairying, poultry
  5. Fisheries
  6. Social forestry and farm forestry
  7. Minor forest produce
  8. Small-scale industries, including food processing
  9. Khadi, village and cottage industries
  10. Rural housing
  11. Drinking water
  12. Fuel and fodder
  13. Roads, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways, communication means
  14. Rural electrification, including electricity distribution
  15. Non-conventional energy sources
  16. Poverty alleviation programme
  17. Education, including primary and secondary schools
  18. Technical training and vocational education
  19. Adult and non-formal education
  20. Libraries
  21. Cultural activities
  22. Markets and fairs
  23. Health and sanitation (hospitals, PHCs, dispensaries)
  24. Family welfare
  25. Women and child development
  26. Social welfare (welfare of handicapped & mentally retarded)
  27. Welfare of weaker sections (SC/ST)
  28. Public distribution system (PDS)
  29. Maintenance of community assets

## COMPULSORY AND VOLUNTARY PROVISIONS

### Compulsory Provisions (Mandatory)
1. Organisation of **Gram Sabha** in a village or group of villages
2. Establishment of panchayats at village, intermediate and district levels
3. **Direct elections** to all seats at all 3 levels
4. **Indirect elections** to chairperson post at intermediate & district levels
5. Voting rights of chairperson and all members
6. Minimum age of **21 years** for contesting elections
7. Reservation of seats for **SCs and STs** (members & chairpersons)
8. Reservation of **1/3rd seats** for women (members & chairpersons)
9. Fixed **5-year tenure** & fresh elections within **6 months** of supersession
10. Establishment of **State Election Commission**
11. Constitution of **State Finance Commission** every 5 years

### Voluntary Provisions (Discretionary)
1. Endowing Gram Sabha with powers and functions
2. Manner of electing chairperson of village panchayat
3. Representation of village chairpersons in intermediate/district panchayats
4. Representation of intermediate chairpersons in district panchayats
5. Representation of MPs and MLAs/MLCs in panchayats at different levels
6. Reservation of seats/chairpersons for **Backward Classes (OBCs)**
7. Granting autonomous powers to function as self-government institutions
8. Devolution of powers to prepare plans & perform 29 Eleventh Schedule functions
9. Granting financial powers (levying, collecting, appropriating taxes/tolls)
10. Assigning state taxes/duties to panchayats
11. Making grants-in-aid from Consolidated Fund of State
12. Creating specific funds for crediting panchayat moneys

## PESA ACT OF 1996 (EXTENSION ACT)

### Background and Applicability
* **Full Title:** Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (**PESA Act / Extension Act**)
* **Assent Date:** **24 December 1996**
* **Coverage:** Extends Part IX provisions to **Fifth Schedule Areas** in **10 States**: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan

### Objectives of PESA Act
1. Extend Part IX to Scheduled Areas with necessary modifications
2. Provide self-rule for bulk of tribal population
3. Village governance with participatory democracy (Gram Sabha as nucleus)
4. Preserve traditions and customs of tribal communities
5. Empower panchayats at appropriate levels with tribal-specific powers
6. Prevent higher-level panchayats from assuming powers of lower level / Gram Sabha

### Key Features / Provisions of PESA Act
1. **Customary Law Conformity:** State legislation on Panchayats in Scheduled Areas must accord with customary law, social/religious practices and traditional management of community resources
2. **Gram Sabha Nucleus:** Every village has a Gram Sabha (all registered voters); competent to preserve traditions, customs, cultural identity, community resources and customary dispute resolution
3. **Development Approval & Beneficiary Selection:** Gram Sabha approves plans/programmes before implementation & identifies beneficiaries for poverty alleviation programmes
4. **Fund Utilisation Certification:** Village Panchayat must obtain Gram Sabha certification for fund utilisation
5. **Reservations:**
   * SC/ST reservation in proportion to population
   * ST reservation **shall NOT be less than 50% (1/2)** of total seats
   * **ALL seats of Chairpersons** at all levels reserved for Scheduled Tribes
   * State Govt may nominate unrepresented STs at intermediate/district levels (max **1/10th** of total elected members)
6. **Mandatory Consultations:**
   * Gram Sabha / Panchayat consulted before **land acquisition** & resettlement/rehabilitation in Scheduled Areas
   * Planning & management of **minor water bodies** entrusted to Panchayats
7. **Mandatory Recommendations:** Gram Sabha / Panchayat recommendation mandatory for granting **prospecting licence / mining lease** for minor minerals & auction for minor mineral concessions
8. **Specific Powers Endowed to Gram Sabha & Panchayats:**
   * Enforce prohibition or regulate/restrict intoxicant sale/consumption
   * **Ownership of minor forest produce**
   * Prevent land alienation & restore unlawfully alienated ST land
   * Manage village markets
   * Control money lending to STs
   * Control social sector institutions & functionaries
   * Control local plans and resources (including tribal sub-plans)
9. **Administrative Pattern:** State Legislature shall endeavour to follow **Sixth Schedule pattern** for district-level administrative arrangements

## FINANCES OF PANCHAYATI RAJ

### Sources of Funds (2nd ARC Observations)
1. Grants from Union Govt based on **Central Finance Commission** recommendations (**Article 280**)
2. Devolution from State Govt based on **State Finance Commission** recommendations (**Article 243-I**)
3. State Government loans / grants
4. Program-specific allocations under **Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS)** & Additional Central Assistance
5. Internal Resource Generation (tax and non-tax)

### Financial Problems & Constraints
1. **Weak Internal Generation:** Thin tax domain & local reluctance to collect revenue
2. **Grant Dependency:** Overwhelming dependence on Union & State grants
3. **Tied Grants:** Major grants are scheme-specific → Limited expenditure discretion
4. **State Reluctance:** State Governments reluctant to devolve funds due to tight fiscal situation
5. **Incomplete Transfer:** Key 11th Schedule subjects (education, health, sanitation) still executed directly by State departments
6. **Core Revenue Disparity:** Gram Panchayats have property/house tax, profession tax, land tax/cess, vehicle tax, license fees; Intermediate & District Panchayats restricted to non-tax/fees (ferry, market, vehicle registration)

---

## REASONS FOR INEFFECTIVE PERFORMANCE
1. **Lack of Adequate Devolution:** Inadequate transfer of **3Fs (Functions, Funds, Functionaries)**; partial implementation of SFC recommendations
2. **Excessive Bureaucratic Control:** Gram Panchayats subordinate to block offices; Sarpanches waste time seeking approvals
3. **Tied Nature of Funds:** Rigid scheme rules prevent local customization & cause under-spending
4. **Overwhelming Dependency on Govt Funding:** Lack of community ownership & absence of social audit requests
5. **Reluctance to Use Fiscal Powers:** Local reluctance of elected representatives to tax neighbours/constituents
6. **Weak Status of Gram Sabha:** Lack of clear powers, operational procedures and penalties in State Acts
7. **Creation of Parallel Bodies:** Missions & parallel agencies bypass PRIs, usurp legitimate space, create disconnect & alienate local bodies
8. **Poor Infrastructure & Capacity:** Absence of full-time secretaries, office buildings, data; illiterate/semi-literate representatives lacking training
