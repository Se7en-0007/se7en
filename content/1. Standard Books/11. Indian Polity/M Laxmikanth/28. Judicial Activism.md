## INTRODUCTION
* **Origin & Development:** Originated and developed in **USA**
* **Coining of Term:** First coined in **1947** by **Arthur Schlesinger Jr.** (American historian and educator) in *Fortune* magazine article
* **Introduction in India:** Introduced in mid-1970s
* **Pioneers in India:** **Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer**, **Justice P.N. Bhagwati**, **Justice O. Chinnappa Reddy** and **Justice D.A. Desai** (laid foundations of judicial activism)

---

## MEANING OF JUDICIAL ACTIVISM
* **Definition:** Proactive role played by judiciary in protection of rights of citizens and promotion of justice in society
* **Core Implication:** Assertive role played by judiciary forcing legislature and executive to discharge constitutional duties
* **Synonym:** Also known as **“Judicial Dynamism”** (antithesis of **“Judicial Restraint”**)
* **Key Definitions & Perspectives:**
  1. **Black’s Law Dictionary:** Mode of exercising judicial power motivating judges to depart from strict adherence to precedent in favour of progressive social policies; social engineering decisions
  2. **Judicial Legislation:** Concept describing judges making positive law / moulding law to suit changing socio-economic scenarios
  3. **Expanded Locus Standi:** Liberalisation of *Locus Standi* in **Public Interest Litigation (PIL)** expanded court jurisdictional limits
  4. **Relationship with Judicial Review:** Falls under broader rubric of judicial review; court intervening and striking down duly enacted legislation
  5. **2 Major Aspects in India:**
     * **Directions to Authorities:** Issuing directions for citizen rights protection and public interest fulfillment (PIL cases)
     * **FR Interpretation:** Expanding scope of Fundamental Rights (**Article 14** Right to Equality, **Article 19** Right to Freedom, **Article 21** Right to Life & Personal Liberty)

## JUSTIFICATION OF JUDICIAL ACTIVISM

### Reasons According to Dr. B.L. Wadehra (5 Points)
1. **Government Collapse:** Near collapse of responsible government when Legislature and Executive fail to discharge functions → Erosion of citizen confidence in Constitution & democracy
2. **Citizen Recourse:** Suffering citizens look up to judiciary for rights and freedoms protection → Pressure on judiciary to step in
3. **Judicial Enthusiasm:** Judges desire to participate in socio-economic reforms → Encourages PIL and liberalises *Locus Standi*
4. **Legislative Vacuum:** Unlegislated areas compel court to indulge in judicial legislation to meet changing social needs
5. **Constitutional Scope:** Constitution itself adopts provisions giving judiciary scope to play an active role

### Eventualities According to Subhash Kashyap (6 Points)
1. **Legislative Failure:** Legislature fails to discharge its responsibilities
2. **Hung Legislature:** Weak, insecure government busy in survival struggle → Unable to take decisions pleasing/displeasing groups
3. **Fear of Hard Decisions:** Ruling authorities afraid of taking honest/hard decisions pass odium of decision-making to courts as legal issues
4. **Basic Rights Neglect:** Legislature and executive fail to protect basic rights (decent life, healthy surroundings, efficient administration)
5. **Misuse of Court:** Court misused by authoritarian ruling party for ulterior motives (e.g., Emergency aberration)
6. **Judicial Weaknesses:** Courts succumb to human weaknesses - craze for populism, publicity, media headlines

## ACTIVATORS OF JUDICIAL ACTIVISM

### Upendra Baxi’s Typology of Social/Human Rights Activists (15 Categories)
1. **Human Rights Groups:** Focus on civil and political rights
2. **Poverty Rights Groups:** Litigate drought/famine relief and urban impoverished issues
3. **Women’s Rights Groups:** Agitate for gender equality, gender-based violence, harassment, rape, dowry murders
4. **Child Rights Groups:** Focus on child labour, literacy right, juveniles in custodial institutions, children born to sex workers
5. **Indigenous People’s Rights Groups:** Agitate for forest dwellers, 5th and 6th Schedule citizens, identity rights
6. **Custodial Rights Groups:** Social action by prisoners' rights groups, women under state protective custody, preventive detainees
7. **Citizens for Environmental Action:** Combat increasing environmental degradation and pollution
8. **Citizen Groups against Large Irrigation Projects:** Agitate against mega irrigation projects
9. **Bar-based Groups:** Agitate for autonomy and accountability of Indian judiciary
10. **Media Autonomy Groups:** Focus on autonomy and accountability of press and state mass media
11. **Assorted Lawyer-Based Groups:** Influential lawyers' groups agitating for various public causes
12. **Assorted Individual Petitioners:** Freelance activist individuals

## APPREHENSIONS OF JUDICIAL ACTIVISM

### Upendra Baxi’s Typology of Fears (5 Types)
1. **Ideological Fears:** Apprehension of courts usurping powers of legislature, executive, or civil society institutions
2. **Epistemic Fears:** Lack of specialized judicial knowledge in economic/scientific matters (e.g., economic expertise of Manmohan Singh or atomic energy experts)
3. **Management Fears:** Addition of PIL workload compounding staggering court arrears
4. **Legitimation Fears:** Depletion of judicial authority if executive bypasses/ignores public interest orders → Erosion of public faith
5. **Democratic Fears:** Uncontrolled growth of PIL depleting democratic potential for future

## JUDICIAL ACTIVISM VS. JUDICIAL RESTRAINT

### Meaning of Judicial Restraint
* **Concept:** Self-control exercised by judiciary; judges encouraging review of executive/legislative actions with deference and hesitation
* **Core Philosophy:** Courts should not substitute their own social/political views for those of elected legislature and executive

### Judicial Restraint in US Supreme Court (6 Assumptions)
1. Court is a judicial body, not a political body
2. Respect for co-equal legislative and executive branches
3. Doctrine of separation of powers
4. Concept of federalism requiring deference to state governments
5. Pragmatic reliance on Congress for jurisdiction/resources and public acceptance for effectiveness
6. Aristocratic notion of law as reason/judgment vs politics as power/influence

### Supreme Court of India Observations on Judicial Restraint (2007 Ruling)
* **Bench:** **Justice A.K. Mathur** and **Justice Markandey Katju** (*Divisional Manager, Aravali Golf Course v. Chander Haas, 2007*)
* **8 Key Directives & Observations:**
  1. **Separation Limits:** Judges unjustifiably performing executive or legislative functions is unconstitutional; courts cannot cross limits
  2. **Humility:** Judges must know limits, have modesty and humility and not behave like "emperors" running government
  3. **Montesquieu’s Warning:** Cited Montesquieu's *'The Spirit of Laws'* on dangers of failing to maintain separation of powers; warned against judicial 'overreach'
  4. **Activism vs Adventurism:** Judicial activism must **NOT** become **judicial adventurism**; adjudication must follow historically validated restraints
  5. **Lack of Expertise:** Courts must not embarrass administrative authorities; administration possesses expertise that judiciary lacks
  6. **Parallel Allegations:** Criticism that executive/legislature fails job is matched by court arrears pending for half a century
  7. **Electoral Remedy:** Remedy for dysfunctional legislature/executive is voter franchise in elections and peaceful lawful demonstrations - **NOT judicial takeover**
  8. **Balance of Power:** Judicial takeover violates delicate constitutional balance of power without resources or expertise to perform executive/legislative functions
