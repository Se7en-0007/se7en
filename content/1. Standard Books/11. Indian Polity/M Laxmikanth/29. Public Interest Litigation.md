## INTRODUCTION
* **Origin & Development:** Originated and developed in **USA** in the **1960s** (Council for Public Interest Law 1976 report: legal representation for unrepresented groups e.g., poor, environmentalists, consumers, racial/ethnic minorities)
* **Indian Context:** Outcome and most popular manifestation of **judicial activism**; introduced in early **1980s**
* **Pioneers in India:** **Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer** and **Justice P.N. Bhagwati**
* **Alternative Nomenclature:** Also known as **Social Action Litigation (SAL)**, **Social Interest Litigation (SIL)** and **Class Action Litigation (CAL)**

---

## MEANING OF PIL
* **Core Mechanism:** Relaxation of traditional rule of ***locus standi*** (traditional rule = only person whose rights are infringed can move court)
* **PIL Exception:** Any public-spirited citizen or social organisation can move court on behalf of disadvantaged persons/groups (poverty, ignorance, social/economic disability)
* **Supreme Court Definition:** Legal action initiated in court for enforcement of public/general interest affecting legal rights or liabilities of community/class
* **3 Primary Objectives:**
  1. Vindication of the **rule of law**
  2. Facilitating effective **access to justice** for socially and economically weaker sections
  3. Meaningful realisation of **Fundamental Rights**

---

## FEATURES OF PIL
1. Strategic arm of **legal aid movement** bringing justice to poor/vulnerable masses
2. **Non-adversarial nature** (unlike ordinary traditional private litigation dispute between two parties)
3. Promotes and vindicates **public interest** (not individual vs individual rights)
4. Redresses violations of rights of poor and disadvantaged that would otherwise go unnoticed
5. **Cooperative effort** between petitioner, State/Public Authority and Court
6. Enforces public duty, redresses public injury and protects collective/diffused rights
7. **Assertive, creative and positive role** of Court (departing from passive role)
8. Flexible procedure while adhering to judicial tenets
9. No adjudication/determination of individual private rights

---

## SCOPE OF PIL
* **Supreme Court Guidelines (1988, modified 1993 & 2003):**

### Categories Ordinarily Entertained as PIL
1. **Bonded labour** matters
2. **Neglected children**
3. Non-payment of **minimum wages** & exploitation of casual workers / Labour Law violations (except individual cases)
4. **Jail petitions** (harassment, premature release / post-14 yrs release, death in jail, transfer, personal bond, speedy trial as FR)
5. Petitions against **police** (refusing case registration, harassment, death in police custody)
6. Atrocities on **women** (bride harassment, bride-burning, rape, murder, kidnapping)
7. Harassment/torture of **SC/ST/economically backward** villagers by co-villagers or police
8. **Environmental pollution**, ecological balance, drugs, food adulteration, heritage/culture maintenance, forest & wildlife
9. Petitions from **riot victims**
10. **Family pensions**

### Categories Ordinarily NOT Entertained as PIL
1. **Landlord-tenant** matters
2. **Service matters** and pension/gratuity issues
3. Admission to **medical and other educational institutions**
4. Petitions for **early hearing** of cases pending in High Courts / Subordinate Courts

## PRINCIPLES OF PIL
* **Key Principles Evolved by SC (*Guruvayur Devaswom Managing Committee v. C.K. Rajan, 2003*):**
  1. Court can entertain petition by interested person under **Articles 32 and 226** for disadvantaged citizens
  2. Court can treat a **letter or telegram** as a PIL (relaxes procedural rules & pleadings)
  3. Injustice to large numbers → Court invokes **Articles 14 and 21** + International Human Rights Conventions
  4. Common rule of ***locus standi* relaxed** for poor, illiterate and disabled
  5. Prima facie satisfaction of constitutional violation → State cannot object to maintainability
  6. Applicability of ***res judicata*** depends on nature, facts and circumstances of petition
  7. Disputes purely in the realm of **private law** between warring groups NOT allowed as PIL
  8. Court can convert personal interest petition into PIL if public interest warrants enquiry
  9. Court may appoint **Commission/fact-finding bodies** or direct management of public institutions
  10. Court will NOT ordinarily transgress into **legislative/executive policy** or exceed jurisdiction
  11. High Court does **NOT possess powers akin to Article 142** of SC (power to do complete justice)
  12. High Court ordinarily should not entertain PIL questioning constitutionality of a statute or rule

---

## GUIDELINES FOR ADMITTING PIL
* **Prevention of Misuse:** Must not degenerate into ‘Publicity Interest Litigation’, ‘Politics Interest Litigation’, ‘Private Interest Litigation’, ‘Paisa Interest Litigation’, or ‘Middle-class Interest Litigation’ (MIL)
* ***BALCO Employees Union v. Union of India (2001):*** PIL is NOT a pill or panacea for all wrongs; meant solely for the weak and disadvantaged
* ***State of Uttaranchal v. Balwant Singh Chaupal (2010) SC Guidelines:***
  1. Encourage genuine/bona fide PIL; effectively discourage/curb PIL filed for extraneous considerations
  2. High Courts to formulate **proper rules** for encouraging genuine PIL and discouraging oblique motives
  3. Verify **credentials of petitioner** *prima facie* before entertaining
  4. Verify **correctness of contents** before entertaining
  5. Ensure **substantial public interest** is involved
  6. Accord priority to petitions involving **grave urgency or public harm**
  7. Curb busybodies / frivolous petitions by imposing **exemplary costs** or adopting novel deterrence
