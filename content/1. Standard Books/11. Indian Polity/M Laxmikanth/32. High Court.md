## INTRODUCTION
* **Position:** Top position in judicial administration of a state (operates below Supreme Court, above subordinate courts in integrated judicial system)
* **Origins & Historical Evolution:**
  * **1862:** First High Courts established at **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** (*under Indian High Courts Act, 1861*)
  * **1866:** 4th High Court established at **Allahabad**
  * **Post-1950:** Provincial High Courts became High Courts for corresponding states
* **Constitutional Provisions:** **Articles 214 to 231** in **Part VI** of the Constitution
* **Territorial Jurisdiction:** Co-terminus with territory of state (or combined states/UTs for common High Court)
* **Common High Court Provision:** **7th Constitutional Amendment Act (1956)** authorised Parliament to establish common High Court for 2 or more states / UTs
* **Present Count:** **25 High Courts** in India:
  * **3 High Courts** have jurisdiction over >1 state (**Bombay, Gauhati, Punjab & Haryana**)
  * **Delhi** = only UT with separate High Court (*since 1966*)
  * **J&K and Ladakh** = common High Court for 2 UTs
  * **Other UTs** fall under jurisdiction of adjacent state High Courts
  * **Parliamentary Power:** Parliament can extend or exclude High Court jurisdiction to/from any UT

## COMPOSITION AND APPOINTMENT
* **Composition:** Chief Justice + such other judges as **President** determines from time to time (*strength NOT fixed in Constitution; left to President's discretion based on workload*)

### Appointment of Judges
* **Appointing Authority:** **President**
* **Consultation Process:**
  * **Chief Justice of High Court:** Appointed by President after consulting **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** + **Governor** of state concerned
  * **Other Judges:** Consult CJI + Governor of state + **Chief Justice of concerned High Court**
  * **Common High Court:** Governors of **all concerned states** are consulted
* **Evolution of Judicial Rulings on Consultation:**
  * ***Second Judges Case (1993):*** No appointment can be made unless in conformity with CJI's opinion
  * ***Third Judges Case (1998):*** CJI must consult **collegium of 2 seniormost SC judges** (*sole opinion of CJI = invalid consultation*)
  * ***99th CAA (2014) & NJAC Act (2014):*** Replaced collegium with NJAC
  * ***Fourth Judges Case (2015):*** SC struck down 99th CAA & NJAC Act as unconstitutional → **Collegium system revived**

## QUALIFICATIONS, OATH AND SALARIES

### Qualifications of Judges
1. Citizen of India
2. Must fulfill **ANY ONE** of the following:
   * Held **judicial office** in territory of India for **10 years**
   * Advocate of a High Court (or HCs in succession) for **10 years**
* **Key Distinctions:**
  * **No minimum age** prescribed in Constitution
  * **No provision for "distinguished jurist"** (*unlike Supreme Court*)

### Oath or Affirmation
* **Administered by:** **Governor** of state (or person appointed by Governor)
* **Swears/Affirms to:** True faith & allegiance to Constitution; uphold sovereignty & integrity of India; perform duties without fear or favour, affection or ill-will; uphold Constitution and laws

### Salaries and Allowances
* Determined by **Parliament**; charged on **Consolidated Fund of State** (cannot be varied to disadvantage except during Financial Emergency)
* **Revision (2018):** Chief Justice salary = **₹2.50 lakh per month**; Judge salary = **₹2.25 lakh per month**
* **Pension:** **50% of last drawn salary** as monthly pension (**charged on Consolidated Fund of INDIA**, not state) + sumptuary allowance, free residence, medical, car, telephone

## TENURE, REMOVAL AND TRANSFER

### Tenure of Judges
* **No fixed tenure** in Constitution; 4 provisions:
  1. Holds office until attaining **62 years of age** (*raised from 60 to 62 by 15th CAA 1963*; age disputes decided by **President** after consultation with CJI; decision final)
  2. Resigns by writing to **President**
  3. Removed by **President** on recommendation of Parliament
  4. Vacates office if appointed to Supreme Court or transferred to another High Court

### Removal of Judges
* **Order:** Issued by **President** after Parliamentary address presented in **same session**
* **Majority Required:** Passed in each House by **Special Majority** (majority of total membership + ≥2/3 members present and voting)
* **Grounds:** **Proved misbehaviour** or **incapacity** (*same as Supreme Court judge*)
* **Judges Enquiry Act (1968) Procedure:**
  1. Removal motion signed by **100 members in Lok Sabha** OR **50 members in Rajya Sabha** submitted to Speaker/Chairman
  2. Speaker/Chairman admits or refuses
  3. If admitted, **3-member committee** constituted: (a) CJI or SC judge, (b) Chief Justice of a High Court, (c) Distinguished jurist
  4. If committee finds judge guilty, House considers motion
  5. Motion passed in both Houses by Special Majority → Address sent to President → President issues removal order
* **Fact:** No High Court judge has been impeached so far

### Transfer of Judges (Article 222)
* **Authority:** **President** transfers judge after consultation with **CJI**
* **Judicial Rulings:**
  * ***1977 SC Ruling:*** Transfer can only be in public interest, not as punishment
  * ***Second Judges Case (1993):*** Judicial review permissible only to eliminate bias/arbitrariness
  * ***Third Judges Case (1998):*** CJI must consult **collegium of 4 seniormost SC judges** + **Chief Justices of BOTH High Courts** (transferring & receiving)

## ACTING, ADDITIONAL AND RETIRED JUDGES

### Acting Chief Justice (Article 223)
* Appointed by **President** when HC CJ office is vacant, CJ is temporarily absent, or unable to perform duties

### Additional and Acting Judges (Article 224)
* **Additional Judges:** Appointed by **President** for temporary period (**max 2 years**) for temporary increase in business or arrears of work
* **Acting Judges:** Appointed by **President** when a judge (other than CJ) is absent, unable to perform duties, or appointed acting CJ
* **Age Ceiling:** Neither additional nor acting judge can hold office after attaining **62 years of age**

### Retired Judges (Article 224A)
* **Appointment:** Chief Justice of High Court can request a retired judge of that/another HC to sit and act as judge temporarily
* **Prerequisites:** **Previous consent of President** + **consent of person** to be appointed
* **Status:** Entitled to allowances determined by President; enjoys all powers/jurisdiction (not otherwise deemed HC judge)

## INDEPENDENCE OF HIGH COURT
* **Constitutional Safeguards:**
  1. **Mode of Appointment:** Executive discretion curtailed by judicial consultation (collegium)
  2. **Security of Tenure:** Removed by President ONLY on constitutional grounds/procedure (does not hold office during pleasure)
  3. **Fixed Service Conditions:** Salaries/allowances non-diminishable during term (except Financial Emergency)
  4. **Expenses Charged on Consolidated Fund:** Salaries/allowances charged on Consolidated Fund of State (pension charged on Consolidated Fund of India) → Non-votable by State Legislature
  5. **Conduct of Judges Cannot be Discussed:** Prohibited in Parliament/State Legislatures except during impeachment motion
  6. **Ban on Practice After Retirement:** Prohibited from pleading/acting except in Supreme Court and other High Courts
  7. **Power to Punish for Contempt:** Vested with power to punish for contempt of itself
  8. **Freedom to Appoint Staff:** Chief Justice appoints HC staff without executive interference
  9. **Jurisdiction Cannot be Curtailed:** Guaranteed jurisdiction cannot be curtailed by Parliament or State Legislature

## JURISDICTION AND POWERS OF HIGH COURT

### 1. Original Jurisdiction
* **Direct Hearing Power:** Hear disputes in 1st instance (not by appeal)
* **Covers:**
  1. Election disputes of MPs and MLAs
  2. Revenue matters / acts done in revenue collection
  3. Enforcement of Fundamental Rights
  4. Cases transferred from subordinate court involving constitutional interpretation
  5. **Original Civil Jurisdiction:** 4 High Courts (**Calcutta, Bombay, Madras, Delhi**) have original civil jurisdiction in cases of higher value
  * *Criminal Jurisdiction Note:* Original criminal jurisdiction of Calcutta, Bombay, Madras HCs fully abolished by **CrPC 1973**

### 2. Writ Jurisdiction (Article 226)
* **Scope:** Issue writs (*Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo Warranto*) for FR enforcement AND **"for any other purpose"** (ordinary legal rights)
* **Territorial Extent:** To any person/authority within territory, OR outside territory if **cause of action** arises within territory (*15th CAA 1963*)
* **SC vs HC Writ Jurisdiction:**
  * **HC (Art 226):** **Wider scope** (FRs + ordinary legal rights); discretionary remedy
  * **SC (Art 32):** **Narrower scope** (FRs only); mandatory remedy (Art 32 is an FR)
  * **Concurrent Nature:** Concurrent with SC (aggrieved can approach either court directly)
* ***L. Chandra Kumar Case (1997):*** Writ jurisdiction under Arts 226 & 32 is part of **Basic Structure**

### 3. Appellate Jurisdiction
* Primary court of appeal in state; wider than original jurisdiction

#### (a) Civil Matters
1. First appeals from district/subordinate courts on law & fact (if above financial limit)
2. Second appeals from district/subordinate courts on **questions of law only**
3. Intra-court appeals (appeal from single judge to division bench of same HC)
4. Appeals from tribunal decisions lie to division bench of HC (*SC 1997 ruling: tribunals subject to HC writ jurisdiction; cannot approach SC directly without going to HC first*)

#### (b) Criminal Matters
1. Appeals against Sessions / Additional Sessions Court judgments if sentence is **imprisonment for >7 years**
2. **Death Sentence Confirmation:** Death sentence awarded by Sessions Court **MUST be confirmed by High Court** before execution (whether appeal filed or not)
3. Appeals against Assistant Sessions Judge / Magistrates per CrPC 1973

### 4. Supervisory Jurisdiction (Article 227)
* **Superintendence:** Over **all courts and tribunals** in territorial jurisdiction (*except military courts/tribunals*)
* **Scope:** Covers administrative AND judicial superintendence; revisional jurisdiction; can be **suo-motu**
* **Nature:** Extraordinary power used sparingly (excess of jurisdiction, natural justice violation, error of law, perverse findings)

### 5. Control over Subordinate Courts
1. Consulted by Governor in appointment, posting, promotion of district judges (**Art 233**) & judicial service (**Art 234**)
2. Administrative control over district courts & subordinate courts (postings, promotions, leave, discipline)
3. Can withdraw case pending in subordinate court involving substantial constitutional question of law
4. Law declared by HC binding on all subordinate courts in its territorial jurisdiction

### 6. A Court of Record (Article 215)
* **Two Powers:**
  1. Judgments, proceedings, acts recorded for perpetual memory and evidentiary value (recognized as legal precedents in subordinate courts)
  2. Power to punish for **contempt of itself and subordinate courts** (*Contempt of Courts Act, 1971*)
* **Power to Review:** Inherent power to review and correct its own judgment/order (even though no specific review power conferred by Constitution, unlike SC)

### 7. Power of Judicial Review
* Examine constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders of both Central and State governments
* **Articles:** **13 and 226** explicitly confer judicial review
* **3 Challenge Grounds:** Infringes FRs, outside competence of framing authority, repugnant to constitutional provisions
* **42nd CAA (1976) vs 43rd CAA (1977):** 42nd CAA barred HC from examining Central laws → **43rd CAA 1977 restored original position**
