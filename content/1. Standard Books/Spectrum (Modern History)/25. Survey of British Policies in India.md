## Administrative Policies
* **Post-1857 Shift** ⇒ Abandoned progressive modernisation → Blatantly reactionary policies (Pretext: Indians unfit for self-governance).

### Divide and Rule
* **Objective** ⇒ Prevent united mass action against British authority.
* **Method** ⇒ Pitted princes vs states' people, region vs region, province vs province, caste vs caste, Hindus vs Muslims.
* **Post-1857 Repression** ⇒ Initial heavy crackdown on Muslims.
* **Post-1870 Shift** ⇒ Propped up Muslim middle & upper educated classes against rising nationalist tide.
* **Tool** ⇒ Exploited competition over scarce resources (education, administrative jobs, political spoils under colonial underdevelopment) to create religious splits.

### Hostility Towards Educated Indians
* **Nationalist Demand** ⇒ Indian participation in administration based on analysis of exploitative colonial rule.
* **British Response (Post-1885 INC Foundation)** ⇒ Treated demands as threat to authority → Adopted hostile stance and opposed anyone advocating modern education.

### Attitude Towards the Zamindars
* **Objective** ⇒ Expand social base and create counterweights against nationalist intelligentsia.
* **Action** ⇒ Restored confiscated lands to **Awadh taluqdars** (prior to 1857) → Propped up landlords as "natural" and "traditional" leaders.
* **Consequence** ⇒ Zamindar privileges protected at expense of peasants ⇒ Zamindars became firm British loyalists.

### Attitude Towards Social Reforms
* **Action** ⇒ Withdrew support for social reforms post-1858 to avoid provoking orthodox anger.
* **Tactics** ⇒ Encouraged caste and communal consciousness to strengthen reactionary social forces.

### Underdeveloped Social Services
* **Cause** ⇒ Disproportionate expenditure on army, civil administration, and imperial wars.
* **Effect** ⇒ Negligible spending on social services (**education, health, sanitation, infrastructure**).
* **Bias** ⇒ Established facilities catered primarily to urban areas and elite sections.

### Labour Legislations
* **Miserable Working Conditions (19th C.)** ⇒ Extremely long hours, low wages, lack of ventilation/light, zero safety measures.
* **Lobbying** ⇒ **Lancashire textile capitalists** demanded regulation of Indian factories to prevent competition from cheap Indian labour.
* **Chronology**:
  * **1875** ⇒ First factory investigation commission appointed.
  * **1881** ⇒ First Factory Act passed.
* **Indian Factory Act, 1881** (Child Labour focus):
  * Employment of children under **7 years** prohibited.
  * Working hours for children (ages 7–12) restricted to **9 hours per day**.
  * Children granted **4 holidays per month**.
  * Mandatory fencing of hazardous machinery.
* **Indian Factory Act, 1891**:
  * Child labor age limits increased: minimum **9 years**, maximum **14 years**.
  * Children's maximum working hours reduced to **7 hours per day**.
  * Women's maximum working hours fixed at **11 hours per day** (with **1.5-hour interval**).
  * Men's working hours remained completely **unregulated**.
  * Weekly holiday provided for all workers.
* **Exemptions** ⇒ Acts did **not apply** to British-owned tea and coffee plantations.
* **Planter Exploitation** ⇒ Government passed contract-enforcement laws (breach of contract was a criminal offence; planters could arrest defaulting laborers).

### Restrictions on Freedom of The Press
* **1835** ⇒ **Charles Metcalfe** lifted press restrictions.
* **1878** ⇒ **Lord Lytton** passed infamous **Vernacular Press Act** to stifle Indian language press.
* **1882** ⇒ **Vernacular Press Act** repealed due to massive public protest.
* **Swadeshi Era (1908 & 1910)** ⇒ Fresh restrictions imposed on press in the wake of anti-partition movements.

### White Racism
* **Doctrine** ⇒ Systematically maintained white superiority.
* **Practices** ⇒ Excluded Indians from higher services; barred them from railway compartments, parks, hotels, clubs; public beatings/murders reported as "accidents."
* **Lord Elgin Statement** ⇒ "We could only govern by maintaining the fact that we were the dominant race... reserve the control to ourselves."

## Revenue Policies
* **Objective** ⇒ Extract maximum land income with zero concern for cultivators.

### Hastings’ System (Izaredari / Farming System)
* **Context** ⇒ 1769–70 Bengal Famine caused by Company employees' corruption and greed.
* **Mechanism** ⇒ Revenue collection rights auctioned to highest bidders (contractors/'farmers') for **5-year terms** (quinquennial settlement), made annual in **1777**.
* **Ousting of Zamindars** ⇒ Traditional zamindars assumed to be mere tax gatherers and excluded from bidding, leading to large-scale dispossession of hereditary zamindars.
* **Consequence** ⇒ Extortion, agricultural decline, reduced production of silk/cotton, impoverishment of the country, and failure to generate expected surplus.

### Permanent Settlement
* **1776** ⇒ **Philip Francis** proposed permanent settlement of land revenue.
* **Inception** ⇒ Lord Cornwallis sent to implement with a committee comprising himself, **Sir John Shore**, and **James Grant**.
* **Philosophy** ⇒ Cornwallis (landed aristocrat) believed zamindars would improve land like English landlords.
* **Territorial Coverage** ⇒ **19%** of British India (Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Banaras, northern Madras).
* **Features**:
  * Zamindars granted **absolute proprietary rights** over land.
  * **1790** (10-year settlement) made permanent in **1793**.
  * Cultivators reduced to the status of **tenants (ryots)**.
  * **Sunset Clause (1794)** ⇒ Zamindari confiscated and auctioned if tax not paid by sunset of the specified date.
  * Zamindars kept **1/10th to 1/11th** of collected revenue, gave the remaining to the Company.
  * **Regulations of 1793, 1799, and 1812** ⇒ Empowered zamindars to seize tenant property without court permission for non-payment of rent.
* **Shortcomings**:
  * Revenue fixed arbitrarily high, leaving no margin for flood or drought.
  * Growth of **absentee-landlordism** (merchants/officials buying auctioned lands).
  * **Subinfeudation** ⇒ High rates forced zamindars to divide estates into smaller lots called **patni taluqs** and lease them out to **patnidars** for fixed rents.
  * **No Pattas Issued** ⇒ Written agreements (pattas) specifying rents were never issued; peasants remained at the mercy of zamindars.
  * No land or agricultural improvement occurred; state revenue became inelastic while expenses mounted.
  * Peasants reduced to serfdom and driven into moneylender debt.

### Ryotwari System
* **Inception** ⇒ **Thomas Munro** and **Captain Alexander Read** introduced it in the **Baramahal** region (Madras Presidency) in **1792**. Munro extended it as Madras Governor in **1820**.
* **Territorial Coverage** ⇒ **51%** of British India (Bombay, Madras, parts of Assam, Coorg, Berar, East Bengal).
* **Rationale** ⇒ Yielded larger revenue by bypassing intermediaries; all extracted wealth went directly to the state.
* **Initial Issues**:
  * Tax set at **1/3rd of gross produce** (nearly equal to economic rental).
  * Extracted ruthlessly regardless of crop failure or market prices.
  * **Putcut Assessment** ⇒ Tax fixed arbitrarily based on past payments on unsurveyed lands.
  * **Coercion & Violence** ⇒ Peasants tortured by collection officials (confirmed by **Madras Torture Commission Report, 1855**).
* **1855 Reform**:
  * Scientific land survey and fresh assessment undertaken.
  * Revenue fixed at **50% of net value of produce** for **30-year settlements** (introduced in **1864**).
* **Bombay Presidency**:
  * Introduced post-1818 by **Mountstuart Elphinstone**.
  * **1824–28 Survey** ⇒ State demand set at **55% of net produce** based on faulty surveys → Overassessment caused cultivators to desert fields.
  * **1836 Reform** ⇒ Modified by **Wingate** and **Goldsmith** (assessed fields by soil type and location, lowering rates).
* **Features**:
  * Ownership & occupancy rights vested in the **ryot** (free to sell, transfer, sublet).
  * No limit on land ownership.
  * Direct payment to Company; revenue set between **45% and 55%** of estimated produce.
  * Revenue **not permanently fixed**; revised periodically.
* **Shortcomings**:
  * Persistent overassessment and coercive, inflexible tax collection.
  * Widespread corruption in land assessment.
  * Allowed **non-cultivating landlords** to register as owners, reducing real cultivators to tenants/bonded labor.

### Mahalwari System
* **Inception** ⇒ Recommended in **1819** by **Holt Mackenzie**, formalised by **Regulation VII of 1822**.
* **1833 Reform** ⇒ **William Bentinck** simplified estimation of produce via the **Regulation of 1833**.
* **Merttins Bird** (Father of Land Settlements in Northern India) supervised.
  * Surveyed boundaries of cultivated and fallow lands.
  * State share set at **66% of rental value** for **30 years**.
* **1855 Reform (Dalhousie)** ⇒ State demand reduced to **50% of rental value**.
* **Regional Names** ⇒ **Mauzawar** (North Western Provinces), **Malguzari** (Central Provinces).
* **Features**:
  * Unit of assessment: **Mahal** (estate/village or group of villages).
  * Village community considered collective owner of land; individual ownership remained with cultivators.
  * Tax collected and paid to Company by the village headman (**lambardar**) or a community of village leaders.
* **Shortcomings**:
  * Complex recording of all cultivator/zamindar rights was practically impossible.
  * Calculations were inaccurate guesswork, manipulated by collectors to raise revenue.
  * Ruined village communities through exorbitant demands.
  * Unpaid tax forced land transfers to moneylenders and merchants, triggering popular uprisings in **1857**.
  * Collection costs often exceeded the actual revenue gathered by the Company.

### Overall Impact of The British Land Revenue Systems
* **Distribution** ⇒ **51% Ryotwari, 30% Mahalwari, 19% Permanent Settlement**.
* **Peasantry** ⇒ Lifelong indebtedness, dispossession, and impoverishment.
* **Land Ownership** ⇒ Created an unequal, saleable, mortgageable, and alienable form of private property.
* **Social Division** ⇒ Shattered the stability of Indian villages, dividing them into hostile classes of **landowning groups** and **landless peasantry**.
* **Artisans** ⇒ Ruined traditional village crafts, converting artisans into landless agricultural laborers.
* **Judicialization** ⇒ Replaced custom-based conflict resolution with formal, expensive law courts and lawyers.
* **Exploitation** ⇒ Exposed peasants to the combined extortion of moneylenders, middlemen, and absentee landlords.
* **Commercialisation** ⇒ Exorbitant revenue demands forced peasants to transition to commercial cash crops.

## British Social And Cultural Policy In India
* **Pre-1813** ⇒ Policy of **non-interference** in social and religious life.
* **Post-1813 Shift** ⇒ Active transformation of Indian society driven by new European ideological developments.

### Factors Influencing The Post-1813 Policy:
1. **Industrial Revolution** ⇒ Growth of industrial capitalism. British manufacturers wanted India as a market for finished goods, requiring partial modernisation of Indian society.
2. **Intellectual Revolution** ⇒ Gave rise to new scientific, moral, and rational attitudes of mind.
3. **French Revolution** ⇒ Popularized liberty, equality, fraternity, democracy, and nationalism.

### Characteristics of New Thought:
* **Rationalism** ⇒ Faith in reason and scientific inquiry.
* **Humanism** ⇒ Belief that every individual is an end in themselves, giving rise to individualism, liberalism, and socialism.
* **Doctrine of Progress** ⇒ Rejection of static societies; belief that man can remodel nature and society on rational lines.

### Schools of Thought:
* **The Conservatives**:
  * Advocated introducing as few changes as possible; respected Indian culture.
  * Believed Western ideas should be introduced gradually and cautiously to preserve social stability.
  * Key figures: **Warren Hastings**, **Edmund Burke**, **Thomas Munro**, **Charles Metcalfe**, **Mountstuart Elphinstone**.
* **The Paternalistic Imperialists (Post-1800)**:
  * Highly critical of Indian society; used cultural criticism to justify political and economic enslavement.
* **The Radicals**:
  * Applied humanistic and rational thought to the Indian situation.
  * Believed India had the capacity to improve; advocated introducing Western science, philosophy, and literature.
  * Supported by Indian reformers like **Raja Rammohan Roy**.
* **Reality** ⇒ Imperial interests predominated; modernisation of India was restricted to facilitating efficient colonial exploitation.

### Indian Renaissance
* **Action** ⇒ Indian reformers (**Rammohan Roy, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, B.M. Malabari**) pressured the government to pass legislations eradicating social evils.

### Dilemma Before The Government
* **Problem** ⇒ Too much modernisation threatened to unleash forces hostile to colonial rule.
* **Solution** ⇒ Opted for **"colonial modernisation"** (partial modernisation—introducing it in certain fields while blocking it in others).

### Role of Christian Missionaries
* **Doctrine** ⇒ Viewed Christianity as superior; sought westernisation to destroy native faith.
* **Alliances**:
  * Supported **Radicals** to undermine traditional culture.
  * Supported **Imperialists** to ensure law and order essential for missionary activities.
  * Allied with **Capitalists** by promising that converts would make better customers for British goods.

### British Retreat
* **Post-1858** ⇒ Modernised Indians began demanding liberty, equality, and self-governance.
* **Consequence** ⇒ British abandoned modernization, sided with socially orthodox/conservative elements, and actively promoted casteism and communalism to divide nationalists.

## British Policy Towards Princely States
* **Subordinate Union Policy** ⇒ Two core objectives post-1857:
  1. Use states as a **bulwark against political unrest**.
  2. Subordinate them completely to British authority.
* **Post-1857 Changes**:
  * Policy of annexation was completely abandoned.
  * Princes rewarded for loyalty; right of adoption respected, and territorial integrity guaranteed.
* **Subordination Milestones**:
  * **1876** ⇒ Queen Victoria adopted the title **Kaiser-i-Hind** (Queen Empress of India), ending the fiction of treaty equality.
  * **Lord Curzon** declared that princes ruled merely as **agents of the British Crown**.
* **Paramountcy** ⇒ British exercised the right to interfere in internal affairs (appointing/dismissing ministers) through Resident officers.
* **Interference Drivers** ⇒ Communication advancements (railways, telegraphs) and the need to suppress democratic/nationalist movements in the states.

## British Foreign Policy In India
* **Subservience** ⇒ Guided purely by the interests of **British imperialism**, though paid for with Indian money and fought with Indian blood.

### Drivers of Foreign Policy:
1. **Consolidation** ⇒ Internal administrative and communication consolidation pushed India's borders toward natural geographical frontiers.
2. **Imperial Objectives**:
   * Protect the Indian empire.
   * Expand British commercial and economic interests in Asia and Africa.
   * Keep European rivals (**Russia, France**) at arm's length in Asia and Africa.
