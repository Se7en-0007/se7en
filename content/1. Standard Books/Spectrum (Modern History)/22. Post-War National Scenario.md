### Two Strands Of National Upsurge
* **Last 2 years of British rule** → **Two distinct, parallel strands** of national upsurge:
  1. **Tortuous negotiations** (Congress, Muslim League, Government) → Accompanied by massive **communal violence** → Culminated in **Freedom + Partition**.
  2. **Sporadic, localized, highly militant, united mass actions** (workers, peasants, states' peoples) → Took shape of a **countrywide strike wave**.
     * **Triggers/Key Events**: **INA Release Movement**, **Royal Indian Navy (RIN) revolt**, **Tebhaga movement**, **Worli revolt**, **Punjab Kisan Morchas**, **Travancore peoples’ struggle (Punnapra-Vayalar)**, **Telangana peasant revolt**.

---

### Change In Government’s Attitude
* **June 1945**: Congress ban lifted + leaders released → Government expected demoralized populace → Met with **tumultuous, impatient crowds** (energy resurfaced after 3 years of heavy repression).
* **Wavell Plan** (Conservative-backed) → Failed to break constitutional deadlock.
* **July 1945**: **Labour Party** formed UK government → **Clement Attlee** (Prime Minister) + **Pethick Lawrence** (Secretary of State for India).
* **August 1945**: Central and provincial assembly elections announced.
* **September 1945**: Government announced **Constituent Assembly** would convene post-elections; working under spirit of **Cripps Offer**.
* **Factors dictating change in British attitude (7 Factors)**:
  1. **Global Power Shift**: UK was no longer a premier global power; **USA & USSR emerged as superpowers** (both favored Indian freedom).
  2. **Sympathetic UK Leadership**: New Labour government was more amenable to Indian demands.
  3. **European Left Wave**: Rise of socialist-radical governments throughout post-war Europe.
  4. **British Economic & Military Exhaustion**: Troops tired + economy shattered (London owed India **£1.2 billion** by 1945; drained by US Lend-Lease, fully paid off only in 2006).
  5. **South-East Asian Anti-Imperialism**: Anti-colonial waves resisting French/Dutch return in **Vietnam** and **Indonesia**.
  6. **Fear of Congress Revolt**: Threat of a 1942-type uprising but more dangerous (attacks on communications, agrarian revolts, labor issues, army disaffection, joined by military-experienced **INA personnel**).
  7. **Electoral Inevitability**: Overdue elections (last held in **1934** for Centre, **1937** for provinces).
* *Outcome*: British retreat was inevitable; Labour government only accelerated the process.

### Congress Election Campaign And INA Trials

#### Election Campaign for Nationalistic Aims
* **Winter 1945–46**: Elections held.
* **Main Campaign Strategy**: Seek mass mobilization against British authority rather than just votes.
  * Glorified **1942 Quit India martyrs**; condemned state repression and officials.
  * Promised enquiries and punishment for guilty officers → **Shattered services' morale** (bureaucrats feared return of Congress provincial ministries).
  * Led to government's realization of need for a **"gentleman's agreement"** with Congress.
* **INA POW Trials**: Mass agitations described as **"an edge of a volcano"** → Shifted British policy.
  * *British blunder*: Decided on public trials of several hundreds, dismissal from service, and detaining **~7,000** without trial.
  * *Red Fort Trial (November 1945)*: Put a Hindu (**Prem Kumar Sehgal**), a Muslim (**Shah Nawaz Khan**), and a Sikh (**Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon**) on trial together → **Unified all communities** against British rule.
  * *Additional Trigger*: Use of Indian Army units to restore French and Dutch colonial rule in **Vietnam and Indonesia** → Outraged urban population and soldiers.

#### Congress Support for INA Prisoners
* **September 1945 (Bombay Session)**: Passed strong resolution supporting INA.
* **Defense Committee**: Organized top legal defense by **Bhulabhai Desai**, **Tej Bahadur Sapru**, **Kailash Nath Katju**, **Jawaharlal Nehru**, and **Asaf Ali**.
* **Relief Action**: **INA Relief and Enquiry Committee** distributed funds, food, and assisted in employment.

#### The INA Agitation - A Landmark on Many Counts
* **Intensity & Media**: Extensive daily press coverage, editorials, threat-laced pamphlets, graffiti.
* **Key Dates**: **INA Day (November 12, 1945)** and **INA Week (November 5–11)**.
* **Geographical Spread**: From main nerve centers (Delhi, Bombay, Calcutta, Madras, UP, Punjab) to remote regions (Coorg, Baluchistan, Assam).
* **Social Diversity**: Funds contributed by film stars, municipal bodies, Gurudwaras, tongawallas, Indians abroad, and the **All India Women’s Conference**.
* **Political Spectrum**: Supported by **Congress**, **Muslim League**, **Communist Party**, **Unionists**, **Akalis**, **Justice Party**, **Ahrars**, **RSS**, **Hindu Mahasabha**, and **Sikh League**.
* **Loyalists & Military Sympathy**: Government employees collected funds; title-holders pleaded for trial abandonment; armed forces personnel attended meetings, received released prisoners in uniform, and contributed funds.
* *Significance*: Questioned Britain's right to decide matters for Indians → Became a pure **"Indian vs. British"** struggle.

### Three Upsurges (Winter Of 1945–46)
* **Three highly volatile confrontations**:
  1. **November 21, 1945**: Calcutta (over INA trials).
  2. **February 11, 1946**: Calcutta (protest against 7-year sentence to INA officer **Rashid Ali**).
  3. **February 18, 1946**: Bombay (strike by **Royal Indian Navy ratings**).

#### Three-Stage Pattern
* **Stage I: Defiance of Authority & State Repression**
  * *Nov 21, 1945 (Calcutta)*: Student march (Forward Bloc, SFI, Islamia College/League/Congress) to Dalhousie Square lathi-charged → Retaliated with stones → Police firing killed **2**.
  * *Feb 11, 1946 (Calcutta)*: Muslim League students (joined by SFI/Congress) defied Section 144 → Lathi-charged → Mass arrests.
* **Rebellion by Naval Ratings**:
  * *Feb 18, 1946*: **1,100 Royal Indian Navy (RIN) ratings** of **HMIS Talwar** went on strike.
  * *Causes*: Racial discrimination (demanding equal pay with white soldiers), bad food, officer abuse, arrest of rating for scrawling **"Quit India"** on HMIS Talwar, INA trials, use of Indian troops in Indonesia.
  * *Actions*: Hoisted **tricolour, crescent, and hammer-and-sickle flags** on rebel fleet mast. Rode in lorries across Bombay holding Congress flags. Citizens provided food; shopkeepers offered goods for free.
* **Stage II: Urban Mass Mobilization**
  * Complete paralysis of Calcutta and Bombay → Processions, hartals, street blockades, attacks on police stations, railways, and banks.
* **Stage III: Sympathy and Countrywide Solidarity**
  * Student boycotts + sympathetic strikes in military bases (**Karachi**, **Madras**, **Visakhapatnam**, **Calcutta**, **Delhi**, **Cochin**, **Jamnagar**, **Andamans**, **Bahrain**, and **Aden**).
  * **Royal Indian Air Force** strikes in Bombay, Poona, Calcutta, Jessore, and Ambala.
  * *Resolution*: **Patel** and **Jinnah** persuaded ratings to surrender (**February 23, 1946**) with assurance of no victimization by national parties.

### Evaluation of Potential and Impact of The Three Upsurges
* **Significance**: Fearless mass action + military rebellion shattered imperial authority; the RIN revolt was hailed as the end of British rule.
* **British Concessions (5 Outcomes)**:
  1. **December 1, 1946**: Only INA members accused of murder or brutal abuse of fellow prisoners would be tried.
  2. **January 1947**: Sentences of the first batch remitted.
  3. **February 1947**: Indian soldiers withdrawn from Indo-China and Indonesia.
  4. **November 1946**: Decision to send parliamentary delegation.
  5. **January 1946**: Decision to send **Cabinet Mission**.
* **Limitations/Gaps**:
  * Direct violent conflict was short-lived and limited to urban centers (unlike the peaceful, all-India INA agitation).
  * Communal unity was organizational/tactical, not popular (Muslim ratings consulted the League; others consulted Congress/Socialists).
  * British repressive apparatus was intact → **Maratha battalion** restored order and rounded up Bombay ratings.

### Congress Strategy
* **Leftist Criticisms**: Alleged Congress indifference was due to fear of losing control of a revolutionary situation and the need for a disciplined military in free India.
* **Congress Perspective**:
  * Upsurges were extensions of prior nationalist consciousness built by Congress (election campaigns, INA advocacy, exposing 1942 excesses).
  * Peaceful negotiations are an integral stage of mass struggle when the British are preparing to depart.
  * **Gandhi's view**: Mutiny was badly advised; ratings should have waited for leadership guidance. If they mutinied for freedom, they were doubly wrong.

### Election Results

#### Performance of The Congress
* Captured **91% of non-Muslim votes**.
* Won **57 out of 102 seats** in Central Assembly.
* Gained majorities in most provinces except Bengal, Sindh, and Punjab (captured majority in NWFP and Assam).

#### Muslim League’s Performance
* Captured **86.6% of Muslim votes**.
* Swept all **30 reserved seats** in the Central Assembly.
* Formed majority in **Bengal** and **Sindh**.
* *Impact*: Established the League as the dominant, undisputed voice of Indian Muslims (unlike 1937).

* **In Punjab**: A **Unionist-Congress-Akali** coalition under **Khizr Hayat Khan** assumed power.

#### Significant Features of Elections
* **Communal voting** predominated, contrasting the anti-British unity of the upsurges.
* **Causal factors**:
  1. **Separate electorates**.
  2. **Highly limited franchise**: <10% voted in provinces, and <1% for the Central Assembly.

### The Cabinet Mission
* **February 1946**: Attlee government sent high-power mission of three cabinet members:
  1. **Pethick Lawrence** (Secretary of State for India — Chairman).
  2. **Stafford Cripps** (President of Board of Trade).
  3. **A.V. Alexander** (First Lord of Admiralty).
* **Objective**: Formulate peaceful transfer of power.

#### Why British Withdrawal Seemed Imminent Now
1. **Nationalist Hegemony**: Deep penetration of nationalist sentiments into untouched areas.
2. **Bureaucratic Demoralization**: European ICS recruitment dropped; British-Indian parity reached as early as **1939** due to Indianisation. War weariness shattered services' confidence.
3. **Shattered Policy Framework**: "Conciliation-repression" loop exhausted. Repressing non-violence exposed state brutality; offering truce exposed state weakness.
4. **Unreliable Armed Forces**: RIN revolt and INA agitations proved military could no longer be trusted to repress another 1942-type mass rebellion.
5. **No Alternative**: Direct, official military rule was impossible due to lack of European officials.
6. **Future Indo-British Ties**: Retaining good future commercial/defense relations required a peaceful exit.
7. **Graceful Withdrawal**: Strategic shift to securing a graceful exit.

#### On The Eve of Cabinet Mission Plan
* **Congress Demand**: Power transferred to a single center; minority issue to be resolved *after* British exit.
* **British Goal**: Active united defense partner for the Commonwealth (divided India is defense-weak).
* **Clement Attlee (March 15, 1946)**: "...cannot allow a minority to place their veto on advance of the majority" (departure from Wavell’s Shimla conference appeasement).

#### Cabinet Mission Arrives
* **March 24, 1946**: Reached Delhi. Discussed:
  1. Interim Government.
  2. Principles and procedures for framing the constitution.
* **May 1946**: Put forward its own plan due to Congress-League deadlock over partition.

#### Cabinet Mission Plan (Main Points)
* **Rejection of full-fledged Pakistan** due to:
  1. Pakistan would include huge non-Muslim minorities (38% NW, 48% NE).
  2. Comminal self-determination would split Bengal and Punjab (Ambala, Jullundur divisions).
  3. Partition of Bengal/Punjab would sever deep-seated regional/cultural ties.
  4. Economic and administrative disruptions (geographical separation of East and West).
  5. Division of armed forces was dangerous.
* **Provinces grouped into 3 Sections**:
  * **Section A**: Madras, Bombay, Central Provinces, United Provinces, Bihar, Orissa (Hindu-majority).
  * **Section B**: Punjab, NWFP, Sindh (Muslim-majority).
  * **Section C**: Bengal, Assam (Muslim-majority).
* **Three-tier governance structure**: Executive + legislature at provincial, section, and union levels.
* **Constituent Assembly (389 members)**:
  * Chosen via proportional representation by provincial assemblies (voted in 3 blocks: General, Muslims, Sikhs).
  * *Anatomy*: **292** from provincial assemblies, **4** from chief commissioner's provinces, **93** nominated by princely states.
* **Drafting Procedure**: Groups A, B, and C sit separately first to draft provincial/sectional constitutions; then combine together to draft the Union constitution.
* **Union Centre**: Common center controlling **Defence, Communication, and External Affairs**.
* **Communal checks**: Simple majority of both communities present and voting on communal questions.
* **Provincial Autonomy**: Provinces given full autonomy + residual powers.
* **Princely States**: British paramountcy ends. Free to negotiate with successor governments or the UK.
* **Opt-out Clauses**: A province was free to leave its group *after* first general elections. Reconsideration of sectional/union constitution allowed after *10 years*.
* **Interim Government** to be set up from the CA.

#### Different Interpretations of The Grouping Clause
* **Congress**: Grouping is **optional**; only one Constituent Assembly; League has no veto.
* **Muslim League**: Grouping is **compulsory** (Cabinet Mission later clarified in favor of the League's interpretation).

#### Main Objections
* **Congress**:
  * Provinces should not wait until first elections to opt out (NWFP and Assam should have immediate options).
  * Compulsory grouping violates provincial autonomy.
  * Nominated princely representatives in CA are undemocratic.
* **League**: Wanted compulsory grouping to develop Sections B and C into sovereign entities for future secession into Pakistan. Hoped Congress rejection would get them invited to form the interim government.

#### Acceptance and Rejection
* **June 6, 1946**: Muslim League accepted the long-term plan.
* **June 24, 1946**: Congress accepted the long-term plan.
* **July 1946**: CA elections held.
* **July 10, 1946 (Nehru statement)**: "We are not bound by a single thing... big probability there would be no grouping as NWFP and Assam would have objections."
* **July 29, 1946**: League withdrew acceptance in reaction to Nehru's press statement; called for **"Direct Action" from August 16, 1946** to achieve Pakistan.

#### Wavell’s ‘Breakdown Plan’
* **May 1946 plan**: Mid-path between "repression" and "scuttle".
* *Mechanism*: Withdraw British military and officials to Muslim-majority provinces in NW & NE, handing the rest to Congress.
* *Significance*:
  * Proved British inability to suppress another Congress rebellion.
  * Highlighted high-official desire to create a "Northern Ireland" of Pakistan.

### Communal Holocaust And The Interim Government
* **August 16, 1946 (Direct Action Day)**: Transformed the scene → massive communal riots (**Calcutta Great Killings**, Bombay, Noakhali, Bihar, Garhmukteshwar).

#### Changed Government Priorities
* Wavell shifted: eager to induct Congress into the Interim Government even without the League (marked departure from Shimla's pro-veto stance to prevent a mass Congress revolt).

#### Interim Government
* **September 2, 1946**: Congress-dominated Interim Government sworn in under Nehru. (In reality, just a continuation of the Viceroy's old executive council; Wavell overruled ministers on INA release in March 1947).
* **October 26, 1946**: Wavell silently inducted the Muslim League without getting them to give up "Direct Action" or accept the Cabinet Mission Plan.

##### 14 Ministers of Interim Government (September 2, 1946– August 15, 1947)
1. **Jawaharlal Nehru**: Vice President of Executive Council, External Affairs and Commonwealth Relations.
2. **Vallabhbhai Patel**: Home, Information and Broadcasting.
3. **Baldev Singh**: Defence.
4. **Dr. John Mathai**: Industries and Supplies.
5. **C. Rajagopalachari**: Education.
6. **C.H. Bhabha**: Works, Mines and Power.
7. **Rajendra Prasad**: Agriculture and Food.
8. **Jagjivan Ram**: Labour.
9. **Asaf Ali**: Railway.
10. **Liaquat Ali Khan (ML)**: Finance.
11. **Ibrahim Ismail Chundrigar (ML)**: Commerce.
12. **Abdur Rab Nishtar (ML)**: Communications.
13. **Ghazanfar Ali Khan (ML)**: Health.
14. **Jogendra Nath Mandal (ML)**: Law.

#### Obstructionist Approach and Ulterior Motives of The League
* League boycotted the **December 9, 1946 Constituent Assembly** (Assembly limited itself to passing Nehru’s **Objectives Resolution**).
* Refused informal cabinet consultations.
* **Liaqat Ali Khan** (Finance Minister) obstructed and encumbered other ministries' funds.
* League used positions as a "continuation of civil war by other means" to fight for Pakistan.
* **February 1947**: 9 Congress members demanded the resignation of the League or threatened self-resignation. League demanded dissolution of CA.

### Birth and Spread of Communalism in India
* Appeared near the end of the 19th century as modern nationalism grew.

#### Characteristic Features of Indian Communalism
* Communalism is an ideology giving primary loyalty to a sectarian/religious group over society. Evolved in **3 stages**:
  1. **Communal Nationalism**: Notion that secular interests of co-religionists are identical.
  2. **Liberal Communalism**: Notion that different religious groups have different, distinct interests in economic, political, and cultural spheres.
  3. **Extreme Communalism**: Notion that different religious groups have incompatible, conflicting interests; they cannot co-exist.
* Modern phenomenon rooted in colonial socio-economic-political structures and mass politics.
* **Bourgeois roots**: Middle-class competition for limited resources. Socio-economic divides often overlapped with religious lines.

#### Reasons for Growth of Communalism
* **Socio-economic**:
  * Modern Western thought not absorbed by traditional Muslim intellectuals.
  * Muslims lagged in modern education, trade, and industry.
  * Reactionary landlords/rich classes retained influence.
  * Extreme competition for limited government jobs → reservation demands based on communal lines.
  * British used favours/reservations to fuel separatism.
  * Late development of political consciousness.

#### British Policy of Divide and Rule
* *Pre-1870s*: Suspected Muslims (Wahabi & 1857 revolts) → repression + Arabic/Persian learning undermined by English education.
* *Post-1870s*: Switched policy to counter emerging nationalism. Rallied Muslims behind government via concessions and favors, using figures like **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** (who exhorted Muslims to avoid Congress and politics).

#### Communalism in History Writing
* Distorted history into a Hindu phase (Ancient) and a Muslim phase (Medieval).
* Exaggerated ruling class conflicts as religious clashes.
* Hindu communal historians projected the ancient era as a peak and the medieval era as decay.

#### Side-effects of Socio-religious Reform Movements
* Movements with militant overtones (**Wahabi**, **Shuddhi**) insulated communities and made religion vulnerable to communalism.

#### Side-effects of Militant Nationalism
* **Hindu religious imagery** in nationalist mobilization:
  * Tilak's Ganapati and Shivaji festivals, anti-cow slaughter campaigns.
  * Aurobindo's Aryanised world; Swadeshi holy dips in Ganga; oath-taking before goddesses.
  * *Result*: Alienated Muslims.
  * *Khilafat (1920–22)*: Unease among some Congressmen as mobilization was based on religious defense of the Caliph, not anti-imperialist economics.

#### Communal Reaction by Majority Community
* **Punjab Hindu Sabha (1909)**: Founded by **U.N. Mukherjee** and **Lal Chand** (argued Hindus should side with British vs. Muslims).
* **All-India Hindu Mahasabha**: First session in **April 1915** under Maharaja of Kasim Bazar.
* **RSS**: Established in **1925**.
* *Swarajist split*: Responsivists joined Hindu Mahasabha.

#### Evolution of The Two-Nation Theory
* **1887**: Dufferin and Colvin prop up **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** and **Raja Shiv Prasad of Bhinga** against Congress.
* **1906**: **Shimla Delegation** (Agha Khan to Lord Minto) demanding separate electorates. Minto assured representation. **All India Muslim League** founded (Agha Khan, Salimullah of Dacca, Mohsin-ul-Mulk, Waqar-ul-Mulk).
* **1909**: Morley-Minto reforms award separate electorates. Punjab Hindu Sabha founded.
* **1915**: Hindu Mahasabha first session.
* **1916 (Lucknow Pact)**: Congress accepts separate electorates demand → gave political legitimacy to the League.
* **1920s**: Arya Samajist **Shuddhi** & **Sangathan** vs. Muslim **Tabligh** & **Tanzeem**. Swarajist Responsivists join Hindu Mahasabha. Ali brothers accuse Congress.
* **Congress errors in negotiating with League**:
  1. Legitimacy given to League's politics.
  2. Undermined secular nationalist Muslims.
  3. Prompted other communities to demand concessions.
  4. Made overall fight against communalism difficult.
* **1932**: Communal Award accepts 14 Points.
* **Post-1937**: League's poor election performance → resorts to extreme communalism. Muslims projected as a separate nation (**Rahmat Ali** / **Iqbal**). Base shifted to mass mobilization.
* **1937–39**: Jinnah blockades conciliation by demanding Congress declare itself a Hindu org and League as sole representative.
* **March 24, 1940**: **"Pakistan Resolution"** passed at Lahore Muslim League Session.
* **WWII**: British grant virtual veto power to League.
* **1947**: Partition of India.
