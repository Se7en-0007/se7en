## Factors Giving Rise To Desire For Reform
*   **Dawn of 19th Century** → Birth of modern, enlightened vision among intellectuals → Process of reawakening ("**Renaissance**") → Shaped modern history (accompanied by some undesirable socio-cultural by-products).
*   ### Impact of British Rule
    *   Colonial presence on Indian soil → Complex, decisive role.
    *   **Earlier Invaders** (settled within Indian frontiers) → Absorbed by superior culture / interacted positively → Became part of the land.
    *   **British Conquest** → Different dynamic → Occurred when India was a **stagnant civilisation** & **decadent/static society** $\leftrightarrow$ Contrasted with enlightened, scientific **18th-century Europe**.
*   ### Social Conditions Ripe for Reform
    *   **Religious and Social Ills**:
        *   Hinduism steeped in **magic, superstition, and obscurantism**.
        *   **Priests** → Exercised overwhelming, unhealthy monopoly over scriptural knowledge.
        *   **Idolatry + Polytheism** → Reinforced priestly dominance; religious ideology dictated social behavior.
    *   **Depressing Position of Women**:
        *   Women treated as **inferior adjuncts** to men; possessed no independent identity.
        *   **Female infanticide** at birth → Highly prevalent.
        *   **Child marriage** → Universal bane of society.
        *   **Polygamy** → Rampant; under **Kulinism** (Bengal), elderly men took very young girls as wives.
        *   **Widowhood** → Expected to commit **Sati** (**Raja Rammohan Roy**: "murder according to every shastra") → If spared, condemned to a life of exclusion, misery, and humiliation.
    *   **The Caste Problem**:
        *   Segregation system → Hierarchically ordained based on **ritual status**.
        *   **Untouchables (Scheduled Castes)** → Suffered severe, inhuman disabilities and restrictions.
        *   Caste division → Splintered population → Major obstacle to a **united national feeling** and democratic growth.
        *   Caste consciousness (especially in marriage) → Prevailed also among **Muslims, Christians, and Sikhs** (practiced untouchability in less virulent form).
        *   Rigid Caste System → Checked social mobility, widened divisions, thwarted individual initiative, violated human dignity.
*   ### Opposition to Western Culture
    *   Colonial rule → Systematic attempt to spread **colonial culture and ideology** as the dominant stream.
    *   Indian response → Reinvigorate traditional institutions + realize potential of traditional culture.
*   ### New Awareness among Enlightened Indians
    *   Impact of Western culture + shock of defeat → Birth of new awakening.
    *   **Core Realisation** → A vast nation like India was colonised by a handful of foreigners due to internal weaknesses in social structure and culture.
    *   **Diverse Reactions**:
        *   *Radical Reformers* (English-educated Bengali youth) → Revulsion for Hindu culture → Deliberately adopted offensive practices (drinking wine, eating beef) to defy tradition.
        *   *Common Consensus* → Urgent need to reform and democratise social institutions and religious outlook.
    *   Late 19th Century → Rising tide of **nationalism and democracy** → Fueled reform movements.
    *   **Key Accelerating Factors**:
        *   Growth of nationalist sentiments.
        *   Emergence of new economic forces.
        *   Spread of education.
        *   Impact of modern Western ideas and culture.
        *   Increased awareness of the global world.
    *   *Crucial Distinction* → Socio-cultural regeneration was **occasioned** by the colonial presence, but **not created** by it.

## Social And Ideological Bases Of Reform
*   ### Middle-Class Base
    *   Social base → Newly emerging **middle class** and educated intellectuals (both traditionally and Western-educated).
    *   *Contrast* → Broadly middle-class ideals (Western-derived) vs. predominantly non-middle-class social base.
    *   **European Model** → Intelligentsia sought to replicate the European middle class (which drove the Renaissance, Reformation, Enlightenment, and democratic revolutions).
    *   **Indian Reality** → Intelligentsia did not emerge from trade/industry (monopolised by British agencies) → Roots lay in **government service** or **professions** (law, education, journalism, medicine), often combined with intermediate land tenures.
*   ### The Intellectual Criteria
    *   Ideological unity forged by **Rationalism**, **Religious Universalism**, and **Humanism**.
    *   **Rationalism**:
        *   Applied as a touchstone to judge social relevance and tradition.
        *   **Raja Rammohan Roy** → Believed in **universal causality**; demonstrability as the sole criterion of truth.
        *   **Akshay Kumar Dutt** → "Rationalism is our only preceptor"; natural/social phenomena are understandable by mechanical processes.
        *   Standpoint of **social utility** → Used to replace blind faith with rationality.
        *   *Impact* → Brahmo Samaj repudiated **Vedic infallibility**; Aligarh Movement reconciled Islamic teachings with modern needs (**Syed Ahmed Khan**: religious tenets are not immutable).
        *   **Swami Vivekananda** → Religion must justify itself through scientific methods of investigation.
        *   *Past as an Instrument* → Appeal to the past was used merely as an aid; neither total blind revival nor complete break with tradition was sought.
    *   **Religious Universalism**:
        *   **Rammohan Roy** → Regarded different religions as national embodiments of a **universal theism** → Defended monotheism of Vedas and unitarianism of Christianity; attacked Hindu polytheism and Christian trinitarianism.
        *   **Syed Ahmed Khan** → All prophets shared the same *din* (faith); different nations had different prophets.
        *   *Objective* → Used the universalist perspective to counter the divisive political and social influence of rigid religious identity.
    *   **Humanism**:
        *   New humanitarian morality → Moral values must favor human progress.
        *   Stressed the **individual's right to interpret scriptures** using human reason and welfare.
        *   Frontal attack on **priestly domination** of religious practices.
        *   Shift in focus → Concentrated on **worldly existence** (here-and-now) rather than other-worldly salvation.
    *   **Alternative Cultural-Ideological System** → Manifested in:
        *   Reconstruction of traditional knowledge.
        *   Development and use of **vernacular languages**.
        *   Creation of alternative educational systems.
        *   Defense of indigenous religion against missionary attacks.
        *   Regeneration of Indian art and literature.
        *   Emphasis on Indian dress, food, and revitalisation of **Indian medicine** (Ayurveda/Unani).
        *   Researching pre-colonial technology for developmental potential.

## Two Streams
*   **Reformist Movements** (Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement) vs. **Revivalist Movements** (Arya Samaj, Deoband Movement).
*   *Shared Ground* → Both depended on an appeal to the **lost purity** of the religion they sought to reform.
*   *Key Difference* → The degree to which they relied on **tradition** vs. **reason and conscience**.

## Direction Of Social Reform
*   Inspired by humanistic ideals of **social equality** and equal worth of individuals.
*   **Socio-Religious Link** → Social ills (untouchability, gender inequity) derived legitimacy from religious dogmas.
*   **Evolution of Reform**:
    *   *Secularisation* → Gradually dissociated from religion and adopted a secular approach.
    *   *Social Base Expansion* → Shifted from narrow upper/middle-class base to penetrating the **lower strata** to reconstruct the social sphere.
    *   *Key Instruments* → Early organisations (Social Conference, Servants of India Society, Christian missionaries) + enlightened individuals + (20th Century) **National Movement** providing mass leadership.
    *   *Media for Propagation* → Vernacular languages used via novels, dramas, poetry, short stories, the press, and (1930s onwards) **cinema**.
*   **Two-Point Agenda**:
    1.  Betterment of the status of women in society.
    2.  Removal of disabilities arising out of untouchability.

*   ### Fight for Betterment of Position of Women
    *   Suppressed by customs: **purdah**, early marriage, ban on widow remarriage, Sati.
    *   Denied education; economically and socially dependent on male relatives.
    *   **Legal Disadvantages**:
        *   *Hindu women* → No right to inherit property; no right to terminate marriage.
        *   *Muslim women* → Inherited property but only **half the share** of men; unequal divorce rights.
        *   *Social Status* → Glorification as wives and mothers was the sole form of recognition.
    *   *Rationale for Reform* → Domestic sphere is where initial socialisation occurs → Reformed homes/women lead to reformed men → Vital for civilisational progress.
    *   *Basis of Appeal* → Individualism and equality; argued that true scriptures did not sanction women's subjection.
    *   **Steps taken to Ameliorate Women’s Position**:
        *   **Abolition of Sati**:
            *   **Raja Rammohan Roy** → Launched a frontal intellectual and social campaign.
            *   **William Bentinck** (Governor-General) → Passed **Regulation XVII, A.D. 1829 of the Bengal Code** → Banned Sati; declared it punishable as culpable homicide.
            *   *Scope* → Bengal first (1829); extended to Madras and Bombay Presidencies in **1830**.
            *   *Pre-colonial precedents* → Early 16th century: Portuguese, Dutch, and French banned Sati in their regions; **1582**: Mughal Emperor **Akbar** issued orders banning coerced Sati and appointed inspectors.
        *   **Preventing Female Infanticide**:
            *   Resorted to by upper-class Bengalis and Rajputs (females viewed as economic burdens).
            *   **Bengal Regulations of 1795 and 1804** → Declared female infanticide equivalent to murder.
            *   **Act of 1870** → Made birth registration compulsory; provided for post-birth verification of female children in secret-practice areas.
        *   **Widow Remarriage**:
            *   **Brahmo Samaj** → Popularised the issue.
            *   **Pandit Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** (Principal, Sanskrit College) → Cited Vedic texts to prove scriptural sanction → Efforts led to **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856** (legalised widow marriage; declared offspring legitimate).
            *   **Vishnu Shastri Pandit** → Founded the **Widow Remarriage Association** (1850s).
            *   **Karsandas Mulji** → Started **Satya Prakash** (Gujarati, 1852) to advocate widow remarriage.
            *   **Professor D.K. Karve** (Western India) → Married a widow (1893); Secretary of Widow Remarriage Association; opened a **Widows' Home in Poona** (vocational training for high-caste widows); founded **Indian Women’s University** (1916).
            *   **Veerasalingam Pantulu** → Spearheaded widow remarriage in Madras.
            *   Other advocates: B.M. Malabari, Narmad, Justice G.M. Ranade, K. Natarajan, **Jyotiba & Savitribai Phule**.
        *   **Controlling Child Marriage**:
            *   **Native Marriage Act (Civil Marriage Act), 1872** → Prohibited child marriage but had limited impact (excluded Hindus, Muslims, other recognized faiths).
            *   **Behramji M. Malabari** (Parsi reformer) → Campaign led to the **Age of Consent Act (1891)** (forbade marriage of girls below 12).
            *   **The Rukhmabai Case (1884)** → Accelerated the Age of Consent Act:
                *   **Rukhmabai Raut** (India's first practicing woman physician) married Dadaji Bhikaji at age 11 → Remained at home to study → Refused to join husband after 12 years.
                *   **Dadaji Bhikaji vs. Rukhmabai (1884)** → Dadaji sued for restitution of conjugal rights.
                *   **Justice Robert Hill Pinhey** → Dismissed petition (marriage in "helpless infancy" was not binding).
                *   *Reversal (1887)* → Chief Justice Sir Charles Sargent and Justice Farran overturned Pinhey's decision; ordered her to join husband or face prison.
                *   *Outcome* → Rukhmabai chose prison → Set aside after **Queen Victoria's personal intervention**.
                *   *Impact* → Most publicised 19th-century court case; **Rukhmabai Defence Committee** (Malabari, Ramabai Ranade) mobilized public opinion against child marriage.
            *   **Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929** (**Sarda Act**, effective 1930) → Pushed marriage age to **18 for boys** and **14 for girls** (framed by **Harbilas Sarda**, judge and Arya Samaj member).
            *   **Child Marriage Restraint (Amendment) Act, 1978** (Free India) → Raised marriage age to **18 for girls** and **21 for boys**.
        *   **Education of Women**:
            *   **Calcutta Female Juvenile Society (1819)** → First set up by Christian missionaries.
            *   **Bethune School (1849)** → Founded by **J.E.D. Bethune** (President, Council of Education, Calcutta) → First powerful institutional effort.
            *   **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** → Associated with 35 girls' schools in Bengal; pioneer.
            *   **Jagannath Shankarsheth 'Nana'** → Cofounder, School Society and Native School of Bombay (later Elphinstone Educational Institution) → Provided housing for Dr. Wilson's girls' school; sent family women there; used 1856 grant-in-aid to start English-Marathi school in Girgaum.
            *   **Bhau Daji Lad** → Ardent promoter; first Indian president of Students' Literary and Scientific Society (girls' school founded in his name).
            *   **Jyotirao & Savitribai Phule** → Opened **first girls' school at Bhidewada, Pune (1848)** → Faced heavy orthodox opposition; taught personally. Savitribai became **first female school teacher of modern India** (taught with **Fatima Sheikh** & **Sagunabai**). Opened night schools for working people. Purely indigenous, non-Brahmin effort.
            *   **Alexandra Society of Parsis (1863)** → Educated Parsi girls. **Cornelia Sorabji** became first female graduate of Bombay University (1887); later lady barrister.
            *   **Wood’s Despatch on Education (1854)** → Laid great stress on female education following Dalhousie's call for "frank and cordial support".
            *   **Women’s Medical Service (1914)** → Trained nurses and midwives.
            *   **Lady Hardinge Medical College (1916)** → Opened in Delhi; health facilities expanded via **Dufferin Hospitals** (1880s).
            *   *Nationalist Integration* → Swadeshi, Anti-Partition, and Home Rule movements acted as liberating experiences → Women participated in trade unions, kisan struggles, revolutionary activities, and legislatures. **Sarojini Naidu** became Congress President (1925) and United Provinces Governor (1947–49).
    *   **Women’s Organisations**:
        *   **Bharat Stree Mahamandal (1910, Allahabad)**:
            *   Convened by **Sarla Devi Chaudhurani** → First major Indian women's organisation set up by a woman.
            *   *Objectives* → Promote female education, abolish purdah, improve socio-economic/political status nationwide.
            *   *Philosophy* → Sarla Devi asserted that men working for women's upliftment lived "under the shade of Manu."
        *   **Ladies Social Conference (Bharat Mahila Parishad) (1904, Bombay)** → Founded by **Ramabai Ranade** under National Social Conference.
        *   **Arya Mahila Samaj** → Founded by **Pandit Ramabai Saraswati** → Pleaded for female medical education before English Education Commission → Led to medical education in **Lady Dufferin College**. Ramabai Ranade founded Bombay branch.
        *   **National Council of Women in India (1925)**:
            *   National branch of International Council of Women; formed by **Mehribai Tata** (argued purdah, caste, and illiteracy blocked progress).
            *   *Executive Committee* → Cornelia Sorabji, Tarabai Premchand, Shaffi Tyabji, **Maharani Sucharu Devi** (daughter of Keshab Chandra Sen).
            *   *Criticism* → Philanthropic style resembled upper-class English women.
        *   **All India Women’s Conference (AIWC) (1927)**:
            *   Founded by **Margaret Cousins**; first conference at Fergusson College, Pune.
            *   *Founding members* → Maharani Chimnabai Gaekwad, Rani Sahiba of Sangli, Sarojini Naidu, **Kamladevi Chattopadhyaya**, Lady Dorab Tata.
            *   *Nature* → First women's organisation with an **egalitarian approach**.
            *   *Objective* → Planned social distribution, social justice, and equal rights.
            *   *Legislative Advocacy* → Sarda Act (1929), Hindu Women’s Right to Property Act (1937), Factory Act (1947), Hindu Marriage and Divorce Act (1954), Special Marriage Act (1954), Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act (1956), Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act (1956), Suppression of Immoral Traffic Act (1958), Maternity Benefits Act (1961), Dowry Prohibition Act (1961), Equal Remuneration Act (1958, 1976).

*   ### Struggle Against Caste-Based Exploitation
    *   Origins → Later-Vedic four-fold division sub-divided into sub-castes due to racial admixture, geographical expansion, and craft diversification.
    *   **Chaturvarnashrama** → Regulated social purity, occupation, marriage, dining, dress, food, housing, water sources, and temple entry.
    *   **Untouchables (Dalits)** → Suffered humiliating, inhuman disabilities based on inequality by birth.
    *   **Factors that Helped to Mitigate Caste-based Discrimination**:
        *   **Colonial Administration Forces (Unintentional)**:
            *   *Economic shifts* → Creation of private property in land + free sale of land upset caste equations; village autarchy crumbled → Caste-vocation link severed.
            *   *Urbanisation + Industrialization* → Created modern commerce avenues; modern transport (railways) increased population mobility.
            *   *Legal reforms* → Uniformly applied system of law established **equality before law**, stripping judicial functions of caste panchayats.
            *   *Administrative services & Secular education* → Opened to all castes on secular lines.
        *   **Social Reform Movements**:
            *   Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Arya Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Theosophists, Social Conference worked to spread education among untouchables and remove temple/water restrictions.
            *   Attacked rigid hereditary basis of caste and the **law of karma** (used as philosophic/religious defense of caste).
            *   **Arya Samaj** → Crusaded against sub-castes, aimed to reconstruct society on the original four-fold division, upheld lowest castes' right to study scriptures.
        *   **National Movement**:
            *   Opposed caste privileges and fought for equal civic rights based on liberty and equality.
            *   Mass participation in demonstrations/satyagrahas diluted caste distinctions.
            *   **Provincial Congress Governments (Post-1937)** → Introduced free education for Harijans in several provinces.
            *   **Princely States** → Rulers of **Travancore, Indore, and Devas** opened all state temples to lower castes by proclamation.
            *   **Mahatma Gandhi** → Fought to eradicate untouchability root and branch based on humanism/reason; rejected Shastras if they endorsed untouchability. Founded **All India Harijan Sangh (1932)**.
        *   **Stirrings Among Lower Castes**:
            *   **Jyotiba Phule** → Led anti-Brahmin movement in Maharashtra; prioritized lower-caste and female education.
            *   **Gopal Baba Walangkar (Gopal Krishna)** → Pioneer in Raigad. theory: Indigenous Indians were untouchables, and caste was created by invading Aryans to control them. Founded **Anarya Dosh-Parihar Mandali** (Society for Removal of Evils Among Non-Aryans). Launched first Dalit newspaper: **Vital Vidhvasak** (Destroyer of Brahmanical/Ceremonial Pollution).
            *   **Kisan Faguji Bansod** → Advocated Dalit upliftment within the Hindu fold; ran a press publishing awakening journals.
            *   **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**:
                *   Started fortnightly **Mooknayak (Leader of the Voiceless, 1920)** → Argued national consciousness cannot develop if social divisions are ignored.
                *   Founded **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924)** → Motto: "**Educate, Agitate and Organise**" (focused on mass mobilisation).
                *   Started **Bahishkrit Bharat (1927)**.
                *   Led **Mahad Satyagraha (March 1927)** → Procession of 2,500 untouchables to drink from **Chawdar Tank**; later burnt the **Manusmriti** (December 1927) to protest inequality.
                *   Founded **All India Scheduled Castes Federation (1942)**.
                *   Advocated **annihilation of the caste system** as a prerequisite for national progress.
                *   Secured special representation for depressed classes in the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
            *   **All India Depressed Classes Association** → Founded in the 1920s by other Dalit leaders.
            *   **Non-Brahmin Movement (1900s)** → Initiated by **Maharaja of Kolhapur**; spread to South India, joined by Kammas, Reddis, Vellalas, and Muslims.
            *   **Self-Respect Movement (1920s)** → Led by **E.V. Ramaswamy Naicker (Periyar)** in South India.
            *   **Sri Narayana Guru** (Kerala) → Lifelong struggle against upper-caste domination. Slogan: "**One religion, one caste, one God for mankind**" (disciple Sahadaran Ayyapan modified to: "no religion, no caste, no God for mankind").
        *   **Constitution of Free India**:
            *   Foreign British government had limitations; could not risk hostile orthodox backlash by taking radical caste-abolition measures.
            *   Economic/political upliftment was essential for social upliftment → Realised in Free India.
            *   **Article 17** → Abolishes untouchability; declares enforcement of any disability unlawful.
            *   Forbids restriction of access to public resources (wells, tanks, bathing ghats, hotels, cinemas).
            *   **Directive Principles** → Mandates state to secure a social order informed by social, economic, and political justice.

## Views
*   **Bombay Governor (1911 to Viceroy)**: "Nationalist power to stir up discontent would be immensely increased if every cultivator could read."
*   **Jawaharlal Nehru**: "The rising middle classes... wanted some cultural roots to cling on to... that would reduce the sense of frustration and humiliation that foreign conquest and rule had produced."
*   **Mahadeo Govind Ranade**: "The dead and the buried... cannot be revived except by a reformation of the old materials into new organised forms."
*   **B.R. Ambedkar**: "A Voltaire among the brahmins would be a positive danger to the maintenance of a civilisation which is contrived to maintain brahminic supremacy."
*   **M.K. Gandhi**: "If untouchability lives, Hinduism perishes, and even India perishes..."
*   **M.K. Gandhi**: "I want the culture of all lands to be blown about my house as freely as possible. But I refuse to be blown off my feet by any."
