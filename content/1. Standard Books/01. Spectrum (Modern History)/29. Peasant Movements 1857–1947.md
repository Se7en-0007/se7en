## Peasantry Under Colonialism
* **Peasant Impoverishment Causes**:
  * **Colonial economic policies** + **Ruin of handicrafts** ⇒ **Overcrowding of land**
  * **New land revenue systems** + **Colonial administrative & judicial systems**
* **Zamindari areas** ⇒ High rents + illegal levies + arbitrary evictions + unpaid labour (**begar**).
* **Ryotwari areas** ⇒ Heavy land revenue levied directly by government.
* **Moneylender Exploitation** ⇒ Farmer approached local moneylenders to avoid eviction ⇒ High interest rates ⇒ Mortgage of land & cattle ⇒ Seizure of belongings ⇒ Reduced to tenants-at-will, sharecroppers, and landless labourers.
* **Peasant Response** ⇒ Resistance against exploitation ⇒ Realized real enemy was **colonial state** ⇒ Sometimes resorted to crime (robbery, dacoity, **social banditry**).

## A Survey Of Early Peasant Movements

### Indigo Revolt (1859–60)
* **Cause** ⇒ **European planters** (Bengal) forced peasants to grow indigo instead of rice (more remunerative) ⇒ Fraudulent contracts + advance payments as leverage.
* **Planter Oppression** ⇒ Kidnappings, illegal confinement, flogging, attacks on women/children, crop destruction, house demolition.
* **Uprising (1859)** ⇒ Led by **Digambar Biswas** and **Bishnu Biswas** (Nadia district) ⇒ Peasants refused to grow indigo + physically resisted planters' lathiyals + formed counter-forces.
* **Rent Strike** ⇒ Peasants refused to pay enhanced rents, resisted evictions, and used legal machinery funded by local collections.
* **Intelligentsia Support** ⇒ Bengali intellectuals supported the cause through newspaper campaigns, mass meetings, preparing memoranda, and supporting legal battles.
* **Outcome** ⇒ **Indigo Commission** appointed ⇒ November 1860 Government Notification: Ryots cannot be compelled to grow indigo; all disputes to be settled legally ⇒ Planters closed factories; indigo virtually wiped out from Bengal by end of 1860.

### Pabna Agrarian Leagues
* **Context (1870s–1880s)** ⇒ Oppressive practices of zamindars in Eastern Bengal to prevent occupancy rights under **Act X of 1859** ⇒ Enhanced rents, evictions, crop seizures, costly litigation.
* **Peasant Action** ⇒ Yusufshahi Pargana peasants (Pabna district) formed an **agrarian league** (1873) ⇒ Rent strike + legal battles financed by joint funds.
* **Nature of Struggle** ⇒ Primarily **legal resistance** with minimal violence; spread across East Bengal.
* **Outcome (1885)** ⇒ Resolved via official persuasion & zamindars' fears of litigation ⇒ Occupancy rights secured, enhanced rents resisted ⇒ **Bengal Tenancy Act of 1885** passed.
* **Intelligentsia Allies** ⇒ Supported by **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee**, **R.C. Dutt**, and **Surendranath Banerjea** (**Indian Association**).

### Deccan Riots
* **Causes** ⇒ Heavy Ryotwari taxation + **Moneylender exploitation** (mostly outsider Marwaris or Gujaratis) + Cotton price crash post-American Civil War (1864) + 50% land revenue hike (1867) + consecutive bad harvests.
* **Social Boycott (1874)** ⇒ Organised by ryots against outsider moneylenders ⇒ Refused to buy, till, or provide services (barbers, washermen, shoemakers).
* **Riots (1875)** ⇒ Social boycott spread (Poona, Ahmednagar, Sholapur, Satara) ⇒ Turned violent ⇒ Attacks on moneylenders' shops/houses ⇒ Debt bonds/deeds seized & burnt.
* **Outcome** ⇒ Repression by government + Conciliatory reform: **Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act, 1879**.
* **Intelligentsia Support** ⇒ Supported by the modern nationalist intelligentsia of Maharashtra.

## Changed Nature Of Peasant Movements After 1857
* **Key Characteristics**:
  * **Direct Fighting** ⇒ Peasants emerged as main force, fighting directly for their own demands.
  * **Economic Issues** ⇒ Focus centered almost wholly on immediate economic issues.
  * **Immediate Targets** ⇒ Directed against local enemies: foreign planters, indigenous zamindars, and moneylenders.
  * **Specific Objectives** ⇒ Limited to particular grievances and redressal; **colonialism was not the target**.
  * **No Systemic Change** ⇒ Did not aim to end the overall system of subordination or exploitation.
  * **Limited Reach & Continuity** ⇒ Territorial reach was limited; lacked long-term organization or continuity of struggle.
  * **Legal Awareness** ⇒ Strong development and assertion of legal rights inside and outside courts.

## Weaknesses
* **Lack of Colonial Analysis** ⇒ Inadequate understanding of colonialism and its structural role.
* **No Alternative Vision** ⇒ Lacked a new ideology, positive conception of alternative society, or new social, economic, and political program ⇒ Operated within the old societal framework.

## The Kisan Sabha Movement
* **Background** ⇒ Restoration of Awadh taluqdars' estates after 1857 strengthened landlords' hold ⇒ Peasants subjected to high rents, arbitrary evictions (**bedakhali**), illegal levies, and **nazrana** (renewal fees).
* **Wartime Strain** ⇒ WWI price hikes worsened peasant conditions.
* **Origin (1918)** ⇒ **United Provinces Kisan Sabha** set up in February 1918 by **Gauri Shankar Mishra** and **Indra Narayan Dwivedi**, supported by **Madan Mohan Malaviya** ⇒ Grew to 450 branches by June 1919.
* **Key Leaders** ⇒ Jhinguri Singh, Durgapal Singh, and **Baba Ramchandra** (invited Nehru to visit UP villages in June 1920).
* **Awadh Kisan Sabha (October 1920)** ⇒ Formed due to differences in nationalist ranks ⇒ Advised kisans:
  * Refuse to till **bedakhali** land.
  * Refuse **hari** and **begar** (unpaid labor).
  * Boycott non-compliers; settle disputes through panchayats.
* **Radicalisation (January 1921)** ⇒ Shifted from mass meetings to looting of bazaars, houses, granaries, and clashes with police (Rai Bareilly, Faizabad, Sultanpur).
* **Outcome** ⇒ Government repression + **Awadh Rent (Amendment) Act** led to decline.

---

## Eka Movement
* **Context (Late 1921)** ⇒ Peasant discontent resurged in northern UP (Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur).
* **Core Issues** ⇒ High rents (50% above recorded rates) + oppression of thikadars (revenue collectors) + share-rent practices.
* **The Eka Vow (Religious Ritual)** ⇒ Peasants vowed to:
  * Pay only recorded rents, but pay on time.
  * Refuse to leave when evicted.
  * Refuse forced labor.
  * Give no assistance to criminals.
  * Abide by panchayat decisions.
* **Leadership** ⇒ Grassroots low-caste leaders like **Madari Pasi** + small zamindars.
* **Outcome** ⇒ Brutal government repression ended the movement by March 1922.

---

## Mappila Revolt
* **Context (1921)** ⇒ Muslim tenants (**Mappilas**) in Malabar region; landlords (**jenmies**) were mostly Hindus.
* **Grievances** ⇒ High rents, lack of security of tenure, renewal fees, and oppressive exactions.
* **Khilafat Convergence** ⇒ Encouraged by local Congress demand for tenancy laws, Mappila movement merged with ongoing **Khilafat-Non-Cooperation Movement** ⇒ Addressed by Gandhi, Shaukat Ali, Azad.
* **Eruption (August 1921)** ⇒ Arrest of respected priest-leader **Ali Musaliar** sparked large-scale riots.
* **Evolution of Targets**:
  * Phase 1: Targets were British authorities (courts, police stations, treasuries) and unpopular landlords.
  * Phase 2: British martial law + brutal repression ⇒ Many Hindus seen helping authorities ⇒ Rebellion acquired **communal overtones** ⇒ Radical isolation completed ⇒ Crushed by December 1921.

## Bardoli Satyagraha
* **Trigger (January 1926)** ⇒ Authorities raised land revenue by **30%** in Bardoli taluqa (Surat district).
* **Protest Organization** ⇒ Congress inquiry committee declared hike unjustified ⇒ **Vallabhbhai Patel** invited to lead in February 1928 ⇒ Bardoli women bestowed him the title **"Sardar"**.
* **Patel's Strategy**:
  * Peasants resolved to withhold revised assessment until an independent tribunal was set up.
  * Established **13 chhavanis** (workers' camps).
  * Issued **Bardoli Satyagraha Patrika** for public mobilization.
  * Set up an intelligence wing to monitor tenant compliance; social boycotts for non-compliers.
  * Massive mobilization of women.
* **Resignations** ⇒ **K.M. Munshi** and **Lalji Naranji** resigned from Bombay Legislative Council.
* **Resolution (August 1928)** ⇒ High tension (prospect of railway strike in Bombay) + Gandhi arrived ⇒ Government compromised:
  * Demanded nominal payment of enhanced rent (not actually paid).
  * Appointed enquiry committee ⇒ Revenue hike declared unjustified ⇒ Recommended rate reduced to **6.03%**.
* **1930s Expansion** ⇒ Impact of Great Depression + Civil Disobedience Movement (no-rent/no-revenue agitations).

## The All India Kisan Congress/Sabha
* **Foundation (April 1936)** ⇒ Established in Lucknow:
  * President = **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati**
  * General Secretary = **N.G. Ranga**
* **Activities** ⇒ Issued a kisan manifesto; started a periodical under **Indulal Yagnik**.
* **Congress Alignment** ⇒ Held joint sessions in Faizpur (1936) ⇒ Strongly influenced Congress' **agrarian manifesto** for the 1937 provincial elections.

---

## Under Congress Ministries
* **High Watermark (1937–39)** ⇒ Unprecedented peasant activity under Congress provincial rules.
* **Mobilisation Methods** ⇒ Held massive kisan conferences/meetings to air demands and pass resolutions; carried out direct village mobilization campaigns.

## Peasant Activity In Provinces

### Kerala
* **Mobilisation** ⇒ Done primarily by **Congress Socialist Party** activists.
* **Karshak Sanghams** ⇒ Active peasant organizations.
* **Method** ⇒ Marching of **jaths** (peasant groups) directly to landlords to demand concessions.
* **Campaign (1938)** ⇒ Campaign for amendment of the **Malabar Tenancy Act, 1929**.

### Andhra
* **Context** ⇒ Decline of zamindar prestige post-election defeat by Congress; active provincial ryot associations.
* **Institutions & Leaders** ⇒ **N.G. Ranga** set up **India Peasants' Institute (1933)** ⇒ Congress socialists organized peasants post-1936 ⇒ Summer schools of economics/politics held (addressed by P.C. Joshi, Ajoy Ghosh, R.D. Bhardwaj).

### Bihar
* **Leaders** ⇒ **Sahajanand Saraswati** joined by **Karyanand Sharma**, **Yadunandan Sharma**, **Rahul Sankritayan**, **Panchanan Sharma**, and **Jamun Karjiti**.
* **Peasant Action (1935)** ⇒ Provincial Kisan Conference adopted anti-zamindari slogans.
* **Rift with Congress** ⇒ Divergence over the **bakasht land** issue (Kisan Sabha rejected unfavorable government resolution) ⇒ Movement faded by August 1939.

### Punjab
* **Earlier Bases** ⇒ Punjab Naujawan Bharat Sabha, Kirti Kisan Party, Congress, and Akalis.
* **New Wave (1937)** ⇒ Led by **Punjab Kisan Committee** against western Punjab Unionist ministry landlords.
* **Issues** ⇒ Resettlement of land revenue in Amritsar/Lahore, water-rate hikes in Multan/Montgomery canal colonies.
* **Outcome** ⇒ Peasants went on strike, successfully winning concessions.
* **Concentration** ⇒ Jullundur, Amritsar, Hoshiarpur, Lyallpur, and Shekhupura (Muslim tenants of west Punjab and Hindu peasants of southeast Punjab/Haryana remained unaffected).
* **Other Areas** ⇒ Peasant activity also organized in Bengal (Burdwan, 24 Parganas), Assam (Surma Valley), Orissa, Central Provinces, and NWFP.

### During the War
* **The Split** ⇒ Communists adopted pro-War line ⇒ AIKS split along communist/non-communist lines ⇒ Sahjanand, Indulal Yagnik, and N.G. Ranga left the Sabha.
* **Famine Work (1943)** ⇒ Remaining Kisan Sabha did notable relief work during the Bengal Famine.

### Post-War Phase

#### Tebhaga Movement
* **Trigger (September 1946)** ⇒ Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha called to implement the **Flood Commission recommendations** of **Tebhaga** (two-thirds share to sharecroppers/bargardars/bagchasi/adhyar instead of traditional 50%).
* **Context** ⇒ Bargardars rented land from **jotedars**.
* **Mobilisation** ⇒ Communist cadres + urban student militias organized bargardars.
* **Central Slogan** ⇒ *"Nij khamare dhan tolo"* (take paddy to sharecroppers' own threshing floor, not to jotedar's house, to enforce Tebhaga).
* **Storm Center** ⇒ North Bengal, particularly among **Rajbanshis** (low-caste of tribal origin) and Muslims.
* **Dissipation** ⇒ Caused by Muslim League ministry's Bargardari Bill concession + intensified state repression + Hindu Mahasabha's separate Bengal agitation + communal riots in Calcutta.

#### Telangana Movement
* **Scale** ⇒ Biggest peasant guerrilla war of modern India (affected **3,000 villages** and **3 million population**).
* **Hyderabad Context (Asafjahi Nizams)** ⇒ Small Urdu-speaking Muslim elite ruling over predominantly Hindu (Telugu, Marathi, Kannada) populace + absolute lack of civil liberties + brutal forced labor (**vethi**) & illegal exactions by deshmukhs, jagirdars, and doras (landlords).
* **Communist Base** ⇒ Built during war through **Andhra Mahasabha** on issues of rationing, vethi, and rents.
* **Uprising (July 1946)** ⇒ Triggered when a deshmukh's retainers murdered a village militant in Jangaon taluq, Nalgonda ⇒ Spread to Warangal and Khammam.
* **Method of Struggle** ⇒ Peasants formed village **sanghams**, using lathis, stone slings, and chili powder.
* **Peak Intensity (August 1947–September 1948)** ⇒ Peasants routed the **Razakars** (Nizam's stormtroopers).
* **Decline** ⇒ Fizzled out after Indian security forces integrated Hyderabad.
* **Major Achievements**:
  * **Vethi** & forced labor disappeared in guerrilla-controlled villages.
  * Agricultural wages raised; illegally seized lands restored.
  * Land ceilings fixed and lands redistributed.
  * Irrigation improved; measures taken to fight cholera.
  * Improvements in the socio-economic status of women.
  * Autocracy of India's biggest princely state dismantled ⇒ Cleared the path for linguistic formation of **Andhra Pradesh**.

## Balance-Sheet Of Peasant Movements
* **Agrarian Reform Catalyst** ⇒ Created the ground and political atmosphere for post-independence reforms (e.g., abolition of zamindari).
* **Landed Power Eroded** ⇒ Directly transformed agrarian structures by eroding the hegemony of the landed classes.
* **National Integration** ⇒ Rooted in the ideology of nationalism, building similar structures of struggle across diverse regions.
