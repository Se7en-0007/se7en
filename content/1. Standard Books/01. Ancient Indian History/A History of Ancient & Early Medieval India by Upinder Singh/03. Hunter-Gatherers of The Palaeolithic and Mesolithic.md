### Foundational Discoveries & Methods In Prehistory
*   **Pallavaram Handaxe (1863)** = Discovered by **Robert Bruce Foote** (Geological Survey of India) at Pallavaram (Chennai) → Quartzite handaxe → First identified palaeolithic tool in India.
*   **Earliest Prehistoric Find (1856)** = **Le Mesurier** found chert arrowhead near Nyagurhee (Central India).
*   **Foote's Contribution** = Presented finds at Vienna International Exhibition (1873) → Documented hundreds of South/Western Indian sites → Laid foundations of Indian prehistory (Ballari Museum named after him).
*   **Artefact Contexts**:
    *   **Primary Context** = Found exactly where made or used.
    *   **Semi-primary Context** = Slightly shifted from original location.
    *   **Secondary Context** = Far removed from original position (e.g., river gravels).
*   **Analytic Techniques**:
    *   **Experimental Toolmaking** = Replicating stone tool production techniques.
    *   **Ethnographic Analogy** = Studying modern stone tool-using communities.
    *   **Microwear Analysis** = Examining regular wear marks, polish, and gloss under powerful microscopes to deduce tool function (cutting plants, chopping meat, scraping hides).
    *   **Gender Division** = Active participation of both men and women in stone tool manufacturing.

### The Geological Ages and Hominin Evolution
*   **Earth Age ≈ 4.5 billion years** | **Human Appearance ≈ 200,000 BP**.
*   **19th-Century Theoretical Foundations**:
    *   **Charles Darwin** (*The Origin of Species*, **1859**) = Species evolution via adaptation and natural selection ("survival of the fittest").
    *   **Charles Lyell** (*Principles of Geology*, **1830–33**) = Surface changes due to continuous natural processes (erosion, earthquakes, volcanism).
    *   **Thomas Henry Huxley** (*Evidence as to Man's Place in Nature*, **1863**) = Applied evolution theory directly to humans.
    *   **Jacques Boucher de Perthes (1836)** = Somme valley flint tools + extinct animal bones → Proved pre-biblical human antiquity (authenticated by **Falconer**, **Prestwich**, and **Evans**).
*   **Geological Eras & Epochs**:
    *   **Cenozoic Era** ('Age of Mammals') = Began **≈ 100 mya** (combines Tertiary & Quaternary).
    *   **Pleistocene Epoch** = **2.6 mya to 12,000 BP** (dramatic climatic shifts, ice ages/interglacials).
    *   **Holocene Epoch** ('Recent Period') = **12,000 BP to present** (warmer, wetter climate).
*   **Biological Terminology**:
    *   **Species** = Genetically distinct organisms sharing physical/behavioral traits and interbreeding (*Homo sapiens*).
    *   **Genus** = Group of related species (*Homo*).
    *   **Hominid** = Modern and extinct Great Apes + humans (orangutans, gorillas, chimps, humans + ancestors).
    *   **Hominin** = Modern humans + extinct human species + immediate ancestors.
*   **Biological Markers of Evolution**:
    *   **Cranial Capacity ↑** = Brain size enlargement (memory storage, learning, complex behavior).
    *   **Bipedalism** = S-shaped backbone, longer legs than arms, pelvic alteration, prehensile hands with 45° rotatable thumb.
    *   **Dental Modification** = Reduced jaws and non-protruding canines due to changing diets.
    *   **Brain Sizes**: Chimpanzees (**393.8 cc**) → *Australopithecus* (**507.9 cc**) → *Homo erectus* (**973.7 cc**) → Modern *Homo sapiens* (**1350 cc** average).
*   **Genetics & Migration Analysis**:
    *   **Mitochondrial DNA** = Passed strictly from mother to daughter.
    *   **Full Genome Analysis** = Nuclear DNA (2 sets of 23 chromosomes) tracking mutations across both parents.
    *   **Human Genome Project (1990–2003)** = 90% sequenced in 2001; Complete sequence by **Telomere-to-Telomere (T2T) consortium (2022)**.
*   **Hominin Species & Fossil Chronology**:
    *   ***Australopithecus / Ardipithecus ramidus*** = **4.4–1.8 mya** (Sub-Saharan Africa; no conclusive toolmaking).
    *   ***Homo habilis*** ('hand-using man') = **≈ 2 mya** (Koobi Fora Kenya, Olduvai Gorge Tanzania; earliest tools at Hadar Ethiopia **2.5 mya**).
    *   ***Homo erectus*** = **1.7 mya** (East Africa → spread to Europe/Asia; Java **1–2 mya**; Zhoukoudian China **800,000–400,000 BP** with 20,000 tools; Dmanisi Georgia).
    *   ***Homo naledi*** = **≈ 285,000 BP** (South Africa; small brain + ritual burials).
    *   **African Co-existence (≈ 315,000 BP)** = *Homo sapiens*, *Homo heidelbergensis/rhodesiensis*, and *Homo naledi*.
    *   ***Homo sapiens*** = Appeared **≈ 200,000 BP** in Africa (Omo & Herto Ethiopia, Singa Sudan, Laetoli Tanzania, Klasies River Mouth & Border Cave South Africa) → Sustained dispersal out of Africa **≈ 100,000 BP**.
    *   ***Homo floresiensis*** = **60,000 BP** (Flores Island, Indonesia).
    *   **Neanderthals** (*Homo neanderthalensis*) = **130,000–39,000 BP** (Eurasia; interbred with *Homo sapiens* → hybrid populations).
    *   **Denisovans** = Discovered in **Denisova Cave** (Siberia, **2008**).
*   **Out of Africa Dispersal Models**:
    *   **Recent African Origin (RAO) Model** = Modern humans arose in Africa (**≈ 250,000 BP**) and expanded globally (**60,000–90,000 BP**), replacing archaic humans.
    *   **Multiregional Evolution (MRE) Model** = Modern humans evolved parallelly in different world regions from regional archaic populations without recent African replacement.

### Hominin Remains In The Indian Subcontinent
*   **Subcontinental Fossil Scarcity** = Result of inadequate investigations, not absence of hominins.
*   **Siwalik 'God-Apes'** = ***Ramapithecus*, *Sivapithecus*, *Brahmapithecus*** (**10–14 mya**, Miocene–Pliocene transition) → Once thought human ancestors, now reclassified.
*   **Darra-i-Kur (Afghanistan, 1966)** = **Louis Dupree** found right temporal bone fragment (**30,000 ± 1900 BP / 28,950 BCE**) → Neanderthal or modern human with Middle Palaeolithic tools.
*   **Hathnora / Narmada Skull (1982)** = **Arun Sonakia** (GSI) discovered fossilized cranium in sandy gravel of Narmada River (Hoshangabad, MP) with vertebrate fossils + Late Acheulian tools.
    *   **Narmada Woman** = 27–32 year old female; Cranial capacity **1155–1421 cc**.
    *   **Taxonomy** = Named *Homo erectus narmadensis* (Sonakia) / Archaic *Homo sapiens* / Intermediate species.
    *   **Subsequent Finds (1983–97, A.R. Sankhyan)** = Clavicle, femur, humerus fragments at Netankheri (**300,000–75,000 BP**).
*   **Odai Baby Skull (Tamil Nadu, 2001)** = **P. Rajendran** found complete fossilized human baby skull in ferricrete deposit dated **166,000 BP** (Middle/Upper Pleistocene).
*   **Uncertain Age Mandibles**:
    *   Mula-Mutha river (Pune) mandibles (**H.D. Sankalia & S.N. Rajaguru**).
    *   Bhimbetka cave mandible (**V.S. Wakankar**).
*   **Sri Lankan Rain Forest Cave Skeletons**:
    *   **Fa-Hien Lena, Batadomba Lena, Beli Lena, Alu Lena** = Anatomically modern *Homo sapiens* (**37,000–10,500 BP**) → Oldest authenticated modern human remains in South Asia.

### Palaeo-Environments
*   **Tectonic Evolution**:
    *   Gondwanaland breakup → Indian plate drifted north (**20 cm/year**) → Collided with Asian plate (**50–35 mya**).
    *   Collision → Tibetan Plateau + Himalayas uplift → River erosion → Creation of fertile northern alluvial plains.
    *   Ongoing tectonic movement = Indian plate pushes north at **5 cm/year**; Himalayas rising **5–10 mm/year**.
*   **Pleistocene Climatic Cycles**:
    *   Alternating **Glacial** (cold, ice sheets ↑, sea levels ↓) and **Interglacial** (warmer, ice melts, sea levels ↑) phases.
    *   Tropical/Semi-tropical regions = Alternating **Pluvial** (wet) and **Interpluvial** (dry) phases.
*   **Lake Toba Volcanic Super-Eruption (≈ 75,000 BP, Sumatra)**:
    *   Tephra ash deposits embedded across peninsular Indian river valleys.
    *   Debate: Severe global cooling & hominin bottleneck vs Limited/regional ecological impact.
*   **Holocene Transition (≈ 12,000 BP)** = Warmer and wetter conditions established modern climate patterns.
*   **Palaeo-environmental Case Studies**:
    *   **Soan (Sohan) River (Potwar Plateau, Pakistan, 1935)** = **H. de Terra & T.T. Paterson** identified 5 river terraces, attempting correlation with Kashmir/European glacial cycles (Soanian contexts now dated to Late Pleistocene).
    *   **Eastern Ghats (1930)** = **L.A. Cammiade & M.C. Burkitt** correlated stone tool stratigraphy with pluvial/interpluvial climate.
    *   **Son & Belan Valleys (MP/UP)** = Late Pleistocene (cool & dry) → Early Holocene (warm & wet → forest expansion, shrinking grasslands).
    *   **Thar Desert / Didwana (Rajasthan)** = Abundant surface water in Pleistocene → Hyper-arid phase in Upper Pleistocene (**25,000–13,000 BP**) → Monsoon ↑ in Mid-Holocene (**6,000–4,000 BP**).

### Classifying The Indian Stone Age
*   **Three-Age System Origins**: Formulated by **P.F. Suhm & Christian Thomsen** (18th/19th C); confirmed by **Jacob Worsaae**.
*   **Subdivisions**:
    *   **John Lubbock (1863)** = Divided into **Palaeolithic** and **Neolithic**.
    *   **Edouard Lartet** = Subdivided Palaeolithic into **Lower, Middle, and Upper** based on faunal changes.
*   **Tripartite Classification Matrix**:
    *   **Lower Palaeolithic** (2 mya – 100,000 BP) = Lower Pleistocene | Core & pebble tools (handaxes, cleavers, choppers) | Foraging.
    *   **Middle Palaeolithic** (100,000 – 40,000 BP) = Middle Pleistocene | Flake tools (Levallois prepared core technique) | Foraging.
    *   **Upper Palaeolithic** (40,000 – 10,000 BP) = Upper Pleistocene | Parallel-sided blades & burins | Foraging.
    *   **Mesolithic** (12,000 BP onwards) = Holocene | Microliths | Hunting, gathering, fishing, incipient animal domestication.
    *   **Neolithic** (Holocene) = Polished stone celts | Food production (plant & animal domestication).
*   **Key Qualifications**: Non-linear regional evolution, overlapping dates, tool persistence (e.g., celts into historic era), and continuation of foraging alongside agriculture.

### The Palaeolithic

#### Lower Palaeolithic
*   **Tool Technology**: Heavy core tools made of quartzite; Fire-shattering technique used to dislodge large rock flakes.
*   **Tool Typology**:
    *   **Core Tool** = Made from the main block/core.
    *   **Flake Tool** = Made from smaller flakes detached from core.
    *   **Biface / Handaxe** = Roughly triangular core tool flaked on both sides.
    *   **Pebble Tool** = Flaked only on working edge.
    *   **Chopping Tool** = Core/pebble tool flaked alternately on both sides (wavy edge).
    *   **Chopper** = Unifacial tool flaked on one side only.
    *   **Cleaver** = Flattish flake tool with broad, straight cutting edge.
    *   **Acheulian Industry** = Assemblage characterized by advanced, symmetrical handaxes and cleavers.
*   **Chronology & Key Lower Palaeolithic Sites**:
    *   **Riwat & Potwar (Pakistan)** = Artefacts embedded in conglomerate dated **2.01 mya** (palaeo-magnetic method); Pinjor beds (**2.4–2 mya**); Dina & Jalalpur (**700,000–500,000 BP**).
    *   **Isampur (Gulbarga, Karnataka)** = **K. Paddayya** excavated 7,200 sq m quarrying/factory hub; Siliceous limestone tools; Bovid enamel dated **1.2 mya** (ESR method) → One of oldest Lower Palaeolithic dates in South Asia.
    *   **Attirampakkam (Tamil Nadu)** = **Shanti Pappu & Kumar Akhilesh** excavated 9 m fluvial sequence; Earliest Acheulian levels dated **1.51 ± 0.07 mya** (Al/Be burial dating & palaeo-magnetism) → Contemporary with African Acheulian.
    *   **Bori (Maharashtra)** = Tephra layer dated **1.4 mya** (Potassium-Argon method).
    *   **Didwana (Rajasthan)** = Dated **390,000 BP** (Uranium-Thorium method).
    *   **Nevasa (MH) & Yedurwadi (KA)** = Dated **350,000 BP**.
    *   **Hiran Valley (Gujarat)** = Dated **190,000–69,000 BP**.
    *   **Patpara & Bamburi (Son Valley, MP)** = Late Acheulian dated **140,000–120,000 BP** (OSL method).
*   **Habitation & Factory Sites**:
    *   **Anangpur / Badarpur Hills (Delhi)** = Thousands of Acheulian tools near ancient Yamuna palaeo-channels.
    *   **Bhimbetka (Raisen, MP)** = Sandstone shelters; 5 lower palaeolithic stone-paved floors; Yellow quartzite tools.
    *   **Paisra (Munger, Bihar)** = Excavated living/working floor with anvils and 8 post-holes for thatched huts.
    *   **Hunsgi-Baichbal Valleys (Karnataka)** = Granite block supports for 63 sq m temporary shelters.
    *   **Northeast & Regional Affinities**: Hoabinhian pebble-tool affinities; Ranyak Khen cave (Nagaland, **AMS 5,560 BP** pre-neolithic context).
    *   **Bangladesh & Nepal**: Petrified wood tools in Lalmai-Mainamati & Habiganj; Babai valley Acheulian handaxes (Gudrun Corvinus).

#### Middle Palaeolithic
*   **Technical Shift**: Smaller, lighter flake tools replacing heavy core tools.
*   **Core Preparation Techniques**:
    *   **Levallois Technique** = Core trimmed centrally from edges inward → Striking platform flattened → Single blow detaches thin, oval/triangular flake with sharp edges ('tortoise core').
    *   **Discoid Core Technique** = Flakes scalloped from core circumference → Produces multiple small flakes.
*   **Tool Typology**: Scrapers (convex, concave, side, end, denticulated), borers, points, knives-on-flake.
*   **Chronology & Key Middle Palaeolithic Sites**:
    *   **Attirampakkam Transition** = Acheulian to Middle Palaeolithic transition dated **385 ± 64 ka to 172 ± 41 ka** (Luminescence dating) → Coincides with African/European middle palaeolithic emergence.
    *   **Nevasan Industry** = Coined by **H.D. Sankalia** after Nevasa (MH); Fine-grained raw materials (agate, jasper, chalcedony).
    *   **Didwana (Rajasthan)** = Dated **150,000–144,000 BP** (TL dating).
    *   **Kalpi (Yamuna cliff, UP)** = Tool-bearing level dated **45,000 BP**; Middle palaeolithic stone & bone tools + elephant tusk/molars.
    *   **Sanghao Cave (NWFP, Pakistan)** = 3 m thick quartz flake-tool deposit with burins, hearths, animal bones.
    *   **Luni Industry** = Middle palaeolithic assemblages west of Aravallis in Thar Desert (Budha Pushkar, Hokra, Baridhani).
    *   **Belan & Son Valleys** = 87 sites in Belan; 86 sites in Son valley (chert, jasper, flint).

#### Upper Palaeolithic
*   **Technical Innovations**: Parallel-sided blades (length > 2× width) & burins (screwdriver-like engraving tools for wood/bone).
*   **Chronology**: Riwat Site 55 (**45,000 BP**); Sanghao Cave (**41,825–20,660 BCE**); Kurnool Caves (**19,224–16,686 BP**); Belan Valley (**25,000–19,000 BP**); Son Valley (**10,000 BP**); Kashmir (**18,000 BP**).
*   **Key Sites & Findings**:
    *   **Kurnool Caves (Muchchatla Chintamanu Gavi, AP)** = **90% tools made of animal bone**; 30+ animal species (thick forest & humid climate indicator).
    *   **Chopani Mando (Belan Valley, UP)** = Stratigraphic sequence from Upper Palaeolithic to Neolithic; Chert tools + wild sheep/goat bones.
    *   **Baghor I & Baghor III (Son Valley, MP)** = **G.R. Sharma & J.D. Clark**; Microwear study (**Sinha**) showed food processing, wood/bamboo gear crafting, and hafted tools.

#### Palaeolithic Art and Cults
*   **Definitions**: Rock art = Petroglyphs (engraving/bruising/chiselling) + Cave paintings.
*   **Palaeolithic Art Evidence in India**:
    *   **Lohanda Nala (Belan Valley, UP)** = Damaged carved bone object → Identified as Mother Goddess figurine or Harpoon.
    *   **Patne (Maharashtra)** = **Engraved ostrich eggshell** with criss-cross panel (**25,000 BP**) + 4 perforated ostrich eggshell beads.
    *   **Bhimbetka (MP)** = Ostrich eggshell bead found on neck of male skeleton in upper palaeolithic burial.
    *   **Bhimbetka Auditorium Cave (III F-24)** = **7 cupules + 1 large cup mark with meandering line** on rock gong → Lower palaeolithic ritual site (world's oldest rock art).
    *   **Baghor I Upper Palaeolithic Shrine (MP, c. 9000–8000 BCE)** = Sandstone rubble circular platform with central natural triangular laminated stone (worshipped as female principle/goddess icon by local Kol & Baiga tribes).
    *   **Daraki-Chattan & Chaturbhujnath Nala (MP)** = Cave with **500+ cupules** on walls.

#### Life-Ways of Palaeolithic Hunter-Gatherers
*   **Band Society Structure**: Small groups (< 100 people), nomadic, kinship-based, division of labor by age/sex, gift reciprocity, no private property or formal government.
*   **Reassessing Hunter-Gatherer Life**:
    *   Not a constant struggle for survival; limited material desires + non-hoarding technology → **Ample leisure time**.
    *    Restraint practiced in resource exploitation to preserve ecosystem.
    *   **Gathering vs. Hunting** = Plant foraging provided major dietary proportion → Women contributed significantly to group subsistence.
*   **Ethno-Archaeological Case Studies**:
    *   **Hunsgi Valley (K. Paddayya)** = 40 wild edible plant species + small game/aquatic fauna → Summer plant scarcity forced increased hunting.
    *   **Andhra Tribes (M.L.K. Murty)** = Chenchus, Yanandis, Yerukulas, Boyas exploit **80+ wild plant species** → Spatial congruence between prehistoric and modern foraging sites.

### The Mesolithic

#### Mesolithic Sites & Technology
*   **Holocene Transition (≈ 12,000 BP)** = Environmental warming/wetness → Population expansion into new ecological niches.
*   **Microliths Definition**: Very small stone tools (**1–5 cm**) made on short parallel-sided blades of crypto-crystalline silica (chert, chalcedony, jasper, agate).
*   **Microlith Classification**:
    *   **Non-geometric** = Micro-blades, points, micro-burins, scrapers.
    *   **Geometric** = Lunates (crescents), triangles, trapezes, rhomboids.
    *   **Composite Tools** = Microliths hafted onto bone/wood shafts using resin/pitch → Created spears, arrowheads, daggers, harpoons, and curved harvesting sickles.
*   **Early Microlith Evidence**: Mehtakheri MP (**48,000 BP**); Fa-Hien Lena Sri Lanka (**48,000–45,000 BP**); Jwalapuram AP (**35,000 BP**).
*   **Key Site Profiles & Absolute Dates**:
    *   **Sarai Nahar Rai (Pratapgarh, UP)** = **c. 8400 ± 150 BCE**; Oxbow lake; 11 oblong burials (9 men, 4 women, 1 child, 1 skeleton with arrow embedded in ribs); Osteoarthritis in bones.
    *   **Mahadaha (Pratapgarh, UP)** = Oxbow lake site; Butchering area + 28 burials of 30 individuals (2 double male-female burials); Bone rings & arrowheads; Tall stature (men up to 1.90 m); **Seasonality analysis (U.C. Chattopadhyaya)** on swamp/hog deer teeth + bandicoot rat → Year-round permanent settlement.
    *   **Damdama (Pratapgarh, UP)** = **c. 6690–6915 BCE**; 41 human burials (4 double burials, ivory pendant); Domesticated rice reported.
    *   **Chopani Mando (Belan Valley, UP)** = Transition from Epi-palaeolithic to Mesolithic; 13 clustered round/oval wattle-and-daub huts, hearths, bamboo storage bin bases, handmade cord-impressed pottery, wild rice.
    *   **Bagor (Bhilwara, Rajasthan)** = Sand dune site on Kothari river (**V.N. Misra, G.S. Khanna, A. Kashyap**); Period I Mesolithic (**5700–3500 BCE** a-ceramic & ceramic phases); Stone-paved floors, butchering areas; **Chalcedony procurement** from Deccan Traps (90 km SE); SEM/EDS starch grain analysis → Plant processing ↑ & incipient agriculture.
    *   **Langhnaj (Gujarat)** = **2550–2185 BCE**; Microliths, wild fauna (rhino, hog deer, swamp deer), handmade pottery, 14 human skeletons.
    *   **Adamgarh (Hoshangabad, MP)** = **c. 5505 BCE** (C-14 shell date); Thousands of microliths + early pottery.
    *   **Langhnaj / Kanewal / Loteshwar (Gujarat)** = Faunal evidence of domesticated cattle, sheep, and goats.
    *   **Teri Sites (Tamil Nadu)** = Red sand dune microlithic sites of coastal fishing communities.
    *   **Sri Lankan Rain Forest Caves (Fa-Hien Lena, Batadomba Lena, Beli Lena)** = **40,000–8,000 BP**; Arboreal monkey and squirrel hunting (70% faunal remains); Quartz microliths & bone tools.

#### Mesolithic Rock Art
*   **Foundational Discoveries**:
    *   **A.C.L. Carlleyle (1867–68)** = Discovered first Indian rock paintings at Sohagighat (Kaimur Hills, Mirzapur, UP).
    *   **V.S. Wakankar (1957)** = Discovered Bhimbetka rock shelters from train window.
*   **Bhimbetka Rock Art Profile (Raisen, MP)**:
    *   642 rock shelters (400 painted); UNESCO World Heritage Site (2003).
    *   **Phases & Pigments (Y. Mathpal)** = 3 main phases (5 mesolithic sub-phases); 16 color shades (geru red from iron oxide, white from limestone, green from chalcedony); Brushes made of semal cotton/animal fur.
    *   **Faunal Depictions** = 29 animal species (chital most frequent, leopard, rhino, elephant); 'X-ray style' (internal organs/foetuses shown); Composite 'Bhimbetka Boar' beast.
    *   **Human & Social Themes**:
        *   Gender division of labor = Men hunt with bows, spears, masks, dogs; Women gather/process food on querns.
        *   Masked dancers ('dancing sorcerers'), sexual activity, family scenes, honey collection, hand/fist prints.
*   **Regional Rock Art Patterns**:
    *   **Odisha (Lekhamoda group)** = Non-figurative abstract/geometric patterns & engravings.
    *   **Kerala (Ezhuthu Guha)** = Geometric figures in sandalwood forest cave.
    *   **Deccan (AP, Karnataka, TN)** = Agropastoral cattle depictions.
    *   **Jaora Abstract Painting (MP)** = Cosmic representation of air, earth, and fire.

### Conclusions
*   Prehistory represents the longest span of South Asian human history.
*   Palaeolithic and Mesolithic communities adapted dynamically to changing Pleistocene and Holocene environments.
*   Shift from heavy core tools (Acheulian) → prepared flake tools (Middle Palaeolithic) → blades/burins (Upper Palaeolithic) → composite microliths (Mesolithic).
*   Subsistence was rooted in hunting and gathering, but animal domestication and plant processing emerged during the Mesolithic phase, setting the foundation for the Neolithic revolution.
