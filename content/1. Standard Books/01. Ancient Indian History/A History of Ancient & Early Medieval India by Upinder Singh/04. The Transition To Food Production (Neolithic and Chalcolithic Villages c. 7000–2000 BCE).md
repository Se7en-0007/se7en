### The Neolithic Age and The Beginnings of Food Production

*   **Neolithic Definition** = Ground, pecked, polished stone tools + advent of food production + pottery + sedentism + self-sufficient village communities.
*   **Neolithic Revolution (V. Gordon Childe)** = Phrase highlighting massive socio-economic shift → gradual, multi-centric process across regions.
*   **Plant or Animal Domestication** = Human control over breeding/rearing under artificial conditions for gain.
    *   *Food Collection* = Grain harvested → all consumed.
    *   *Plant Domestication* = Grain harvested → part consumed + part intentionally sown.
    *   *Animal Keeping* = Capture & containment of wild species.
    *   *Animal Domestication* = Wild species bred & reared under artificial conditions for profit.
*   **Subsistence Continuum** = Simple Foraging (Food Collection) → Complex Foraging (Intensive Wild Exploitation) → Incipient Agriculture → Developed Agriculture.
*   **Food-Producing Society Definition** = Meets ≥ 50% of food needs for part of the year via domesticated plants/animals outside natural habitats.

#### **Why Domestication ?**
*   **Oasis / Desiccation Theory (V. Gordon Childe, 1952)**:
    *   Post-Pleistocene climate change → Northward shift of summer rains → Desiccation in West Asia.
    *   Human + plant + animal concentration near water (oases/rivers) → Forced closeness → Interdependence → Domestication.
*   **Nuclear Zone Theory (Robert J. Braidwood, 1960)**:
    *   Rejected environmental determinism (climate shifts occurred earlier without agriculture).
    *   Domestication = Natural outcome of human experimentation in **nuclear zones** (habitats naturally rich in domesticable wild ancestors).
    *   *Critique:* Lacks explanation for specific incentives/pressures to alter lifestyles.
*   **Demographic Stress / Binford's Model (Lewis R. Binford, 1968)**:
    *   Equilibrium exists when population & resources remain constant.
    *   Equilibrium broken by **Demographic Stress** = Internal (local population rise) or External (immigration).
    *   Post-Pleistocene sea level rise → Coastal groups forced inland → External demographic stress → Search for new food strategies → Agriculture.
    *   *Critique:* Lack of archaeological proof for post-Pleistocene coastal-to-inland migrations.
*   **Feed-back Systems Model (Kent Flannery, 1969)**:
    *   Focus on process & adaptive advantage over hunting-gathering.
    *   *Negative Feedback System* = Balanced exploitation; resists change.
    *   *Positive Feedback System* = Human interference increases resource productivity (e.g., wild grass teosinte → domesticated maize cob size ↑ & grain count ↑) → Incentive to intensify domestication.
*   **Environmental Amelioration Theory (Recent Studies)**:
    *   Holocene onset → Milder, warmer, wetter climate → Expansion of natural habitats of wild cereals → Amelioration (not crisis) triggered domestication.

#### **The Identification of Domestication and Food Production In The Archaeological Record**
*   **Morphological Changes in Animals**:
    *   Early domesticates = Smaller than wild counterparts (size increases later under optimal breeding).
    *   Face shortens relative to cranium; teeth reduce in size (premolars/3rd molars may disappear).
    *   Horns shrink; cattle develop weak muscle ridges; draught animals show specific muscle strengthening.
    *   Hair shortens; coat color varies.
*   **Faunal Demographics**:
    *   Wild ratio = 1:1 male to female.
    *   Domesticated ratio = Young males/castrates culled early; females kept to old age for breeding/milk.
*   **Morphological Changes in Plants**:
    *   Wild cereals (wheat/barley) = Larger grains, brittle ears, fragile spikes (maximizes natural seed dispersal).
    *   Domesticated cereals = Tough rachis (non-shattering ears; break only during threshing).
    *   *Root crops (potatoes/yams)* = Soft tissues, asexual reproduction → minimal genetic/morphological changes; rare survival in archaeological record.
*   **Direct & Indirect Evidence**:
    *   *Direct:* Carbonized seeds/grains, grain/husk impressions on clay/pottery.
    *   *Indirect:* Sickles, grinding stones (can also reap/grind wild plants), art depictions of animal tending, pollen/mollusc environmental changes.

#### **The Analysis of Ancient Plant Remains**
*   **Palaeobotany / Archaeobotany** = Study of ancient plant remains.
*   **Macro-botanical Remains** = Seeds, grains, charcoal preserved via desiccation, waterlogging, or charring.
    *   **Flotation Technique** = Dried plant-bearing soil matrix poured into liquid/water medium → Heavy inorganic sediment sinks; light carbonized seeds float to surface → Filtered via sieves for microscopic analysis.
*   **Micro-botanical Remains**:
    *   **Phytoliths** = Microscopic silica bodies in plant parts (roots, stems, flowers); differentiates wild vs. domesticated species.
    *   **Parenchyma** = Soft tissue cells in plant stems/fruit pulp; useful for identifying root crops.
    *   **Palynology** = Analysis of pollen grains (durable outer exine shell); profiles track climate shifts, forest clearance, paddy cultivation.
*   **Advanced Scientific Methods**:
    *   **AMS (Accelerator Mass Spectrometry) Dating** = Direct C-14 dating of tiny seed/cob fragments.
    *   **DNA Studies** = Maps chromosome structures to identify wild progenitors and genetic origins.

### The Transition To Food Production In The Indian Subcontinent

*   **Subcontinental Features**:
    *   Mesolithic roots: Pottery & animal keeping pre-date Neolithic at certain sites.
    *   A-ceramic Neolithic phases exist (e.g., Mehrgarh I, Gufkral IA).
    *   Varied sedentism: Nomadism vs. permanent villages exist on a continuum.
    *   Coexistence: Farmers/pastoralists coexisted alongside contemporary hunter-gatherers.
*   **Chronological Framework**:
    *   **Phase I**: c. 7000–3000 BCE.
    *   **Phase II**: c. 3000–2000 BCE.
    *   **Phase III**: c. 2000–1000 BCE onwards.

### The Earliest Village Settlements, c. 7000–3000 BCE

#### **The North-West**

*   **Mehrgarh** (Bolan Valley, Kachi Plain, Baluchistan):
    *   Location = Strategic trade/movement route between Indus plains and Afghan mountains via Bolan Pass. Site covers 200 ha over low mound.
    *   **Period I (IA & IB)** = A-ceramic Neolithic (c. 7000/6000–5500 BCE).
        *   *Architecture:* Rectangular mud-brick houses (standardized bricks with rounded ends & finger impressions); small room units = granaries.
        *   *Tools:* Microlithic blade industry, bitumen-hafted microlithic sickles, ground celts, bone needles/awls, stone vessels.
        *   *Burials:* Oval pits, flexed bodies covered in red ochre. Grave goods = bitumen-lined baskets, stone/shell beads, bone pendants, copper bead (1 burial), turquoise (Iran/Central Asia), lapis lazuli (Afghanistan), marine shells (Makran coast 500 km away) → Long-distance trade.
        *   *Period IB:* 150-burial cemetery; mud-brick walled niches, double burials, secondary burials.
    *   **Period II (c. 6000–4500 BCE)** = Ceramic Neolithic.
        *   *Structures:* Double rows of storage cells containing barley seeds.
        *   *Finds:* Bitumen-hafted sickles, copper ring/bead/ingot, ivory tusk, unbaked male figurine. Wheel-made pottery appears in Period IIC.
        *   *Microwear Study (P. Vaughan):* Tools used for animal butchery, hide processing, bone working.
    *   **Period III (Late 5th Millennium BCE)** = Chalcolithic.
        *   *Crafts:* Wheel-made painted pottery (6 m pottery debris + 3 kilns), steatite micro-beads, semi-precious stone beads (lapis, turquoise, agate), micro-drills for shell, terracotta humped bulls, copper crucibles (smelting).
        *   *Burials:* 99-skeleton cemetery; heads rested on bricks; 1 collective burial with 2 painted pots.
    *   **Subsistence Sequence at Mehrgarh**:
        *   *Cereals:* Dominant crop = **six-row naked barley** (*Hordeum vulgare nudum*), plus hulled 6-row & wild 2-row barley → Proves Kachi plain was a primary nuclear area for barley domestication. Wheat = einkorn, emmer, naked wheat (*Triticum sphaerococcum*). Ber, dates, and early **cotton** (*Gossypium sp.*) seeds in Period II. Period III = Oats added; wheat surpasses barley.
        *   *Animals:* Period IA lower levels = Wild animals dominant (gazelle, blackbuck, nilgai, wild goat, onager, buffalo, pig). Upper IA → Period III = Wild species ↓; domesticated cattle (*Bos indicus*), sheep, goat ↑ (Cattle dominant).
        *   *Dental Health (J.R. Lukacs):* Low caries in early phases (high fluoride in water + coarse diet); dental decay rises in Period III (refined food).
    *   **Periods IV–VI Expansion**:
        *   Period IV = Polychrome ware, female figurines with tubular bodies.
        *   Period VI = Red ware with pipal leaf motifs, well-fired grey ware, elaborate female figurines with heavy hairstyles/breasts (cultic significance).
*   **Kile Gul Mohammad (KGM) & Damb Sadaat** (Quetta Valley):
    *   *KGM I (c. 5500–4500 BCE):* A-ceramic; mud/wattle-and-daub huts; microliths; domesticated cattle, sheep, goat, ass/horse.
    *   *KGM II & III:* Handmade basket-marked ware (II) → Wheel-made black-on-red ware + copper (III).
    *   *KGM IV & Damb Sadaat I:* **Kechi Beg Ware** (thin, buff-colored, black/red geometric painting).
    *   *Damb Sadaat II (c. 3000 BCE):* Multi-room mud-brick houses with limestone foundations, tandoor-like hearths. **Quetta Ware** (black-on-buff) & **Faiz Mohammad Grey Ware**. Terracotta bull/female figurines, house models, copper dagger blade.
*   **Anjira & Siah Damb** (Kalat Plateau; Beatrice de Cardi):
    *   Period I–II = Semi-nomadic → mud-brick on stone boulder foundations; burnished red slip & grey ware.
    *   Period III = Dressed stone foundations; **Togau Ware** (red ware painted with stylized ibex, bird, goat motifs) & **Zari Ware** (black/white painting).
    *   Period IV–V = Dressed square stone blocks; Nal-type pottery.
*   **Mundigak** (Southeast Afghanistan; J.M. Casal):
    *   Period I (c. 4000–3500 BCE) = Pressed earth/sun-dried brick cells, wheel-made pottery, alabaster vessels, lapis beads, copper needle.
    *   Period II = Domesticated club wheat (*Triticum compactum*), ber, cattle, sheep, goat.
*   **Zhob–Loralai Sites** (Anambar & Loralai Valleys):
    *   *Sur Jangal:* Cattle-painted pottery, goggle-eyed 'Zhob Mother Goddess' female figurines.
    *   *Rana Ghundai:* Period I (c. 4500–4300 BCE) = Semi-nomadic, handmade plain pottery, domesticated animals + horse/ass teeth debate. Period II = Wheel-made pottery with friezes of stylized humped bulls & blackbuck.
*   **Gomal Valley** (Dera Ismail Khan District):
    *   *Gumla:* Period I = A-ceramic, community ovens. Period II = Painted pottery (cattle, fish), terracotta female figurines (Central Asian Turkmenistan parallels).
    *   *Sheri Khan Tarakai* (Bannu Basin, c. 4500–3000 BCE): Mud-brick on stone foundations, ground celts, rusticated pottery, marine chank shells, barley, cattle, sheep, goat, buffalo.
*   **Potwar Plateau**:
    *   *Sarai Khola I (4th Millennium BCE):* Handmade burnished red/brown pottery with mat-impressed bases, ground stone celts, bone points, terracotta toy wheels.
*   **Nal Culture** (Khozdar, Baluchistan):
    *   *Settlement:* Stone boulder/quarried stone houses.
    *   *Burials:* Fractional pot burials, complete graves, child mud-brick chamber burial.
    *   *Pottery:* **Nal Polychrome Ware** (disc bases; painted in blue, red, yellow with fish & ibex).
    *   *Metal & Water:* Copper-nickel-lead adze. Water systems = Stone hill-slope terracing embankments + basin dam/canal systems.
*   **Kulli Culture** (Kolwa Tract, South Baluchistan):
    *   *Sites:* Kulli (12 ha), Mehi, Nindowari, Adam Buthi (3500–3000 BCE, earliest).
    *   *Pottery:* **Kulli Ware** = Elongated cattle with large round eyes set in landscape scenes.
*   **Bala Kot** (Makran Coast, c. 4000–3000 BCE):
    *   Period I = Mud-brick houses, Nal-type wheel-made pottery, barley, domesticated cattle, sheep, goat, buffalo, pig, marine shells.
*   **Cholistan Desert / Ghaggar-Hakra River (M.R. Mughal)**:
    *   **Hakra Wares Sites** (c. 3500 BCE onwards): 99 sites identified (52% campsites, 45% permanent, craft centers).
    *   *Pottery:* **Hakra Ware** = Mud-applique coating mixed with pot sherds, incised lines, black-slipped carinated vases.
    *   *Finds:* Valwali (32 bull figurines, copper), Jalilpur (Hakra ware, terracotta net sinkers for fishing, sheep/goat/cattle/gazelle bones).
*   **Early Harappan Transitions**:
    *   *Harappa (Ravi Aspect of Hakra Phase, c. 3500/3300–2800 BCE):* Reed-plastered huts, potsherds with pre-firing marks & post-firing graffiti = Formative Harappan script.
    *   *Kunal IA (Haryana):* Pit dwellings with wattle-and-daub, Hakra ware painted with pipal leaves & curved-horn bulls, copper fishhooks & arrowheads.
    *   *Bhirrana IA (Haryana):* Shallow mud-plastered dwelling pits (34–58 cm deep), Hakra wares, bi-chrome ware, lapis lazuli, carnelian beads.

#### **The Vindhyan Fringes, Middle Ganga Plains and Other Areas**

*   **Vindhyan Fringes (Southern UP & Northern MP)**:
    *   Emergence = Evolved directly out of Mesolithic baseline (e.g., Chopani Mando wild rice, Damdama domesticated rice).
    *   **Koldihwa** (Belan Valley, Allahabad): Cord-marked/net-marked pottery, red ware, black-and-red ware. Yielded wild & cultivated rice (*Oryza sativa*) imprints in pottery/clay. *Dates:* C-14 dates fall into two clusters (8th–6th millennium BCE vs. much later).
    *   **Mahagara** (Belan Valley): 20 wattle-and-daub huts. Central fenced cattle pen (12.5 × 7.5 m, 20 post-holes) with 40–60 cattle hoof-marks + sheep/goat hoof tracks outside. Domesticated cattle, sheep, goat, horse; rice husk imprints.
    *   **Kunjhun** (Son Valley, MP, 4th Millennium BCE): Specialized factory site for heating stone & making blades; wild & domesticated rice.
*   **Middle Ganga Plain**:
    *   **Lahuradeva** (Sant Kabir Nagar, UP; Rakesh Tewari):
        *   *Sequence:* 5-fold sequence (Period IA to V).
        *   *Period IA (Neolithic, 8300–5000 BP / c. 6500–3000 BCE):* Wattle-and-daub huts, corded ware, hand-made red/black-and-red pottery.
            *   *Flora:* AMS dated domesticated rice (*Oryza sativa*) husk at **8360 BP (c. 6500 BCE)**; paddy field phytoliths (8300 BP) & diatoms (9720 BP); foxtail millet, job's tear.
            *   *Fauna:* Exclusively wild game (gaur, deer, nilgai, mud turtle, fish, water birds); **zero domesticated animal bones**.
        *   *Period IB (c. 5000–4000 BP / 3000–2000 BCE):* Aridity rises. Rice ↑; double cropping introduced (wheat, barley, lentil, field pea). Domesticated cattle & goat introduced alongside wild hunting.
        *   *Period II (c. 2000–1300 BCE):* Wetter climate. 40+ grain silos/granary; painted black-slipped ware. Domesticated animals dominate (cattle, sheep, goat, buffalo). Butchered cattle bone cluster (*Bos indicus*) = **1,800 kg meat feast** ritual.
    *   **Jhusi** (Ganga-Yamuna Confluence, Allahabad): 16.5 m deposit. Early C-14 dates (7th millennium BCE) for cultivated rice (*Oryza sativa*), bread/dwarf wheat, barley, lentils, flax, grape vine.
    *   **Rajasthan Salt Lakes (Didwana, Lunkaransar, Sambhar)**: Cereal-type pollen & charcoal spike c. 7000 BCE → Indirect evidence of vegetation clearance & early agriculture.

### Neolithic, Neolithic–Chalcolithic and Chalcolithic Communities, c. 3000–2000 BCE

#### **The North and North-West**

*   **Kashmir Valley Karewas** (Ancient Lake Beds):
    *   **Burzahom** (16 km NE of Srinagar; T.N. Khazanchi):
        *   *Period I (Pre-c. 2920 BCE):*
            *   **Pit Dwellings:** Round/oval mud-plastered pits (up to 3.96 m deep, 4.57 m base diameter) with post-holes for pinewood/birch thatched roofs, steps, ladders, hearths, and peripheral storage pits. Central area contained square/rectangular pit chambers (6.4 × 7 m).
            *   *Tools & Pottery:* Coarse handmade grey/red/brown mat-impressed pottery. Ground stone axes, chisels, ring stones/mace heads, perforated stone **harvesters** (rectangular double-holed knives). Rich bone industry (needles, harpoons, awls, daggers).
            *   *Burials:* None found in Period I.
        *   *Further Discussion | Pit Dwellings Controversy (Coningham & Sutherland):*
            *   Traditional view = Winter pit homes.
            *   Alternative view = Smoke inside unvented pits makes them uninhabitable; fires were lit to dry mud plaster/clear damp. Pits functioned as **underground seasonal grain silos** used during spring/summer occupancy, sealed in winter when humans migrated to lower valleys.
        *   *Period II (Ends c. 1700 BCE):*
            *   Shift to ground-level mud/mud-brick houses over filled-in pits.
            *   *Burials:* Inhumation & secondary burials under house floors in lime-plastered oval pits; red ochre staining; skull **trepanning** (boring holes).
            *   *Animal Burials:* Dogs, wolves, ibex, deer, cattle, sheep buried with humans or in separate pits (e.g., 5 dogs + antlers).
            *   *Deluxe Ware:* Black burnished high-necked globular jars, funnel-shaped vases.
            *   *Art:* Two engraved stone slabs—one depicting a hut, the other a **hunting scene** (female hunter with spear & male with bow attacking a antlered stag).
            *   *Agriculture & Trade:* Direct grain evidence (wheat, barley, lentils). Horned deity painted pot = Kot Diji connection.
    *   **Gufkral** (41 km SE of Srinagar; 'Cave of the Potter'):
        *   *Period IA (A-ceramic Neolithic, c. 3000 BCE):* Shallow pit dwellings, red-ochre plastered floors. Polished stone tools, large querns, bone needles/arrowheads with charred tips. Wild animals dominant + early domesticated sheep/goat. Grains = barley, wheat, lentils.
        *   *Period IB (c. 2468–2139 BCE):* Handmade mat-impressed grey pottery. Pit dwellings disappear → compact clay/lime floors, mud/rubble walls. Domesticated animals ↑ (cattle, sheep, goat, dogs), common pea added. Destruction by extensive fire.
        *   *Period IC (c. 2000–1300 BCE):* Wheel-made pottery, dish-on-stand with perforated stems. Double-holed harvesters, stone/terracotta spindle whorls (wool weaving), copper spiral-headed hairpin. Animal domestication fully dominant over hunting.
*   **Swat Valley (Pakistan)**:
    *   *Ghaligai Cave (c. 3000 BCE):* Coarse handmade pottery, pebble tools, bone points.
    *   *Swat Grave Sites (Loebanr III, Aligrama, Kalako-deray):* Pit dwellings, wheat, barley, rice, lentils, field pea, grape seed (*Vitis vinifera*), Central Asian jade beads.

#### **Rajasthan**

*   **Ganeshwar–Jodhpura Culture** (NE Rajasthan, Baleshwar/Khetri Copper Belt):
    *   *Jodhpura (Sahibi River):* Wheel-made orange-red ware with incised designs, dish-on-stand (c. 2800–2700 BCE).
    *   *Ganeshwar (Sikar District, 1.2–1.6 ha):*
        *   Period I (c. 3800 BCE) = Microliths, wild animal hunting.
        *   Period IIa (c. 2800 BCE) = Beginning of metallurgy (5 arrowheads, 3 fishhooks, spearhead, circular pebble huts).
        *   Period IIb (c. 2800–2000 BCE) = **Metallurgical expansion** (1,000+ copper celts, chisels, arrowheads, rings, bangles; micaceous red-slipped ware).
        *   *Significance:* Major copper-processing supplier center catering to Early/Mature Harappan sites (e.g., Banawali reserved slip ware, double spiral-headed copper pins).
*   **Ahar / Banas Culture** (SE Rajasthan, Banas/Berach Rivers, c. 3300–1500 BCE):
    *   *Diagnostic Pottery:* Black-and-red ware painted with white linear/dotted designs.
    *   *Ahar (Udaipur, 11 ha):* Period Ia–Ic. Mud houses on stone foundations, multi-mouthed ovens. **Local copper smelting** (copper sheets, slag, socketless axes; no microliths). Rice, cow, buffalo, goat, sheep, deer. Period Ib/Ic yields early iron artifacts (nail/ring).
    *   *Gilund (10.5 ha):* Mud-brick complex (30 × 24 m), burnt-brick wall on stone rubble foundation, terracota long-horned bull figurines.
    *   *Balathal (Udaipur, 2 ha, c. 3700–2000 BCE):*
        *   Early Period I = Circular wattle-and-daub huts.
        *   Late Period I = **Massive mud fortification wall** (4.8–5 m thick with bastions enclosing 500 sq m), central street, multi-roomed mud-brick/stone complexes, 2 potter's kilns.
        *   *Artefacts & Diet:* Reserve slip ware, 1,000s of copper items (razors, chisels, barbed arrowheads). 73% cattle bones, wheat, barley, millets, moong, black gram, tawas, u-shaped chulhas.
    *   *Ojiyana (Bhilwara):* Hillside stone complex, stone fortification wall, barley, wheat, rice, jowar, ragi, foxtail millet, chickpea, sesame.
    *   *Trade:* Coastal marine shells, etched carnelian beads, Rangpur lustrous red ware → Harappan links.

#### **The Malwa Region**

*   **Kayatha Culture** (Ujjain, MP, c. 2400–2000 BCE):
    *   *Pottery Types:* 1. Sturdy wheel-made Kayatha Ware (brown slip, violet/deep-red rim paintings), 2. Buff Ware (red geometric designs, lotas), 3. Red Combed Ware (zigzag comb patterns).
    *   *Finds:* Mud/reed huts, domesticated cattle & horse bones. 2 cast copper axes (Ganeshwar indentation marks), 28 copper bangles, 2 stone bead necklaces (175 & 160 agate/carnelian beads), **40,000 steatite micro-beads** in 1 pot.
    *   *Outcome:* Abrupt desertion c. 1800 BCE; later reoccupied by Ahar culture.

#### **The Western Deccan**

*   **Savalda Culture** (Tapi-Godavari Valley, N. Maharashtra, c. 2450–1700 BCE):
    *   *Diagnostic Pottery:* Wheel-made chocolate ware with thick crackled slip painted with weapons, tools, and geometric motifs.
    *   *Kaothe (20 ha):* Shallow nomadic deposit, round/oval huts, bone tools, millets, gram, moong.
    *   *Daimabad (Pravara River):* Multi-room mud houses, courtyards, lanes, agate phallus object. Wheat, barley, pea, lentil, moong, black gram.

#### **The Middle Ganga Plain and Eastern India**

*   **Sohagaura** (Gorakhpur, UP): Period I = Neolithic cord-impressed & rusticated pottery.
*   **North Bihar Alluvial Plains**:
    *   **Chirand** (Saran District, Ganga-Sarayu Confluence, mid-3rd millennium BCE):
        *   *Architecture:* Underground circular pits (early) → ground-level wattle-and-daub huts; semi-circular hut with oblong community cooking ovens.
        *   *Industry:* Polished stone celts (quartzite, basalt, granite). **Extensive bone/antler tool industry** (celts, scrapers, chisels, needles, pins, tortoise bone & ivory bangles).
        *   *Finds:* Terracotta humped bulls, birds, snakes, soot-stained clay smoking pipe, spindle whorls. Rice, wheat, barley, moong, masoor. Elephant, rhino, deer, domesticated cattle, fish scales, river snails.
    *   **Chechar-Kutubpur** (Vaishali): Circular huts, bone/antler tools, steatite micro-beads.
    *   **Senuar** (Rohtas, Bihar; Kudra River):
        *   *Period IA (Neolithic, c. 2500–1770 BCE):* Red/grey burnished corded ware, microliths, polished celts, bone points. Double cropping (rice *Oryza sativa*, barley, dwarf wheat, sorghum, ragi, lentil, pea). Domesticated cattle, buffalo, sheep, goat, pig, cat, dog.
        *   *Period IB (Neolithic–Chalcolithic, c. 1770–1400 BCE):* 19 copper objects (Rakha mines source), basalt celts, faience beads, bread wheat, chickpea/gram added.
    *   **Maner & Taradih**: Corded/burnished wares, celts, bone tools, rice, wheat, barley.
*   **Odisha**: **Kuchai** = Faceted hoes, chisels, mace heads, grit-tempered reddish-brown pottery.

#### **South India**

*   **Southern Neolithic** (c. 2900–1000 BCE; Raichur & Shorapur Doabs, Pennar/Krishna/Tungabhadra):
*   **The Mystery of the Ash Mounds**:
    *   *Discovery & Science:* T.J. Newbold (1830s) → Robert Bruce Foote (cow dung hypothesis) → F.E. Zeuner & Raymond Allchin (chemical/microscopic confirmation of excessively burnt cattle dung at Utnur & Kudatini).
    *   *Nature of Mounds:* Huge heaps of cattle dung accumulated inside timber-penciled cattle pens, repeatedly burnt deliberately.
    *   *Functions & Theories:*
        1.  *Allchin:* Ritual annual purification bonfires during seasonal transhumance (analogous to modern cattle-driving fires).
        2.  *Paddayya (Budihal Excavation):* Ash mounds were integral parts of permanent sedentary pastoral villages; dung cleared from pens was piled and burnt for sanitation & pest prevention.
    *   *Absence in Cuddapah/Pennar:* Dung was utilized as crop manure due to higher reliance on millet/pulse agriculture.
*   **Key Sites & Features**:
    *   **Utnur** (Telangana, c. 2800–2200 BCE; earliest ash mound c. 2500 BCE): Timber cattle pen held 540–800 cattle; cattle hoof-prints preserved in ash layer. Burnished grey/buff ware.
    *   **Piklihal** (Raichur): Multi-period granite hill-outcrop settlement.
    *   **Watgal** (Karnataka, c. 2900–1000 BCE; Non-ash mound site):
        *   Period IIA (c. 2700–2300 BCE): Storage pits. Earliest South Asian **areca / betel nut** (*Areca catechu*) seeds. Marine shell beads.
        *   Period IIB (c. 2300–2000 BCE): Extended & urn burials with grave pots, terracotta female torso figurine.
    *   **Budihal** (Gulbarga, Karnataka, c. 2450–1600 BCE; K. Paddayya):
        *   *Locality I:* Central ash mound (east cattle pen, west dung dump) surrounded by stone-walled dwelling units, child pit/pot burials, chert tool workshop area.
        *   *New Directions | Community Feasting Area:* 200–250 sq m plastered kankar/ash floor. Strewn with chopped cattle/sheep/goat/wild animal bones, sandstone chopping blocks, bone tool production, 3 meat-roasting pit hearths → Evidence of large-scale community butchering and ceremonial feasting.
        *   *Flora & Fauna:* Wild ber, amla, domesticated horse gram, *Bos indicus* cattle dominant.
*   **Subsistence, Crafts, & Trade**:
    *   *Fauna:* Cattle (*Bos indicus*) dominant, sheep, goat, fowl, wild game.
    *   *Flora:* Staple = millets, plus pulses, horse gram, chickpea (Piklihal).
    *   *Trade:* Tekkalakota gold earrings (Kolar gold fields supplied Harappans); marine shells from west coast.
    *   *Chalcolithic Transition:* Singanapalli & Ramapuram (Kurnool, c. 2455–2041 BCE) = Lime-plastered floors, wheel-made black-on-red pottery.

### The Life of Early Farmers

*   **Coexistence & Interaction**: Foraging continued alongside farming; hunter-gatherers traded forest goods (timber, honey, wax, hides, spices) with farmers.
*   **Social Dynamics of Food**: Food procurement became a social/symbolic act (hospitality, feasting, taboos, rituals).
*   **Health, Nutrition, & Demography**:
    *   Hunter-gatherer diet = High protein, varied, balanced, healthy.
    *   Farmer diet = High carbohydrate (cereals/roots), lower protein → Caries (cavities) & bone lesions ↑.
    *   Sedentism + Cereal diet → Reduced birth intervals → Birth rates ↑ → **Population growth ↑**.
    *   Sedentism → High vulnerability to crowd infectious diseases & epidemics.
*   **Labor & Time Ethic**: Shift from immediate daily consumption to long-term seasonal planning, food storage, scheduling, and risk management (vulnerability to drought, pests, crop storage rot).
*   **Gender Roles**:
    *   Women likely pioneered early plant domestication (horticultural foraging link).
    *   Women & children heavily involved in multi-stage clay processing, fuel gathering, kiln management, and pottery decoration.
*   **Social & Political Structure**:
    *   Internal differentiation by age, sex, and craft specialization (e.g., Kunjhun blade factory, Ganeshwar copper working).
    *   Spatial segregation of activities (living, butchering, tool making, dung dumping).
    *   Grave goods & house size variations → Emergence of **social ranking** & institutionalized leadership for conflict resolution and labor coordination.

---

### Changes In Cultic and Belief Systems

*   **Fertility & Symbolic Shifts**: Shift to agriculture → Enhanced magico-religious focus on crop/animal fertility.
*   **Female Figurines**:
    *   *Historiographical Critique:* "Mother Goddess" label replaced with **"female figurines with likely cultic significance"**.
    *   *Functions:* Could represent worshipped deities, domestic votive offerings, toys, decorative items, or ordinary portraits (e.g., Mehrgarh, Zhob, Mundigak, Chirand, Balathal).
*   **Bull Figurines**: Terracotta humped bulls present across NW, Rajasthan, Malwa, and Bihar → Probable sacred/fertility symbolism.
*   **Burial Customs & Afterlife**:
    *   Standardized burial practices ↑.
    *   Habitation floor burials = Connection with ancestors or fear of the dead.
    *   *Ritual Marks:* Body painted in red ochre (Mehrgarh/Burzahom = fertility/rebirth symbol); trepanned skulls (Burzahom).
    *   *Animal Interment:* Joint human-animal graves & pet burials (dogs/antlers at Burzahom) = Emotional/ritual bonds.
    *   *Afterlife Evidence:* Grave goods (pots, ornaments, weapons, food offerings).
    *   *Multi-stage Funerary Rituals:* Secondary burials (exposure prior to skeletal collection) & fractional pot burials (Nal).

### Conclusions

*   **Regional Diversity**: Multi-centric origins across Baluchistan, Vindhyan fringes, Middle Ganga valley (c. 7000–3000 BCE), followed by widespread expansion into Kashmir, Rajasthan, Malwa, Deccan, and South India (c. 3000–2000 BCE).
*   **Complex Mosaic**: Continuous coexistence and exchange between hunter-gatherers, pastoralists, rural farmers, and contemporary urban Harappan communities.
*   **Historical Impact**: The Neolithic transition established the economic, technological, demographic, and symbolic foundations that enabled the rise of proto-urban and urban subcontinental civilizations.
