## Reading Ancient Texts From A Historical Point of View

*   **Past = Complex** → Multiple perspectives; no single, final, perfect history.
*   **Historian’s Task** = Studying available sources, searching for fresh evidence and conducting rigorous analysis to approximate the past.
*   **Sources Division** = **Textual** (written or oral compositions) vs. **Archaeological** (tangible, material remains).
    *   *Note:* Distinctions are not absolute. Manuscripts are material remains; coins/inscriptions are material texts.
*   **Visual Sources** (architecture, sculpture, painting) = Distinct type of material source.
*   **Socio-Political Bias** = Ancient texts largely reflect perspectives of political, social, or religious elites. Written by upper-class men for other upper-class men; marginalized groups are excluded.
*   **Text vs. Literature**:
    *   **Text** = Any composition consisting of words.
    *   **Literature** = Text using language in creative, expressive and imaginative ways (e.g., drama, poetry).
*   **Refracted Image** = Texts do not directly mirror society; they offer a complex, refracted representation of the past.
*   **Oral Transmission** = Early religious texts were recited, heard and performed; passed orally across generations even after written manuscripts existed.
*   **Methodological Issues in Textual Analysis**:
    *   **Age vs. Manuscript**: Texts are much older than their surviving manuscripts.
    *   **Period of Composition** = Duration of text growth/change before final compilation (can last centuries). Chronological layers and later additions (**interpolations**) must be identified.
    *   **Critical Edition** = Prepared by comparative study of different manuscripts; extracts common parts as the original core. Uses a **critical apparatus** to highlight manuscript variations and commentarial interpretations.
    *   **Authorship** = Often works of multiple anonymous authors. Requires identifying their social background, perspectives and biases (class, religion, gender).
    *   **Circulation Context** = Where composed? Where and in what form circulated? Who transmitted them? Target audience? Place in power structures and cultural traditions?
    *   **Genre Framework** = Texts must be understood in their specific genre (e.g., poetry/drama require sensitivity to contemporary conventions and aesthetics).
    *   **Normative/Prescriptive Texts** = Describe ideal situations from the author's viewpoint, not necessarily actual ground reality.
    *   **Myths** = Tell us indirectly about history; but Myth ≠ History. Tease out meanings by reading creatively "against the grain" and cross-referencing other sources.

### Ancient Palm Leaf Manuscripts
*   **Paper Invention** = China, c. **2nd century BCE**. Block printing = **8th century**; moveable type = **11th century**.
*   **Indian Tradition** = Continued using traditional writing materials/methods for centuries.
*   **Palm Leaf Manuscripts** (*talapatra* in Sanskrit, *olai* in Tamil):
    *   **Leaves used**:
        *   **Talipot palm** (*Corypha umbraculifera*; *tali* in Sanskrit/Tamil) = Larger, thinner, more flexible, durable (~90 × 8–9 cm).
        *   **Palmyra palm** (*Borassus flabelliformis*; Skt *tala*, Tamil *panai*) = (~50 × 3–4 cm).
    *   **Preparation** = Leaves cut to size → pierced (left, middle, right top) → string woven through holes and wound around → string knotted or tied to peg (shell, wood, button) → wooden, dry petiole, or ivory cover attached.
    *   **Writing Method** = Letters engraved parallel to length with **stylus** (pointed pen) → smeared with soot/charcoal mixed with vegetable juice to fill grooves for legibility.
        *   *Layout:* Verses/long texts written in 2–3 columns; commentaries written above, below, or around the text; page numbers in the right margin.
    *   **Storage Hazards** = Heat, insects, water, fungus, dust, fire, human destruction. Scribes preserved traditions by repeatedly copying old manuscripts.
    *   **Preservation Techniques**:
        1.  Fumigation/insecticides (thymol, chloromate, formaldehyde, phosphene, ethylene oxide).
        2.  Solvent cleaning (water, detergents, ethyl alcohol).
        3.  Repairing damaged sections (thin paper + water-soluble PVA + methyl cellulose).
        4.  Oiling for flexibility → polishing with dry cloth → restringing.

### The Classification of Textual Sources: Language, Genre and Content
*   **Language Families**:
    *   **Indo-European** = Hindi, Punjabi, Marathi, Bengali, Assamese, Gujarati, Sindhi, Odia, Nepali, Kashmiri, Sanskrit, Persian, Greek, Latin, German, French, Dutch, Italian, Spanish, Armenian.
    *   **Dravidian** = Tamil, Malayalam, Telugu, Kannada, Tulu, Brahui (Baluchistan), Gondi (Central India), Malto (Rajmahal hills).
    *   **Austro-Asiatic** = Santali, Khasi, Mundari (Eastern India).
    *   **Tibeto-Burmese** = Manipuri, Bodo, Garo, Lushai (Northeast India).
    *   **Andamanese** = Unrelated to any known language family.
*   **Sanskrit** = Language of oldest surviving texts (**Vedas**). Belongs to Indo-Iranian branch of Indo-European family.
*   **Middle Indo-Aryan / Middle Indic** = Pali and Prakrit (dialects include Maharashtri, Shauraseni, Magadhi).
*   **Apabhramsha** = Further development of Prakrit (up to end of 1st millennium CE), blending later Prakrit with regional elements.
*   **Dravidian Literature** = **Tamil** has oldest literature, followed by **Kannada**. Regional languages took shape c. **1000–1500 CE**.
*   **Language Evolution**:
    *   Pre-classical Sanskrit (Rig Veda) ≠ Classical Sanskrit.
    *   **Classical Sanskrit codification** = Rules established by **Panini** in *Ashtadhyayi* (5th/4th century BCE) and **Patanjali** in *Mahabhashya* (2nd century BCE).
    *   **Oldest Prakrit Grammar** = **Vararuchi’s** *Prakritaprakasha*.
    *   **Oldest Tamil Grammar** = *Tolkappiyam* (parts date to early centuries CE).
*   **Religious vs. Non-religious (Secular)**:
    *   English "religion" suggests fixed, rigid boundaries.
    *   Sanskrit ***dharma*** / Pali ***dhamma*** = Broader path/way of life (conduct codes, social practices, worship, rituals, philosophy). Interweaving of religious and secular themes is common.

### The Vedas
*   **Status**:
    *   ***shruti*** ('that which has been heard') = Eternal, self-existent truth realized by *rishis* (seers).
    *   ***smriti*** ('remembered') = Vedangas, Puranas, Epics, Dharmashastras, Nitishastras.
*   **Veda** = From root *vid* ('to know') = 'knowledge'.
*   **The Four Vedas**:
    1.  **Rig Veda** = World's oldest surviving poetry. Samhita contains 1,028 hymns (*suktas*) in 10 books (*Mandalas*). Books 2–7 (family books) are the oldest core.
    2.  **Sama Veda** = 1,810 verses (mostly borrowed from Rig Veda) arranged for musical notation (melodies are lost).
    3.  **Yajur Veda** = Sacrificial rituals (*yajnas*). Divided into:
        *   *Shukla (White) school* = Contains only mantras. Recensions: Madhyandina, Kanva.
        *   *Krishna (Black) school* = Mantras + commentary. Recensions: Kathaka, Kapishthala, Maitrayani, Taittiriya.
    4.  **Atharva Veda** = Latest Veda; hymns, spells and charms reflecting popular beliefs/practices. Recensions: Shaunaka, Paippalada.
*   **Four Parts of each Veda**:
    *   **Samhita** = Collection of hymns.
    *   **Brahmanas** = Prose explanations of Samhitas; details of sacrificial rituals and outcomes.
    *   **Aranyakas** ('forest books') = Symbolic and philosophical interpretations of rituals.
    *   **Upanishads** = 108 total (13 principal); philosophical ideas of body/universe, closely associated with ***atman*** (soul) and ***brahman*** (ultimate reality).
*   **Vedic Schools** (*charanas* / *shakhas*):
    *   **Shakala shakha** = Only surviving recension of the Rig Veda.
    *   **Sama Veda recensions** = Kauthuma, Ranayaniya, Jaiminiya (or Talavakara).
    *   **Atharva Veda recensions** = Shaunaka, Paippalada.
*   **Historical references** = Rare. Book 7 of Rig Veda mentions **Battle of Ten Kings** (Sudas defeated a confederation).
*   **Vedic Chronology**:
    *   **Early Vedic literature** (Rig Veda) = c. **1500–1000 BCE**.
    *   **Later Vedic literature** = c. **1000–500 BCE**.
*   **Socio-Cultural Context** = Brahmanical tradition (composed/transmitted by Brahmana men). Reflects northwestern and northern India.
*   **Vedanga** ('limbs of Veda') = c. **500–200 BCE**; tools for proper recitation and understanding of Vedas:
    *   *shiksha* (phonetics)
    *   *chhanda* (metre)
    *   *vyakarana* (grammar)
    *   *nirukta* (etymology) – e.g., **Yaska’s** *Nirukta* (c. 5th century BCE)
    *   *kalpa* (ritual)
    *   *jyotisha* (astronomy)

### The Two Sanskrit Epics: The Mahabharata and The Ramayana
*   **Classification** = *smriti* + *itihasa* (traditional history); *Ramayana* is also classified as *kavya* (literature).
*   **Composition Dates**:
    *   *Mahabharata* = c. **400 BCE – 400 CE** (Hiltebeitel: mid-2nd century BCE to year zero).
    *   *Ramayana* = c. **5th/4th century BCE – 3rd century CE** (Brockington: identifies 5 chronological/cultural stages).
*   **Geographical and Social Evolution**:
    *   *Mahabharata* setting = Indo-Gangetic divide and upper Ganga valley. Displays earlier social stage: stronger women characters, practice of ***niyoga*** (levirate).
    *   *Ramayana* setting = Middle Ganga valley (political gravity shifted eastwards). Displays stricter social controls over women.
*   **The Mahabharata**:
    *   Consists of 18 *Parvas* (books). Northern and southern recensions.
    *   **Core story** = Conflict between Kauravas and Pandavas; great war at Kurukshetra (likely a small-scale family conflict transformed by bards, dated c. **1000 BCE**).
    *   **Authorship** = Traditionally Vyasa, but is a multi-author compilation over centuries. Includes the **Bhagavad Gita** (Krishna's discourse to Arjuna).
*   **The Ramayana**:
    *   Consists of 7 *Kandas* (books). Bala Kanda (1st) and Uttara Kanda (7th) are later interpolations.
    *   **Core story** = Banishment of Rama (Kosala prince), abduction of Sita by Ravana, rescue, return to Ayodhya.
    *   **Authorship** = Single poet core, traditionally **Valmiki**.
    *   **Divinity** = References to Rama as Vishnu incarnation are concentrated in 1st and 7th books (shows transition from heroic epic to religious text).
*   **Historical Context / Crisis Response** = James L. Fitzgerald argues *Mahabharata* was a Brahmanical response to threats to the Brahmanical order (popularity of Buddhism/Jainism and support from Nanda/Maurya dynasties).
*   **Diverse Tellings**:
    *   *Jaina Mahabharata* = Central figure is 22nd tirthankara **Arishtanemi**; war is between Jarasandha (with Kauravas) and Yadavas (with Pandavas).
    *   *Razmnama* = Persian telling of Mahabharata sponsored by Mughal emperor **Akbar** (16th century).
    *   *Buddhist Rama story* = *Dasharatha Jataka* (Pali).
    *   *Jaina Rama story* = *Paumachariu* of Vimalasuri (Prakrit).
    *   *Regional/Global Rama story* = Kamban’s *Iramavataram* (Tamil), Tulsidas’ *Ramcharitmanas* (Awadhi, 16th century). Also narrated in Tibet, Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia, Indonesia.

### Archaeology and The Mahabharata
*   **Mahabharata sites** (Hastinapura, Kurukshetra, Panipat, Tilpat, Baghpat, Mathura, Bairat):
    *   Yielded **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)** pottery dating to **c. 1000 BCE**.
    *   Indicates pastoral-cum-agricultural lifestyle.
*   **Hastinapura Flood**:
    *   *Puranic statement:* Capital shifted to Kaushambi under King Nichakshu due to a Ganga flood.
    *   *Archaeological evidence:* Excavations at Hastinapura showed a Ganga flood, followed by desertion for several centuries.
*   **Indraprastha (Purana Qila, New Delhi)**:
    *   Local tradition marks it as Pandava capital.
    *   14th century: Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi mentions Indraprastha as pargana (district) headquarters; Naraina village stone inscription mentions it.
    *   16th century: Ain-i-Akbari states Humayun’s fort was built on Indraprastha site. Village named Indarpat existed inside fort walls till 19th century.
    *   *Excavations (1954–1971, 2013–14, 2017–18):* Levels from 4th century BCE to 19th century CE. Stray PGW pieces suggest older nearby settlement; pre-Maurya level found.
*   **Ayodhya excavations**:
    *   ASI excavations (2002–03) beneath Babri Masjid.
    *   Revealed **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)** settlement dated **c. 1000–300 BCE** based on radiocarbon dates.
*   **Epistemological Crux** = Qualitative difference between literary and archaeological evidence. Epics weave event-centered narratives; archaeology tells us of general patterns of material culture, not individuals or specific events.

### The Puranas
*   **Meaning** = 'old'.
*   **Authorship** = Traditionally Vyasa, but is a multi-author, multi-age compilation. Composition stretched c. **4th–5th centuries CE** and later.
*   **Classification** = 18 **Mahapuranas** (great Puranas, including Vishnu, Matsya, Vayu, Brahmanda, Bhagavata, Skanda, Skanda) and many **Upapuranas** (secondary).
*   **Five Characteristics (***pancha-lakshanas***)**:
    1.  *sarga* (creation of the world)
    2.  *pratisarga* (re-creation)
    3.  *manvantaras* (periods of various Manus)
    4.  *vamsha* (genealogies of gods and rishis)
    5.  *vamshanucharita* (accounts of royal dynasties, traced to Suryavamshi/sun and Chandravamshi/moon).
*   **Time Conception** = Cyclical.
    *   4 *yugas* (*krita, treta, dvapara, kali*) = 1 *mahayuga*.
    *   1,000 *mahayugas* = 1 *kalpa* (divided into 14 *manvantaras*, each presided by a Manu).
    *   Cycle is connected with decline and revival of dharma.
*   **Historical Value**:
    *   Linear political narratives embedded within cyclical yugas.
    *   Post-Kurukshetra genealogies of kings of the *kali* age are given in the future tense (as a prophecy).
    *   Key Puranas for history: **Vayu, Brahmanda, Brahma, Harivamsha, Matsya, Vishnu**.
    *   Mention dynasties: Haryankas, Shaishunagas, Nandas, Mauryas, Shungas, Kanvas andhras (Satavahanas) and 'Naga' kings (Northern/Central India).
    *   Dynastic lists end with **Guptas** (4th–6th centuries CE) → Indicates compilation time.
    *   Later Puranas: Skanda Purana (6th/7th century), Bhagavata Purana (10th century).
*   **Socio-Cultural Function**:
    *   Useful for **historical geography** (accounts of mountains, rivers, places).
    *   Reflects emergence of **devotional religion** (*bhakti*) toward Vishnu, Shiva and Shakti (temple image worship, *tirtha*/pilgrimage, *vrata*/vows).
    *   Vehicles of Brahmanical values; reflect interaction of Brahmanical and non-Brahmanical cultures.

### Dharmashastra
*   **Dharma** = From root *dhri* ('to maintain, support, sustain'). Proper, ideal conduct in society.
*   **Purusharthas** (Goals of human life):
    1.  *dharma* (righteous conduct) – regulates others.
    2.  *artha* (material well-being)
    3.  *kama* (sensual pleasure)
    4.  ***moksha*** (deliverance from rebirth cycle) – added later.
*   **Dharmashastra Corpus division**:
    1.  **Dharmasutras** (earliest, c. 6th or 3rd century BCE onwards).
    2.  **Smritis** (e.g., Manu Smriti, Yajnavalkya Smriti).
    3.  **Commentaries & Digests** (9th–19th centuries CE) = *tikas* and *bhashyas* (commentaries), *nibandhas* (digests), *sangrahas* (compendia).
*   **Kalpasutras** (Vedanga aphorisms on ritual) are divided into:
    *   **Shrautasutras** = Vedic sacrifices requiring three fires.
    *   **Grihyasutras** = domestic sacrifices with one fire. Describe ***samskaras*** (life-stage rituals for upper three varnas, e.g., *upanayana*/initiation, *vivaha*/marriage, *antyeshti*/funerary rites).
    *   **Dharmasutras** = deals with dharma.
*   **Three Sources of Dharma** = *shruti* (Vedas), *smriti* (Smriti texts) and *sadachara* / *shishtachara* (good custom of learned, cultured people).
*   **Varnashrama Dharma**:
    *   Dependent on gender, age, marital status, ***varna*** and ***ashrama***.
    *   **Four Varnas** = Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra.
    *   ***dvija*** ('twice-born') = Upper three varnas who have the right to the sacred-thread ceremony (*upanayana*).
    *   **Ashrama System** (for dvija males):
        1.  ***brahmacharya*** (celibate studenthood)
        2.  ***grihastha*** (householder)
        3.  ***vanaprastha*** (partial renunciation)
        4.  ***sannyasa*** (complete renunciation) – optional.
        *   *Note:* Did not apply to women or Shudras.
*   ***Samanya / Sadharana Dharma*** = Universal virtues (nonviolence, truthfulness, control of senses) applicable to all.
*   **Legal Character** = Normative and prescriptive rules composed by Brahmana experts; not direct records of applied penal/civil codes.
*   **Elasticity** = Built-in flexibility to accommodate social reality and change.

### Theory and Practice In The Dharmashastra
*   **Theory vs. Practice Tension**:
    *   Four varnas vs. numerous **jatis** (castes), explained via ***varna-samkara*** (inter-mixture of varnas).
    *   ***apaddharma*** = Concession allowing duties of other varnas during emergencies.
    *   Recognition of regional (*desha-dharma*), caste (*jati-dharma*) and family (*kula-dharma*) customs.
*   **Manava Dharmashastra (Manu Smriti)** (c. 2nd–3rd centuries CE) examples of divergence:
    *   *Cross-cousin marriage:* Manu Smriti and 10th-century commentator **Medatithi** forbid it. But 14th-century South Indian commentator **Madhava** defends it using Vedic passages and custom.
    *   *Inter-varna marriage:* Manu Smriti condemns marriage between dvija man and Shudra woman. But details inheritance shares for sons of such marriages.
    *   *Widow remarriage:* Manu Smriti forbids it. But fixes waiting times for missing husbands and inheritance rights of sons of twice-married mothers.
    *   *Meat eating:* Manu Smriti forbids it in one place. But includes meat among items to be offered to Brahmanas at *shraddha* ancestor ceremonies.

### Buddhist Texts
*   **Division** = Canonical (basic tenets) vs. Non-canonical.
*   **Tipitaka** ('The Three Baskets'):
    *   **Language** = **Pali** (literary language developed from Eastern Indian/Magadhan dialects).
    *   **Three Books**:
        1.  ***Sutta Pitaka*** = Buddha’s discourses on doctrines in dialogue form. Consists of 5 **Nikayas**: *Digha, Majjhima, Samyutta, Anguttara, Khuddaka*.
            *   ***Jatakas*** (Buddha's previous births) = One of 15 books of the Khuddaka Nikaya.
            *   *Dhammapada* (ethical sayings), *Theragatha* (songs of monks), *Therigatha* (songs of nuns).
        2.  ***Vinaya Pitaka*** = Rules for monks/nuns of the *sangha* (monastic order). Includes the *Patimokkha* (list of transgressions and atonements).
        3.  ***Abhidhamma Pitaka*** = Later work systemizing Sutta doctrines via lists, summaries and Q&A.
*   **Tipitaka Chronology**:
    *   Core composed between **5th and 3rd centuries BCE**.
    *   Written down in **1st century BCE** in Sri Lanka under King **Vattagamani**.
    *   Produced by **Mahavihara** monks to define themselves against **Abhayagiri** rivals.
*   **Non-canonical Pali texts**:
    *   *Milindapanha* (1st BCE–1st CE) = Dialogue between King Milinda (Indo-Greek **Menander**) and monk **Nagasena**.
    *   *Nettigandha* / *Nettipakarana* = Connected account of Buddha’s teachings.
    *   **Buddhaghosha** (5th century CE) = Compiled major commentaries on Tipitaka.
    *   *Nidanakatha* (1st century CE) = First connected life story of the Buddha.
    *   **Sri Lankan Chronicles** = *Dipavamsa* (4th–5th centuries) and *Mahavamsa* (5th century) – accounts of Buddha, Buddhist councils, Ashoka and Sri Lankan kings.
*   **Sanskrit and Hybrid Sanskrit Buddhist texts** (Mahayana and non-Mahayana):
    *   ***Mahavastu*** = Hagiography of Buddha and monastic order.
    *   *Lalitavistara* (1st/2nd century CE) = Hagiography with Mahayana elements.
    *   **Ashvaghosha** (1st/2nd century CE) = *Buddhacharita* (biography of Buddha), *Saundarananda*, *Sariputraprakarana*.
    *   **Avadana Texts** (moral deeds) = *Avadanashataka* (2nd century CE) and *Divyavadana* (4th century CE) – stories of Buddha and Ashoka.
    *   *Ashtasahasrika-prajnaparamita* (1st century CE) and *Saddharma-pundarika* (Lotus Sutra) = Accounts of ***bodhisattvas*** and Mahayana doctrines.
    *   **Mahayana Thinkers** (wrote in Sanskrit) = Nagarjuna, Vasubandhu, Asanga, Aryadeva, Buddhapalita, Dignaga.
*   *Historical Value:* Offers a non-Brahmanical (specifically Buddhist) window into ancient polity, society and economy.

### Songs of Buddhist Nuns
*   ***Therigatha*** = Songs of Buddhist nuns; rare ancient text composed by women.
*   **Ubbiri’s Song** = Lay-woman (*upasika*) from Shravasti who attained *nibbana*. Grieving her dead daughter Jiva, Buddha comforts her: "84,000 daughters named Jiva have burned... For which one do you grieve?" → arrow of grief removed → takes refuge in Buddha, Dharma, Sangha.
*   **Mitta’s Song** = Sakya woman of Kapilavastu. Fasted on 8th, 14th, 15th days of fortnight to be reborn among gods → shaved head & robes, eats one meal a day → no desire to be a god, no fear.

### Jaina Texts
*   **Canon** = **Siddhanta** or **Agama**.
*   **Language** = **Ardha-Magadhi** (eastern Prakrit dialect).
*   **Sectarian Division** = **Shvetambara** and **Digambara** schools (c. **3rd century CE**).
    *   *Shvetambara Canon:* 12 Angas, 12 Uvamgas (Upangas), 10 Painnas, 6 Cheya Suttas, 4 Mula Suttas, Nandi Sutta, Anugodara.
    *   *Digambara Canon:* Rejects Shvetambara redaction; accepts Angas but groups them differently (e.g., Angabahyas).
*   **Compilation Chronology**:
    *   Angas compiled at **Pataliputra** council.
    *   Entire canon compiled at **Valabhi** (Gujarat) council (5th/6th century CE), presided by **Devarddhi Kshamashramana**.
    *   Core dates to 5th–4th century BCE; additions made until 6th century CE.
*   **Non-canonical Jaina Works**:
    *   Prakrit (especially **Maharashtri**) and Sanskrit (from early CE).
    *   **Commentaries**: *Nijjuttis* (Niryuktis), *Bhashyas*, *Churnis* (in Maharashtri); *Tikas, Vrittis, Avachurnis* (in Sanskrit).
    *   *Pattavalis* and *Theravalis* = Precise but sometimes contradictory chronological lists of Jaina saints.
    *   **Puranas (Charitas)** = Hagiographies of ***tirthankaras*** (ford makers):
        *   *Adi Purana* (9th century) by **Jinasena** = Life of 1st tirthankara Rishabha (Adinatha).
        *   *Harivamsha Purana* (8th century) = Jaina version of Kauravas, Pandavas, Krishna, Balarama.
        *   *Trishashtilakshana Mahapurana* (9th century) by **Jinasena & Gunabhadra** = Lives of saints, life-cycle rituals, town planning, warrior duties, statecraft.
        *   *Parishishtaparvan* (12th century) by **Hemachandra** = History of early Jaina teachers, political history details.
        *   *Prabandhas* (12th century onwards, Gujarat) = Semi-historical accounts of saints/kings.
    *   **Story Literature (katha)** = Didactic story literature in Sanskrit, Prakrit, Apabhramsha reflecting everyday life.

### Sangam Literature and Later Tamil Works
*   **Language** = Old Tamil (Early Classical Tamil).
*   **Sangam Legend** = Three literary gatherings (*Sangams*) in ancient times:
    *   1st: Madurai (held for 4,440 years).
    *   2nd: Kapatapuram (held for 3,700 years).
    *   3rd: Madurai (held for 1,850 years).
    *   *Historicity:* Legend is mythical, but 3rd Sangam kings/poets are historical. Alternatively, based on the 5th-century Jaina sangha in Madurai.
*   **Sangam Corpus**:
    *   **Ettutokai** (The Eight Collections) – includes 6 of the 8 anthologies of poems.
    *   **Pattuppattu** (The Ten Songs) – includes 9 of the 10 songs.
    *   **Tolkappiyam** = Oldest surviving Tamil grammar (phonology, semantics, syntax, literary conventions). Earliest parts date to early centuries CE.
*   **Composition & Compilation**:
    *   Composed between **3rd century BCE and 3rd century CE**.
    *   Compiled into anthologies c. **mid-8th century CE**.
*   **Classification of Poems**:
    *   ***akam*** = Love/interior themes.
    *   ***puram*** = War/exterior themes (public poetry, good/evil, community, kingdom).
*   **Authorship** = 2,381 poems ascribed to 473 poets (including 30 women). Diverse backgrounds: teachers, merchants, carpenters, astrologers, blacksmiths, soldiers, kings.
*   **Post-5th Century Didactic Works**:
    *   ***Tirukkural*** by **Tiruvalluvar** (5th–6th century CE) = Ethics, polity, love (author possibly a weaver or drummer).
*   **Tamil Epics** (c. 5th–6th century CE):
    *   Origins in late Sangam compositions (*Kalittokai* and *Paripatal*), not northern epics.
    *   **The Silappadikaram** (The Song of the Anklet) by **Ilankovatikal** ('prince ascetic'):
        *   30 cantos, 3 books.
        *   *Plot:* Kovalan (rich merchant's son) abandons wife Kannaki for courtesan Madhavi. Returns, travels to Madurai with Jaina nun Kavundi to sell Kannaki's gold anklet. Falsely executed for stealing queen's anklet (which was filled with pearls, while Kannaki's had rubies). Kannaki proves innocence, king dies of remorse, queen dies of grief. Kannaki burns Madurai, ascends to heaven, worshipped as ideal wife (*pattini* cult).
        *   *Symbolism:* Anklet is worn in happiness, removed in abandonment, causes death, proves innocence, worn again in heaven.
    *   **The Manimekalai** (The Jewel Belt) by **Sattanar**:
        *   30 cantos. Strong Buddhist tone.
        *   *Plot:* Manimekalai (daughter of Kovalan & Madhavi) rejects Prince Udayakumara to become Buddhist nun. Takes form of Kaya-Chandikai to feed poor using magic alms-bowl. Prince killed by real Kaya-Chandikai's jealous husband. Manimekalai imprisoned, freed, feeds poor in famine-struck Kanchi, joins Buddhist sangha.
*   **Early Medieval Devotional Poetry (Alvars & Nayanars)**:
    *   ***Alvars*** (Vaishnava saints) & ***Nayanars/Nayanmars*** (Shaiva saints).
    *   **Vaishnava Compilation** = **Nathamuni** (10th century) compiled Alvar hymns into the canon ***Nalayira Divya Prabandham***. *Alvarvaipavam* is their biography.
    *   **Shaiva Compilation** = **Nambi Andar Nambi** (10th century) compiled Nayanar hymns into the ***Tirumurai*** canon; wrote *Tiruttondar Tiruvantati*.
    *   ***Periyapuranam*** (12th century) = Compilation of Shaiva saints' life stories.
*   **New Poetic Genres (Early Medieval)**:
    *   *Kalampakams* = Poems where the last part of one verse forms the beginning of the next.
    *   *Kovai* = Poems in thematic sequence. E.g., *Pantikkovai* (6th/7th century, for Pandya king Netumaran); *Tirukkovaiyar* by **Manikkavachakar** (9th century); *Tanchaivanan Kovai* by **Poyyamolip Pulavar** (13th century).
    *   *Ula* = Songs in praise of deities in procession.
    *   *Tutu* = Messenger poetry (message to god, lover).
    *   **Avvaiyar** (9th/10th century) = Famous poetess, wrote popular moral aphorisms.
    *   **Kamban's** *Iramavataram* = Famous Tamil rendering of Ramayana.

### Early Kannada and Telugu Texts
*   **Kannada**:
    *   Inscriptions date from **5th/6th century CE**.
    *   **Oldest surviving literature** = ***Kavirajamarga*** (9th century CE) on poetics, written by or during the reign of Rashtrakuta king **Amoghavarsha I** (implied, though the text mentions many lost earlier works).
    *   **Stronghold of Jainism**: Jaina themes dominate.
    *   **The Three Gems of Kannada Literature** (10th century):
        *   **Pampa** = Wrote *Adi Purana* (life of Rishabha) and *Vikramarjunavijaya* (based on Mahabharata).
        *   **Ponna** = Wrote Jaina Puranas, titled *Ubhaya-kavi-chakravarti* (imperial poet in Sanskrit and Kannada).
        *   **Ranna** = Wrote Jaina Puranas.
    *   **Chavunda Raya** (Ganga minister) = Wrote *Chavundaraya Purana* (continuous prose Jaina history of 24 tirthankaras).
    *   **Nagachandra / Abhinava Pampa** (12th century) = Wrote *Ramachandracharitra Purana* (Jaina Ramayana).
    *   **Neminatha** (12th century) = Wrote *Lilavati* (love story of Kadamba prince in *champu* mixed verse/prose).
*   **Telugu**:
    *   Inscriptions suggest antiquity from 2nd century CE; classical form in 5th-6th centuries CE.
    *   **Oldest surviving literature** = **Nannaya’s** (11th century) Telugu translation of the first 2.5 books of the *Mahabharata* in mixed prose/verse (*champu*). Written for Eastern Chalukya king **Rajarajanarendra**. Nannaya is called *Vaganushasanundu* (Maker of Speech).
    *   **Tikkana** (minister of Manumasiddhi) = Added 15 Parvas to the Mahabharata; wrote *Uttararamayanamu*.
    *   **Nanne Choda** = Wrote *Kumara-sambhavamu* (ruler of Orayuru).
    *   **Maturity** = 14th century (Kakatiya period) and peak in 16th century under Vijayanagara king **Krishnadevaraya** (1509–1529 CE).

### Other Ancient Texts, Biographies and Histories
*   **Sanskrit Poetry and Drama**:
    *   **Ashvaghosha** (1st/2nd century CE) = *Buddhacharita*, *Saundarananda*, *Sariputraprakarana* (drama).
    *   **Bhasa** = *Pancharatra, Dutavakya, Balacharita, Svapna-Vasavadatta*.
    *   **Kalidasa** (4th–5th century CE) = Dramas: *Abhijnanashakuntala, Malavikagnimitra, Vikramorvashiya*; poems: *Raghuvamsha, Kumarasambhava, Meghaduta*.
    *   Early medieval: **Bharavi**, **Rajashekhara** and poetess **Vijayanka**.
*   **Historical Dramas** = ***Mudrarakshasa*** by **Vishakhadatta** (5th/6th century CE) – Chanakya's maneuvers to win Nanda minister Rakshasa over to Chandragupta Maurya's side.
*   **Narrative Literature (Folk Tales)**:
    *   *Panchatantra* (3rd/4th century CE).
    *   *Kathasaritsagara* by Somadeva (11th century CE).
*   **Technical Literature (Shastras)**:
    *   *Statecraft:* **Kautilya’s** *Arthashastra*.
    *   *Astronomy/Math:* **Aryabhata’s** *Aryabhatiya*, **Varahamihira’s** *Brihatsamhita*.
    *   *Performing Arts:* *Natyashastra* (drama/dance).
    *   *Sensual Pleasure:* *Kamasutra* by Vatsyayana.
    *   *Medicine:* *Charaka Samhita*, *Sushruta Samhita*.
    *   *Architecture/Sculpture:* *Shilpashastras*.
*   **Philosophy**:
    *   Six orthodox schools (*Darshanas*) = Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva Mimamsa, Uttara Mimamsa.
    *   Heterodox schools mentioned in commentaries: **Charvaka / Lokayata** (materialist school - texts lost).
*   **Biographical Literature (Caritas)**:
    *   ***Harshacharita*** by **Banabhatta** (7th century CE) = Biography of Harsha Vardhana of Pushyabhuti dynasty. Oldest surviving biography in India.
    *   *Gaudavaho* by **Vakpati** (8th century CE) = In Prakrit, about Yashovarman of Kanauj.
    *   *Vikramankadevacharita* by **Bilhana** (12th century CE) = About Chalukya king Vikramaditya VI.
    *   *Nandikkalambakkam* (9th century CE) = Anonymous Tamil biographical poem for Pallava king Nandivarman III.
    *   *Kalinkattupparani* by **Cheyankontar** (11th century CE) = Tamil work on Chola Kulottunga I's war with Kalinga.
    *   ***Ramacharita*** by **Sandhyakara Nandi** (11th/12th century CE) = Sanskrit work with double meaning (*shlesha*), simultaneously telling story of Ramayana and Pala king **Ramapala** of Bengal.
    *   *Kumarapalacharita* by **Hemachandra** (12th century CE) = Sanskrit & Prakrit poem telling history of Chaulukya kings of Gujarat while illustrating grammar rules.
    *   *Prithvirajaraso* by **Chand Bardai** = Braj-bhasha epic on Prithviraja Chauhan.
*   **Historical Aphorisms**:
    *   *Krishi-Parashara* (6th–11th century CE) = Sanskrit work on agriculture in Bengal.
    *   *Dakar Bachan* & *Khanar Bachan* = Old Bengali aphorisms on agriculture, medicine, astrology.

### Banabhatta and His Royal Biography
*   **Author's Background** = Born in Pritikuta (Brahmana village, Kanyakubja), Vatsyayana branch of Bhargava Brahmanas. Educated by Bharchu. Wayward youth, travelled with actors, gamblers, dancing girls. Summoned to court of **Harsha**.
*   **The Harshacharita**:
    *   Genre = ***akhyayika*** (genre related to *itihasa* tradition).
    *   Masterly Sanskrit prose. Selected episodes narrated for literary/aesthetic effect.
    *   Uses political symbolism: Ends with rescue of Harsha's sister **Rajyashri** (meaning 'royal glory') → symbolically represents successful acquisition of royal power.
    *   Pathak argues work is complete as it contains 5 stages of action (beginning, effort, hope, certainty, conclusion).
    *   Nuances: Hints at a fratricidal struggle behind the idealized brotherly love of Harsha and **Rajyavardhana**.

### The Nature of Ancient Indian Historical Traditions
*   **Forms of History (Romila Thapar)**:
    *   **Embedded History** = Historical consciousness hidden within other forms (e.g., myth, epic, genealogy). Connected with lineage-based societies.
    *   **Externalized History** = Conscious, self-reflective historical writing (e.g., chronicles, biographies). Connected with state societies.
*   **Vedic Historical Elements**:
    *   *danastutis* (hymns praising king's generosity)
    *   *gathas* (songs in praise of kings)
    *   *narashamsis* (ritual songs praising heroes)
    *   *akhyanas* (narrative dialogue hymns).
    *   *Note:* Directly linked to sacrifice (*yajna*) performance.
*   **Sustaining Bards** = ***sutas*** and ***magadhas*** (maintained and transmitted historical traditions/genealogies).
*   **Major Historical Works**:
    *   ***Rajatarangini*** (River of Kings) by **Kalhana** (12th century CE) = 8,000 Sanskrit verses in 8 cantos (*tarangas*). History of Kashmir kings. Used 11 lost earlier works and *Nilamatapurana*. Evaluated as India's first genuine historical text (combines poetry, character sketches, comments on karma/fate and critical assessment of sources).
    *   Inscriptions often contain ***prashasti*** (panegyric) with royal genealogies.
    *   **Official Archives** = Kautilya's *Arthashastra*, Xuanzang and Al-Biruni's *Tahqiq-i-Hind* (mentioning Shahi kings of Kabul) indicate that royal archives existed in ancient India, though none survive.

### Accounts of India From Outside The Subcontinent
*   **Greek & Roman Accounts**:
    *   *Indica* of **Ktesias** (4th century BCE) = Unreliable; bizarre hearsay collected while living in Persia as royal physician.
    *   *Megasthenes’* **Indica** = Greek envoy to Chandragupta Maurya's court. Original lost, preserved in fragments/paraphrases by later writers (Arrian, Strabo, Pliny).
    *   *Trade Texts* (2nd century BCE – 2nd century CE) = Pliny the Elder's works and anonymous ***Periplus Maris Erythraei*** (Periplus of the Erythraean Sea) – crucial for Indian Ocean trade.
*   **Chinese Buddhist Pilgrims**:
    *   **Faxian** (c. 337–422 CE) = Travelled northern India.
    *   **Xuanzang** (c. 600–664 CE) = Spent over 10 years travelling throughout India.
    *   **Yijing** (c. 635–713 CE) = Took sea route, spent 10 years at **Nalanda** monastery.
*   **Arab Accounts & Translation Projects**:
    *   Caliph **Al-Mamun** (9th century Abbasid, Baghdad) established ***Beyt-al-Hikma*** (House of Wisdom) → translated Sanskrit, Greek, Persian works into Arabic (e.g., *Kalila-wa-Dimma* based on *Panchatantra*).
    *   **Al-Biruni** (Abu Rihan, native of Khwarizm/Khiva) = 11th century intellectual. Wrote ***Tahqiq-i-Hind*** covering Indian scripts, sciences, geography, astronomy, philosophy, social organization, laws.
        *   **Gupta Era Dating** = Al-Biruni recorded that the Gupta era began 241 years after the Shaka era (78 CE) → places beginning of Gupta era in **319–20 CE**.
    *   Travelers: **Sulaiman** (early medieval) wrote geographical accounts of Indian trade.
*   **Persian Accounts**:
    *   ***Chachnama*** = Describes Brahmana Chach usurping the Sindh throne (mid-7th century) and subsequent Arab conquest by Muhammad bin Qasim.
    *   *Shahnama* of **Firdausi** and *Gulistan* of **Saadi** = Incidental references to Indian trade.

### Al-Biruni On The Writing of The Hindus
*   **On Writing** = Art of writing spreads news over space and time.
*   **Writing Materials**:
    *   Hindus do not write on animal hides (unlike Greeks or early Muslims who wrote Koran on gazelle hides).
    *   In the South: write on leaves of a slender tree called ***tari*** (length of 1 yard, width of 3 fingers), bound by a cord running through a middle hole.
    *   In Central and Northern India: use bark of the ***tuz*** tree (also used for bow covers).
*   **Alphabet Origin** = Vyasa rediscovered the alphabet of 50 letters by divine inspiration. A letter is called an ***akshara***.
*   **Alphabet Features** = Hangs down from a **ground-line above** (straight line above every character), writing left to right. Signs above are grammatical marks for pronunciation.
*   **Scripts in Hind**:
    *   *Siddhamatrika* = Most widely used (Kashmir, Varanasi, Kanauj).
    *   *Nagara* (Malwa), *Ardhanagari* (Bhatiya/Sindh), *Malwari* (Sindh), *Karnata* (Karnatadesha), *Andhri* (Andhradesha), *Dirwari* (Dravidadesha), *Lari* (Latadesha/Gujarat), *Gauri* (Purvadesha/East), *Bhaikshuki* (Udunpur, script of Buddha).

## Archaeology and The Early Indian Past

*   **Archaeology** = Study of the human past through material remains (structures, artefacts, bones, seeds, pollen, seals, coins, sculpture, inscriptions).
*   **Key Concepts**:
    *   **Culture** (Anthropological) = Patterns of learnt behavior, thinking and doing.
    *   **Artefact** = Any portable object made/altered by human hands.
    *   **Industry** = Similar artefacts of the same material found at a site (e.g., microlith industry).
    *   **Assemblage** = All the industries found at a site.
    *   **Archaeological Culture** = When similar assemblages are found across several sites.
    *   *Note:* Archaeological cultures change slowly and do not coincide with dynastic changes.
*   **Field Archaeology**:
    *   **Exploration** = Identifying sites (places of past human activity). Often seen as **mounds** in plains (formed by centuries of rubbish & rebuilding).
    *   **Discovery Methods** = Accidental, textual clues, regional surveys, aerial photography, metal probes, remote sensing (**LANDSAT** satellite imagery – identifies palaeochannels, buried settlements).
    *   **Preservation Bias** = Inorganic materials (stone, clay, metal) survive; organic (wood, bone) decay. Tropical regions (acidic soil, rain, heat) are hostile to preservation.
    *   **Stratigraphy** = Basic principle that lower layers are older (unless disturbed). Precising stratigraphic context of artefacts is vital.
    *   **Excavation**:
        *   **Horizontal** = Exposes a large surface area (reveals spatial layouts of structures).
        *   **Vertical** = Digs small surface area to uncover chronological stratigraphic sequence.
        *   *Note:* Excavation is destructive; results must be published.
    *   **Modern Trends** = Contextualizing sites in larger landscapes, non-destructive regional surveys (surface walking & recording).

### Letters From Alexander Cunningham To J. D. M. Beglar
*   **Alexander Cunningham** = First professional archaeologist of India, first Director General of ASI (est. 1871).
*   **Letters (1871–1886)** = 193 letters to assistant **J. D. M. Beglar** acquired in 2005. Detail colonial archaeological expeditions:
    *   *Transport:* Horses, elephants, bullock trains, carriage dak, camel (e.g., camel sat in Ahsin River, destroying boxes).
    *   *Challenges:* Extreme heat, mosquitoes, illness, tight finances.
    *   *Discoveries:*
        *   Proof that **Pataliputra** was on the Son river (identifying Son as Erannoboas of Greek texts).
        *   Caves at **Rajgir** (including two 70-ft long caves inhabited by bears).
        *   **Herakles killing the Nemean Lion** sculpture found in Mathura (used as a drinking trough for bullocks).

### H. D. Sankalia: Born For Archaeology
*   **Hasmukh Dhirajlal Sankalia** (1908–89) = Pioneer of prehistory & protohistory in independent India. Professor at Deccan College, Pune.
*   **Contributions**:
    *   Led first **Gujarat Prehistoric Expedition** (1941–42) → discovered Mesolithic phase; found first skeletal remains of stone-age humans in India at **Langhnaj**.
    *   Traced Paleolithic sites in Godavari valley.
    *   Excavated sites: Jorwe, Nashik, Nevasa, Chirki, Inamgaon (Maharashtra); Langhnaj, Dwarka (Gujarat); Navdatoli, Maheshwar, Tripuri (MP); Tekkalakota, Sanganakallu (Karnataka); Ahar (Rajasthan).
    *   Wrote: *Indian Archaeology* (1962), *Prehistory and Proto-history of India and Pakistan* (1962–63), *Stone Age Tools...* (1964).
    *   Book: *Ramayana in Historical Perspective* (1982) – argued Lanka was in eastern Madhya Pradesh.
    *   **Inamgaon Excavations** (1968–83) = With students M. K. Dhavalikar and Z. D. Ansari. Set high standard for scientific methods in Chalcolithic farming studies.

### Scientific Techniques In Archaeology
*   **Dating Methods**:
    *   **Radiocarbon (Carbon-14) Dating** = Organic material (wood, charcoal, bone, shell). Margin of error (standard deviation, e.g., ± 100). Base year for 'BP' (Before Present) is 1950. Requires tree-ring calibration (Cal. BCE/CE).
    *   **Thermoluminescence** = Heated inorganic material (pottery, terracotta, burnt flint).
    *   **Potassium-Argon** = Volcanic rocks older than 100,000 years.
    *   **Electron Spin Resonance** = Bone, shell.
    *   **Uranium series** = Calcium carbonate-rich rocks.
    *   **Fission-track** = Rocks, minerals, obsidian, glass.
    *   **Palaeomagnetic** = Baked clay, volcanic lava, magnetized sediments.
    *   **Amino acid analysis** = Bone (up to 100,000 years).
    *   **Dendrochronology** = Tree rings (outside tropics, up to 8,000 years).
*   **Archaeometry** = Scientific measurements to analyze ancient objects.
    *   Chemical composition of pottery/metals → traces production techniques and ore sources.
    *   Soil chemical analysis → nitrogen content indicates human/animal activity (e.g., high nitrogen in **Inamgaon** courtyards → animals tied there).
*   **Palaeontology & Osteology**:
    *   **DNA & Molecular Biology** → maps hominin evolution & migration patterns.
    *   **Teeth & Bones** → reveal diet (via trace elements / SEM analysis of enamel), diseases (arthritis, tuberculosis marks bones) and demographic stats (mortality, life expectancy, status/gender differences in nutrition).
    *   **Palaeo-pathology** = study of ancient diseases in bones.
*   **Faunal Analysis (animal bones)**:
    *   Differentiates wild vs. domesticated species.
    *   Identifies butchering, cooking, bone tool making zones and draft animal joint fusion.
    *   E.g., Marine fish bones and shells found at **Inamgaon** (200 km inland) → indicates trade contacts with coastal communities.
*   **Environmental Archaeology** = Studies human-environment adaptation. Includes **palaeo-botanical studies** (pollen, seeds, charcoal, sediment analysis).
*   **Geospatial Techniques** = GIS and remote sensing cameras (satellite LANDSAT, IRS, SPOT) map palaeochannels, buried moats, roads and guide conservation.

### Interpreting Archaeological Evidence
*   **New Archaeology / Processualism** (1960s) = Problem-oriented, holistic approach linking culture to ecology and adaptation. Stressed theory, generalization and scientific explanation.
*   **Post-Processual Archaeology** = Challenged processualism's claim of objective knowledge. Saw material culture as active, dynamic, reflecting human thoughts, choices and symbolic meanings.
*   **Historical Role** = Primary source for prehistory and protohistory. Often sidelined as secondary/corroborative once texts are available. Tells of everyday life, subsistence strategies (**foodways** = social, political, cultural contexts of food), technology and trade routes.
*   **Limitation** = Archaeological culture does not necessarily correspond to a linguistic, political, or social group (tribe/clan).

### Ethno-archaeology
*   **Definition** = Study of living communities to interpret archaeological remains of past communities.
*   **Examples**:
    *   Modern carnelian bead making in **Khambhat** (Gujarat) → helps interpret Harappan bead technology & social organization.
    *   Women's role in subsistence & crafts identified by studying modern hunter-gatherers and shifting cultivators.
*   **Primary Sources | The social and cultural aspects of technology**:
    *   Archana Choksi's study of pottery in **Gundiyali** and **Lodai** villages (Kutch, Gujarat):
        1.  **Form-Function Correlation:** Dry grain vessels have wide mouths; water vessels have small mouths (spillage ↓); cooking pots have wide mouths & thick rims (handling ↑); eating pots are open/shallow with stable bases.
        2.  **Socio-economic Demand:** Gundiyali has farmers/laborers; Lodai has farmers/herders. Client demand is shaped by lifestyle, occupation, community and rituals.
        3.  **Continuity & Change:** Potters resist change in forms/designs. Changes happen during major socio-economic shifts (e.g., shapes modified for modern urban kitchens, but decorations remain same).

### Protecting Sites
*   **Threats** = Rural & urban expansion.
*   **Salvage Archaeology** = Identifying and saving endangered sites from destruction.
*   **Nagarjunakonda Salvage Operation (1954–1960)**:
    *   Valley in Guntur (AP) submerged by Nagarjunasagar dam on Krishna river.
    *   ASI explored, excavated and transplanted 9 major structures to the top of Nagarjunakonda hill; built replicas of 14 others.

## Epigraphy: The Study of Inscriptions

*   **Epigraphy** = Study of inscriptions (writing engraved on stone, wood, metal, ivory, bricks, pottery, clay, shell).
*   **Palaeography** = Study of ancient writing systems.
*   **Ancient and early medieval scripts**:
    *   **Oldest script** = Harappan script (undeciphered).
    *   **Oldest deciphered** = late 4th century BCE Brahmi and Kharoshthi (e.g., Ashoka's inscriptions).
    *   **Writing origin debates** = Not mentioned in early Vedic texts, but later Vedic texts' metrics & arithmetic imply its possibility.
    *   **Textual mentions** = First definite references in Buddhist Pali texts (*Jatakas*, *Vinaya Pitaka*) and Panini's *Ashtadhyayi* (uses *lipi*).
    *   **Pre-Maurya Brahmi evidence** = Potsherds with short inscriptions from **Anuradhapura** (Sri Lanka, early 4th century BCE) and Tamil-Brahmi on potsherds in Tamil Nadu (**Porunthal, Kodumanal, Keezhadi**).
    *   **Script Classifications**:
        *   *Logographic* (symbol = word)
        *   *Syllabic* (symbol = syllable)
        *   *Alphabetic* (symbol = phonetic sound, independent vowels)
        *   *Brahmi & Kharoshthi* = semi-syllabic or semi-alphabetic.
    *   **Kharoshthi Script**:
        *   Core area = Gandhara (Indo-Gangetic-Kabul valley). Ashoka's **Shahbazgarhi** and **Mansehra** inscriptions.
        *   Direction = **right to left**.
        *   Origin = derived from North Semitic **Aramaic** script. Declined c. 3rd century CE.
    *   **Brahmi Script**:
        *   Direction = **left to right**.
        *   Parent of all indigenous South Asian scripts.
        *   D.C. Sircar's Stages: Early (3rd-1st BCE), Middle (1st BCE-3rd CE), Late (4th-6th CE).
        *   Late 6th century: evolved into **Siddhamatrika** (or *Kutila*, with sharp lower angles).
        *   Modern North Indian scripts (Nagari, proto-Bengali/Gaudi, Sharada) emerged from Siddhamatrika c. 1000 CE onwards.
    *   **South Indian Scripts**:
        *   **Tamil-Brahmi**: adapted Brahmi for Tamil. Phases: Early (2nd BCE-1st CE), Late (2nd-4th CE).
        *   **Three Southern Scripts (Early Medieval)**:
            1.  ***Grantha*** = derived from Southern Brahmi, used for writing Sanskrit.
            2.  ***Tamil*** = simplified from Grantha with Vatteluttu additions, widespread by Chola period.
            3.  ***Vatteluttu*** = emerged from Tamil-Brahmi c. 5th century, used in Chera kingdom.
        *   Telugu-Kannada scripts split c. 7th/8th century CE; Malayalam split from Grantha c. 14th century CE.

### Deciphered and Undeciphered Scripts
*   **Brahmi Decipherment** = James Prinsep (1837) deciphered 3rd century BCE Maurya Brahmi, building on Wilkins, Troyer, Mill, Stevenson.
*   **Piyadasi Identity** = George Turnour identified King Piyadasi as Ashoka using the Sri Lankan Pali chronicle *Dipavamsa*.
*   **Kharoshthi Decipherment** = Deciphered by Prinsep, Lassen, Masson, Cunningham, Norris using bilingual/bi-script Greek-Kharoshthi coins of Indo-Greek kings.
*   **Undeciphered scripts**:
    *   **Ornate Brahmi** = Highly stylized variation of Brahmi used for names/signatures.
    *   ***shankhalipi*** = 'Conch-shell script' (4th–8th century CE, except far South) used for names/signatures.
    *   Terracotta script in Chandraketugarh/Tamluk.
    *   Undeciphered Kharoshthi-like script in Afghanistan.
*   **Bi-script Inscriptions**:
    *   Short Brahmi-Kharoshthi in Northwest.
    *   **Pattadakal pillar inscription** (8th century CE, Chalukya Kirttivarman II) = Sanskrit text written in both North Indian Siddhamatrika and Southern proto-Telugu-Kannada scripts.

### Languages of Ancient and Early Medieval Inscriptions
*   **Prakrit** = Earliest Brahmi inscriptions (including Ashoka).
*   **Prakrit-Sanskrit mix** = 1st to 4th centuries CE.
*   **Pure Sanskrit** = Appeared 1st century BCE. First long Sanskrit inscription is **Junagadh rock inscription of Western Kshatrapa Rudradaman** (c. 150 CE).
*   By late 3rd century CE, Sanskrit replaced Prakrit in North Indian royal records; appeared in Deccan/South (e.g., Nagarjunakonda) in late 3rd/early 4th century CE.
*   Post-Gupta: Sanskrit became elite/power language of India & Southeast Asia. Regional languages arose.
*   **Tamil**: Inscriptions from 2nd century BCE (Tamil-Brahmi). Bilingual **Sanskrit-Tamil** grants arose under Pallavas (7th century), Cholas, Pandyas (genealogy/invocation in Sanskrit, grant details in Tamil).
*   **Kannada**: Inscriptions from late 6th/early 7th century CE. Bilingual Sanskrit-Kannada or trilingual (Sanskrit-Prakrit-Kannada at Kurgod, 12th century).
*   **Telugu**: Inscriptions start late 6th century (Telugu Cholas).
*   **North Indian Vernacular Inscriptions**: Marathi and Odia (11th century), Hindi (13th century), Gujarati and Malayalam (15th century).
*   **Arabic Inscriptions**: Appeared with Arab rule in Sindh/trade on west coast.
*   **Kollam Copper Plates** (9th century) = Mostly Tamil (Vatteluttu/Grantha), but 6th plate has witness signatures in Arabic (Kufic script), Pahlavi and Judaeo-Persian (Hebrew script).

### Dating The Inscriptions
*   **System** = Regnal years or eras (numbers/words). Combine solar and lunar units (month, fortnight/paksha, tithi, weekday). Year/day specification began 2nd century BCE.
*   ***bhutasankhya*** (Chronograms) = Words standing for numbers (e.g., *bhumi*=1, *kara*=2, *loka*=3, *veda*=4) read backwards. E.g., 'kara-veda-bhumi' = 142.
*   **Key Eras**:
    *   **Vikrama Era** = 58 BCE
    *   **Shaka Era** = 78 CE
    *   **Kalachuri-Chedi Era** = 248 CE
    *   **Gupta Era** = 319–20 CE
    *   **Kollam Era** = 824 CE (Kerala/Tamil Nadu)
    *   **Chalukya-Vikrama Era** = 1076 CE (Karnataka)
    *   **Harsha Era** = Debate: 612, 619, or 648 CE.
*   **Date Conversion Formula**:
    *   For BCE era (e.g., Vikrama): **Era Year - Initial BCE Year = CE Year** (e.g., Vikrama Year 179 = 179 - 58 = 121 CE).
    *   For CE era (e.g., Shaka): **Era Year + Initial CE Year = CE Year** (e.g., Shaka Year 179 = 179 + 78 = 257 CE).

### The Classification of Inscriptions
*   **Official Records** = Issued by kings (edicts, land grants).
*   **Private Records** = Issued by individuals/guilds (donations to temples, Buddhist/Jaina sangha).
*   **Purpose Categories**:
    *   *Donative* (temple railings, cave excavations, images, money deposits).
    *   *Dedicative* & *Commemorative* (e.g., Lumbini pillar of Ashoka).
*   **Memorial Stones**:
    *   **Hero stones** (*viragal*) = Commemorate dead heroes or women who committed sati.
    *   **Jaina memorials** = Honour voluntary ritual death (*sallekhana*).
    *   **Coast memorials** = For sailors lost in sea battles.
    *   **Pet memorials** = E.g., hunting dog Punisha (Gollarahatti), dog Kali (Atkur), pet parrot of Kadamba king (Tambur).
*   **Royal Land Grants**:
    *   Engraved on stone or copper plates. Record tax-exempt grants to Brahmanas/temples.
    *   Earliest stone land grants = 1st century CE (Satavahanas/Kshatrapas at Nashik).
    *   Oldest copper plate grant = **Patagandigudam grant of Ikshvaku Ehavala Chantamula** (late 3rd century CE).
*   ***Prashastis*** (Panegyrics) = Entire or partial eulogies. E.g., **Hathigumpha inscription of Kharavela** (1st BCE), **Allahabad prashasti of Samudragupta** (4th CE).

### Memorializing Death In Stone
*   **Andhra chhaya stambhas** (Nagarjunakonda memorial stones):
    *   In honor of kings, queens, soldiers, generals, artisans.
    *   **Chantamula I Memorial** = 12-ft pillar with inscription listing 29 mourning Ikshvaku women. Carvings depict: Chantamula I distributing gifts, riding elephant, surrounded by women (musicians/dancers), on throne and palace/heaven.
    *   **Artisan Mulabhuta Memorial** = Simple pillar, depicts narrow-necked vase (symbol of artisan guild).
*   **Karnataka Hero Stones** = 3,000 hero stones (5th–13th centuries). Commemorate cattle raids (e.g., death of a queen/woman in Siddhenahalli/Kembalu) and defending others (Shikaripur).

### Sudarshana Lake History (Junagadh Rock Inscriptions)
*   **Granite rock at Junagadh bears three major records showing 1,000 years of water management**:
    1.  **4th century BCE**: Construction begun under Maurya **Chandragupta** (provincial governor Pushyagupta).
    2.  **3rd century BCE**: Completed under **Ashoka** (governor Tushaspha).
    3.  **c. 150 CE (Rudradaman's inscription)**: Reconstructed after a major storm/breach by Shaka ruler **Rudradaman** (governor Suvishakha).
    4.  **5th century CE (Skandagupta's inscription)**: Repaired after lake burst its banks due to excessive rains under Gupta king **Skandagupta** (governor Parnadatta/Chakrapalita).

### An Ancient Theatre, An Ancient Love Story
*   **Surguja district (Chhattisgarh), Ramgarh hill caves**:
    *   Inscriptions in Prakrit (Brahmi, 3rd/2nd century BCE).
    *   **Sitabenga Cave**: Crescent-shaped rock-cut benches, terraces, aisles. Inscription speaks of poets, swing festival, jasmine garlands. Functioned as an **ancient theatre**.
    *   **Jogimara Cave**: 5-line inscription about a love story: *"Sutanuka by name, a devadasi. The excellent among young men, Devadinna by name, the rupadaksha, loved her."*
        *   *Terms:* **Sutanuka** = "one with a slender/beautiful body"; **rupadaksha** = skilled sculptor, scribe, coinage officer, or painter.
        *   Represents the early history of emotions.

### Inscriptions As A Source of History
*   **Advantages** = Durability, contemporaneous, localized in time/place, reflects actual practice (not theory).
*   **Limitations** = Moveable inscriptions not always found *in situ*.
*   **Dynastic skipping** = E.g., Skandagupta & Ramagupta omitted in late Gupta genealogies because they were not in the direct line of succession.
*   **Conflicting claims** = E.g., Gurjara-Pratihara claims Vatsaraja conquered Karnataka, while contemporary Rashtrakutas claim to have defeated Vatsaraja and ruled Karnataka.
*   **Administrative value** = Unravels technical vocabulary (fiscal, official titles like designations, land measures).
*   **Religious sects** = Gives details of sects with no surviving literature (e.g., **Ajivikas**, **yaksha & naga cults**).
*   **Art history** = Inscribed monastic seals (e.g., Kapilavastu at Piprahwa) fix site locations. Label inscriptions on Chidambaram Nataraja temple gateway describe **108 dance poses** quoting Bharata's *Natyashastra*. 7th-century **Kudumiyamalai inscription** records musical notes for 7 classical ragas.

## Numismatics: The Study of Coins

*   **Coinage** = Metal currency of definite size, shape, weight standard with issuing authority stamp (obverse = main message-bearing side; reverse = other side).
*   **Metrology** = Measurement and arrangement of coins by weight. Wear & tear analysis helps construct chronological sequence of hoards.
*   **Metal Analysis** = Physical (sound resonance, ductility bite, water displacement for specific gravity), chemical tests (destructive) and non-destructive **X-ray fluorescence (XRF)** spectrometry.
*   **Indian Coinage History**:
    *   **Vedic Terms** (*nishka, nishka-griva, hiranya-pinda*) = Gold ornaments/globules, not coins. Later terms (*suvarna, shatamana, pada*) = metal pieces of definite weight.
    *   **Ratti seed** (*raktika/ratti* of gunja berry) = Basic weight unit of North Indian coins. In South India: based on *manjadi* and *kalanju* beans.
    *   **Punch-Marked Coins** (6th–5th centuries BCE onwards) = Oldest coinage in India. Made of silver/copper. Blanks cut from sheets or globules; symbols hammered separately.
        *   *North Indian Series:*
            1.  *Taxila-Gandhara* (heavy weight, single punch).
            2.  *Kosala* (heavy weight, multiple punches).
            3.  *Avanti* (light weight, single punch).
            4.  *Magadhan* (light weight, multiple punches - replaced others as empire expanded).
    *   **Uninscribed Cast Copper Coins** (late 3rd/early 2nd century BCE) = Made in clay/metal moulds (e.g., clay moulds at Ayodhya, Kaushambi; bronze mould at Eran).
    *   **Uninscribed Die-Struck Coins** (c. 4th century BCE) = Struck with carved dies. Found at Taxila, Ujjain.
    *   **Indo-Greek Coins** (2nd/1st century BCE) = Die-struck, round/square silver and copper. Bear **ruler's portrait and name** on obverse; religious symbols on reverse. **Bilingual and bi-script** (Greek on obverse, Prakrit/Kharoshthi on reverse). Show portraits of aging rulers (e.g., Menander, Strato I).
    *   **Kushana Coins** (1st–4th centuries CE) = First to mint large-scale **gold coins** (*dinaras*), rare silver, abundant copper (expanded monetization). Features Bactrian language in Greek script (from Kanishka onwards) and diverse pantheon of deities.
    *   **Local/Tribal/Janapada Coins** (3rd BCE–4th CE) = Copper/bronze non-monarchical issues (Yaudheyas, Malavas - use word *gana*). City issues (Tripuri, Vidisha, Varanasi) and guild issues (*negama*, *pancha-nekame* of Taxila).
    *   **Satavahana Coins** (Deccan) = Made of lead, potin, copper, silver. Legends in Prakrit/Brahmi; silver portrait coins use **Dravidian language** (Tamil) and Brahmi script. Counter-struck Kshatrapa Nahapana's coins.
    *   **Gupta Coins** = High-quality gold coins (*dinaras*) with metrical Sanskrit legends. Obverse depicts king in martial poses or playing the vina (Samudragupta, Kumaragupta I). Performers of *ashvamedha* shown on coins. Purity declined under late Skandagupta.
    *   **Early Medieval Coinage Debate**:
        *   *Feudal Decline Hypothesis:* Postulates decline in coinage, trade and urban centers.
        *   *Counter-Argument (John S. Deyell):* No decline in volume of circulation; decline was only in aesthetic quality, types and message content (often anonymous).
        *   *Early Medieval series:* Billon coins in Gurjara-Pratihara; cowries and *hundikas* (bills of exchange) in Kashmir; silver **Harikela coins** in Bengal (7th–13th centuries).
        *   *Chola Coins:* Feature tiger (Chola), fish (Pandya), bow (Chera) to assert suzerainty. King Kulottunga I's gold coins bear title *sung* (abbreviation of *sungandavirttarulina* = abolisher of tolls).
        *   *Islamic-Indian syncretic issues:* Mahmud of Ghazni's Lahore coins (Arabic *shahada* on obverse, Sanskrit in Nagari on reverse); Muhammad Ghuri's coins depict Hindu goddess **Lakshmi**.

### Counter-Struck Coins of The Kshatrapas and Satavahanas
*   **Jogalthembi Hoard** (Nashik, 1906) = 13,250 silver coins of Kshatrapa king **Nahapana** (1st century CE), with 9,270 counter-struck by Satavahana king **Gautamiputra Satakarni**.
*   **Counter-striking** = Original strike (*undertype*) re-struck by new authority (*overtype*). Re-striking authority must be later/contemporary.
*   **Nahapana's coin symbols** = Portrait (obverse); thunderbolt & arrow (reverse).
*   **Gautamiputra's counter-strike** = Arched hill (obverse); four circles joined by cross (*Ujjain* symbol) (reverse).
*   **Shiva Satakarni and Nahapana** = Counter-struck each other's issues, proving they were exact contemporaries.
*   **Significance** = Marks political rivalry, but also prevents 'circulatory shock' (user mistrust) by maintaining familiar motifs (e.g., Nahapana kept Satavahana elephant & tree-in-railing in Nashik/Junnar, but replaced Ujjain symbol on reverse with thunderbolt/arrow).

## Visual Sources

*   **Context** = Ancient Indian art was largely religious, not "art for art's sake". Creators (artists, sculptors, painters) were often peripatetic (moving across regions and religious boundaries) and organized into guilds.
*   **Attitudes to the Body** = High celebration of the **sensuous bodily form** (aesthetically pleasing). Physical beauty was a sign of political & spiritual power (idealized body, not realistic representation).
*   **Art as Historical Evidence**:
    *   Conveys ideas/practices missing in texts.
    *   **Iconography** links to textual descriptions of deities, but is not a mechanical translation.
    *   **Patronage:** Donative inscriptions on art reveal social/political processes. Art was used to accumulate merit and assert political status (e.g., political allegory/double meaning).

## Conclusions

*   Reconstructing ancient/early medieval India relies on synthesizing **textual, archaeological, epigraphic, numismatic and visual sources**.
*   Integration is challenging due to qualitative differences in data types (e.g., event-centered texts vs. material pattern-centered archaeology).
*   The written word, material remains and visual images offer partial, refracted images of the past; oral, personal and emotional histories remain dimly visible.
*   Reconstructing history requires rigorous analysis, reason, objectivity and asking new questions of the sources.
