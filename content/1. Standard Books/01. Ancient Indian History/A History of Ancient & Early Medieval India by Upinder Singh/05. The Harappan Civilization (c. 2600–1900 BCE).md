### Discovery and Early Exploration
* **1826 (Charles Masson)** = Deserted East India Company soldier visited Harappa mounds → Mistook site for Alexander's 4th-century BCE battle with Porus.
* **1831 (Alexander Burnes)** = Visited Harappa → Recognized importance, but significance unknown.
* **1850s & 1872 (Alexander Cunningham)** = 1st Director General of ASI (est. 1871) → Mounds heavily damaged by railway contractors mining bricks → Discovered stone tools, pottery, and bull seal (misidentified as foreign due to lack of hump).
* **Early 20th Century**:
    * **Pandit Hirananda Sastri** = Considered Harappa unpromising.
    * **D. R. Bhandarkar** = Dated Mohenjodaro to < 250 years old.
* **1920 (Daya Ram Sahni)** = Began systematic excavations at **Harappa**.
* **1921 (R. D. Banerji / Rakhaldas Banerji)** = Began systematic excavations at **Mohenjodaro**.
* **1924 (Sir John Marshall)** = Director General of ASI (1902–28) formally announced discovery of Indus/Harappan Civilization → Pushed Indian urban history back by ~2,500 years to 3rd millennium BCE (contemporaneous with Mesopotamia & Egypt).

### Civilization and Urbanization: Definitions and Implications
* **Civilization = Meaning** → Complex culture marked by cities, state organization, writing, social stratification, and ceremonial/monumental architecture.
    * *Origin* = 18th-century France → Colonial binary ('civilized' vs 'primitive') now abandoned by modern historians.
    * *Exceptions*: **Jericho** (Jordan valley, 8th mill. BCE) & **Çatal Höyük** (Turkey, 7th mill. BCE) = Urban without writing; **Maya** & **Mycenae** = Civilizations without true cities; **Inca** = Civilization without true writing.
* **Urbanization = Meaning** → Emergence and development of cities.
* **V. Gordon Childe (1950)** = Formulated concept of **'Urban Revolution'** → Cumulative outcome of centuries of gradual socio-economic evolution (not sudden or violent).

#### The 10 Characteristics of Cities, According To Childe
1. **Size & Population** = Larger and more densely populated than contemporary villages.
2. **Occupational Specialization** = Population includes full-time craftspersons, merchants, transporters, officials, and priests → Supported by agricultural surplus produced by farmers.
3. **Surplus Extraction** = Farmers deliver surplus produce as tax or tribute to a ruling elite.
4. **Monumental Public Buildings** = Symbolize concentration of social surplus and wealth in elite hands.
5. **Social Trade-off** = Ruling class lives off social surplus → Provides peace, security, civic planning, and organization in return.
6. **Systems of Recording** = Invention of writing and numerical notation to meet administrative needs.
7. **Exact Sciences** = Development of predictive/practical sciences (arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, calendar).
8. **Artistic Expression** = Conceptualized, sophisticated, and standardized artistic styles.
9. **Long-Distance Trade** = High volume and broad geographical reach of trade.
10. **State Organization** = Political organization based on territorial residence rather than kinship → State provides protection and raw materials to specialist craftspersons.

* **Debates on Urbanization**:
    * **Childe** = Emphasized technology and subsistence factors (food surplus, copper-bronze metallurgy, wheel transport, sail, plough).
    * **Robert McC. Adams** = Emphasized social factors and **city-hinterland interdependence** → City as node for surplus redistribution, institutions, safe storage, expenditure, and intellectual/religious center.
    * **Gideon Sjoberg (1964)** = Emphasized political control and state/empire stability as pivotal for trade, security, and elite concentration.
    * **Modern Debates (Marcus, Sabloff, Monica L. Smith)** = Distinction between **urbanization** (emergence/growth) and **urbanism** (city form and lived experience).

### Recent Discoveries and Changing Perspectives
* **Decentering Mesopotamia** = Shift away from viewing Harappan culture through a Mesopotamian lens → Acknowledging independent indigenous development.
* **Redressing Urban Bias**:
    * Beyond Mohenjodaro and Harappa → Discovery of equal/larger sites: **Lurewala** & **Ganweriwala** (Cholistan), **Rakhigarhi** (Haryana), **Dholavira** (Gujarat).
    * Focus on small rural/village sites: **Allahdino** (5 ha near Karachi; rich metal/jewellery hoards) and **Balu** (Kaithal, Haryana; fortified rural site with diverse crop remains).
* **Reinterpreting Structures**:
    * **Fentress (1984)** = Questioned whether 'Great Granaries' at Mohenjodaro and Harappa were actually granaries.
    * **Leshnik (1968)** = Proposed Lothal dockyard was an irrigation reservoir (largely refuted by Rao and others).
* **Methodological Advances** = Precise stratigraphic residential analysis, palaeo-botanical/osteological science, and human genome/DNA analysis.

### Harappan, Indus, or Sindhu–Sarasvati Civilization ?
* **Nomenclature Evolution**:
    * 'Indus Valley Civilization' = Obsolete; sites spread far beyond Indus valley across 680,000–800,000 sq km.
    * 'Indus–Sarasvati' / 'Sindhu–Sarasvati' = Used due to dense concentration of sites along Ghaggar-Hakra river (identified with Vedic Sarasvati).
    * **'Harappan Civilization'** = Preferred standard per archaeological convention (named after type-site Harappa).
* **Geographical Boundaries**:
    * **North** = **Manda** (Jammu & Kashmir).
    * **South** = **Malvan** (Surat district, Gujarat).
    * **West** = **Sutkagen-dor** (Makran coast, Baluchistan, Pakistan).
    * **East** = **Alamgirpur** (Saharanpur district, UP).
    * **Isolated Northern Outpost** = **Shortughai** (Badakhshan, NE Afghanistan).
* **Gregory L. Possehl (2003)** = Proposed breaking Harappan monolith into regional **'Domains'** → Simultaneously exhibits cultural uniformity and regional specificity.
* **Archaeological Diagnostic Checklist**:
    1. Black-on-Red painted pottery.
    2. Terracotta cakes (triangular or round).
    3. Standardized brick size in **1:2:4 ratio**.
    4. Specific stone blade and copper artefact types.
* **Three-Phase Sequence (Jim Shaffer terminology in brackets)**:
    * **Early Harappan** (c. 3300–2600 BCE) = Formative / Proto-urban phase (*Regionalization Era*).
    * **Mature Harappan** (c. 2600–1900 BCE) = Urban phase / Full civilization (*Integration Era*).
    * **Late Harappan** (c. 1900–1300 BCE) = Post-urban phase / Decline (*Localization Era*).
* **Chronology**:
    * John Marshall = c. 3250–2750 BCE (Mesopotamian cross-dating).
    * Revised Cross-Dating = c. 2350–2000/1900 BCE (Ur royal graves, re-dated by Julian Reade to c. 2400–2300 BCE).
    * **Calibrated C-14 Core Range** = **c. 2600–1900 BCE** (Mature urban phase). Broad timeline:
        * **Early Harappan** = c. 3300–2600 BCE
        * **Mature Harappan** = c. 2600–1900 BCE
        * **Late Harappan** = c. 1900–1300 BCE
    * *Alternative (Dilip K. Chakrabarti)* = Early (3500+ BCE), Mature (2700+ BCE), Late (2000+ BCE).

### Origin: The Significance of The Early Harappan Phase
* **Refutation of Diffusionist Theories**:
    * **E. J. H. Mackay, D. H. Gordon, S. N. Kramer** = Proposed migration of people from Sumer (Mesopotamia).
    * **Mortimer Wheeler** = Proposed migration of ideas ("civilization in the air").
    * *Refutation* = Striking structural differences (Harappan script unique, lack of Mesopotamian canal systems, different settlement layouts, lower bronze usage in Harappan).
* **Indigenous Roots**:
    * Long prelude in Baluchistan farming villages from 7th mill. BCE (Mehrgarh).
    * **Amalananda Ghosh (1965)** = Identified pre-Harappan **Sothi culture** (Rajasthan) as 'proto-Harappan' based on ceramic parallels across Zhob, Quetta, Kalibangan, and Kot Diji.
    * **M. R. Mughal (1977)** = Replaced 'pre-Harappan' with **'Early Harappan'** → Showed pre-mature levels already possessed fortified settlements, specialized stone/metal/bead crafts, wheel transport, and trade networks (lacking only mega-cities and high-level craft specialization).
* **Key Early Harappan Sites (c. 3300–2600 BCE)**:
    * **Balakot** (Period II) = Polychrome Nal pottery, microliths, humped bull figurines, lapis lazuli beads, barley, legumes.
    * **Amri** (Period I, c. 3500 BCE) = Phases 1A–1D; mud-brick structures, cellular storage compartments (1C), geometric wheel-made pottery.
    * **Kot Diji** (Period I, c. 3300 BCE) = Massive limestone/mud-brick fortification wall, citadel + lower town, short-necked ovoid pots painted with **'horned deity'**, pipal leaves, and fish scales (*Kot Dijian level*).
    * **Nausharo** = Clear stratigraphic transition Period IC (early) → mature (dated c. 2600–2550 BCE).
    * **Gumla** & **Rehman Dheri** (Gomal Valley) = Rehman Dheri Period I (c. 3380–3040 BCE, > 20 ha, planned rectangular grid streets, mud-brick wall, Kot Dijian pottery with graffiti, lapis lazuli, turquoise).
    * **Bannu Basin**:
        * **Lewan** = Specialized factory site for stone tools (microliths, querns, long axes, hammer stones) and beads.
        * **Tarakai Qila** = Wheat, barley, lentils, field pea, sickle blades with harvesting sheen.
    * **Sarai Khola** (Period II) = Pit dwellings → mud-brick houses, Kot Dijian ware, copper pins/rings, lapis lazuli.
    * **Harappa** (Period I = Ravi aspect of Hakra phase; Period II = Early Harappan > 25 ha, 2 mounds with mud-brick fortifications, circular kilns, hearths, lapis/carnelian/steatite beads, early script graffiti, standardized weights).
    * **Cholistan Tract (M. R. Mughal)** = Nomadic campsites dropped from 52.5% (Hakra phase) to 7.5% (Kot Diji phase) → Shift to permanent settlements with kilns (e.g., **Jalwali** 22.5 ha, **Gamanwala** 27.3 ha).
    * **Kalibangan** (Period I, c. 2920–2550 BCE) = 4 ha fortified settlement, mud-brick houses (3:2:1 brick ratio), moustache-scroll painted pottery, **ploughed field surface** (intersecting N-S and E-W furrows south of mound).
    * **Indo-Gangetic Divide**:
        * **Kunal** = Period IA (Hakra pit dwellings) → IB (Kalibangan I ware + Harappan red beakers) → IC (ground-level 1:2:3 & 1:2:4 mud-brick houses, 6 steatite seals, 1 shell seal, **jewellery hoard in silver pot**: 2 silver tiaras, silver armlet, 6 gold pendants, lapis/agate beads).
        * **Banawali** = Early mud-brick houses, hearths, chert blade, Kalibangan I type pottery.
        * **Rakhigarhi** (Period I) = Planned mud-brick structures, graffiti pottery, chert weights, hopscotch stacks (*pithu* game).
        * **Bhirrana** = IA (Hakra) → IB (Early Harappan 1:2:3 mud-brick 6-room house, chullahs, toy carts with radiating spokes, shale button seal, copper arrowheads).
    * **Gujarat** = **Padri**, **Kuntasi**, and **Dholavira** (early fortified levels with 1:2:4 mud-bricks, dish-on-stand, perforated jars).

### The Relationship Between The Early and Mature Harappan Phases
* **Cultural Convergence (Allchins)** = Gradual integration of diverse regional traditions into pan-regional cultural uniformity.
* **Incipient Mature Traits in Early Phase**:
    * Craft specialization, merchant seals (Kunal, Nausharo), wealth concentration (Kunal silver tiaras), early script symbols (Padri, Kalibangan, Banawali, Dholavira, Harappa), and iconographic spread of the **'horned deity'** (Kot Diji, Rehman Dheri, Kalibangan cake).
* **Transition Catalysts (Dilip K. Chakrabarti)** = Development of copper metallurgy in Rajasthan + agricultural expansion in active Indus floodplain via organized irrigation + decisive political leadership / new ideology.
* **Gaps and Discontinuities**:
    * Mature sites without Early levels = **Lothal**, **Desalpur**, **Chanhudaro**, **Mitathal**, **Alamgirpur**, **Ropar**.
    * Early sites without Mature continuation = Potwar plateau sites; most Cholistan sites (only Chak 76, Gamanwali, Sandhanawala Ther continued).
    * Destruction / Burning evidence at transition = **Kot Diji**, **Gumla**, **Amri**, **Nausharo** (major fires); **Kalibangan** (earthquake break).

### The General Features of Mature Harappan Settlements
* **Rural–Urban Symbiosis** = Cities relied on villages for food surplus and labor; urban craft items circulated back to small villages.
* **Settlement Hierarchy & Sizes**:
    * **Mega-Cities (> 100–200 ha)** = Mohenjodaro (> 200 ha), Harappa (> 150 ha), Dhalewan (150 ha), Lakhmirwala (225 ha), Rakhigarhi (> 100 ha), Dholavira (~100 ha), Ganweriwala (> 81.5 ha), Lurewala (~35,000 pop).
    * **Moderate Towns (10–50 ha)** = Judeirjodaro, Kalibangan.
    * **Small Towns (5–10 ha)** = Amri, Lothal, Chanhudaro, Rojdi.
    * **Villages / Craft Sites (1–5 ha)** = Allahdino (1.4 ha), Kot Diji, Ropar, Balakot, Surkotada, Nageshwar, Nausharo, Ghazi Shah.
* **Civic Planning & Layouts**:
    * Grid system (imperfect but structured roads/alleys).
    * **Bipartite Layout** = Raised Citadel (West) + Lower Town (East) (Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Kalibangan).
    * **Integrated Citadel** = Citadel within main settlement (Lothal, Surkotada).
    * **Tripartite Layout** = Castle, Middle Town, Lower Town (Dholavira).
* **Building Materials & Construction**:
    * Villages = Mud-brick, mud, reeds.
    * Cities = Sun-dried and burnt bricks; dressed stone in rocky Kutch/Saurashtra (Dholavira stone fortifications & polished pillars).
    * **English Bond Style** = Alternate rows of stretchers and headers for maximum load-bearing strength.
    * **Standard Brick Ratio = 1:2:4** (7 × 14 × 28 cm for houses; 10 × 20 × 40 cm for city walls).
* **Residential Architecture**:
    * Rooms arranged around central open courtyard; entrance doors/windows open into side lanes (privacy focus).
    * Two-storeyed structures (thick surviving walls up to 5 m at Mohenjodaro, staircases), roofs of wooden beams + reeds + clay, 3m+ high ceilings.
* **Hygiene & Sanitation**:
    * **Bathing Areas** = Watertight floors of tightly fitted bricks on edge, sloped toward wall drains leading to street sewers.
    * **Toilets** = Sunken floor commode pots with lota jars for washing; exit channels discharging into street cesspits/jars.
    * **Drainage Systems** = Terracotta drain pipes, street drains of baked bricks with corbelled arches, rectangular soak-pits at regular intervals for solid waste.
    * **Water Resources** = Mohenjodaro (~700 wedge-brick wells; 1 in 3 houses); Harappa (fewer wells, central tank/reservoir); Dholavira (16+ stone-cut reservoirs/cisterns).

### Profiles of Some Harappan Cities, Towns, and Villages

#### **Mohenjodaro** (Sindh, ~200 ha, est. pop. ~41,250):
* **Citadel Mound** (West, 12m high, 400 × 200 m mud-brick platform, 6m thick retaining wall):
    * **Great Bath** = 14.5 × 7 m, 2.4 m deep; finely fitted bricks on edge + gypsum mortar + **bitumen (asphalt) waterproofing layer**; N & S staircases; brick colonnades; 8 small private bathing rooms north of bath.
    * **'College of Priests'** = 69 × 23.4 m multi-room building with 10m square courtyard and staircases.
    * **'Great Granary'** = 50 × 27 m solid brick foundation with 27 square/rectangular blocks separated by air ventilation channels; loading dock; identification as granary questioned (Fentress).
    * **'Assembly Hall'** = 27 × 27 m square building divided into 5 aisles by brick piers.
* **Lower Town** (East, > 80 ha):
    * HR area = Seated stone male sculpture ('priest-king' style); double row of 16 single-room units (workmen's quarters or shops).
    * Workshops = Copper working, bead making, dyeing, pottery, shell working. ~700 wedge-brick wells.

#### **Chanhudaro** (Sindh, 4.7 ha, single unfortified mound):
* Major craft manufacturing hub: **Steatite bead factory**, carnelian/agate/crystal beads, drills, seal making, shell work, stone weights. Covered burnt-brick street drains.

#### **Harappa** (Punjab, ~150 ha):
* **Citadel Mound** = Parallelogram (415 × 195 m), mud-brick wall with towers/gateways; Cemetery R-37 to south.
* **Mound F (Northern Suburb)**:
    * 15 workmen's quarter units (17 × 7 m in 2 rows).
    * 18 circular brick threshing platforms (diameter > 3m) with central wooden mortars (husks of barley & wheat found).
    * **'Granary'** = 12 units in 2 rows of 6 (15.2 × 6.1 m each) with sleeper walls and air passages.
* **Mound E (Lower Town)** = Southern gateway market area, halting/resting spot outside gate.

#### **Kalibangan** (Ghaggar River, Rajasthan):
* **KLB-1 (Citadel Mound)** = Divided into N & S sectors.
    * *Southern Sector* = Mud-brick platforms with a row of **7 clay-lined 'fire altars'**, well, bath pavements, cattle bone/antler pit → Congregational sacrificial rituals.
    * *Northern Sector* = Priestly residences.
* **KLB-2 (Lower Town)** = Fortified parallelogram; houses contain oblong fire altars with central stele; street drains absent (troughs/jars used instead); terracotta/shell bangles, copper bull, stone phallic emblem, horned figure sherd.
* **Burial Ground** = Extended burials + circular grave pits with grave goods (no human bones).

#### **Banawali** (Hissar, Haryana):
* Fortified (300 × 500 m), semi-elliptical citadel with moat + lower town.
* Merchant house (kitchen, washbasin toilet, many seals/weights).
* Jeweller house (gold, lapis, carnelian beads, **touchstone with gold streaks**).
* Seals found ONLY in lower town. Terracotta plough model, apsidal structure with fire altars.

#### **Rakhigarhi** (Hissar, Haryana, 5 mounds):
* Citadel platforms, well, fire altars.
* **Lapidary workshop** = 3,000 unfinished carnelian/chalcedony/agate/jasper beads, polishers, hearth. Bone & ivory working area.
* Cemetery with 8 brick-lined burials (1 wooden coffin). Female skeleton 16113 DNA study.

#### **Bhirrana** (Fatehabad, Haryana):
* Multi-room house complexes, tandoors/chullahs, red ware sherd with incised female figure in **Mohenjodaro 'dancing girl' pose**.

#### **Farmana** (Rohtak, Haryana):
* Periods IIA–IIC (c. 2600–2000 BCE). Planned grid, pear-shaped potter's kiln, 70 burials (extended, secondary, symbolic). Crop remains: barley, wheat, pulses, **garlic, ginger, turmeric, clove**.

#### **Lothal** (Saurashtra, Gujarat, 280 × 225 m):
* **Acropolis** + **Warehouse** (65 terracotta sealings with cloth/reed impressions).
* 'Bazaar street' shops, coppersmith/bead kilns, house fire altars.
* **Dockyard** = Eastern trapezoidal burnt-brick basin (212m/215m × 37m/35m) with sluice gate, spill channel, and western mud-brick wharf embankment.

#### **Dholavira** (Kadir Island, Rann of Kutch):
* Tripartite layout: **Castle** (acropolis), **Bailey**, **Middle Town**, **Lower Town**, and **'Stadium'** (ceremonial ground).
* Massive stone rubble fortifications + dressed limestone veneer + **polished limestone pillars** (earliest monumental stone architecture in South Asia).
* **Dholavira 'Signboard'** = Northern gateway side-room; 9–10 signs made of white gypsum paste inlaid into wooden board (each sign 37 × 25–27 cm).
* **Water Management** = 16+ deep stone-cut reservoirs/cisterns, dams across Manhar & Mandsar streams.

#### **Kanmer** (Kachchh, Gujarat):
* Fortified settlement, faience furnace, unicorn sealings, barley/wheat/cotton/meats.

#### **Allahdino** (Karachi District, 1.4 ha unfortified village):
* Small-diameter wells (60–90 cm) leveraging hydraulic pressure for field irrigation.
* Rich jewellery hoard in small jar: 36-long carnelian bead belt, multi-strand silver necklace, gold/bronze ornaments.

### The Diversity of The Harappan Subsistence Base
* **Climatic Context Debate**:
    * *Wheeler & Piggott* = Proposed wetter climate (burnt brick fuel requirements, gabarbands, tiger/rhino/elephant seals, drainage).
    * *Gurdip Singh (1971)* = Pollen analysis from Rajasthan salt lakes (Sambhar, Didwana, Lunkaransar) → Rainfall ↑ c. 3000 BCE, rainfall ↓ c. 1800 BCE.
    * *Enzel et al. (1999)* = Lunkaransar dried up by 3500 BCE → Aridity set in long before Harappan emergence.
* **Agricultural Economy**:
    * **Cereal Crops** = Wheat (Mohenjodaro, Harappa), Barley (Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Kalibangan), Rice (Harappa, Kalibangan, Lothal, Rangpur), Millets (Surkotada, Shortughai, Gujarat).
    * **Pulses & Cash Crops** = Peas, sesamum, mustard, cotton, dates, watermelon, grapes, henna.
    * **Balu (Haryana)** = Earliest evidence of **garlic**, plus barley, wheat, rice, horse gram, green gram, chickpea, field pea, dates, grapes.
    * **Cropping Pattern** = Double cropping → *Kharif* (summer: rice, sesamum, cotton, millets) + *Rabi* (winter: wheat, barley, mustard).
    * **Tillage & Irrigation** = Terracotta plough models (Banawali, Bahawalpur); ploughed field at Kalibangan; irrigation canals at Shortughai (Afghanistan) and Haryana (Francfort).
* **Pastoralism and Animal Exploitation**:
    * Domesticated = Humped (*Bos indicus*) and humpless cattle, water buffalo, sheep, goat, dog, cat.
    * Wild Game & Fauna = Deer, pig, boar, rhinoceros, elephant, tiger, leopard, birds, fish, turtles, molluscs. (Marine catfish at Harappa = inland trade of dried sea fish).

#### The Rhinoceros and The Harappans (Shibani Bose, 2020)
* Rhinoceros (*Rhinoceros unicornis*) inhabited alluvial river floodplains/marshy grasslands.
* Bones found at Nausharo, Harappa, Kalibangan, Lothal, Surkotada, Shikarpur → Shoulder blades used as stone tool anvils; meat consumed.
* Depicted naturalistically on terracottas and seals standing before a manger/trough → Religious/ritualistic significance.
* Waning presence in post-Harappan times due to forest clearance and agricultural shift favoring draught animals (elephants/horses).

* **Equid & Camel Debates**:
    * *Camel* = Reported at Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Surkotada, Kalibangan; minor role.
    * *Horse (Equus caballus)* = Reported at Surkotada (Sándor Bökönyi identified 6 true horse bones; disputed by Meadow & Patel) and Rana Ghundai (Ross teeth ID disputed by Zeuner). Horses domesticated in Pontic-Caspian steppes (c. 3500–3000 BCE); played no major role in Harappan civilization.

#### Animal Bones At Shikarpur (P. K. Thomas et al., 1995)
* 15,483 bone fragments analyzed (53.5% ID'd, 47 species).
* Domesticated animals > 85% of total assemblage (Cattle ~77.8%, Sheep/Goat 4.6–11.2%, Buffalo ~4.6%).
* Cattle/buffalo killed at mature age (3–8+ yrs) = Draft work and dairying.
* Sheep/goats killed young (6–24 months) = Primary meat rearing.
* Late Mature phase shift to wild game = Agricultural stress or environmental pressure.

### Harappan Crafts and Techniques
* **Ceramics & Kilns**:
    * Funnel-shaped updraft closed kilns (Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Nausharo, Chanhudaro).
    * **Typical Pottery** = Fine, wheel-made **Black-on-Red Ware** with bright red ochre (*geru*) slip and black iron/manganese painted designs.
    * Motifs = Fish scales, pipal leaves, intersecting circles, peacocks, geometric lines. Human figures rare.
    * Vessel Types = Dish-on-stand, S-profile vase, cylindrical perforated jar (fermentation/brewing or ritual), pointed foot goblet, ring stand.
    * Glazing = Earliest vitreous glaze on purplish grey ware at lowest levels of Mohenjodaro.
* **Terracottas & Stoneware**:
    * Figurines (bulls, dogs, monkeys, toy carts, human figures), masks (Mohenjodaro/Harappa), faience (efflorescence technique for bangles, rings, miniature vessels).
    * **Stoneware Bangles** = High-fired, burnished red/grey-black, 5.5–6 cm diameter, tiny incised letters.
* **Stone Tool Technology**:
    * Mass-produced chert blades using **crested guided ridge technique** (quarries at Rohri Hills, Sindh; Datrana, Gujarat).
* **Metallurgy**:
    * Pure copper > Bronze alloy (alloying with tin, arsenic, nickel increased from 6% to 23% in upper levels at Mohenjodaro).
    * Tools = Flat shaftless axes, knives, spears, fishhooks, chisels, mirrors.
    * Copper kilns at Harappa (16) and Lothal. Gold/silver jewellery hoards (Allahdino, Harappa). Lead plumb bobs. Rare iron traces at Lothal (39.1% & 66.1% Fe).
* **Seal Making**:
    * Square/rectangular steatite seals (2.54–6.35 cm) with perforated rear boss. Carved in intaglio, alkali-coated and fired for white lustrous surface.
    * Motifs = Unicorn (most frequent), humped bull, tiger, elephant, rhino, pipal tree, horned deity.

#### Sculpture In Stone and Metal
* **Stone**: 'Priest-King' bust (17.78 cm, Mohenjodaro); male red jasper & grey stone torsos (Harappa); stone lizard (Dholavira).
* **Bronze**: **'Dancing Girl'** (10.8 cm, Mohenjodaro HR area, **lost-wax technique / cire perdue**) = Naked female, 24–25 bangles on left arm, 4 on right arm, low bun, defiant posture.

* **Bead Making & Shell Working**:
    * Specialized drills made of rare metamorphic rock (**'Ernestite'** drills).
    * Factories at Chanhudaro, Lothal, Bagasra (clay-lined stone storage silos). Long barrel cylinder carnelian beads exported to Ur (Mesopotamia).
    * Shell centers = **Nageshwar** (exclusive shell bangle production site), Balakot, Chanhudaro, Dholavira, Kuntasi. Ivory combs/pins.
* **Textiles & Wild Silk**:
    * Cotton cloth traces at Mohenjodaro (silver jar contact), Harappa (mirror/razor handles), spindle whorls.
    * **Wild Silk Discovery (Irene Good et al., 2009)** = SEM analysis of thread in beads/bronze at Harappa & Chanhudaro (c. 2450–2000 BCE) = *Antheraea assamensis* & *Antheraea mylitta* silk moth fibres → **Earliest evidence of silk outside China**.

#### The Making of Long Carnelian Beads (Mark Kenoyer, Massimo Vidale, K. K. Bhan)
* Ethno-archaeology at Khambhat + Chanhudaro experiments.
* Carnelian nodules sun-dried → heated in ovens → roughouts chipped → drilled with 'Ernestite' bow drills (3–8 days per 6–13 cm bead, 480 workdays for 36-bead belt) → polished.
* Centralized merchant control at Chanhudaro vs opportunistic short-term production at Mohenjodaro Moneer area. Terracotta red-painted imitations for non-elites.

* **Weights and Measures**:
    * Cubical chert weights.
    * **Binary System** in lower weights (1:2:8:16:32:64; base unit 16 = 13.63 g; max weight 10.865 kg at Mohenjodaro).
    * **Decimal System** in higher weights (160, 200, 320, 640).
    * Shell scale at Mohenjodaro, ivory scale at Lothal, angle measuring shell tool in Saurashtra.

### Networks of Trade
* **Media of Exchange** = Barter / commodity exchange; no coinage.
* **Raw Material Provenance (Randall Law 2011 Study on Harappa)**:
    * Chert = Sukkur and Rohri Hills (Sindh).
    * Copper = Khetri deposits & Ganeshwar–Jodhpura complex (Rajasthan); Oman / Baluchistan.
    * Lead / Zinc = Rajasthan, J&K, Baluchistan.
    * Tin = Tosam (Haryana), Afghanistan, Central Asia.
    * Gold = Kolar fields (Karnataka, via neolithic herders trade); upper Indus river sands.
    * Steatite & Agate/Carnelian = Gujarat.
    * Lapis Lazuli = Badakhshan (Sar-i-Sang mines, Afghanistan); Chagai hills Baluchistan unproven.
* **Transport Modes**:
    * Two-wheeled ox-carts with solid wheels; pack animal caravans (oxen, donkeys, sheep); river boats with cabins/stern platforms; seafaring masted ships with sharp keels.
* **Internal Trade Routes (Nayanjot Lahiri, 1992)**:
    * Baluchistan–Sindh (Kirthar passes: Bolan, Mula, Gomal); Sindh–Punjab; East Punjab–Rajasthan (Cholistan tract); Coastal route: Gujarat (Lothal, Dholavira) to Makran coast (Sutkagen-dor).
* **Long-Distance International Trade**:
    * **Turkmenistan** = Altyn Depe (rectangular Harappan seal, ivory dice, metal ladle).
    * **Iran** = Hissar, Susa, Tepe Yahya, Jiroft (etched carnelian beads, seals).
    * **Persian Gulf** = Bahrain (Ras-al-Qala, Hamad, Hajjar), Failaka (round 'Persian Gulf' seals with Harappan script/bull).
    * **Oman Peninsula** = Umm-an-Nar, Maysar, Ras al-Junayaz (Harappan seals, inscribed potsherds, ivory combs, chert weights). Imports: chlorite vessels, shell, mother-of-pearl.
    * **Mesopotamia** = Sargon of Akkad inscriptions (2334–2279 BCE) mention ships from **Dilmun** (Bahrain), **Magan** (Oman/Makran), and **Meluhha** (Indus Valley).
        * *Meluhha Exports*: Lapis lazuli, carnelian beads, gold, silver, copper, ebony, ivory, tortoiseshell, chicken-like bird, cat, monkey.
        * *Mesopotamian Imports to Indus*: Wool, silver (though few Mesopotamian artefacts found in Indus).
* **Trade Importance Debates**:
    * **Shereen Ratnagar (1981)** = Lapis trade vital; decline caused Harappan fall.
    * **Dilip K. Chakrabarti (1990) & Jim Shaffer (1982)** = Long-distance trade was indirect, limited, and non-essential for Harappan urbanism.
    * **Julian Reade (2008)** = Lowered Mesopotamian chronology; Harappan cities preceded Mesopotamian trade.

#### Sources of Rocks and Minerals (Randall Law, 2011)
* Analyzed ~3,000 artefacts from Harappa across Periods I–V using XRD, EMPA, INAA, CDA.
* Confirmed most rocks/minerals came from highland areas north/west of Indus within or near culture zone; external trade was minor.

#### Shortughai - A Harappan Trading Post In Afghanistan (D. K. Chakrabarti, 1990)
* Oxus-Kokcha confluence site (2 ha, 4 periods).
* Period I = Full Harappan assemblage (pottery, seals with rhino, lapis/carnelian beads, weights, 1:2:4 bricks, graffiti). Irrigation canals + flax field.
* Linked to Badakhshan lapis mines, tin mines, and camel trade.

### The Nature and Uses of Writing
* **Script Characteristics**:
    * **Logo-syllabic** (~400–450 basic signs; each symbol = word or syllable).
    * Direction = **Right-to-left** (evident from cramping on left edge & pottery scratches); multi-line inscriptions in **Boustrophedon** (alternating directions).
    * Inscriptions = ~6,000 objects (50% Mohenjodaro, 37% Harappa). Average length = 5 signs; longest = 26 signs.
    * Language Theories = **Asko Parpola & Iravatham Mahadevan** = Dravidian language family; others suggest Indo-Aryan. Farmer, Sproat & Witzel non-linguistic symbol theory refuted by Parpola (2008).
* **Uses of Inscribed Objects**:
    * Seals & Sealings = Commercial authentication (clay sealings on merchandise bales), trading tokens, identity cards/amulets for elites.
    * Miniature steatite/faience tablets & copper tablets (Mohenjodaro/Harappa).
    * Pottery = Pre-firing marks, post-firing graffiti, pointed goblet seal impressions.
    * Personal Possessions = Gold ornaments, stoneware bangles, bronze tools.
    * Civic Use = **Dholavira Signboard** (9–10 gypsum paste characters inlaid into wooden board, 37 × 25–27 cm each).
    * Perishable media = Wooden tablets with hanging holes at Mohenjodaro. Script disappeared by ~1700 BCE with de-urbanization.

### Religious and Funerary Practices
* **Sir John Marshall (1931)** = Formulated baseline interpretation of Harappan religion.
* **Female Goddess / Mother Goddess Worship**:
    * Terracotta female figurines with fan-shaped headdresses, pannier/cup-like head attachments with black oil soot residue.
    * Seal depicting head-downwards nude woman with plant issuing from vagina = Prototype of **Shakambhari** (Earth Mother).
    * *Critique (Ardeleanu-Jansen)* = Not all female terracottas are goddesses; slim decorated type vs matronly pot-bellied type holding babies. Discarded in secondary contexts = Short-term household ritual cycle / votive offerings. Absent at Kalibangan, Lothal, Surkotada.
* **Pashupati Seal & Male Deities**:
    * Mohenjodaro steatite seal = Male seated in yogic posture (*Mahayogi*), buffalo horn headdress, flanked by 4 animals (elephant, tiger, rhino, buffalo) + 2 ibexes/antelopes below seat. Marshall identified as proto-Shiva / Pashupati.

#### Man, God, or Goddess ?
* Critiques by Atre (goddess 'lady of beasts'), Dhavalikar, etc. Marshall's male yogi proto-Shiva view remains persuasive.

* **Lingas & Yonis** = Stone/terracotta/faience phallic lingas & ring stone yonis. Kalibangan terracotta linga on yonipitha base confirms cultic role. (Dales argued architectural/measurement use for ring stones).
* **Nature & Animal Worship**:
    * Pipal tree (*Ficus religiosa*) veneration, tree spirits, 7 braided figures seal (proto-seven rishis / seven mothers).
    * Sacred animals = Humped bull (virility symbol), tiger, rhino, elephant, snake, gharial, mythical **Unicorn** (one-horned bovine), composite animals (e.g., human-tiger-bull).
    * Amulets & Symbols = **Svastika**, terracotta masks/puppets.

#### The ‘Fire Altars’ (B. B. Lal, 1984)
* Kalibangan Citadel S sector = 7 mud-brick pits with central clay stele, ash, charcoal, terracotta cakes, nearby well + bath pavements + cattle bone pit = Congregational sacrificial rituals.
* Domestic fire altars at Banawali, Lothal, Amri, Nageshwar, Rakhigarhi.

* **Rituals & Sacrifices**: Great Bath ceremonial bathing; Kalibangan cake (horned deity + dragged animal = animal sacrifice); Kalibangan seal (2 men with swords over woman = human sacrifice?).
* **Funerary Practices**:
    * Primary extended burials (head north) with modest grave goods (pottery, mirrors, ornaments).
    * Wooden coffins & reed shrouds (Harappa R-37).
    * Symbolic/cenotaph burials without skeletons (Kalibangan, Dholavira).
    * Fractional burials & urn cremations (Mohenjodaro, Harappa).
    * Multiple/couple burials (Lothal, Rakhigarhi).
    * **ABSENCE of Temples** = No temple structures identified at any site.

### The Harappan People
* **Physical Attire & Recreations**:
    * Women = Short cotton/wool skirts, elaborate hairstyles (braids, buns, turbans, fan-frames), heavy jewellery (necklaces, bangles, anklets, belts).
    * Men = Usually nude or dhoti-like lower garment + shoulder cloak/shawl, beards (goatee, combed spread style), buns/braids.
    * Children's Toys = Terracotta balls, rattles, whistles, animal carts with moving parts, spinning tops with copper tips, clay marbles, mini furniture, *pithu*-like disc games, pet dogs with collars.

#### How Healthy Were The Harappans ? (Kennedy, Lukacs, Lovell, Hemphill)
* Harappa Cemetery R-37 (90 skeletons): Low trauma/infection/rickets; common arthritis (knee/spine/neck from load-carrying); high dental caries (43.6% individuals, 6.8% rate = typical farmers), enamel hypoplasia.
* Biological continuity between R-37, Late Harappan Cemetery-H, and modern Punjab populations.

* **Genetic Studies**:
    * **Rakhigarhi female skeleton 16113 DNA** (Vasant Shinde, Niraj Rai, David Reich et al., 2019) = Genetic mix of ancient Iranian & SE Asian hunter-gatherers; **NO immediate steppe pastoralist or Anatolian farmer contribution**. Primary ancestral contribution to modern South Asians.

#### What Exactly Did The Harappans Eat ? (Lipid Residue Studies)
* Kotada Bhadli (Gujarat, 2300–1950 BCE, Kalyan Sekhar Chakraborty et al., 2020) = Potsherd lipid residue analysis proved **dairy fat consumption** from cattle & water buffalo = **1st direct proof of dairying in South Asia**.
* North India sites (Rakhigarhi, Farmana, Alamgirpur, etc., Akshyeta Suryanarayan et al., 2021) = 172 potsherds showed cooking of dairy products, ruminant meat (cattle/goat), non-ruminant fats/plants across rural & urban sites.

### The Ruling Elite
* Weak evidence of military force / warfare compared to Egypt/Mesopotamia, but fortifications (Dholavira, Harappa) exist. Political stability lasted ~700 years.
* **Theories on Political Structure**:
    * **Stuart Piggott & Mortimer Wheeler** = Centralized totalitarian empire ruled by autocratic priest-kings from twin capitals (Harappa & Mohenjodaro), control via granaries & standardized items.
    * **Walter A. Fairservis** = No state/empire; Mohenjodaro = ceremonial center; ruled by elaborate village administration / chiefdom based on religion & tradition.
    * **S. C. Malik** = Chiefdom stage (Elman Service model).
    * **Shereen Ratnagar** = Harappan Empire (state society with centralized control).
    * **Jim Shaffer** = Rejects empire model; homogeneity due to internal trade networks; equality of wealth access across villages & cities; no royal tombs/palaces.
    * **Gregory L. Possehl** = Corporate state ruled by corporate councils rather than kings.
    * **J. M. Kenoyer** = Multiple competing urban elite classes (merchants, ritual specialists, landowners). Square stamp seal animals = **clan totems** (Unicorn = executive merchant/aristocrat clan; rare animals like Bull/Elephant/Tiger = top ruling apex elite).

#### Defining A State
* **Elman R. Service** = Band → Tribe → Chiefdom (hereditary rank, no formal force/bureaucracy) → State (civil law, official government, actual/implied force).
* **Ronald Cohen** = Bureaucracy + central force + ability to counter political fission.
* **Morton H. Fried** = Social stratification + differential resource access; **Pristine states** (indigenous emergence) vs **Secondary states** (modeled/triggered by existing states).
* **Claessen & Skalnik** = Early state (inchoate → typical → transitional).
* **James C. Scott (2017)** = State markers (walls, tax collection, scribes, cereal tax crops); state fragility (disease, deforestation, soil salinization).

#### A Priest-King ?
* 'Priest-King' stone bust from Mohenjodaro (beard, diadem, trefoil robe) & Dholavira seated figure = Unproven as priest or king. Lack of royal palaces/tombs indicates a different type of state.

### The Decline of Urban Life
* **Timeline**: Settled in Mohenjodaro by 2200 BCE, ended ~2000 BCE. Overall de-urbanization c. 1900–1300 BCE.
* **Paces**: Gradual (Mohenjodaro, Dholavira) vs Sudden (Kalibangan, Banawali).
* **Refutation of Aryan Invasion Theory**:
    * *Ramaprasad Chanda (1926), Mortimer Wheeler (1947)* cited Rig Veda *puramdara* (Indra = fort destroyer), *Hariyupiya* (= Harappa), and Mohenjodaro 'slaughtered' skeletons.
    * *Refutation (P. V. Kane, George Dales, B. B. Lal, K. A. R. Kennedy)* = Skeletons belong to different stratigraphical layers (not single massacre event), none on citadel; sterile layer between Mature Harappan and Cemetery-H; skeletal continuity shows no new population influx.
* **Natural Disaster & Environmental Causes**:
    * **Indus Floods & Tectonic Faulting** = M. R. Sahni, Robert Raikes, George Dales (tectonic dam at Sehwan creating lake around Mohenjodaro); H. T. Lambrick (Indus river course shift 30 miles east).
    * **Desiccation of Ghaggar-Hakra River** = Tectonic river capture — Yamuna diverted to Ganga, Sutlej diverted to Indus → Ghaggar dried up → catastrophic abandonment of Cholistan/Ghaggar settlements (M. R. Mughal).
    * **Sea Level / Coastal Uplift** = Rise in Arabian Sea coast → Disrupted coastal trade & ruined river mouths.
    * **Climate Change / Aridity** = Gurdip Singh (pollen study → aridity onset c. 1800 BCE).
    * **Environmental Over-exploitation** = Fairservis (population & cattle growth > carrying capacity → deforestation, soil salinization, over-grazing).
    * **Decline in Trade** = Shereen Ratnagar (decline in Mesopotamian lapis trade) — disputed as trade was minor.

---

### The Significance of The Late Harappan Phase
* **Regional De-urbanization & Ruralization (c. 1900–1300 BCE)**:
    * **Jhukar Culture** (Sindh) = Jhukar, Chanhudaro, Amri; decline in seals/weights, writing on pottery only.
    * **Cemetery H Culture** (West Punjab / Ghaggar-Hakra) = Harappa Mound AB/E upper levels & Cemetery H; painted red jars with peacocks, stars, suns.
    * **Bara Culture & Bhagwanpura** (Eastern Punjab / Haryana) = Overlap with Painted Grey Ware (PGW).
    * **Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) / Copper Hoard Culture** (Ganga-Yamuna Doab) = Bargaon, Ambakheri, Hulas, Sanauli burials, Mandi 10kg gold jewellery hoard.
    * **Rangpur Phase** (Gujarat) = Lothal, Rangpur, Bet Dwarka seal with 3-headed animal, Prabhas Patan.
    * **Daimabad** (Maharashtra) = 20 ha, late Harappan 4 potsherds with Harappan script.
* **Agricultural Diversification & Shift**:
    * Eastward and southward migration of population.
    * Multi-cropping & new summer crops (Millets, Sorghum, Rice at Pirak in Baluchistan, Kachi plain, Gujarat, Hulas in UP - rice, wheat, barley, oats, jowar, finger millet, pulses, almond, walnut, cotton).
* **Survival & Continuity**: Urban features (cities, script, long-distance trade) collapsed, but rural networks expanded, shaping the agricultural baseline for subsequent Iron Age and Second Urbanization cultures.

---

### Conclusions
* South Asia's first urban culture.
* Evolved from proto-urban Early Harappan; de-urbanized into Late Harappan rural networks.
* High integration of agriculture, crafts, trade, and civic planning.
