## Scope Through Development And Development of Anthropology

### Paradox of Anthropology
- **Paradox** = "Oldest subject, yet youngest discipline."
  - **Oldest Subject** → Curiosity about self since origin of humanity → Unsystematic inquiry (lacks scientific methodology or theories).
  - **Youngest Discipline** → Formally established in late 19th Century with scientific methods, fieldwork and academic chairs.
- **Informal  or  Unsystematic Anthropology**:
  - Generalizations or stereotypes made without methodology (e.g., parents, politicians judging human groups).
  - **Aristotle's Definition** → *"Anthropology is a gossip that revolves around men and an anthropologist is a gossiper who talks about himself."*
  - **Herodotus (Father of History)** → Early empirical documentation of customs like **Couvade** (husband imitates pregnant wife's behavior or labor pains to assert paternity or kinship; Unit 2.5).

### Evolutionary Trajectory: Unsystematic → Systematic Anthropology
1. **Classical Antiquity** → Herodotus & Aristotle → Initial observations of human diversity.
2. **European Dark Ages** → Freezing of inquiry → Dogma; all phenomena explained via Church or God.
3. **Age of Enlightenment** → Reason & scientific revolution → Inventions (ship-building, navigation, steam engine).
4. **European Discoveries & Voyages** → Exploration → Documentation of native racial & behavioral traits (cannibalism, infanticide, polygyny, polyandry). Data reached European libraries.
5. **Colonialism & Native Resistance** → Native revolts → Colonial administrators or scholars forced to study indigenous social structures to govern effectively.
6. **Darwinism & Social Darwinism**:
   - **HMS Beagle Expedition** → **Charles Darwin** → *Origin of Species* (1859) → Biological evolution.
   - **Herbert Spencer** → Coined *"Survival of the Fittest"* → **Social Darwinism** (favorable societies survive; unfavorable perish).
7. **Advent of Classical Evolutionism (First Systematic Theory)**:
   - Combined Discovery Data + Colonial Documentation + Darwinism + Social Darwinism.
   - **E.B. Tylor** → First Chair of Anthropology at Oxford → *Primitive Culture* (1871) → Father of Classical Evolutionism.
   - **L.H. Morgan** → *Ancient Society* (1877).
   - **James Frazer** → *The Golden Bough*.
   - **Outcome** → Origin of Anthropology as an academic university discipline.

### Forces Shaping Anthropology
- **Product of Three Forces** → (1) Developments in Natural Sciences + (2) European Discoveries + (3) Colonialism.
- **Debatable Academic Formulations**:
  - *"Child of Darwinism"* → **R.R. Marett**.
  - *"Child of Revolt and Rebellion"* → **Claude Lévi-Strauss**.
  - *"Child of Imperialism  or  Colonialism"* → **Kathleen Gough**.

## T.K. Penniman's 4 Phases of Development (*100 Years of Anthropology*, 1935)

- **T.K. Penniman (1935)** → Traced historical development of anthropology into **4 distinct phases**:

### 1. Formulary Phase (Before 1835)
- **Features** → Collection of unscientific raw data about native populations from voyages, discoveries and early colonial encounters.
- **Key Focus** → Cataloging exotic customs and physical traits without systematic theoretical framework.

### 2. Convergent Phase (1835–1859)
- **Features** → Convergence of diverse disciplines (biology, geology, history) toward evolutionary ideas.
- **Milestones** → Darwin's HMS Beagle voyage completion (1833–1836); Herbert Spencer's early formulations of Social Darwinism; publication of *Origin of Species* (1859).

### 3. Constructive Phase (1859–1900)
- **Features** → Construction of first systematic anthropological theory (**Classical Evolutionism**).
- **Key Works & Scholars**:
  - **E.B. Tylor** → *Primitive Culture* (1871).
  - **L.H. Morgan** → *Ancient Society* (1877).
  - Institutionalization of anthropology in universities (Oxford Chair).

### 4. Critical Phase (1900–1935 & Beyond)
- **Features** → Critical analysis & rejection of Unilineal Evolutionism → Emergence of new theoretical paradigms:
  - **Historical Particularism** (Franz Boas) & **Diffusionism** (Elliot Smith, Perry, Graebner, Schmidt).
  - **Functionalism** (Bronislaw Malinowski, 1922) & **Structural Functionalism** (A.R. Radcliffe-Brown, 1922).
  - **Culture-and-Personality School** (Ruth Benedict, Margaret Mead, Ralph Linton, Abram Kardiner, 1920s–1930s).
- **Post-1935 Expansion** → Neo-evolutionism, Structuralism, Symbolic Anthropology, Postmodernism and **Digital  or  Metaverse Anthropology**.

## Scientific Nature & Modern Scope Expansion

### Anthropology as a Science
- **Science Definition** = Body of empirical, verifiable, testable, replicable and correctable knowledge.
- **Self-Correcting Discipline** → Testing and refuting flawed theories (e.g., Critical Phase replacing Classical Evolutionism).

### Evolution of Human Interaction & Digital Spaces
- **Interaction Continuum** → Physical presence → Letters → Telephony → Video calls → Virtual classrooms → **Metaverse  or  VR spaces** (concerts, VR marriages, virtual sports).
- **Digital Anthropology** → As human interaction shifts to digital or virtual spaces, anthropology expands to study human behavior in meta-spaces using its hallmark **holistic approach**.

### Scope Trajectory: Limited to Expanded

> **EVOLUTION OF ANTHROPOLOGY'S SCOPE**
> 
> - Traditional or Limited Scope
>   - Time & Space → Restricted
>   - Subject Matter → "Primitive" or Tribal Societies
>   - Evolutionary View → "Primitive" → "Savage" → Civilized
>   - Geographic Focus → Remote or Non-Western Societies
> 
>                           ↓
> 
> - Modern or Expanded Scope
>   - Time & Space → All Times & All Places
>   - Subject Matter → Simple → Complex Societies
>   - Cultural Scope → Tribal → Urban → Industrial → Global Societies
>   - New Domains → Digital or Virtual or Meta-Space Human Behaviour

- **Traditional Field Studies**:
  - **Franz Boas** → Central Eskimos.
  - **Bronislaw Malinowski** → Trobriand Islanders (Polynesian or Melanesian Pacific).
  - **L.H. Morgan** → Iroquois Indians (North America).
- **Modern Scope** = **All Aspects** (Boassian Four-Field) + **All Levels of Development** (Simple → Complex → Digital) + **All Times & Places** (Marvin Harris).

## Relationship With Other Disciplines

### Three Broad University Divisions of Knowledge

> **DIVISIONS OF KNOWLEDGE**
> 
> - **Natural Sciences**
> 	- Study Universal Laws
> 	- Explain Natural Phenomena
> 
> - **Social Sciences**
> 	- Study Social Laws or Patterns
> 	- Explain Human Behaviour & Social Relations
> 
> - **Humanities**
> 	- Study Human Experiences & Particularities
> 	- Emphasize Meaning, Values & Interpretation

#### 1. Natural  or  Physical Sciences
- **Focus** → Physics, Chemistry, Biology.
- **Objective** → Discover universal laws governing physical phenomena.
- **Nature of Laws** → Absolute, verifiable, universal (e.g., **Newton's Law of Gravitation** operates everywhere).

#### 2. Social Sciences
- **Focus** → Sociology, Political Science, Economics.
- **Premise** → Social phenomena are governed by social laws just as physical phenomena are governed by physical laws.
- **Objective** → Discover social laws or patterns via empirical research (e.g., **Marriage** is a social generalization fulfilling biological, economic and social functions).

#### 3. Humanities
- **Focus** → History, Literature, Arts, Philosophy, Individual Psychology.
- **Premise** → Human culture consists of unique, non-generalizable particularities.
- **Objective** → Understand specific, unique context (e.g., History of India ≠ History of Europe; Sentinelese culture cannot be generalized to all humans).

### Position of Anthropology: Interdisciplinary & Cross-Disciplinary
- **Anthropology = Natural Science + Social Science + Humanities**.
  - **Natural Science Link** → Human genetics, DNA, skeletal biology, physical evolution.
  - **Social Science Link** → Social structure, kinship, marriage, political organization.
  - **Humanities Link** → Ethnography, folklore, history, particular cultural contexts.
- **Why Anthropology Emerged Alongside Specialized Disciplines**:
  - Specialized subjects are isolated or fragmented (Sociology ignores biology or archaeology; Medicine ignores socio-cultural beliefs).
  - **Anthropology Bridges Gaps** → Provides a **holistic picture** of human existence.

## Anthropology vs. Sociology

| Dimension | Sociology | Anthropology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subject Matter  or  Scope** | Restricted strictly to social aspects | **Wide** → Biological, Socio-cultural, Linguistic, Archaeological |
| **Societies Studied** | Modern, complex, urban, industrial societies | **All societies** → Primitive to modern, simple to complex |
| **Methodology** | Questionnaires, surveys, statistical analysis | **Fieldwork via Participant Observation** (Hallmark) |
| **Approach** | Generalizing (formulates general social laws) | **Descriptive & Qualitative** (explains *why* & contextual relationships) |

- **Inter-disciplinary Interconnections**:
  - **Shared Methodologies & Literature**: L.H. Morgan's evolutionary anthropology directly inspired **Karl Marx** and early sociologists.

## Unique Attributes of Anthropology

> *"Anthropology is the most scientific of all humanities and the most humanistic of all sciences."* - **Eric Wolf**

### 1. Holistic Approach
- **Core Premise** → Integrates all aspects of human life (biological, socio-cultural, linguistic, archaeological).
- **Theoretical Foundations**:
  - **Franz Boas** → Four-Field Approach.
  - **Bronislaw Malinowski** → Concept of culture as an *"integrated whole"*.
- **Biocultural Case Studies**:
  - **Hot & Humid Climatic Adaptation**:
    - *Biological Mechanism* → Sweating, hyperthermia prevention, vasodilation.
    - *Cultural Mechanism* → Electrolyte-rich diet (buttermilk in South or Central India; pani or ORs), loose clothing (Lungi or Dhoti in South India vs full body cloth in dry-hot Rajasthan), desert coolers or AC.
  - **Climatic Symbolism**: "Warm hug" valued in cold Western climates vs Rain celebrated in Indian culture (agriculture lifeline, fertility, poetry).
  - **Disease as Biocultural - Sickle Cell Anemia (SCA)**:
    - *Biological Aspect* → Mutant **HBS** gene vs normal **HBA** gene. Homozygous (HBS-HBS) = Sickle cell disease; Heterozygous Carrier (HBA-HBS) = **Malaria Resistance** (selective survival advantage in endemic zones).
    - *Socio-Cultural Aspect* → Disease proliferates through cultural **endogamy**, cousin marriages and tribal inbreeding in tropical forest ecologies.
  - **Nutrition as Biocultural - Lactose Intolerance**:
    - *Biological Fact* → ~70% of world's adult population is lactose intolerant (lactase enzyme gene turns off post-weaning).
    - *Co-Evolution* → ~30% adult lactase persistence co-evolved over ~10,000 years alongside pastoralism, dairy farming and animal husbandry.
  - **Anatomical Co-evolution**: Human jaw or facial reduction resulting from cooked soft food and stone tool invention.

### 2. Cultural Relativism
- **Pioneered By** → **Franz Boas** (*The Central Eskimo*, *Race, Language and Culture*).
- **Concept** = Ideology that no culture is inherently superior or inferior; every culture is a unique adaptation to its specific environment. Evaluates cultures objectively from within.
- **Source of Objectivity** → Prevents ethnocentric bias in socio-cultural research.
- **Operational Boundary** → Valid up to the threshold of **Human Rights** (does not justify domestic violence, dowry abuse, or human rights violations).

### 3. Participant Observation
- **Pioneered By** → **Bronislaw Malinowski** during his 4-year fieldwork in the Trobriand Islands.
- **Concept** = Immersive long-term fieldwork where the researcher lives among the community, learns the native language and observes daily life firsthand.

### 4. Emic vs. Etic Perspective
- **Emic Perspective** = Insider's viewpoint (native's subjective rationale, beliefs and feelings).
  - Coined by **Kenneth Pike**; strongly advocated by **Bronislaw Malinowski**.
- **Etic Perspective** = Outsider's viewpoint (objective scientific analysis by the researcher).
  - Advocated by **Marvin Harris** and **William Rathje**.
- **Case Study - William Rathje's Tucson Garbology Project**:
  - *Emic Self-Report* → Residents claimed via questionnaires to consume only 2 beer bottles or month.
  - *Etic Garbage Analysis* → Garbage audits revealed actual consumption averaged 10 beer bottles or month.
  - *Significance* → Demonstrates frequent contradictions between Emic claims and Etic realities.

### 5. Diachronic vs. Synchronic Perspective
- **Diachronic Approach** = Study of cultural change over time (historical or evolutionary).
  - Advocates: **Franz Boas**, **A.L. Kroeber**, **E.E. Evans-Pritchard**.
- **Synchronic Approach** = Study of culture at a single given point in time ("here and now").
  - Advocates: **Bronislaw Malinowski**, **A.R. Radcliffe-Brown**.

### Concluding Synthesis
- **Boundary Dissolution** → Interdisciplinary boundaries are disappearing because the core objective of anthropology is the **holistic understanding of human behavior**, transcending artificial academic divides.
