## Applied Biological Anthropology (Relevance of Biological Anthropology)

### 1. Ergonomics & Anthropometry
- **Ergonomic Design** = Product engineering tailored to human anatomical & biomechanical parameters → Maximize comfort + Minimize physical distress/strain.
- **Practical Application** = Ergonomically designed tools (e.g., pens preventing finger calcifications during 6-hour daily writing sessions).

### 2. Demography & Population Dynamics
- **Demographic Winter** = Fall of Total Fertility Rate (TFR) below replacement level.
  - **Indian Context** = All-India average TFR ≈ **2.0** vs **2.1** ideal replacement rate → Concerns regarding future population decline.
- **Causal Chain of Population Decline**:
  - **TFR < 2.1** → **Population Growth ↓** → Market potential loss + Underutilized resources → **Population Aging ↑** → Dependent elderly ratio ↑ + Tax-paying youth ratio ↓ → **Double burden on State resources**.
- **Gerontology & Geriatrics**:
  - **Gerontology** = Multidisciplinary study of aging (biological, psychological, social changes).
  - **Geriatrics** = Medical branch dealing with healthcare & clinical needs of elderly population.

### 3. Fertility & Contraception
- **Biocultural Nature of Fertility** = Biological factors (hormonal balance, genetics) + Cultural/behavioral factors (marriage age, sex taboos, customs).
- **Role of Anthropologists vs Doctors**:
  - **Medical Doctor** → Focuses primarily on biological/clinical mechanisms.
  - **Anthropologist** → Integrates biological parameters with cultural/ethnographic realities.
- **Contraceptive Persuasion** = Anthropologists build rapport & trust → Persuade indigenous/tribal groups to adopt family planning without cultural coercion.

### 4. Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) & Delivery Customs
- **High IMR → Compensation Mechanism** → High child mortality → Parents reproduce more to ensure offspring survival → Total birth rate ↑.
- **Harmful / Unsanitary Traditional Delivery Customs**:
  - **Self-Delivery** = Women perform labor unassisted (hanging legs upside and pushing).
  - **Unsterilized Umbilical Cord Cutting** = Crude iron implements, unsterilized knives, sharpened bamboo sticks, or sharpened stones → Infection risk ↑ for mother & neonate.
  - **Pre-lacteal Feeding** = Customary dropping of beer/alcohol into neonate's mouth prior to colostrum/mother's milk → Infant toxicity.

#### Case Study: Steven Beckerman – Polyandry & Infant Survival Among Bari Tribe
- **Anthropologist** = **Steven Beckerman**.
- **Tribe & Location** = **Bari tribe**, Amazonian rainforest, Venezuela.
- **Marriage System** = **Polyandry** (Multiple husbands per woman).
- **Native Cultural Belief** = A Bari child cannot be created by a single male insemination → Requires **multiple inseminations from multiple men** to produce a *healthy Bari child*.
- **Ethnographic Findings & Statistics**:
  - Documented women with single vs. multiple husbands.
  - **Finding** = Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) was **significantly lower** in families with **multiple husbands** (2–3 husbands).
- **Causal Mechanics**:
  - **Harsh Amazonian Terrain** → **Multiple Husbands** → Food security ↑ + Hunting protection ↑ + Support for pregnant/nursing mother ↑ → **Infant Mortality ↓**.
- **Anthropological Significance** = Demonstrates that seemingly superstitious beliefs can serve adaptive biological survival functions → Enables culturally sensitive health interventions.

## Applied Socio-Cultural Anthropology (Relevance of Socio-Cultural Anthropology)

### 1. Promotion of Peace, Tolerance & Acceptance
- **Mechanism** = Employs **Cultural Relativism** (Franz Boas) → Eliminates ethnocentric bias → Fosters mutual respect across caste, religious and ethnic groups → Conflict reduction → Peace & social harmony.

### 2. Sustainable Development
- **Core Problem** = Modern development causes ecological destruction due to consumerism, hyper-reality, spectacle and alienation from nature.
- **Role of Anthropology** = Studies traditional, time-tested sustainable practices of simple/tribal societies.

#### Case Study 1: Triloknath Pandit – Sentinelese Model
- **Anthropologist** = **Triloknath Pandit**.
- **Group** = **Sentinelese** (Andaman Islands).
- **Significance** = Sentinelese live in complete ecological equilibrium → Global mirror/case study for sustainable living.

#### Case Study 2: L.P. Vidyarthi – Nature-Man-Spirit Complex (Malair Tribe)
- **Anthropologist** = **L.P. Vidyarthi**.
- **Tribe & Region** = **Malair (Maler Paharia)**, Rajmahal Hills, Jharkhand (erstwhile Bihar).
- **Concept** = **Nature-Man-Spirit Complex (N-M-S Complex)**.
- **Three Interconnected Components**:
  1. **Nature** = Forest/Jungle → Provides food, timber for huts, herbal medicine and land for *Khallu* (shifting agriculture).
  2. **Man** = Kinship, family and community networks.
  3. **Spirit** = Deities called **Gossians** (Supreme spirits residing in the forest).
- **Relocation Event & Consequence**:
  - Government relocated Malair tribals to modern houses in the plains → Tribals believed *Gossians* were angered → Illness, mortality ↑ → Tribals abandoned modern houses & returned to forest.
- **Policy Lesson** = Development policies must align with indigenous eco-cultural matrices rather than imposing alien templates.

### 3. Protection of Women's Rights & Gender Dynamics

#### Subsistence Contribution Hypothesis
- **Principle** = Status of women is directly linked to their contribution to primary subsistence activities.
  - **High Subsistence Contribution** → **Higher Socio-Economic Status**.

#### Case Study: Patricia Draper – !Kung San Gender Dynamics
- **Anthropologist** = **Patricia Draper**.
- **Group** = **!Kung San (!Kung Bushmen)**, Kalahari Desert, Southern Africa.
- **Comparative Findings**:
  - **Nomadic Foraging Phase** = Men hunt + Women gather → Equal subsistence contribution → **Equal status between men and women**.
  - **Sedentary Wage-Earning Phase** = Market forces/sedentarization → Men became industrial wage-earners + Women became housewives → Female mobility restricted → **Female status degraded relative to men**.

#### Impact of Agriculture & Surplus on Gender Status
- **Foraging (Hunter-Gatherer)** → Perishable food → No surplus/storage → Minimal stratification → High/equal female status.
- **Agriculture** → Grain storage → Surplus accumulation → Male-dominated heavy labor → Female subsistence contribution perceived as low → **Polygyny rises** (more wives = more farm labor = higher surplus/power) → **Female status declines**.

#### Theories on Gender Division of Labor
1. **Strength Theory** = Men possess higher muscle mass/testosterone for heavy tasks; women weak (Rebutted by: Tchambuli female fishing, Tibet steep-slope water carrying by women, Agta female hunting).
2. **Expandability Theory** = Male lives are reproduction-expandable (1 fertile male can impregnate multiple females; male loss does not jeopardize group survival) → Men assigned high-risk tasks (hunting, warfare).
3. **Childcare Compatibility Theory** = Nursing/lactation requires mothers to stay near infants → Foraging/hunting with infants poses hazards → Division of labor selected by evolutionary adaptation for offspring survival.

#### Metaphor: Leela Dube – "Earth and Seed" Metaphor
- **Anthropologist** = **Leela Dube**.
- **Concept** = **Earth and Seed Metaphor** (*Beej aur Dharti*).
- **Metaphorical Mapping**:
  - **Mother or Woman** = **Earth** (Passive nurturer, field/vessel).
  - **Father or Man** = **Seed** (Active agent, carrier of lineage & genealogy).
- **Social Consequence** = Lineage, family trees and property traced exclusively through male line → Daughters viewed as seeds of *other* families (*Paraya Dhan*).

#### Matrilineal Counter-Examples in India
- **Khasi (Meghalaya)** = Matrilineal inheritance (mother to daughter), but maternal uncles retain political authority.
- **Nayar (Nair) of Kerala – Kathleen Gough's Ethnography (1950s–1960s)**:
  - **Anthropologist** = **Kathleen Gough**.
  - **Residential Unit** = **Tarawad** (Female-headed matrilineal household).
  - **Tarawad Structure** = Headed by eldest female → Contains sisters, daughters, brothers, sons. **No co-resident husbands**.
  - **Ritual Marriage** = **Tali-kettu-Kalyanam** ceremony.
    - Puberty/menstruation ritual → Gold ornament (*Tali*) tied around girl's neck by a *Ritual Husband*.
    - Ritual husband leaves without consummating marriage.
  - **Visiting Husband System (Sambandham)**:
    - Mature Nayar woman forms relationships with multiple lovers (*Sambandham*).
    - Lovers must belong to equal or higher caste (Nayar warriors or Nambudiri Brahmins).
    - Lovers visit *Tarawad* at night; no co-habitation or financial obligation. Children belong exclusively to mother's *Tarawad*.
- **Moplah Muslims of Lakshadweep (Leela Dube)**:
  - Muslim community adhering to Islamic faith, yet maintaining **Matrilineal Tarawad** system due to regional historical influence from Kerala/Malabar.

### 4. Tribal Development & Policy Contributions

- **Institutional Framework** = Tribal Research Institutes (TRIs), Planning Bodies.
- **Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP)** = Formulated during 5th Five-Year Plan based on recommendations of **L.P. Vidyarthi** and **S.C. Dube**.
- **PESA Act 1996** (Panchayats Extension to Scheduled Areas Act) = Formulated on recommendations of **Dileep Singh Bhuria Committee**.
- **PVTGs (Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups)**:
  - **Total Groups** = **75 PVTGs** in India.
  - **Origin** = Identified based on **U.N. Dhebar Commission (1960–61)** recommendations.
  - **Identification Criteria** = Pre-agricultural technology, stagnant/declining population, extremely low literacy, high economic backwardness.
  - **PM-JANMAN Mission** = Targeted government scheme for holistic PVTG protection.
- **National Forest Policy 1988**:
  - Based on recommendations of **B.K. Roy Burman**.
  - **Paradigm Shift** = Forest bureaucracy mindset changed from viewing tribals as "forest exploiters" to "forest developers/protectors".
  - **Outcome** = Introduced **Joint Forest Management (JFM)** (Tribals + Forest Department collaboration).

#### Legal & Conceptual Tribal Classifications
- **Tribe** = Socio-cultural category (community defined by common territory, language, culture, lineage).
- **Scheduled Tribe (ST)** = Constitutional/administrative category under **Article 342** (Presidential notification) and **Article 366(25)** (Definitions).
  - 2011 Census = 704 Scheduled Tribes (plus recent additions like Hattis of Himachal Pradesh).
- **Denotified Tribes (DNTs)** = Communities previously notified as "Criminal Tribes" under British **Criminal Tribes Act 1871** ("hereditary criminals" label) → Denotified post-Independence.
- **Indigenous People (ILO Convention 169 vs Indian Position)**:
  - **ILO Criteria** = Historical continuity (pre-colonial presence), distinct culture, backwardness.
  - **Indian Government Stand (Refusal to Ratify ILO Convention)**:
    1. *Historical Continuity* = Applies to all Indians (all lived pre-colonially).
    2. *High Civilizational Integration* = **K.S. Singh (ASI People of India Project)** showed **85%–93% cultural trait overlap** between tribes and castes.
    3. *Internal Safeguards* = Robust constitutional protections (Schedule 5, Schedule 6, Art 342, electoral/educational quotas).

### 5. Rural Development Methodologies

- **RRA (Rapid Rural Appraisal)** = Quick, expert-led top-down assessment of village assets, disaster damage (floods, epidemics) and needs for rapid policy response.
- **PRA (Participatory Rural Appraisal)** = Anthropologist co-resides with villagers, co-evaluates local assets/problems and drafts a joint policy blueprint.
- **PLA (Participatory Learning and Action)**:
  - Concept derived from **Action Anthropology** (Anthropologist as a **catalyst / facilitator**).
  - **Example** = Anthropologist smokes hookah with village elders near a polluted pond → Triggers reflection ("Don't you think dirty water causes typhoid?") → Villagers self-identify solutions (sand/gravel filtration) and execute action independently with pride.

## Applied Linguistic Anthropology

### 1. Conservation of Endangered or Dying Languages

#### Statistics & Survey (Ganesh Devy – PLSI 2010)
- **Survey** = **People's Linguistic Survey of India (PLSI, 2010)** by **Ganesh Devy**.
- **Documented Languages** = **780 languages**.
- **Dying / Vulnerable Languages** = **600 out of 780 languages**.
- **Extinct Languages** = **> 200 languages lost since Independence**.

#### UNESCO Standard
- **Endangered Language** = Any language spoken by **< 10,000 speakers**.

#### Recent Language Extinctions in India
- **Bo Language** (Andaman Islands) → Extinct in **2010** (death of last speaker, Boa Sr.).
- **Maji Language** (Sikkim) → Extinct in **2015**.

#### Causes of Language Death
1. **Lack of Cultural Pride & Westernization** → Parents avoiding native dialects at home (e.g., Haryanvi/Hindi shift to English).
2. **Economic / Employment Pressure** → Urban migration forces adoption of dominant regional lingua franca (e.g., tribal migrants in metros).
3. **Lack of Vernacular Education** → Absence of mother-tongue instruction in schools.
4. **Displacement** → Industrial/infrastructure displacement breaking language communities.

#### Consequence of Language Death
- **Language Death = Culture Death = Knowledge Death** → Permanent loss of indigenous knowledge systems (ethno-medicine, ecological knowledge, oral literature).

#### Preservation Interventions
- **Adivani App** = Government translation & communication application for tribal languages.
- Digital language archiving, AI chatbots and Large Language Models (LLMs) for language preservation.

### 2. Mitigation of Linguistic Conflicts

- **Socio-Political Reality** = Language is a potent identity anchor → Triggers major geopolitical events (1971 Bangladesh Liberation War, 1953 Andhra Pradesh creation, 1960 Gujarat/Maharashtra split, 1966 Punjab/Haryana split, anti-Hindi imposition protests).

#### Theoretical Foundations
- **Franz Boas – Linguistic Relativity**:
  - Extension of Cultural Relativism.
  - **Core Thesis** = Every language is unique; no language is superior or inferior.
  - **Principle** = **Language shapes the worldview of its speakers**.
- **Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis**:
  - Formulated by **Edward Sapir** & **Benjamin Lee Whorf**.
  - **Core Thesis** = **Structure of language shapes cognition/thought, which in turn influences human behavior**.
  - **Whorf's Chemical Plant Case Study**:
    - Factory workers smoked near gas cylinders labeled *"Inflammable"*.
    - **Cognitive Failure** = In English, prefix *"in-"* usually denotes negation (*invalid*, *inability*). Workers falsely cognized *"inflammable"* as *"non-flammable"* → Threw lighted cigarettes → Mass explosion.
    - **Conclusion** = Linguistic structure directly governs cognitive perception and behavioral action.

#### Policy Application
- Language imposition disregards linguistic relativity → Triggers resistance & socio-political turmoil.
- Applied linguistic anthropology guides non-coercive, inclusive language policies (e.g., balanced implementation of the Three-Language Formula).
