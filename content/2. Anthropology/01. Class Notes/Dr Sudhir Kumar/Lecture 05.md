## Applied Archaeological Anthropology

### Core Concept & Scope
- **Definition** → Study of human past through **material remains** left behind by ancestors.
- **Material Remains Categories**:
  1. **Biological  or  Skeletal Remains** → Fossilized bones (Hominins & Fauna).
  2. **Artifactual Remains** → Tools (Stone, Bone, Ivory, Wood).
  3. **Floral Remains** → Pollen grains, charcoal, plant fibers, seeds.
  4. **Geological  or  Mineral Remains** → Rock types, soil layers, sediment profiles (**Geoarchaeology**).

### Evolutionary Chronology & Milestones
- **4.5 Billion Yrs Ago** → Earth formation.
- **3.8 Billion Yrs Ago** → First life origin.
- **65 Million Yrs Ago** → Appearance of **LUCA (Last Unknown Common Ancestor of Primates)**.
- **6 Million Yrs Ago** → Hominin-Chimpanzee lineage divergence (Bipedal ancestors).
- **500,000 – 300,000 Yrs Ago** → Neanderthal emergence (**Svante Pääbo** → 2022 Nobel Prize in Medicine for ancient Neanderthal DNA extraction & paleogenomics).
- **Early Tool Evidence** → **Olduvai Gorge (Tanzania)** → Earliest Oldowan stone tools (~2.6 mya).

### Major Applications of Archaeological Anthropology

#### 1. Conservation & Preservation of Archaeological Heritage
- Restoration of historic monuments & sites (e.g., **Humayun's Tomb** restored by **Aga Khan Trust for Culture**; sites protected by **Archaeological Survey of India - ASI**).

#### 2. Museology
- **Definition** → Scientific curation, classification, & spatial arrangement of museum specimens.
- **Function** → Public heritage education & artifact conservation (e.g., **National Museum New Delhi**, **Tribal Museum Port Blair**).

#### 3. Public Archaeology
- Community engagement by professional archaeologists to foster **heritage awareness, educational outreach and site protection**.

#### 4. Bioarchaeology
- **Definition** → Interdisciplinary study of biological remains (bones, teeth, pollen, plant materials) from archaeological contexts.
- **Key Analytical Dimensions**:
  - **Faunal Remains → Past Diet**: Identification of hunting-gathering targets (e.g., Nilgai, deer, fish bone deposits).
  - **Hominin Skeletal Analysis**:
    - **Nutritional Deficiencies** → Enamel hypoplasia & bone lesions evidencing nutritional decline during **Hunter-Gatherer → Agricultural transition**.
    - **Pathologies & Traumas** → Evidence of violence vs healing.
    - **Social Care & Altruism**:
      - **Shanidar Cave (Iraq)** → Neanderthal fossil showing severe physical trauma & blindness that survived years past injury → Proof of **kin care, altruism & social support**.
      - **La Chapelle-aux-Saints (France)** → Toothless Neanderthal ("Old Man") → Evidenced survival through **pre-chewed food provided by group members**.
    - **Burial Rituals & Respect for Dead**:
      - **Shanidar Cave Pollen Analysis** → High concentration of floral pollen grains around burials → Evidences placement of **flowers on deathbed  or  ritual burial**.
      - **Mortuary  or  Ritual Cannibalism** → Cut marks on hominin bones indicating ritual consumption.

#### Bioarchaeology Case Study: Debunking The "Aryan Invasion Theory"
- **Colonial Theory (Mortimer Wheeler, 1947)**:
  - **Premise** → 37 skeletal remains at **Mohenjo-daro** (Indus Valley Civilization) = Victims of violent massacre by invading foreign **Aryans** (citing Rigvedic *Purandara* references).
- **Bioarchaeological Re-examination**:
  - **Finding** → Stratigraphic & radiometric analysis proved 37 skeletons belonged to **different chronological time strata or periods**.
  - **Outcome** → Disproved Wheeler's Aryan massacre theory → Proved site abandonment was due to ecological or climate shifts, not foreign invasion.
- **Recent Genetic Support (David Reich & Lalji Singh)**:
  - **Publication** → *Who We Are and How We Got Here* (David Reich, Harvard).
  - **Co-investigator** → **Dr. Lalji Singh** (Father of Indian DNA Fingerprinting  or  CCMB Hyderabad).
  - **Genetic Findings** → Caste-based **endogamy in India began ~1900 years ago** (Gupta Period shift), disproving sudden ancient racial replacement.

#### 5. Forensic Archaeology
- **Definition** → Application of archaeological & osteological principles to legal or criminal investigations across historical timeframes.
- **Case Study: Taung Child (*Australopithecus africanus*)**:
  - **Discovery** → 1924 by **Raymond Dart** at Taung Cave, South Africa (Fossil ~2.5 mya; 5-year-old hominin child).
  - **Forensic Question** → Cause of death (Hominin violence vs animal predation?).
  - **Forensic Analysis** → Identified two distinct puncture holes on top of the cranium matching raptor talon dimensions → Proved **killed by a bird of prey (prehistoric eagle)**.

## Development of Anthropology

### The Paradox
- **"Anthropology is the oldest subject, yet the youngest discipline."**
  - **Oldest Subject** → Driven by innate human **self-curiosity** since primeval origins.
  - **Youngest Discipline** → Formally established as a **systematic academic discipline** in the **late 19th century**.

### Historical Timeline of Anthropological Thought

#### 1. Classical Antiquity (5th – 3rd Century BC)
- **Herodotus (5th C. BC)** → *"Father of History"*:
  - Travelled Mediterranean Greek colonies; documented racial & cultural variations.
  - **Ethnographic Case Study: Couvade Custom**:
    - Custom where husband imitates pregnant wife’s behavior during labor (wears her clothes or jewelry, lies in bed, experiences sympathetic labor pains, observes food taboos).
    - **Anthropological Interpretation** → Indirect assertion of **paternity** & shift of authority in family.
    - Investigated natural head of family (**Patriarch  or  Paterfamilias** in Greco-Roman culture).
- **Aristotle (3rd C. BC)** → *"Father of Anthropology"*:
  - **First Definition of Anthropology** → *"Anthropology is a gossip that revolves around men and an anthropologist is a gossiper who talks about himself."*
  - Focus on human self-inquiry; lacked systematic, empirical field methodologies.

#### 2. The European Dark Ages (5th – 15th Century AD)
- Dominated by religious dogma & Church theology.
- Stifled empirical scientific inquiry (e.g., Bishop Ussher's Biblical calculation of Earth creation in 4004 BC).

#### 3. Renaissance, Enlightenment & Scientific Revolution (17th – 18th Century)
- Emergence of **reason, rationality, & empirical science**.
- Technological breakthroughs in **navigation, shipbuilding, steam power, & cartography**.
- **Maritime Voyages & Global Exploration** → HMS Beagle expedition (1831–1836) carrying **Charles Darwin**.

#### 4. Darwinism & Social Darwinism (19th Century)
- **Charles Darwin** → *Origin of Species* (1859) → **Biological Evolution Principles**:
  1. **Over-reproduction** → Offspring produced > Survival capacity.
  2. **Struggle for Existence** → 3 Levels: **Intraspecific** (within species), **Interspecific** (between species), **Environmental** (against climate or hazards).
  3. **Variations** → Favorable vs Unfavorable traits.
  4. **Natural Selection & Differential Reproduction** → Favorable traits passed to next generation.
  5. **Origin of New Species** → Accumulation of favorable variations over generations.
- **Herbert Spencer** → Coined phrase *"Survival of the Fittest"* (misattributed to Darwin).
- **Social Darwinism** → Misapplied biological evolution to societies:
  - **Fit Societies** = Civilized European nations (destined to survive or rule).
  - **Unfit Societies** = Native  or  Tribal cultures (destined to perish  or  be colonized).

#### 5. Imperialism, Colonialism & Classical Evolutionism (Late 19th Century)
- Colonial expansion → Resistance or rebellions by native populations → Necessity for colonial powers to study native customs for governance.
- Colonial administrators & voyagers accumulated massive cultural data in European libraries.
- Armchair scholars (**E.B. Tylor** & **L.H. Morgan**) analyzed comparative data → Formulated **Classical Evolutionism** (First formal theory in Anthropology) → **Birth of Systematic Anthropology**.

### Academic Perspectives on The Origin of Anthropology

- **R.R. Marett** (Student of E.B. Tylor at Oxford) → *"Anthropology is a child of Darwinism."*
- **Claude Lévi-Strauss** → *"Anthropology is a child of revolt and rebellion."*
- **Kathleen Gough** → *"Anthropology is a child of imperialism."*
- **Synthesis Question  or  Perspective** → *"Anthropology is a product of developments in natural sciences, European discovery and colonialism."*

### Answer Writing
- **Question**: *"Anthropology is a child of Darwinism. Discuss."* (10 Marks  or  150 Words)
- **Structure**:
  1. **Introduction**: Attribute quote to **R.R. Marett**; state context (Darwinian impact on 19th C. thought).
  2. **Arguments in Favor**:
     - Evolutionary framework inspired **Classical Evolutionists** (Tylor, Morgan).
     - Biological evolution (*Descent of Man*) laid foundation for **Biological Anthropology**.
  3. **Arguments Against**:
     - Subject origins trace back to **Herodotus & Aristotle** (Ancient Greece).
     - Broader socio-historical catalysts: **Industrial Revolution, Maritime Discoveries, Colonial Administration, Imperialism** (Kathleen Gough) and **Revolts** (Lévi-Strauss).
  4. **Conclusion**: Partially true - while Darwinism provided the conceptual framework for evolutionism, anthropology was born from a confluence of scientific, colonial and historical forces; Darwinism specifically made anthropology **systematic**.

## Primate Dental Formulae & Taxonomy

- **Dental Formula Architecture** → Expressed per quadrant (Incisors : Canines : Premolars : Molars):
  - **Humans (Adults)** → **2 : 1 : 2 : 3** (32 total teeth).
  - **Apes (Chimpanzee, Gorilla, Orangutan, Gibbon)** → **2 : 1 : 2 : 3**.
  - **Old World Monkeys (Catarrhini - Asia or Africa)** → **2 : 1 : 2 : 3**.
  - **New World Monkeys (Platyrrhini - Americas)** → **2 : 1 : 3 : 3**.
  - **Prosimians (Lemurs, Lorises)** → **2 : 1 : 3 : 3**.
- **Dietary & Anatomical Diagnostic Value**:
  - Tooth morphology (cusp patterns, enamel thickness) & dental arch shape (parabolic vs U-shaped) provide far greater dietary insight than dental formula alone.
  - **Monkeys vs Apes**: Monkeys possess tails & smaller bodies; Apes lack tails, have larger brain or body size, & complex shoulder girdles for brachiation.
