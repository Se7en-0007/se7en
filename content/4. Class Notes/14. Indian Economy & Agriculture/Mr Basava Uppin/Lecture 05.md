## Newspaper Reading & Current Affairs

### Front Page & Business Page Analysis (*The Hindu*)
* **Inflation Trends**: CPI Inflation hit 8-year low at **~1.5%**.
* **Inflation Targeting Framework**:
  * **Target Rate** = **4%** ideal inflation rate (with flexible tolerance band of **2% to 6%**, i.e., 4% ± 2%).
  * **Zero Inflation Pitfall** = Zero inflation indicates economic stagnation/non-growth.
* **Tax vs. Cess**:
  * **Tax** (e.g. Income Tax, Corporate Tax, GST) = Unearmarked general revenue → Can be used for any government expenditure.
  * **Cess** = Earmarked levy imposed for a specific purpose/cause.
  * **Health & Education Cess** = **4%** levied on payable income tax (NOT on total taxable income).
  * **Road & Infrastructure Cess** = Imposed on purchase of petrol and diesel per liter.
* **Dedicated Funds for Cess Collections**:
  * **Education Funds**: Primary (*Prathamik Shiksha Kosh*), Secondary (*Madhyamik Shiksha Kosh*), Higher (*Uchhatar Shiksha Kosh*).
  * **Health Fund**: *Swasthya Suraksha Nidhi*.
* **CAG Report Critique**: Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) reported government failure to transfer collected cess revenue into dedicated earmarked funds.
* **Key Terms for Taxation**: Surcharge vs. Cess.

## Short-Term Government Borrowings from RBI: Ways and Means Advances (WMA)

### Legal & Operational Framework
* **Governing Statute** = **FRBM Act, 2003**.
* **Eligibility** = BOTH **Central Government** and **State Governments** (unlike short-term market borrowings which are Center-only).
* **Core Purpose** = To address temporary cash balance mismatches within a financial year.
* **Duration** = **90 Days** (< 1 Year).
* **Rate of Interest** = **Repo Rate** (identical to rate at which commercial banks borrow short-term liquidity from RBI).

## RBI Retail Direct Scheme & Market Mechanics

### Core Concepts: Financial Markets & Demat Account
* **Primary Market** = Financial instruments purchased directly from the issuer (Issuer → Investor X).
* **Secondary Market** = Financial instruments traded between investors (Investor X → Investor Y via BSE, NSE, NDS-OM).
* **Demat Account** = Electronic account required to hold financial assets (shares, bonds, G-Secs); opened via banks (ICICI, HDFC) or discount brokers (Zerodha, Groww, Angel One).

### Prelims Pointers on Retail Direct Scheme
* **Launch Year** = **2021**.
* **Eligible Investors** = All retail individual investors (both **Resident** and **Non-Resident** Indians).
* **Account Type** = **Retail Direct Gilt Account** opened directly with RBI.
* **Maintenance & Opening Fee** = **₹0** (Free of cost).
* **Eligible Instruments**:
  1. Dated Bonds / Securities
  2. State Development Loans (SDLs)
  3. Treasury Bills (T-Bills)
  4. Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB)
  5. Sovereign Green Bonds
  6. Floating Rate Savings Bonds
* **Investment Limits**: Minimum = **₹10,000**; Maximum = Differs by instrument.
* **Market Coverage** = Operates in BOTH **Primary Market** (direct purchase from RBI) and **Secondary Market** (trading via Stock Exchanges, NDS-OM and Retail Direct Platform).

## Role of RBI as Banker to Banks

### Key Functions
1. **Account Maintenance** = Mandates all commercial banks to open accounts with RBI.
2. **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** = Requires banks to hold minimum cash reserves with RBI.
3. **Liquidity / Loans** = Lends short-term capital to banks at **Repo Rate**.
4. **Inter-Bank Payment & Settlement** = Settles inter-bank transactions via **RTGS** and **NEFT** (both platforms set up and owned by RBI).
5. **Monetary Advice** = Advises banks on credit & monetary parameters.
6. **Lender of Last Resort** = Provides emergency liquidity to solvent banks facing temporary liquidity crises.

## Lender of Last Resort & Asset-Liability Mismatch (ALM)

### Bank Balance Sheet Structural Mismatch

![[Pasted image 20260928183241.png|500]]

* **Asset-Liability Mismatch (ALM)** = Classical banking issue where short-term liabilities (deposits) are locked in long-term illiquid assets (housing or infrastructure loans).
* **Inter-Bank Borrowing** = Primary mechanism to resolve daily ALM liquidity gaps.
* **Bank Run** = Panic situation where depositors simultaneously rush to withdraw cash deposits due to loss of confidence.
* **Lender of Last Resort Mechanism** = When a bank faces ALM/bank run and no other bank/market entity extends loans, RBI steps in to supply liquidity.
* **Rationale for RBI Intervention** = To prevent **contagion effect / domino collapse** of the banking system and protect public trust.
* *Note: The term "Bankrupt" applies to insolvent borrowers, NOT banks.*

## Role of RBI as Regulator

### 1. Regulation of Banks: Public Sector Banks (PSBs) vs. Private Sector Banks

#### History of Bank Nationalization
* **Pre-1947**: Private sector dominance → Focused on urban areas and corporate class (**Class Banking**).
* **1955**: Nationalization of Imperial Bank of India → **State Bank of India (SBI)** via *SBI Act, 1955*.
* **1969**: Nationalization of **14 major private banks** via *Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1970*.
* **1980**: Nationalization of **6 private banks** via *Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Act, 1980*.
* **Objective** = Shift from Class Banking to **Mass Banking** (rural penetration, agricultural credit).
* **Present Count** = **12 Public Sector Banks** (reduced via mergers, e.g. Vijaya Bank + Dena Bank merged into Bank of Baroda).

#### Dual Regulation of Public Sector Banks (PSBs)
* **Dual Regulators** = **RBI** (under *Banking Regulation Act, 1949*) + **Central Government** (under *SBI Act 1955* / *Bank Nationalization Acts 1970 & 1980*).
* **Limited Powers of RBI over PSBs** (BR Act provisions NOT applicable to PSBs to prevent duplication):
  1. **No RBI Licensing Required** = PSBs created directly by Parliament.
  2. **No Power over Board Qualifications** = Government sets qualifications for Board of Directors/Chairperson.
  3. **No Power to Remove Directors** = RBI cannot remove/supersede Board of Directors of PSBs (only Govt can).
  4. **No Power to Force Mergers** = RBI cannot force compulsory merger of PSBs (Govt decides).
* **Private Sector Banks Regulation** = RBI is the **SOLE regulator** under *BR Act, 1949* (has full powers to issue licenses, set board qualifications, remove corrupt management and force compulsory mergers, e.g. Lakshmi Vilas Bank merged into DBS Bank).
* **NPA / Fraud Concentration** = Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) and major frauds heavily concentrated in PSBs (e.g. Nirav Modi PNB scam, Vijay Mallya loans) due to management governance constraints.

### 2. Regulation of Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)
* **Concept** = Known as **Shadow Banks** (perform bank-like lending functions without holding a banking license).
* **Key Distinction from Banks** = Cannot accept **demand deposits** (savings/current accounts); can only accept fixed term deposits (and only deposit-taking NBFCs).
* **Statute & Licensing** = Mandatory license from RBI under **RBI Act, 1934** (Banks are under *BR Act, 1949*).
* **Examples** = Muthoot Finance, Manappuram Gold Loan, Bajaj Finance.

### 3. Regulation of Credit Information Companies (CICs)
* **Function** = Collect credit history/repayment records from banks/NBFCs and calculate individual **credit scores**.
* **Mandatory Reporting** = RBI directs ALL banks and NBFCs to mandatorily share borrower data with CICs.
* **Governing Statute** = **Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005**.
* **Exhaustive List of 4 Registered CICs in India**:
  1. **CIBIL** (Credit Information Bureau India Limited - TransUnion)
  2. **Equifax**
  3. **Experian**
  4. **CRIF High Mark**
* **Ownership Constraint** = **NONE are government-owned**; ALL 4 are privately owned by foreign entities.
* **Distinction: CICs vs. Credit Rating Agencies (CRAs)**:

| Parameter | Credit Information Companies (CICs) | Credit Rating Agencies (CRAs) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Scope** | Individual retail loan histories & credit scores | Corporate and Sovereign Bond ratings |
| **Regulator** | **RBI** (*CIC Regulation Act, 2005*) | **SEBI** (Securities and Exchange Board of India) |
| **Key Examples** | CIBIL, Equifax, Experian, CRIF High Mark | CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, Moody's, Fitch, S&P |

### 4. Regulation of Authorized Dealers
* **Definition** = Entities/shops/banks licensed by RBI under *FEMA* to deal in foreign exchange (Forex conversion).

### 5. Regulation of Primary Dealers (PDs)
* **Definition** = Entities licensed by RBI under *RBI Act, 1934* to underwrite/purchase G-Secs in primary market auctions via E-Kuber.

## Developmental Role of RBI

### 1. Priority Sector Lending (PSL)
* **Objective** = Mandatory credit allocation to vulnerable, high-risk, or socially vital sectors neglected by commercial banks.
* **Mandatory PSL Targets**:
  * **Most Commercial Banks** = **40%** of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC).
  * **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) & Small Finance Banks (SFBs)** = **75%** of ANBC.

#### Exhaustive List of 8 Priority Sectors
1. **Agriculture**
2. **MSMEs** (Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises)
3. **Education**
4. **Housing**
5. **Renewable Energy**
6. **Social Infrastructure** (Schools, hospitals, sanitation, drinking water, irrigation)
7. **Startups**
8. **Loans to Poor and Vulnerable Sections** (Women, SC/ST, small/marginal farmers)

### 2. Lead Bank Scheme (1969)
* **Committee Recommendation** = **Nariman Committee (1969)**.
* **Mechanism** = RBI assigns one designated commercial bank (public or private) as the **Lead Bank** for every district in India.
* **Role** = Lead Bank acts as a coordinator for all financial institutions in the district to drive credit creation, branch expansion and financial inclusion.

### 3. Service Area Approach (1989)
* **Launch Year** = **1989** (as a sub-component/refinement of Lead Bank Scheme).
* **Core Distinction** = Focuses on planned development of specific **underdeveloped blocks / clusters of villages** within a district rather than the district as a whole.
