### Lok Sabha Bill On Banning Online Money Gaming (Real Money Gaming)
* **Definition of Online Money Gaming**: Applications where users pay real money to test skill or try luck in anticipation of winning monetary returns (e.g. **Rummy Circle**, **Dream11**, **My11Circle**).
* **Allowed vs. Banned Gaming Classification**:
  * **Allowed** = Standard online/video games (e.g. **FIFA**, **Cricket video games**) where users pay a one-time fee/download cost for entertainment (no betting/monetary staking involved).
  * **Banned** = Real-money gaming apps where users bet money to win financial returns.

### Core Rationale For Ban (Government Standpoint)
1. **Money Laundering Channel**: Unregulated gaming apps used as a conduit to convert unaccounted black money into white money.
2. **Adverse Socio-Economic Impact**:
   * **Massive Financial Drain**: From Jan–July 2025, Indian citizens spent **~$4 Billion** on gaming apps - an amount **equal to total national expenditure on medicines** during the exact same timeframe.
   * **Youth & Demographic Toll**: Teenagers and unemployed youth in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities spend full days on betting apps, leading to academic neglect, severe addiction and financial debt traps.

### Economic Drawbacks & Industry Challenges of The Ban
1. **Government Revenue Loss**: Loss of a major, rapidly growing tax/GST revenue stream.
2. **Workforce Dislocation**: Threatens direct employment of **~2 Lakh workers** in the Indian gaming industry.
3. **Startup & Unicorn Setback**: Severe blow to Indian gaming startups (e.g. **Dream11**, which achieved Unicorn status). *Historical Contrast*: PM Narendra Modi had previously praised gamers and hosted a gaming industry conclave a year prior.

### Future Alternative Business Models For Gaming Apps
To survive without betting mechanics, gaming companies must pivot to:
1. **Subscription-Based Model**: Recurring fee structure granting unlimited gameplay access (similar to the **Netflix** subscription framework).
2. **In-App Advertising Model**: Monetizing gameplay through third-party ad placements.

## Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) Framework

### Conceptual Definitions: Layman/NCERT vs. RBI
* **Layman / NCERT Definition of CRR** = Percentage of **deposits** maintained by banks with the RBI.
* **RBI Technical Definition of CRR** = Percentage of **NDTL** maintained by banks with the RBI.
* *UPSC Prelims Rule*: Terms "Deposits" and "NDTL" are used interchangeably in exam statements; both formulations are considered correct.

### Classification of Bank Liabilities & Assets

![[Bank BS.png|900]]

#### 1. Liabilities Towards Other Banks
* **Interbank Deposits**: Money deposited by other banks with this bank.
* **Interbank Borrowings**: Short-term funds borrowed from other banks (*not loans extended*).

#### 2. Liabilities Towards Public (Deposits)

##### 2.1 Time Liabilities
Funds that cannot be demanded at will; bank is legally liable to repay only upon completion of a specified time frame or condition.
* **Fixed Deposits (FD)**: Lumpsum capital deposited for a fixed maturity tenor (1, 2, 3, or 5 years).
* **Recurring Deposits (RD)**: Fixed-frequency deposits (e.g., ₹10,000 monthly for 1 year = ₹1.2 Lakh total). Functions like a Mutual Fund SIP, but operates as a bank deposit earning fixed interest.

##### 2.2 Demand Liabilities vs. Savings Account Nuance
* **Demand Liabilities** = Liabilities payable by the bank immediately upon demand.
* **Current Account Deposits** = **100% Demand Liabilities** (higher minimum balance, zero interest, unlimited transactions, offers **Overdraft - OD** instant loan facility when balance hits zero).
* **Savings Account Breakdown (Crucial RBI Rule)**:
  * **Balance Over & Above Minimum Balance** (e.g., ₹90,000 out of ₹1 Lakh) = **Demand Liability** (withdrawable anytime at customer's discretion).
  * **Mandatory Minimum Balance Requirement** (e.g., ₹10,000) = **Time Liability** (can legally be claimed without penalty only upon closing the account; unauthorized withdrawal triggers bank penalty).

### Mathematical Formula & Structure of NDTL

$$\text{NDTL} = \text{Liabilities Towards Banks} - \text{Assets With Other Banks} + \text{Liabilities Towards Public}$$

* **Net Liabilities Towards Banks** = $\text{Liabilities Towards Banks} - \text{Assets With Other Banks}$.
  * *Example*: Bank owes ₹200 to other banks but holds ₹100 deposited in other banks $\rightarrow$ Net Interbank Liability = **₹100**.
* **Final Consolidated NDTL Formula**:

$$\text{NDTL} = \text{Net Liabilities Towards Banks} + \text{Public Deposits}$$

* **Distinction**: Public Deposits reflect only customer deposits, whereas NDTL includes public deposits **plus net interbank liabilities**.

## Monetary Policy Tools: Quantitative vs. Qualitative

![[Pasted image 20260928183026.png|900]]

### Quantitative Tools
* **Function**: Alters the **total quantity/volume** of money circulating in the macroeconomy.
* **Mechanism**: Expands money supply during recessions; contracts money supply during inflation.

### Qualitative Tools
* **Function**: Directs credit flow toward **productive sectors** while restricting flow to unproductive sectors, without altering overall money volume.
* **Productive vs. Unproductive Examples**:
  * **Tractor Loans vs. SUV Loans**: Higher credit allocation for agriculture/tractors (productive) vs. restricted credit for personal SUVs (less productive).
  * **Education Loans vs. Foreign Trip Loans**: Prioritizing human capital/education over personal leisure consumption.

## Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)

### Definitions & Statutory Framework
* **NCERT Definition** = Percentage of **deposits** maintained by banks with RBI in the form of **cash**.
* **RBI Definition** = Percentage of **NDTL** maintained by banks with RBI in the form of **cash**.

### Dual Objectives of CRR
1. **Safety Cushion**: Secures a fractional liquidity reserve with RBI to protect depositors if a bank faces distress.
2. **Monetary Control**: Primary tool to regulate systemic liquidity and inflation.

### Transmission Causal Chains

$$\text{Inflation} \rightarrow \text{RBI increases CRR} \rightarrow \text{Banks transfer more cash to RBI} \rightarrow \text{Lending capacity} \downarrow \rightarrow \text{Lending Interest Rates} \uparrow \rightarrow \text{Money Supply} \downarrow$$

$$\text{Recession} \rightarrow \text{RBI decreases CRR} \rightarrow \text{Banks transfer less cash to RBI} \rightarrow \text{Lending capacity} \uparrow \rightarrow \text{Lending Interest Rates} \downarrow \rightarrow \text{Money Supply} \uparrow$$

### Quantitative Parameters & Cost Dynamics
* **Statutory Limits**: Can range from **0% to 100%** (100% deployed temporarily during 2016 demonetization).
* **Current Rate (Aug 2025 Context)** = **4%**.
* **Interest Paid by RBI** = **0% (Zero Interest)**.
* **Cost to Banks**: CRR is a **net operational cost** for commercial banks—banks pay interest to depositors on 100% of deposits, but earn zero interest on the CRR cash locked with RBI.

### Applicability & Penalties
* **Applicability Matrix**:
  * **Scheduled Banks**: Mandatory CRR maintained with **RBI** under *RBI Act, 1934*.
  * **Non-Scheduled Banks**: Allowed flexibility to maintain CRR with **RBI or with themselves** under *Banking Regulation Act, 1949*.
  * **NBFCs**: **NOT applicable** to any NBFC.
* **Shortfall Penalty Mechanism**:
  * Penalty Rate = **3% above Bank Rate** on the shortfall amount.
  * *Numerical Calculation Example*: Shortfall = ₹100; Bank Rate = 5.75% $\rightarrow$ Penalty Rate = $3\% + 5.75\% = 8.75\%$ $\rightarrow$ Penalty Paid = **₹8.75**.

## Incremental Cash Reserve Ratio (I-CRR)

### Definition & Key Characteristics
* **Definition** = Percentage of **incremental (additional) deposits** received during a specified period that banks must mandatorily park with RBI as cash.
* **Nature**: Temporary, non-permanent tool deployed exclusively during exceptional liquidity surges.
* **Interest Rate**: **Zero Interest** paid by RBI (identical to normal CRR).
* **Historical Deployments**:
  1. **2016 Demonetization**.
  2. **2023 Withdrawal of ₹2,000 Banknotes**.

### 2023 Case Study: ₹2,000 Note Withdrawal Liquidity Shock
1. **Pre-Withdrawal Baseline**: Total bank deposits = **~₹200 Lakh Crores** (Normal 4% CRR = ₹8 Lakh Cr).
2. **Withdrawal Impact**: **~85%** of circulating ₹2,000 notes were **deposited into bank accounts** rather than exchanged.
3. **Liquidity Surge**: Total bank deposits surged to **~₹210 Lakh Crores** (Incremental deposit = **₹10 Lakh Crores**).
4. **Inflation Threat**: Unchecked lending of ₹10 Lakh Cr would flood the economy with excess money supply, triggering severe inflation.
5. **RBI Intervention**: Imposed **10% Incremental CRR** on the ₹10 Lakh Cr incremental deposit $\rightarrow$ Impounded **₹1 Lakh Crore** in cash reserves with RBI.
6. **Phased Release Timeline**: 25% released on Sept 9, 2023; 25% released on Sept 23, 2023; remaining 50% released on Oct 7, 2023 (fully phased out).

### Comparison: Normal CRR vs. Incremental CRR (I-CRR)

| Feature | Normal CRR | Incremental CRR (I-CRR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Base Calculation** | Percentage of **Total NDTL / Deposits** | Percentage of **Incremental (Additional) Deposits** |
| **Target Period** | Normal economic times | Exceptional liquidity shock events |
| **Duration / Nature** | Permanent, continuous feature | Temporary, time-bound feature |
| **Interest Earned** | **Zero Interest** from RBI | **Zero Interest** from RBI |
| **Maintenance Asset** | Cash only | Cash only |

## Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)

### Definition & Eligible Instruments
* **Definition** = Percentage of deposits/NDTL that banks must maintain **with themselves** in liquid assets.
* **Eligible Assets (Cash, Gold, or Approved G-Secs)**:
  1. **Cash**
  2. **Gold**
  3. **Dated Government Securities**
  4. **State Development Loans (SDLs)**
  5. **Treasury Bills (T-Bills)**
  6. **Cash Management Bills (CMBs)**
  7. **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)**
  8. **Sovereign Green Bonds**
* **Ineligible Assets**: **Special Government Securities** (e.g. Oil Bonds, Recapitalization Bonds, UDAY Bonds) are **NOT eligible** for SLR.

### Comprehensive Comparison Matrix: CRR vs. SLR

| Parameter | Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) | Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maintained With** | **RBI** | **Banks Themselves** |
| **Form of Assets** | **Cash ONLY** | **Cash, Gold, or Approved G-Secs** |
| **Governing Statute** | *RBI Act, 1934* (Scheduled) / *BR Act, 1949* (Non-Scheduled) | *Banking Regulation Act, 1949* (Both Scheduled & Non-Scheduled) |
| **NBFC Coverage** | **NOT Applicable** to any NBFC | **Applicable ONLY to Deposit-Taking NBFCs** (e.g., Bajaj Finance, Muthoot); Not applicable to non-deposit taking (e.g., Tata Capital) |
| **Statutory Ceiling** | 0% to 100% | **Maximum 40%** (*Exception*: **75% for Payment Banks**) |
| **Current Rate (Aug 2025)**| **4%** | **18%** |
| **Profit Potential** | **Zero Profit** (0% interest from RBI) | **Scope to Earn Profit** (Yield from G-Sec coupon interest + Gold price appreciation) |

### Impact of SLR Reduction (2015 UPSC PYQ Causal Chain)

$$\text{RBI reduces SLR} \rightarrow \text{Banks hold less liquidity with themselves} \rightarrow \text{Lending capacity} \uparrow \rightarrow \text{Commercial Banks cut Lending Rates} \rightarrow \text{Credit Expansion} \rightarrow \text{GDP Growth} \uparrow$$

### Concept = Financial Repression
* **Definition** = Statutory policy forcing banks to allocate a fixed portion of deposits (SLR) into low-yielding Government Securities rather than higher-yielding private/corporate investments.
* **Risk-Return Trade-Off**:
  * **Corporate Bonds** = Higher Risk $\rightarrow$ Higher Coupon Rate $\rightarrow$ Higher Bank Profits.
  * **Government Securities** = Zero Risk $\rightarrow$ Lower Coupon Rate $\rightarrow$ Lower Bank Profits.
* **Impact**: Suppresses bank profitability (**repression**), but provides the Government with guaranteed, captive low-cost borrowings.

## Why Banks Buy Government Securities & The Repo Mechanism

### Three Primary Reasons Banks Purchase G-Secs
1. **Fulfill SLR Statutory Requirements** (18% mandatory holding).
2. **Safe Sovereign Investment** (Earning stable coupon interest).
3. **Collateral for RBI Repo Window Borrowings**.

### Mandatory Collateral Rule

$$\text{Mandatory SLR G-Secs} \neq \text{Repo Collateral (PROHIBITED)}$$

$$\text{EXCESS G-Secs (Held above 18\% SLR)} = \text{Eligible Repo Collateral}$$

* **Rule**: Banks **cannot** pledge G-Secs locked under mandatory SLR to borrow money under the Repo window. Only **excess G-Secs** held over and above the SLR mandate can be pledged as collateral.

### Repo & Repo Rate Mechanism
* **Repo (Repurchase Option)** = Tool through which RBI lends short-term money to commercial banks against G-Sec collateral.
* **Repo Rate** = Rate of interest charged by RBI on short-term repo lending.
