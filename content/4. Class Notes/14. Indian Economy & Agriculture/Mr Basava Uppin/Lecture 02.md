## Categorization of Sectors: Organized vs. Unorganized

### Economic Survey Classification Criterion
* **Organized Enterprise** = Employs **10 or more workers** (irrespective of formal registration status).
* **Unorganized Enterprise** = Employs **less than 10 workers**.
* **Examples**:
  * **Kirana Store** (1–2 workers) → **Unorganized**
  * **Vishal Mega Mart** (>10 workers) → **Organized**
  * **Individual Farmer / Fruit / Vegetable / Tea Vendor** (Self-employed) → **Unorganized**
  * **Food Processing Unit (e.g. Kisan Jam)** (>10 workers) → **Organized**
  * **Cottage / Household Industry (e.g. Pickle / Papad)** (4–5 workers) → **Unorganized**

### Structural Employment Distribution in India
* **Agriculture** = **100% Unorganized** (Employs **46%** of total Indian workforce).
* **Workforce Share by Sector**:
  * **Unorganized Sector** → **~82%** of total workforce.
  * **Organized Sector** → **~18%** of total workforce.

## Classification of Workers: Formal vs. Informal

* **Enterprise** = Categorized as **Organized** vs. **Unorganized**.
* **Worker** = Categorized as **Formal** vs. **Informal** (*Unorganized Workers' Social Security Act, 2008* defines unorganized/informal workers).

### Formal Worker Criteria
1. **Income Security** = Fixed, regular monthly wages.
2. **Job Security** = Permanent employment status (protection against arbitrary dismissal).
3. **Social Security Benefits** = Access to pension, health insurance, Provident Fund (PF), etc.

### Informal Worker Criteria
* Lacks **Income Security** (variable/unpredictable earnings), **Job Security** (contractual/daily wage) and **Social Security Benefits**.
* **Examples**:
  * **Gig Workers** (Platform workers: Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Uber, Ola, Urban Company)
  * **Contractual Workers** & **MGNREGA Workers**

### Cross-Sectoral Workforce Composition
* **Organized Sector Workforce**:
  * **Formal Workers** = **53%** (e.g. Permanent software engineers/coders at Zomato, managers at Foxconn).
  * **Informal Workers** = **47%** (e.g. Delivery partners at Zomato, contractual engineers at Foxconn, contractual doctors/nurses in government hospitals).
* **Unorganized Sector Workforce**:
  * **Informal Workers** = **98.2%**
  * **Formal Workers** = **1.8%** (e.g. Long-term manager in a Khadi/Handloom unit employing <10 people who receives fixed monthly salary & social security).
* **Overall National Workforce Matrix**:
  * **Informal Workers** ≈ **90%** of total Indian workforce.
  * **Formal Workers** ≈ **10%** of total Indian workforce.

## Current Economic Concepts & News

### Goldilocks Zone
* **Goldilocks Zone** = Ideal economic state characterized by **moderate inflation** + **robust GDP growth** + **low unemployment**.
* **Complicating Factors in India's Goldilocks Scenario**:
  1. High **Food Inflation** (tracked via Consumer Food Price Index - CFPI).
  2. Rising **Income Inequality** (richer becoming richer, poorer becoming poorer).
  3. **Stagnant Real Wages**.
* **Inflation Categories**:
  * **Headline Inflation** = Total inflation including volatile food & energy prices.
  * **Core Inflation** = Headline Inflation minus volatile food & energy items.

### Banking Developments: Small Finance Bank (SFB) → Universal Bank
* **AU Small Finance Bank** = First SFB permitted by RBI to transition into a **Universal Bank**.
* **Regulatory Timeline**:
  * **2014**: RBI issued Universal Bank licenses to **Bandhan Bank** and **IDFC Bank** (after 11-year gap).
  * **2016–17**: RBI introduced **Small Finance Banks** (originating mostly from Micro Finance Institutions - MFIs like Ujjivan, Utkarsh, AU).
* **Universal Bank vs. Small Finance Bank**:
  * **Universal Bank** = No restrictions on deposit acceptance or loan disbursement.
  * **Small Finance Bank (SFB)** = No deposit restrictions; **Mandatory loan constraint**: At least **50% of total loan portfolio** must consist of small-value loans **≤ ₹25 Lakhs**.

## Difference: Central Bank vs. Commercial Bank

| Parameter | Central Bank (e.g. RBI) | Commercial Bank (e.g. SBI, ICICI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | **Apex monetary authority** sitting at top of banking structure | Functions under control & supervision of Central Bank |
| **Establishment** | Set up as a **Statutory Body** via Special Parliament Act (*RBI Act, 1934*) | Registered under *Companies Act* / Specific Banking Statutes |
| **Ownership** | **100% Government Owned** (in India) | Both **Public / Government Owned** (SBI) & **Private Owned** (ICICI, HDFC) |
| **Primary Objective** | **Price Stability** (controlling inflation) while supporting growth | **Profit Maximization** |
| **Currency Issue** | **Monopoly power** to print & issue Bank Notes | **No authority** to print notes (only distributes cash) |
| **Clientele / Role** | Acts as **Banker to Government** & **Banker to Banks** | Acts as **Banker to Public** (accepts deposits, extends loans) |
| **Credit Mechanism** | **Controls Credit Creation** ("Puppeteer" directing monetary flow) | **Generates Credit / Gives Loans** directly to borrowers |
| **Historical Examples** | **Sweden's Riksbank** (World's 1st Central Bank, 1668); Bank of England; US Federal Reserve | SBI, ICICI, HDFC, Axis, Yes Bank |

## Historical Evolution of RBI (Chronology)

* **1770s: Bank of Hindustan**
  * Established as a private bank (owned by East India Company traders).
  * **Significance** = Introduced **paper-based notes on a large scale** in India for the first time. (Closed down within 10–15 years).
* **1806–1843: Presidency Banks**
  * **Presidency Bank of Kolkata (Bengal)** (1806), **Presidency Bank of Bombay** (1840), **Presidency Bank of Madras** (1843).
  * **Semi-government owned** entities.
  * Issued localized notes valid **only within respective Presidencies** (No unified national currency).
* **1861: Paper Currency Act, 1861**
  * Granted **monopoly of note issuance** to the British Crown / Government.
  * Stripped Presidency Banks of note-printing rights.
  * Established **Controller General of Currency** as nodal printing agency.
* **1921: Imperial Bank of India**
  * Formed via **merger of 3 Presidency Banks** (Kolkata, Bombay, Madras).
  * Acted as **Banker to the British Government** (deposits & loans). Note issuance remained with Controller General of Currency.
* **1924: Hilton Young Committee** *(Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance)*
  * Appointed by British Government to examine central banking setup.
  * **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's Input** = Presented thesis *"The Problem of the Rupee: Its Origin and Its Solution"*, laying foundational concepts.
  * **Committee Recommendations**:
    1. Set up Central Bank as a **Statutory Body**.
    2. Set up Central Bank as a **Private Shareholders' Bank** (capital funded by public shareholders, not government).
    3. Minimum Capital = **₹5 Crores** (divided into shares of ₹100 face value).
* **1934: RBI Act, 1934**
  * Enacted by Imperial Legislative Council implementing Hilton Young recommendations.
* **April 1, 1935: Establishment of RBI**
  * Commenced operations as a **Private Shareholders' Bank** with initial capital of **₹5 Crores**.
* **1947–1948: Partition Era**
  * Post-independence, RBI functioned as Central Bank for **both India and Pakistan** (until State Bank of Pakistan was established in 1948).
* **January 1, 1949: Nationalization of RBI**
  * Government enacted *RBI (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948*.
  * Acquired 100% private shares by paying ₹100 market compensation per share → RBI transformed into a **100% State-Owned Statutory Body**.
* **1955: SBI Act, 1955**
  * **Imperial Bank of India nationalized** → Rechristened as **State Bank of India (SBI)**.

## Objectives of RBI

* **Primary Objective** = Maintain **Price Stability** (controlling inflation).
* **Secondary Objective** = Support economic **Growth** (keeping in mind growth objectives).
* **Core Rule**: Price Stability takes precedent over growth target. *(UPSC Prelims 2022 PYQ)*.

## Acts Governing Functioning of RBI

1. **RBI Act, 1934**: Establishes RBI, Central Board, currency management, surplus transfer.
2. **Banking Regulation Act, 1949**: Empowers RBI as **Regulator & Supervisor of Commercial Banks** (licensing, governance, inspections).
3. **Government Securities Act, 2006**: Empowers RBI as **Debt Manager to Central & State Governments** (manages public market debt borrowings).
4. **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**: Empowers RBI to regulate payment systems (Cash, Cheques, UPI, RTGS, NEFT, IMPS, Prepaid Payment Instruments like Metro cards, Amazon Pay balance, mobile wallets).
5. **Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999**: Replaced FERA; empowers RBI to regulate **Foreign Exchange Market & Exchange Rates**.

## Structure & Governance of RBI: Central Board

* Established under **Section 8 of RBI Act, 1934**.
* Holds ultimate powers of **general superintendence and direction** of RBI.
* **Maximum Strength** = **21 Members**.

### Composition Breakdown
1. **Official Directors (Maximum 5)**:
   * **1 RBI Governor**
   * **Maximum 4 Deputy Governors**
2. **Non-Official Directors (Maximum 16)**:
   * **4 Directors** = Nominated from 4 Regional / Local Boards (**Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai**).
   * **10 Independent Directors** = Nominated by Central Government (experts in finance/banking/economy).
   * **2 Ex-Officio Government Officials** = **Secretary, Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)** + **Secretary, Department of Financial Services (DFS)**.

### Tenures & Service Conditions
* **Official Directors (Governor & Deputy Governors)**:
  * Maximum Statutory Tenure = **5 years** per term.
  * **Convention**: Appointed initially for **3 years**, followed by **2-year extension** based on performance.
  * **Reappointment**: Fully **eligible for reappointment** without term limits (e.g. Shaktikanta Das served 3 + 3 = 6 years).
* **Non-Official Directors**: Maximum tenure = **4 years**.
* **Qualifications**: **NO formal qualifications** prescribed for Governor / Deputy Governor under the *RBI Act, 1934*.

### Selection & Appointment Process
* **Recommending Authority** = **Financial Sector Regulatory Appointment Search Committee (FSRASC)**, chaired by **Cabinet Secretary** (India's senior-most IAS officer).
* **Appointing Authority** = **Cabinet Committee on Appointments (ACC)**, chaired by the **Prime Minister**.
* **Current RBI Governor** = **Sanjay Malhotra** (appointed for initial 3-year term).
* **Meeting Frequency**: Central Board must meet **at least 6 times a year** (and at least once every quarter).

---

## Important Committees under RBI Central Board

1. **Board for Financial Supervision (BFS)**:
   * Primary regulator for commercial banks, NBFCs and financial institutions.
   * **Chaired by**: **RBI Governor**. Set up under *RBI Act, 1934*.
2. **Board for Regulation and Supervision of Payment and Settlement Systems (BPSS)**:
   * Apex regulatory body for payment ecosystems (UPI, RTGS, NEFT, cards).
   * **Chaired by**: **RBI Governor**.
   * **Statutory Basis**: Set up under *Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007* (NOT RBI Act).

---

## Important Sections of RBI Act, 1934

* **Section 7**:
  * Empowers **Central Government to issue directions to RBI Governor** in matters of **public interest** (after consultation with Governor).
  * *Note*: Section 7 is a **statutory power**, NOT a constitutional mandate.
* **Section 47**:
  * Mandates RBI to **transfer surplus profits (dividend)** to the Central Government every financial year.

## Role of RBI: Issue of Bank Notes

### Bank Notes vs. Rupee Coins
* **Bank Notes**:
  * Printed and issued exclusively by **RBI** (Denominations: ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500; ₹2000 withdrawn).
  * Bear signature of **RBI Governor**.
  * RBI holds **sole legal authority** to issue Bank Notes.
* **One Rupee Note**:
  * Printed directly by **Government of India** (Ministry of Finance).
  * Bears signature of **Finance Secretary** (IAS officer).
  * Legally classified as **Rupee Coin** under *Coinage Act*, NOT a Bank Note.

### Monetary Economics: Legal Tender vs. Trust
* **Intrinsic Value of Paper Currency** = **Zero** (inherently just paper).
* **Legal Tender** = Currency legally sanctioned by government; creditors/sellers are bound by law to accept it for debt discharge.
* **Trust & Universal Acceptability** = Fundamental core of money function.
  * Money serves as a **medium of exchange** because of public trust in its universal acceptability (illustrated by *Shawshank Redemption* prison economy where cigarettes functioned as currency due to universal acceptability despite non-smokers holding them).
