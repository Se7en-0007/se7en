## Nature of Legal Tender: Unlimited vs. Limited

### Conceptual Classification
* **Legal Tender** = Money which a creditor is legally compelled to accept in settlement of debt/claims.

| Parameter | Unlimited Legal Tender | Limited Legal Tender |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Obligation** | Absolute legal obligation to accept | Obligation to accept **up to a statutory limit** |
| **Beyond Limit** | No limit; compulsory acceptance for any amount | Acceptance becomes **voluntary** beyond limit |
| **Coverage in India** | **Bank Notes** (₹2 to ₹10,000 under *RBI Act, 1934*) | **Coins** & **₹1 Notes** |

### Statutory Limits for Limited Legal Tender (*Coinage Act, 2011*)
* **Coins of ₹1 and above** (₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20 coins) + **₹1 Notes** = Mandatory acceptance up to **₹1,000**. Beyond ₹1,000 → Acceptance is voluntary.
* **50 Paise Coins** = Mandatory acceptance up to **₹10** (i.e. up to 20 coins of 50p). Beyond ₹10 → Acceptance is voluntary.

### Cash Transaction Restriction (*Finance Act, 2017*)
* **Core Provision** = Accepting cash payment of **> ₹2 Lakhs** in single transaction / day is **illegal** (aimed at checking black money & cash hoarding).
* **Mandatory Electronic Mode** = Payments exceeding ₹2 Lakhs must be made via electronic channels (**RTGS**, **NEFT**, **UPI**, internet banking).
* **UPSC Exam Priority Rule**:
  * *RBI Act, 1934* defines Bank Notes as **Unlimited Legal Tender**.
  * *Finance Act, 2017* imposes statutory ceiling on cash acceptance at ₹2 Lakhs.
  * In Prelims conceptual questions on legal tender status → Follow **RBI Act, 1934** (Unlimited Legal Tender).
* **Case Study / Marketing Anomaly**: Buyer purchased ₹12 Lakh Mahindra Bolero using only 1-rupee coins. Showroom voluntarily accepted full amount as a free marketing publicity strategy (ad cost avoided vs. counting inconvenience).
* **Tax Evasion & Big Data Enforcement**:
  * Income tax paid by only **~1.5%** of Indians (despite national per capita income > ₹1 Lakh).
  * Income Tax Dept uses **Big Data Analytics**, **AI**, e-commerce purchase tracking and social media scrutiny to detect un-reported income in informal/private professions (e.g. private doctors, lawyers running independent clinics/practices).

## Coinage Framework In India

### Statutory Governance
* **Governing Statute** = **Coinage Act, 2011** (repealed British-era coinage laws).
* **Minting Authority** = **Central Government** (mints at government mints via SPMCIL - Security Printing and Minting Corporation of India).
* **Design, Material & Denominations** = Decided exclusively by **Central Government**.
* **Distribution Authority** = Exclusively handled by **RBI** (RBI distributes all coins and ₹1 notes minted/printed by Govt).
* **Denomination Ceilings**:
  * Minimum Denomination = No statutory lower limit.
  * Maximum Denomination = Up to **₹1,000** under *Coinage Act, 2011*.

### Commemorative Coins
* **Definition** = Special edition coins minted to mark historic milestones or anniversaries.
* **Examples**: ₹150 coin (150 years of Bombay Stock Exchange), ₹125 coin (125th Birth Anniversary).
* **Issue Mechanism** = Sold via SPMCIL at a premium price (e.g., ₹125 face-value coin sold at ₹4,500 to collectors/numismatists).
* **Legal Tender Status** = **Legally valid legal tender**, but practically retained as collectibles.

### Summary

| Currency Type | Nodal Agency / Issuer | Governing Statute | Signatory | Minimum Reserve System (MRS)? | Legal Tender Type & Limit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bank Notes** (₹2–₹10,000) | **RBI** (Issue Dept) | *RBI Act, 1934* | Governor, RBI | **Yes** (₹200 Cr reserves) | **Unlimited** Legal Tender |
| **₹1 Note** | **Central Government** | *RBI Act, 1934* | Finance Secretary | **No** (Zero reserves required) | **Limited** Legal Tender (Up to ₹1,000) |
| **Coins** (50p to ₹1,000) | **Central Government** | *Coinage Act, 2011* | None (Mint Mark) | **No** (Zero reserves required) | **Limited** Legal Tender (50p up to ₹10; ₹1+ up to ₹1,000) |

## Role of RBI As Banker To The Government

### Core Operational Functions
Just as commercial banks serve individual clients, RBI performs 5 primary banker functions for the government:
1. **Account Opening** = Maintains central banking accounts for governments.
2. **Minimum Balances** = Mandates governments to maintain minimum cash balances with RBI.
3. **Credit Provision** = Provides short-term advance loans to bridge cash flow gaps via **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)**.
4. **Receipts & Payments (Remittances)** = Handles incoming receipts (taxes, tolls, traffic penalties, utility bills) and outgoing payments (salaries, pensions, subsidies, contractor bills).
5. **Financial Advisory** = Provides expert advice on monetary, public debt and financial matters.

### Statutory Legal Framework (*RBI Act, 1934*)

![[RBI Act.png|700]]

* **Sikkim Exception**: **State Bank of Sikkim** (owned by Sikkim Govt, established prior to 1975 merger) acts as banker to Sikkim State Government (*NOT SBI or RBI*).

### Present Operational Mechanism: Agency Banks
* **Problem** = RBI overburdened with monetary policy, inflation control, bank regulation, note printing, forex management and payment system supervision.
* **Solution / Outsourcing** = RBI outsourced **receipts and payments (remittance) operations** to authorized **Agency Banks**.
* **Agency Bank Types** = Both **Public Sector Banks** (e.g. SBI) and **Private Sector Banks** (e.g. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank).
* **Allocation Model**:
  * Specific Ministries/Departments assigned to designated Agency Banks (e.g., Finance Ministry → SBI; Ministry of Defence → HDFC Bank).
  * Transactions executed only through **designated specialized branches**.
* **Remuneration** = RBI pays a **commission** to agency banks for handling government banking transactions.

## Role of RBI As Debt Manager

### Fundamentals of Government Borrowings
* **Debt** = Cumulative borrowings raised by government.
* **Causal Chain for Borrowings**:
  Developing Economy → Tax Receipts < Essential Expenditure → Fiscal Gap → Mandatory Government Borrowings.

### Two Core Objectives of Government Borrowings
1. **Welfare Promotion** = Funding social schemes (PM Awas Yojana, Swachh Bharat Mission, Jal Jeevan Mission, Saubhagya Scheme, PM Ujjwala Yojana).
2. **Growth & Infrastructure Creation**:
  * **Railways**: 1853 (1st line Bombay-Thane) → 1951 (50,000 km built by British) → 2023 (65,000 km total root length; only +15,000 km added in 75 years; gauge conversion focused).
  * **National Highways**: Account for **2% of total road network**, but carry **40% of total road traffic** (requires massive capital expansion).
  * **Modern Infrastructure**: World-class airport terminals (Bangalore T2, Delhi T3, Noida/Jewar, Ayodhya).

### Sources of Government Borrowings
* **External Borrowings** (Outside India) = Multilateral (IMF, World Bank, ADB) + Bilateral sources → Managed directly by **Ministry of Finance**.
* **Internal Borrowings** (Within India) → Managed exclusively by **RBI as Debt Manager**.

### Internal Investor Categories
1. **RBI** (Direct monetization / loans).
2. **Financial Institutions** = Commercial Banks, Insurance Companies, Pension Funds, Mutual Funds.
3. **Retail Investors** = Individual citizens/small investors (buying G-Secs via RBI Retail Direct Scheme).
4. **Primary Dealers (PDs)** = Specialized entities authorized by RBI to underwrite and buy Government Securities directly.
   * **Bank Primary Dealers** = All commercial banks holding a banking license (SBI, ICICI, HDFC).
   * **Non-Banking Primary Dealers** = Specialized non-bank financial entities (e.g., ICICI Securities, Kotak Securities, Discount and Finance House of India - DFHI).

### Borrowing Duration & Eligibility Rule

| Duration Category | Time Horizon | Central Government Eligibility | State Government Eligibility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Short-Term Borrowings** | **< 1 Year** | **Allowed** | **NOT Allowed** |
| **Long-Term Borrowings** | **> 1 Year** | **Allowed** | **Allowed** |

### Purpose of Borrowings: Deficit Financing vs. Temporary Cash Mismatches

* **Addressing Temporary Mismatches in Cash Balances**:
  * Short-term intra-year cash gap (e.g., heavy expenditure in April vs. delayed tax inflows in May).
  * Borrowed funds are **repaid within the same financial year** from seasonal tax/non-tax receipts.
* **Financing Deficit**:
  * Structural annual gap where Total Annual Expenditure exceeds Total Annual Receipts.
  * Long-term borrowings **not repaid within the same financial year**; carried forward as debt.

## Mechanism of Borrowings & Types of Government Securities (G-Secs)

### General Market Issuance Mechanism (E-Kuber Platform)
1. **Step 1**: Government creates and issues Government Security (G-Sec).
2. **Step 2**: RBI conducts competitive auctions to sell G-Secs on its proprietary **E-Kuber Platform**.
3. **Step 3**: Authorized investors (PDs, Banks, FIs, Retail) bid and pay funds to RBI.
4. **Step 4**: RBI transfers collected capital to Government account.
5. **Step 5**: Upon maturity, RBI repays face value + interest to investors on behalf of Government (earning transaction commission).

## Categorization of Government Securities (G-Secs)

### Market Borrowings (Within India)

#### 1. Long-Term Market Borrowings (> 1 Year)
* **Central Government** → Issues **Dated Bonds / Dated Securities**.
  * Features: Face Value + Maturity (> 1 Year up to 40 Years) + **Coupon Rate** (fixed or floating annual interest).
* **State Governments** → Issues **State Development Loans (SDLs)**.
  * Features: Identical to dated bonds (Face Value, Maturity > 1 Year, Coupon Rate); issued specifically by state governments.

#### 2. Short-Term Market Borrowings (< 1 Year) - Central Government ONLY
* **Treasury Bills (T-Bills)**:
  * Issued exclusively by Central Government (**States CANNOT issue T-Bills**).
  * Maturity = **< 1 Year**.
  * **Zero-Coupon Securities** = Issued at a **Discount** to face value and redeemed at **Par / Face Value** upon maturity (Yield = Face Value - Discounted Purchase Price; *No explicit interest/coupon rate paid*).
  * **Standard Maturity Tenors**:
    1. **T-91** (Maturity: 91 Days)
    2. **T-182** (Maturity: 182 Days)
    3. **T-364** (Maturity: 364 Days)
  * *Numerical Example*: T-Bill with Face Value ₹100 purchased at auctioned discount price of ₹95 → On Day 91, investor receives ₹100 → Profit = ₹5 (Zero Coupon Yield).
* **Cash Management Bills (CMBs)**:
  * Issued exclusively by Central Government (**States CANNOT issue CMBs**).
  * Zero-coupon discount instruments identical to T-Bills.
  * Distinction: Maturity is **strictly less than 91 Days** (used to meet sudden, ultra-short-term cash flow mismatches).

### Four Types of Government Securities

| Instrument | Issuer | Duration | Coupon / Interest Status | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dated Bonds** | Central Govt | Long-Term (> 1 Year) | Pays Coupon Rate | Standard long-term sovereign bond |
| **State Development Loans (SDLs)** | State Govts | Long-Term (> 1 Year) | Pays Coupon Rate | Issued by states; managed by RBI |
| **Treasury Bills (T-Bills)** | Central Govt | Short-Term (91, 182, 364 Days) | **Zero Coupon** (Discounted) | 3 fixed tenors; states prohibited |
| **Cash Management Bills (CMBs)** | Central Govt | Ultra Short-Term (< 91 Days) | **Zero Coupon** (Discounted) | Used for temporary liquidity gaps |

## Government Borrowings from RBI: Monetization of Deficit

### Workflow of Direct RBI Financing (Monetization)
1. **Step 1**: Central Government issues G-Sec to RBI.
2. **Step 2**: RBI **does NOT sell** the G-Sec on E-Kuber platform to market investors.
3. **Step 3**: RBI **Issue Department** holds the G-Sec directly as a matching asset in its balance sheet.
4. **Step 4**: As Issue Department assets increase, RBI prints equivalent value of **fresh new Bank Notes**.
5. **Step 5**: RBI lends these newly printed notes directly to Central Government to finance its expenditure.

![[RBI Money To Govt.png|800]]

### Concept = Monetization of Deficit
* **Definition** = Creation/printing of **fresh new money** by the Central Bank to directly fund government deficit.
* **Market Borrowing vs. Monetization**:
  * *Market Borrowing* = Re-circulates existing money supply from investors/banks to government (No change in total money stock).
  * *Monetization of Deficit* = Injects **brand new currency** into the banking system → Expands total money supply.

### Sovereign Default Risk Paradox
* Government borrowing in domestic currency can **never default** in nominal terms because the sovereign holds the monetary authority to print unlimited legal tender currency.
* **Causal Risk Chain**:
  Unrestricted Money Printing → Excessive Money Supply → Money Chasing Goods → Severe / Hyper-Inflation → Currency Devaluation.

### Legal Status in India: FRBM Act, 2003 Framework
* **Not Completely Prohibited** in India, but tightly restricted.
* **FRBM Act, 2003 Provisions** = Prohibits RBI from subscribing to primary issuances of Government Securities under normal conditions.
* **Escape Clause / Exceptional Circumstances** = Monetization of deficit permitted **ONLY** under specific national emergencies:
  1. **National Security Threats**
  2. **War**
  3. **National Calamity / Pandemics**
  4. **Collapse of Agriculture**
  5. **Structural Economic Reforms**
  6. **Severe Decline in Real GDP Growth**
