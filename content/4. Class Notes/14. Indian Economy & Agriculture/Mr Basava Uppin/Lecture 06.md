### GST Rate Rationalization & Editorial Keywords
* **PM Independence Day Speech Theme**: Announced big-bang GST rate rationalization ("Diwali Gift").
* **Core GST Keywords**:
  * **Input Tax Credit (ITC)** = Tax paid on input purchases reduced from tax payable on output sales.
  * **Inverted Duty Structure** = Tax rate on raw materials/inputs > Tax rate on finished goods (leads to ITC accumulation & tax refund bottlenecks).

### Infrastructure & Highway Toll Collection (PAC Report)
* **Text & Context Page (*The Hindu*)**: Public Accounts Committee (PAC) report on highway toll practices.
* **National Highway Building Models**: Executed by National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) or private sector under Public-Private Partnership (PPP).
* **Cost Recovery Principle**:
  * Total recoverable cost = Project capital expenditure + Maintenance cost (e.g. ₹100 Cr capex + ₹25 Cr maintenance = ₹125 Cr).
  * Objective: Toll collection must cease once project cost is fully recovered.
* **Core Flaw / Concern = Perpetual Tolls**:
  * Tolls collected indefinitely beyond capital recovery period.
  * **Causal Chain**: Perpetual Tolls → Transportation cost ↑ → Commercial goods freight ↑ → Raw material & finished goods cost ↑ → Manufacturing competitiveness & exports ↓.
* **Mains Fodder Point**: Cite Public Accounts Committee (PAC) report on perpetual tolls when answering challenges in India's road/highway infrastructure.

## RBI's Economic Capital Framework (ECF)

### Background & Asset-Liability Structure (2018–19)
* **Total Assets of RBI (Issue + Banking Depts)** ≈ **₹36 Lakh Crores**.
* **Total Contingency Reserves (Buffer)** = Accumulated over time (piggy bank model) ≈ **₹10 Lakh Crores**.
* **Combined Capital Base** = Assets (₹36 Lakh Cr) + Reserves (₹10 Lakh Cr) = **₹46 Lakh Crores**.

### Purpose of RBI Contingency Reserve Buffer
1. **Lender of Last Resort**: Funds required to rescue commercial banks facing bank runs / liquidity crises.
2. **Note Issuance Asset Matching**: Under Minimum Reserve System (MRS), printing fresh currency liabilities requires purchasing matching assets (Gold, FCA, G-Secs) using buffer reserves.

### The 2018–19 Government vs. RBI Conflict
* **Government Demand**: Finance Ministry demanded transfer of at least **1/3rd of RBI reserves** (~**₹3.33 Lakh Crores**), claiming RBI was over-capitalized.
* **RBI Governor (Urjit Patel) Counterarguments**:
  1. Buffer reserves are essential for central bank core functions (Lender of Last Resort & currency backing).
  2. RBI already transfers annual net profits as dividends to Government; dipping into accumulated contingency capital damages central bank autonomy and solvency.
* **Government Counterargument**: Major global central banks (e.g. **US Federal Reserve**) maintain significantly lower reserve-to-asset ratios than RBI.
* **Escalation**: Government threatened to invoke **Section 7 of RBI Act, 1934** (empowers Government to issue binding directions to RBI in "public interest").
* **Outcome**: Resignation of RBI Governor **Urjit Patel**.

### Core Underlying Structural Problems
1. **No objective criteria** for division of RBI net profits between internal reserves buffer vs. dividend transfer to Government.
2. **No objective criteria** to determine the ideal size / adequacy of central bank reserves.

### Bimal Jalan Committee & ECF
* **Appointment**: High-level committee headed by former RBI Governor **Bimal Jalan**.
* **Key Mandate**: Formulate objective **Economic Capital Framework (ECF)** to govern surplus distribution and reserve requirements.
* **Structural Reform**: Changed RBI accounting year to align with standard financial frameworks.
* **Current Status**: RBI set to adopt updated Economic Capital Framework in **2025–2026**.

## Deposit Insurance Framework: DICGC

### Core Provisions & Limits
* **Maximum Insurance Cover** = **₹5 Lakhs** per depositor per bank (inclusive of principal + interest).
  * *Coverage Examples*: Deposit ₹50,000 → Get ₹50,000; Deposit ₹10 Lakhs → Get ₹5 Lakhs max; Deposit ₹1 Crore → Get ₹5 Lakhs max.

### Coverage & Exclusion Criteria

| Parameter | Included in Deposit Insurance | Excluded from Deposit Insurance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deposit Types** | Savings, Current, Fixed Deposits (FD), Recurring Deposits (RD) | None (All commercial deposit types covered) |
| **Depositor Category** | Retail individuals, private entities, small businesses | **Inter-bank deposits**, **Central/State/Foreign Government deposits** |
| **Entity Category** | Commercial Banks, Small Finance Banks, Regional Rural Banks, Cooperative Banks | **Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)**, Chit Funds (e.g. Laxmi Chit Fund) |
### Insurance Premium Rules
* **Payer** = **100% borne by Banks** (RBI rules strictly prohibit passing premium costs to depositors).
* **Insurer Entity** = **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC)** (100% subsidiary of RBI).
* **Pre-2021 Premium System**: Flat rate ceiling of **0.15% of deposits** (15 paise per ₹100) uniform for all banks.
  * *Flaw*: Ignored risk profile (high-risk bank like Laxmi Vilas Bank paid same rate as low-risk SBI).
* **DICGC Amendment Act, 2021 Reform**: Introduced **Risk-Based Differential Premium** (premium varies bank-to-bank depending on risk profile).

### Payout Triggers & Timelines
* **Traditional Triggers**: Bank Liquidation (sale of assets) or Merger (highly time-consuming, > 1 year).
* **The PMC Bank Crisis & Moratorium Controversy**:
  * Punjab & Maharashtra Cooperative (PMC) Bank collapsed due to **> 70% NPAs** (bad loans).
  * Senior citizens (who deposited retirement funds for high ~11% interest) faced bank run.
  * RBI imposed **Moratorium** (temporary halt restricting deposit withdrawals to ₹50,000 over 6 months).
  * Issue: Moratorium severely impacted elderly emergency medical and personal needs.
* **DICGC Amendment Act, 2021 Reform**: Mandates payout of ₹5 Lakh deposit insurance within **90 days (3 months)** of a bank being placed under **Moratorium**.

### Recovery Rule
* **Post-Payout Recovery**: Once DICGC pays ₹5 Lakhs to depositors on behalf of a failed bank, the bank **remains legally liable to repay that amount back to DICGC** at a later date from asset liquidations.

## Green Deposits Framework

### Regulatory Guidelines (RBI)
* **Applicability**: Commercial Banks, Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) and Housing Finance Companies (HFCs, e.g. Indiabulls).
* **Eligible Deposit Nature**: Restricted strictly to **Fixed Deposits (FD)**.
* **Denomination Rule**: MUST be **Rupee-denominated only** (foreign currency / dollar deposits prohibited).
* **Mandatory Fund End-Use**: Funds raised must be strictly allocated as green loans for:
  1. Renewable energy projects (Solar, Wind, Hydro).
  2. Public transportation infrastructure (Metro rail).
  3. Climate change adaptation and mitigation.
  4. Energy efficiency projects (LED conversion, green buildings).

## Unified Public Tech Platform For Frictionless Credit (UPTPFC)

### Context: Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) / Digital Public Goods
* **UPI (Unified Payments Interface)**: Developed by NPCI as an **Open API** (Application Programming Interface); acts as a free "digital rail" for payment apps (PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm, WhatsApp).
* **Other Digital Public Goods**: **CoWIN** (vaccination platform API), **ONDC** (Open Network for Digital Commerce API).

### Public Tech Platform Features
* **Nodal Agency** = Developed and operated directly by **RBI**.
* **Nature** = Open API platform (**NOT a lending platform itself**; functions as an information aggregator).
* **Core Objective** = Streamlines and automates credit appraisal process ("UPI 2.0 for credit delivery").
* **Benefits**: Enables seamless data flow, eliminates physical paper movement between desks/branches, reduces loan processing time drastically.

### Integrated Stakeholder Databases
1. **Credit Information Companies (CICs)** (Credit history / CIBIL scores).
2. **Income Tax Department** (Income Tax Returns - ITR data).
3. **GST Network (GSTN)** (GST returns / business turnover verification).
4. **State Land Records** (Property titles & agricultural land ownership).
5. **Financial Asset Repositories** (Bank Fixed Deposits, Mutual Fund holdings).
6. **Aadhaar / PAN Databases** (Digital e-KYC, biometric verification, e-Sign).

   * Manages IT requirements, cybersecurity and tech infrastructure of RBI.

## Monetary Policy Fundamentals: Inflation & Causes

### Definition of Inflation
* **Layman Definition** = Rise in prices.
* **Economic Definition** = A **general** (across almost all goods & services) and **persistent** (continuous over time) increase in price levels, resulting in the erosion of purchasing power.

### Demand-Pull Inflation Cues & Examples
* **Mechanism**: Surge in demand pulls prices upward.
* **Examples**:
  * Surge in demand for N95 masks & sanitizers during COVID-19.
  * Rising per capita income driving demand for 4-wheeler passenger vehicles.
  * High Tatkal train ticket demand during festival travel seasons.
  * Viral consumer trends (e.g. Labubu dolls, Cryptocurrency/Bitcoin demand).
  * Festive seasonal demand for Gold (Akshaya Tritiya).
  * Seasonal demand for air purifiers and room heaters in Delhi winters.

### Cost-Push (Supply-Side) Inflation Cues & Examples
* **Mechanism**: Decrease in supply or surge in raw material input costs pushes final product prices upward.
* **Examples**:
  * Russia-Ukraine War → International crude oil prices ↑ → Domestic petrol/diesel freight costs ↑.
  * China export ban on rare earth magnets → Electric vehicle (EV) battery manufacturing costs ↑.
  * China Zero-COVID lockdowns → Global semiconductor supply chain disruption → Mobile phone, tablet & car prices ↑.
  * Extreme weather / unseasonal rain → Crop damage → Tomato / vegetable supply shocks.

### Dominant Factor Rule
* When both demand surges and supply drops simultaneously (e.g. festival tomato prices), the inflation type is classified based on whichever factor is **statistically dominant**.
