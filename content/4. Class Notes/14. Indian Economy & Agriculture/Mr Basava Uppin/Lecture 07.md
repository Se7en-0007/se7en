## Causes of Inflation: Demand-Pull vs. Cost-Push (Supply-Side)

### Core Mechanics
* **Underlying Cause** = **Demand-Supply Mismatch**, where demand and supply function as two **independent factors** (demand is independent of supply, and supply is independent of demand).

### Classification of Inflation Causes

| Parameter | Demand-Pull Inflation | Cost-Push / Supply-Side Inflation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Driver** | **Increase in Aggregate Demand** (assuming supply remains constant) | **Decrease in Supply** OR **Increase in Raw Material / Input Costs** |
| **Key Catalysts** | Surge in money supply, consumer income, seasonal demand | Natural disasters, supply chain shocks, export bans, raw material spikes |
| **Real-World Examples** | 1. AC price hikes during summer<br>2. Room heater price hikes during winter<br>3. Festive surge in gold demand<br>4. COVID-19 pandemic surge in mask demand | 1. **Heat Waves** → Wheat crop failure → Wheat prices ↑<br>2. **China Rare Earth Export Ban** → Electric battery prices ↑<br>3. **Russia-Ukraine War** → Spikes in crude oil, fertilizers & sunflower oil<br>4. **Monsoon Flooding of Coal Mines** → Electricity generation cost ↑ |

## Effects of Inflation: Stakeholder Perspective

### 1. Consumer Perspective
1. **Erosion of Affordability**:
   * Essential goods & services become unaffordable to low-income/poor households, depressing standard of living.
   * **Political Sensitivity Case Study (Onions)**:
     * High onion price spikes historically destabilize incumbent governments (e.g. historical fall of Indira Gandhi Govt; 2013 UPA Govt fall when onion prices hit **₹150–₹200/kg**).
   * **Recent Example (Tomatoes)**: Tomato prices touching **₹200/kg** forces a daily wage earner / rickshaw puller (earning ₹400–₹500/day) to completely eliminate tomatoes from their diet (richer households remain unimpacted).
2. **Decrease in Purchasing Power of Currency**:
   * **Definition** = With the same nominal amount of currency, a consumer can buy a **lesser quantity** of goods over time.
   * **Mathematical Proof**:
     * **Year 2025**: Income = ₹100; Apple Price = ₹10/apple → Purchases **10 apples**.
     * **Year 2026**: Income = ₹100; Apple Price = ₹20/apple → Purchases **5 apples**.
   * **Childhood Memory Anchor**: Finding a ₹1 coin in childhood yielded 4 orange candies (25 paise each); today a ₹1 change is ignored or replaced by shopkeepers with unwanted cheap toffees (reflects eroded purchasing power).

### 2. Producer Perspective
* **Illusion of Higher Profits**: Producers earn higher nominal prices on finished products, BUT experience **ZERO increase in Real Profit**.
* **Reason** = Inflation is a *general and persistent* rise in prices; raw material/input costs rise proportionately.
* **Input Cost Chain**:
  * **Inputs**: Seeds, fertilizers, water, electricity, pesticides.
  * **2025**: Input Cost = ₹5; Apple Selling Price = ₹10 → Net Profit = **₹5**.
  * **2026**: Input Cost rises by ₹10 (to ₹15); Apple Selling Price rises by ₹10 (to ₹20) → Net Profit = **₹5** (Unchanged).
* **Producer as Consumer Paradox**:
  * **2025**: Farmer sells 1 apple at ₹10 → Buys 1 orange at ₹10.
  * **2026**: Farmer sells 1 apple at ₹20 → Goes to market, but orange price also rose to ₹20 → Can still purchase only **1 orange**.
* **Interlinked Economic Ecosystem**:
  * Producers are simultaneously consumers; no entity operates in isolation.
  * **Local Ecosystem Example (Old Rajinder Nagar)**: Student volume directly dictates rental prices, which sequentially dictates business revenues of local hotels, restaurants, chaiwalas, and street vendors.

### 3. Overall Economy (Stagflation)
* **Causal Chain to Economic Slowdown**:
  Rising Input/Production Costs → Discourages Capital Investment → Job Creation Drops (Unemployment ↑) → Production of Goods/Services Drops → GDP Growth Rate Falls.
* **Concept = Stagflation**:
  * **Stagflation = Stagnation + Inflation**
  * Definition = High inflation rate simultaneously accompanied by economic stagnation (falling GDP growth + rising unemployment).

## Deflation and Economic Recession

### Core Definitions & Distinction
* **Deflation** = Sustained, persistent decrease in the general price level of goods and services.
* **Mathematical Relationships**:
  * **Inflation + Economic Slowdown = Stagflation**
  * **Deflation + Economic Slowdown = Recession**

### Causes of Deflation
1. **Decrease in Aggregate Demand** (assuming supply constant):
   * *Example*: COVID-19 national lockdowns halted mobility → Demand for petrol and diesel collapsed → Drastic fall in fuel prices.
2. **Increase in Aggregate Supply** (assuming demand constant):
   * *Example*: Bumper production harvest of tomatoes → Market glut → Price crash → Farmers forced to dump tomatoes on highways.

### Effects of Deflation
* **Primary Impact on Producer**: Falling prices directly shrink producer profit margins.
* **Secondary Impact on Consumer**:
  * Initial price drops offer short-lived consumer joy, BUT falling corporate profits lead to **layoffs and wage cuts**.
  * Income loss / Unemployment → Consumers lose purchasing power entirely (cannot buy goods even at low prices).
* **Impact on Economy**: Capital Investment ↓ + Job Creation ↓ + GDP Growth ↓ → **Recession**.

### The Virtuous Cycle of Moderate Inflation
* **Ideal State = Moderate Inflation (~4% in India)**:
  * Aggregate demand runs **slightly ahead of supply** (analogous to *Vikram and Betaal*: Demand is always 1 step ahead; Supply runs to catch up).
  * **Producer Incentive**: Moderate price rise (e.g. 4%) encourages producers to invest in capacity expansion because expected profit growth (e.g. 8%) exceeds input cost inflation.
  * **Consumer Stability**: Consumers accept a 4% price rise because general economic expansion / GDP growth (~6%) drives income growth above inflation (net real gain = +2%).
* **Virtuous Cycle Flowchart**:
  Moderate Inflation (Demand > Supply) → Producer Investment ↑ → Production & Output ↑ → Job Creation ↑ → Public Income Levels ↑ → Sustained Higher Demand → Repeat.
* **Global Target Benchmarks**:
  * **India (Developing Economy)** = Target **4%** (CPI).
  * **United States (Developed Economy)** = Target **2%** (US Federal Reserve).

## Basics of Monetary Policy & Interest Rate Transmission

### Core Definitions
* **Monetary Policy** = Policy tool utilized by the central bank (RBI) to regulate total money supply and liquidity in the economy.
* **Policy Stances**:
  * **Contractionary / Tight / Dear Monetary Policy**: Applied during **Inflation** → Increases policy rates to suck out excess liquidity.
  * **Expansionary / Cheap / Easy Monetary Policy**: Applied during **Recession** → Decreases policy rates to inject liquidity into the economy.

### Interest Rate Dynamics & Mechanics
1. **Loan Rate vs. Deposit Rate**: Rate of interest charged on loans is **always higher** than rate paid on deposits (Difference = Bank's Net Interest Margin/Spread).
2. **Co-movement**: Deposit and loan interest rates increase and decrease **simultaneously**.
3. **Indirect Influence**: RBI does **NOT** directly dictate commercial bank deposit or lending rates; RBI alters its policy rates (Repo / Reverse Repo) to nudge/force banks to adjust their rates.

### Taylor's Rule Framework
* **Core Rule**: Central banks should raise policy rates during inflation and lower policy rates during recession.
  * **Inflation** → Prices ↑ → Policy Rates ↑ → Borrowing Cost ↑ → Money Supply ↓.
  * **Recession** → Prices ↓ → Policy Rates ↓ → Borrowing Cost ↓ → Money Supply ↑.

## Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) & Inflation Targeting

### Statutory Background & Structure
* **Establishment**: Created in **2016** under the **RBI Act, 1934** (amended) based on recommendations of the **Urjit Patel Committee**.
* **Status**: **Statutory Body**.
* **Composition = 6 Members**:
  * **3 RBI Representatives**:
    1. **RBI Governor** (*Ex-officio Chairperson*).
    2. **RBI Deputy Governor** (in charge of monetary policy; 1 of 4 Deputy Governors).
    3. **1 Officer nominated by RBI Central Board** (out of 21 board members).
  * **3 Government Nominees**: Appointed by Central Government (experts in economics/banking).

### Governance Rules Matrix

| Parameter | Governing Provision |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tenure** | **4 Years** for nominated members (*Not eligible for reappointment*). |
| **Decision Mechanism** | Decisions taken by **Majority Vote**. |
| **Tie-Breaker** | In case of tie, RBI Governor possesses a **Casting Vote** (1 additional vote). |
| **Veto Power** | RBI Governor **DOES NOT enjoy Veto Power** (cannot override majority decision). |
| **Target Setting** | Central Government sets inflation target under *RBI Act, 1934* **every 5 years** in terms of **CPI**. |
| **Target Rate** | **4%** Consumer Price Index (CPI) with flexible tolerance band of **±2%** (**2% to 6%**). |
| **MPC Tool Scope** | MPC fixes **ONLY the Repo Rate** (all other rates like CRR, SLR, SDF are set by RBI Governor). |
| **Binding Status** | Decisions of MPC are **legally binding on RBI**. |
| **Meeting Cadence** | Statutory mandate = At least **4 meetings/year**; Present practice = **Bi-monthly** (every 2 months). |

### Accountability Framework
* **Bi-Annual Reporting**: MPC must submit a **Monetary Policy Report** every **6 months** to the Central Government.
* **Target Failure Protocol**:
  * **Failure Criteria** = RBI fails to maintain CPI inflation within the 2%–6% band for **3 consecutive quarters** (9 calendar months).
  * **Obligation** = RBI must submit a formal explanatory report to Government specifying:
    1. Reasons for failure.
    2. Remedial actions proposed.
    3. Estimated time frame required to achieve inflation target.

## Assets and Liabilities of Commercial Banks

### Classification Matrix

![[Balance Sheet (Bank).png|800]]

### Detailed Asset Breakdown & Nuances
1. **Cash Balances with RBI (CRR)**:
   * **Asset for Bank | Liability for RBI**.
   * *Logic*: Bank places customer cash reserves with RBI; money belongs to the bank, held at RBI.
2. **Call, Notice, and Term Money** (Interbank Lending < 1 Year):
   * **Call Money** = Interbank loan for **1 Day**.
   * **Notice Money** = Interbank loan for **2 to 14 Days**.
   * **Term Money** = Interbank loan for **> 14 Days and < 1 Year**.
   * *Convention*: Evaluated strictly from the perspective of the **lending bank** (hence classified as an Asset by default).
3. **Cash in Hand with Banks**:
   * Physical cash held inside bank vaults/branches is an **Asset**.
   * *Double-Entry Accounting Mechanics*: Customer deposits ₹1 Lakh cash → Bank logs ₹1 Lakh under **Deposits (Liability)** and simultaneously logs ₹1 Lakh under **Cash in Hand (Asset)** → Assets & Liabilities balance perfectly.

## Fixed Deposit (FD) vs. Certificate of Deposit (CD)

| Parameter | Fixed Deposit (FD) | Certificate of Deposit (CD) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Traditional time deposit placed by investor with bank | Short-term tradable money market debt instrument issued by bank |
| **Duration / Tenor** | Flexible: Short-term or Long-term (**< 1 year or > 1 year**) | Strictly **Short-Term** (Always **< 1 year**) |
| **Minimum Investment** | Very low (e.g. **₹500 / ₹1,000**) | High minimum entry barrier = **₹5 Lakhs** |
| **Collateral Eligibility** | **Can be used as collateral** to obtain bank loans | **Cannot be used as collateral** |
| **Tradability** | **Non-tradable** (cannot transfer to third parties) | **Tradable** in money markets (can be transferred) |
