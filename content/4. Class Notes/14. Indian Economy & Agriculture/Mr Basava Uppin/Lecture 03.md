## Role of RBI In Printing Bank Notes

### Common Misconceptions & Core Concepts
* **Childhood Queries**: On what basis does RBI print notes? Can RBI print unlimited notes?
* **Note Denominations in Circulation (India)**: ₹1 (printed by Govt), ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500 (*₹2000 notes withdrawn from circulation*).

### Terminology: Layman vs. Legal Classification
* **Layman Term** = "Currency Notes".
* **Legal Classification** = **Bank Notes** (Notes printed exclusively by RBI, i.e., ₹2 and above).
* **₹1 Note Legal Status** = Legally treated as a **Rupee Coin** (printed by Government of India).
* **Sole Authority**: RBI holds **sole authority** to print **Bank Notes** in India.

### Internal Structural Framework of RBI
* **Pre-1935 Setup**:
  * **Note Printing** → Handled by **Controller General of Currency**.
  * **Banker to Government** → Handled by **Imperial Bank of India**.
* **Post-1935 Setup (RBI Establishment)**:
  * Dual functions merged under RBI, split into two dedicated departments:
    1. **Issue Department** = Sole authority to print Bank Notes (does not perform banking operations).
    2. **Banking Department** = Acts exclusively as Banker to Government & Commercial Banks (does not print notes).

## Legal Framework, Design & Demonetization of Bank Notes

### Statutory Legal Framework
* **Statute** = **RBI Act, 1934** (empowers Issue Dept to issue notes and lays down governing rules).

### Design of Bank Notes
* **Visual Themes**: Red Fort, Sanchi Stupa, Rani ki Vav, etc.
* **Approval Process**:
  * **Recommended by** → **Central Board of RBI**.
  * **Approved by** → **Central Government**.
* **News Cue / Case Study**: Proposal by a State Chief Minister to print images of Hindu Gods/Goddesses (Lord Ganesha, Goddess Lakshmi) → Legally invalid for Govt to unilaterally alter design; proposal **must originate from Central Board of RBI**.

### Demonetization Process
* **Authority & Workflow**: Same as design approval.
  * **Recommended by** → **Central Board of RBI**.
  * **Approved by** → **Central Government**.
* **2016 Demonetization Controversy**: Opposition claimed proposal did not originate from RBI Central Board but was unilaterally declared by Govt (challenged in Supreme Court).

## Denomination Limits & Demonetization History

### Statutory Denomination Limits (*RBI Act, 1934*)
* **Minimum Denomination** = **₹2** (₹1 is legally a rupee coin).
* **Maximum Denomination** = **₹10,000**.

### Historical Chronology of High Denomination Notes
* **₹10,000 Note History**: Printed and demonetized **twice** in Indian history (**1938** & **1954**).
* **Demonetization Chronology in India**:
  1. **1st Demonetization** = **1946** (High denomination notes).
  2. **2nd Demonetization** = **1978** (₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000 notes under Morarji Desai Govt).
  3. **3rd Demonetization** = **2016** (₹500 & ₹1,000 notes under Modi Govt).

### Rationale: Why RBI Avoids High Denomination Notes
* **High Denomination Notes (e.g. ₹10,000 / ₹2,000)**:
  * High value + Low volume → Easy storage/transport → Facilitates **Bribes**, **Corruption**, **Black Money** hoarding (under mattresses/pillows).
  * Transaction friction → Severe shortage of change for small daily transactions.
* **Lower Denomination Strategy**:
  * High bulk volume → Harder to hoard/transport illicit cash → Checks black money & corruption.

## Why People Accept Bank Notes: Acceptance & Legal Tender

### Two Pillars of Acceptance
1. **Universal Acceptance**: Public confidence that accepted notes will be accepted by others in exchange (*Memory Anchor: Shawshank Redemption analogy*).
2. **Legal Tender Status**: Mandatory acceptance enforced by law under **RBI Act, 1934**.

### Legal Tender Concept: Layman vs. Technical Definition
* **Layman Definition**: Legal obligation on sellers/creditors to compulsorily accept notes; cannot insist on payment in Gold or US Dollars.
* **Technical Definition**:
  * **Creditor** = Entity/person to whom money is owed (e.g., seller, bank, cab driver).
  * **Debtor** = Entity/person who owes money (e.g., buyer, borrower, passenger).
  * **Legal Tender** = Money which a creditor is legally compelled to accept in settlement of their claims.
* **UPSC PYQ Check**: "Legal tender money = Money which a creditor is under compulsion to accept in settlement of his claims."

## Basis for Printing Notes: Assets, Liabilities & Bonds

### Asset vs. Liability Matrix
* **Asset** = Something that one **owns**.
  * Ownership is primary criterion (regardless of returns or depreciation).
  * May depreciate in value (e.g., car, mobile phone) or give zero returns (e.g., gold, old painting, stagnant share), but remains an asset.
  * Can be tangible (car, gold) or intangible (patents, goodwill).
* **Liability** = Something that one **owes** (belongs to another entity; mandatory obligation to repay/return).
  * Examples: Bank loans, credit card debt, unfulfilled promises (tangible/intangible).
* **Banking Operations Perspective**:
  * **Bank Deposit**: Asset for Depositor | Liability for Bank.
  * **Bank Loan**: Asset for Bank (if unrecovered → **Lost Asset**) | Liability for Borrower.
* **Bank Notes Perspective**:
  * **Holding Public**: Bank Note = **Asset** (earned and owned).
  * **RBI (Issuer)** = Bank Note = **Liability** (Inscribed promise: *"I promise to pay the bearer the sum of..."* signed by RBI Governor).

### Concept of Bonds
* **Bond** = Financial instrument issued by companies/governments to borrow money directly from investors.
* **Core Characteristics**:
  1. **Face Value** = Amount of total borrowings (e.g., ₹100).
  2. **Maturity** = Duration of borrowing (e.g., 5 years).
  3. **Coupon Rate** = Annual interest rate paid by issuer on face value (e.g., 5% = ₹5/year).
* **Secondary Market Dynamics**: Investor X can sell bond to Investor Y before maturity for liquidity; issuer pays annual coupon to current bondholder and repays face value at maturity.
* **Bond vs. Share**:
  * **Bondholder** = **Lender** to company (no ownership, fixed interest income).
  * **Shareholder** = **Owner** of company (equity stake, variable dividend/profit).
* **Financial Classification**:
  * **For Investor** = Bond is an **Asset** (earns coupon income + capital return).
  * **For Issuer** = Bond is a **Liability** (borrowed capital to be repaid).

## Mechanism of Printing Notes: Minimum Reserve System (MRS)

### Minimum Reserve System Framework
* **Regulatory Standard**: Followed by RBI Issue Department for note issuance.

### Two Mandatory Conditions under MRS
1. **Condition 1: Minimum Reserves = ₹200 Crores at all times**:
   * **₹115 Crores** in **Gold**.
   * **₹85 Crores** in **Foreign Currency Assets (FCA)**:
     * Foreign Currencies (e.g., USD, GBP, JPY, EUR, RMB).
     * Foreign Government Bonds (e.g., **US Treasury Bonds**).
2. **Condition 2: Balance Sheet Equality**:
   * **Total Liabilities of Issue Department = Total Assets of Issue Department**.
   * **Liabilities** = Total face value of Bank Notes in circulation.
   * **Assets** = Gold + Foreign Currency Assets + Government Securities (G-Secs) + ₹1 Notes/Coins.

### Empirical Data & Quantitative Limits
* **Circulation Statistics**: Total bank notes in circulation ≈ **₹33 Lakh Crores** = Total Liabilities of Issue Dept = Total Assets held by Issue Dept (matched up to two decimal places; highest share held in **Foreign Currency Assets**).
* **Quantitative Limit on Note Printing**:
  * **NO quantitative ceiling** on printing notes.
  * RBI can print unlimited currency provided it maintains **equivalent matching assets**.
* **Constraint against Unlimited Printing**: Excess money printing → Artificial demand surge → Severe **Inflation**.

## Government Printing of ₹1 Notes & Legal Tender Framework

### Guarantee Clause & ₹1 Note Mechanism
* **Note Inscription**: "Guaranteed by the Central Government".
* **Underlying Logic**: If RBI Issue Dept fails to fulfill its promise, Central Government steps in to honor the obligation on behalf of RBI.
* **Fulfilment Mechanism**: Govt issues **₹1 Notes** (and coins) to fulfill guarantee.

### ₹1 Note Features
* **Nodal Agency** = **Government of India** (under *RBI Act, 1934*).
* **Signatory** = **Finance Secretary** (NOT RBI Governor).
* **Promise Clause** = **ABSENT** (does not contain *"I promise to pay..."*).
* **Asset Reserve Requirement** = **ZERO** (Govt is NOT required to back ₹1 notes or coins with gold/forex assets; runs purely on public trust & universal acceptance).
* **Distribution** = Handled by **RBI**.

### Exhaustive List of Legal Tender Items in India
* Only **4 items** are legal tender in India:
  1. **Bank Notes** (₹2 to ₹10,000)
  2. **₹1 Notes**
  3. **Coins** (under *Coinage Act*)
  4. **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC / e-Rupee)**
* *Note: Cheques, Demand Drafts, Credit Cards and Cryptocurrencies are NOT legal tender.*

## Seigniorage: Net Profits on Currency Issuance

### Definition & Formula
* **Seigniorage** = Net profit earned by RBI Issue Department on printing bank notes.
* **Formula**:
  * **Seigniorage = Profits earned on printing notes - Cost of printing notes**
* **Costs Involved**: Security paper, specialized magnetic inks, advanced anti-counterfeiting security features (watermarks, security threads).

### Mechanism of Profit Generation by Issue Department
1. **Appreciation in Asset Value (e.g., Gold)**:
   * *Example*: Gold assets rise from ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 while liability remains fixed at ₹3,000 → RBI sells surplus gold (worth ₹3,000) to realize net cash profit while keeping liabilities and assets balanced.
2. **Interest Yield on Assets**:
   * Coupon interest earned on **US Treasury Bonds** (FCA) and **Indian Government Securities (G-Secs)**.

### Utilization of Seigniorage Profits
* **Buffer Allocation (Piggy Bank)** = Portion retained internally as contingency reserve (**Buffer**) to purchase assets during adverse asset value drops.
* **Dividend Transfer** = Surplus profit transferred to Government of India as **Dividend**.
* **Scale of Profit**: Issue Dept profit ≈ **₹2.7 Lakh Crores/year** (*higher than average monthly national GST collection of ~₹2 Lakh Crores*).

### Case Study: RBI Buffer Conflict & Governor Resignation (2018–19)
* **Background**: RBI contingency buffer reached **~₹10 Lakh Crores** (equivalent to India's annual direct tax collections).
* **Conflict**: Finance Ministry demanded 1/3rd transfer of buffer; Governor **Urjit Patel** resisted (arguing buffer was essential for currency backing & asset coverage) → Led to resignation and creation of **Economic Capital Framework (Bimal Jalan Committee)**.

## Assets & Liabilities Structure: Issue Dept vs. Banking Dept

| RBI Department | Liabilities | Assets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Issue Department** | **Bank Notes in circulation** (≈ ₹33 Lakh Cr) | Gold (ID), Foreign Currency Assets (ID), Government Securities (ID), ₹1 Notes & Coins |
| **Banking Department** | Govt Deposits, Bank Deposits (**Cash Reserve Ratio - CRR**) | Loans to Govt (**Ways & Means Advances - WMA**), Loans to Banks, Gold (BD), FCA (BD), G-Secs (BD) |

### Key Technical Terms
* **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** = Percentage of deposits commercial banks must mandatorily maintain as cash reserves with RBI.
* **Ways and Means Advances (WMA)** = Temporary loan facility provided by RBI to Central and State Governments to bridge cash flow mismatches.
