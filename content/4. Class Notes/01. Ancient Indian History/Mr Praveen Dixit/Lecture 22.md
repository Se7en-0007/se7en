## Western Kshatrapa Numismatics & Gupta Continuity

* **Western Kshatrapa Coinage Mechanics**:
  * **Design Origin**: Modeled on **Indo-Greek cast die-struck coins** in portrait style.
  * **Primary Metal**: Majority minted in **Silver**.
  * **Stylistic Motif**: Half-face profile portrait of king with long hair locks curling at ends (nobility symbol across cultures) + symbols (pyramidal horses, weapons).
  * **Nahapana Counter-Stamping**: Overstamped by Satavahana King **Gautamiputra Satakarni** following victory.
* **Gupta Coinage in Gujarat & Historical Proof**:
  * **Scythian-Style Gupta Coins**: Discovered in Gujarat region from **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)** onwards (early 5th c. AD) through **Kumaragupta** and **Skandagupta**.
  * **Economic Principle**: Conquerors adopt loser's coinage style to ensure economic continuity & tax collection without disrupting local commerce (e.g., EIC retaining Rupee, Muhammad Ghori retaining Goddess Lakshmi on coins, Guptas adopting Scythian style in Gujarat).
  * **Political Proof**: Complete absence of Western Kshatrapa coins post-early 5th c. AD proves **Gupta Empire permanently ended Western Kshatrapa rule**.
* **Coins as Source of Political History (5 Anchors)**:
  1. Victory / Overstamping (Gautamiputra Satakarni over Nahapana).
  2. Territorial Conquest (Gupta Scythian-style silver coins in Gujarat).
  3. Foreign Presence / Influx (Indo-Greek cast die-struck coins).
  4. Dynastic Origin / Homeland (Early Satavahana lead coins found in Pratisthan / Maharashtra vs. Andhra).
  5. Religious Alignment (Shiva / Buddha / Parsi deities on Kushana coins).

## Western Cave Architecture & Origins of Temple Architecture

* **Karla Maha-Chaitya (1st Century AD)**:
  * **Patron**: Built by Western Kshatrapa King **Nahapana**.
  * **Significance**: Benchmark / signature of **Chaitya architecture**; inspired all subsequent Chaityas (e.g., Ajanta, Kanheri).
  * **Structural Anatomy**:
    * **Plan**: **Apsidal Plan** (semi-circular / curved at the inner end).
    * **Interior Layout**: 19 pillars per side; monks meditate in side aisles between pillars and wall; central Stupa at the apsidal end.
    * **Ceiling & Arch**: Semi-circular vaulted ceiling supported by **wooden arches** (preserved for 2000+ years without termite damage).
    * **Ajanta Parallel**: Ajanta Chaitya replicates Karla's apsidal plan and wooden arches, adding a **standing Buddha image carved onto the Stupa**.
* **Evolution of Deccan Temple Architecture (Chalukya Dynasty)**:
  * **Chronology & Style**: Earliest structural temples in Deccan built by **Chalukya Dynasty** (7th c. AD) in **Vesara Style**.
  * **Apsidal Plan Adaptation**: Earliest Chalukya temples built on an **apsidal plan** directly inspired by rock-cut Chaitya caves of Deccan (e.g., **Durga Temple at Aihole**).

## Parthians (Pahlavas)

* **Origins & Migration**:
  * Native to **Iran**; gained independence from Seleucid Empire in 2nd c. BC.
  * Expanded eastward in 1st c. BC → Pushed **Scythians (Sakas)** toward India.
  * Inscribed as **Pahlava** in ancient Indian literature (frequently coupled as *Shaka-Pahlava*).
  * Infiltrated NW India by late 1st c. BC → Conquered Indus region.
* **Dynastic Highlights**:
  * **King Gondophares (1st c. AD)**: First prominent ruler; capital at **Taxila**.
  * **Arrival of St. Thomas**: Received **St. Thomas** (Apostle of Jesus Christ) at Taxila court; earliest recorded source = 17th-century Portuguese literature.
  * **Kushana Subjugation**: Eastern Parthian domain (Afghanistan & Indus) conquered by **Kushanas** in mid-1st c. AD.
* **Geopolitical & Trade Impact**:
  * **Persian Gulf Blockade**: Blocked Persian Gulf ports to Greek traders in 1st c. AD → Forced Greeks to navigate deep Arabian Sea routes.
  * **Monsoon Discovery (1st c. AD)**: Greek naval commander **Hippalus** discovered monsoon trade winds (June–July SW monsoon to India; Oct–Nov NE monsoon back) → Revolutionized direct Indian Ocean maritime trade.
  * **Sassanid Successors (3rd – 7th c. AD)**: Parthians absorbed by **Sassanid Empire** (Zoroastrian); Sassanids contemporary of **Guptas** and **Chalukyas** (e.g., embassies exchanged between Chalukya King **Pulakeshin II** and Sassanid King **Khosrow II**, depicted at Ajanta).
  * **Islamic Conquest & Refugee Migration**: 7th-century Umayyad conquest of Iran forced Zoroastrians (**Parsis**) to migrate to Gujarat and Maharashtra (ruled by Chalukyas) → India as continuous safe haven for persecuted minorities (Jews, Parsis).

## Kushanas: Origins & Dynastic Lineage

* **Nomenclature & Migration**:
  * **Yuezhi / Yueh-chi Tribe**: Native to **Western China**.
  * **Westward Push**: Defeated by Xiongnu (Chinese) in 2nd c. BC → Migrated west to Central Asia for 200 years → Displaced Scythians southward (who displaced Indo-Greeks into India).
  * **Guishuang (Gujuang)**: Central Asian branch name → Phonetic shift (G → K in western dialects) → **Kushwan / Kushana**.
  * **Indian Literature Anchor**: Mentioned in ancient Indian texts as **Tushara**.
  * **Subjugation of Sakas**: Moved south in 1st c. AD → Conquered Afghanistan and Indus from Parthians → Subordinated Sakas in Northern/Western India (Sakas ruled as Kushana Satraps).
* **Key Kings & Achievements**:
  1. **Kujula Kadphises (30 – 70 AD)**:
     * First king to lead Kushanas into India by defeating Parthian Empire.
     * Capital: **Kapisa (Kabul)**.
     * Religion: Follower of **Shaivism** (Shiva depicted on majority of coins).
  2. **Vima Kadphises**:
     * Expanded empire across Punjab to Mathura (Yamuna River as eastern boundary).
     * Dual Capitals: **Kapisa (Kabul)** and **Pushkalavati** (Purushapura / Peshawar).
     * Religion: Follower of **Shaivism** (Shiva on coins).
  3. **Kanishka (4th King - 2nd Century AD)**:
     * **Territorial Peak**: Bengal, Central India, Balochistan, Central Asia and Western China.
     * **Tri-Capital Architecture**: **Kapisa** (Begram/Kabul), **Pushkalavati** (Peshawar) and **Mathura**.
     * **Rabatak Inscription (Afghanistan)**: Epigraphic text recording Kanishka's Central Asian victories; shifted Kanishka's chronology from 78 AD to 2nd c. AD.
     * **Peace Treaty with Pataliputra**: Defeated Pataliputra → Demanded & acquired 2 things: **Begging Bowl of Buddha** and Sanskrit poet/scholar **Ashvaghosha**.
     * **Religious Shift**: Follower of **Sarvastivada Buddhism** → Later patronized **Mahayana Buddhism**.
     * **Coin Deities**: Majority depicted Buddha; others depicted Shiva and Zoroastrian/Persian deities.
  4. **Huvishka (150 – 180 AD)**:
     * Son of Kanishka; maintained empire's full territorial extent.
     * Religion: Follower of **Zoroastrianism** (Parsi deities on majority of coins; Shiva & Buddha on some).
  5. **Vasudeva**:
     * Last strong Kushana emperor.
     * Decline: Rulers post-Vasudeva known as **Little Kushanas**.
     * 3rd c. AD Collapse: Western territories lost to **Sassanid Empire**; Eastern territories lost to **Naga Dynasty (Bharashiva)** and **Gana-Sanghas**.
     * Subordinate Status: Mentioned in Samudragupta's **Prayag Prasasti** as a minor subordinate ruler in NW India.

## Fourth Buddhist Council & Buddhist Schisms

* **Fourth Buddhist Council at Kundalvana (Kanishka)**:
  * **Patron**: **Kanishka**; **Time**: 2nd Century AD.
  * **Location**: **Kundalvana** (Kashmir); **President**: **Vasumitra**.
  * **Core Objective**: Compile elaborate commentaries on Buddhist philosophy in **Sanskrit** to make Buddhism attractive to commoners and foreigners.
  * **Primary Results**:
    1. **Abhidhamma Mahavibhasha Shastra**: Master compilation of philosophical commentaries (*Bhaskya* = elaborate commentary).
    2. **Vaibhashika Sect**: Emerged using *Mahavibhasha Shastra* as representative literature.
    3. **Birth of Mahayana**: Kundalvana Council marks the official formalization and spread of **Mahayana Buddhism**.
* **Fourth Theravada Council in Sri Lanka**:
  * **Rejection**: Theravada / Hinayana traditions reject Kundalvana Council as illegitimate.
  * **Location & Date**: **Anuradhapura**, Sri Lanka (25 BC) under King **Vattagamani (Walagamba)**.
  * **Objective & Result**: Recompiled and preserved Buddhist literature following a severe famine by writing the canon down on **Palm Leaf Manuscripts**.
* **Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi Tree & Bodh Gaya History**:
  * **Sri Lankan Sapling**: Ashoka sent a branch of original Bodhi tree to Anuradhapura → **Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi Tree** (still survives).
  * **Bodh Gaya Tree Destruction**: Original Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya cut down in 7th c. AD by Gauda King **Shashanka** during conflict with Harshavardhana.

## Philosophical Foundations: Sarvastivada vs. Mahayana vs. Hinayana

* **Sarvastivada Philosophy (Transcendental Buddhism)**:
  * **Core Doctrine**: Universe governed by eternal laws of Buddha existing in Past, Present and Future (*Yatra-Tatra-Sarvatra* → *Sarvastivada*).
  * **Ontology**: Buddha is not merely a historical human (*Siddhartha Gautama*); the entire cosmic universe is Buddha and all beings exist inside him. Formed the bed-rock of **Mahayana**.
* **Mahayana vs. Hinayana (Theravada) Contrast**:
  * **Hinayana Critique**: Accused Mahayana of copying Upanishadic / Vedantic monism (*Brahma / Atman*) and abandoning historical human Buddha.
  * **Mahayana Innovations**: Celestial Buddhas (**Vairochana**, **Amitabha**), Bodhisattvas of compassion (**Avalokiteshvara / Padmapani**), future Buddha (**Maitreya**) and worship of Buddha images.

## Kushana Economy, Trade Networks & Currency

* **Unified Market & Monopoly**:
  * Unified Bengal, Central India, Balochistan, Central Asia and Western China into a single market.
  * Acted as chief middlemen of **China-Europe Silk Trade**.
  * Monopolized entire **Uttarapatha**: Controlled land exports to China, Central Asia, West Asia, Europe and North Indian exports to SE Asia.
  * Controlled **Makran Coast** (Karachi-Balochistan) → Dominance over Persian Gulf branch of Indian Ocean trade network.
* **4 Trans-Continental Routes to Europe**:
  1. *Uttarapatha / Silk Road*: Overland via Khyber Pass to Europe.
  2. *Iran Route*: Overland via Bolan Pass & Kandahar to Iran/Europe.
  3. *Persian Gulf Route*: Maritime to Tigris-Euphrates basin to Europe.
  4. *Red Sea Route*: Maritime to Egypt / Mediterranean Europe.
  * **Kushana Dominance**: Controlled **3 out of the 4 global routes** to Europe.
* **Key Trade Arteries & Cities**:
  * **Karakoram Highway**: Connected Mathura directly to China via Karakoram Pass (prospered trade hubs: Turpan, Kashgar, Bamiyan, Kandahar, Ghazni, Kabul).
  * **Gujarat-Mathura Highway**: Connected Mathura to Gujarat ports via Mewar (South Rajasthan).
* **Coinage & High Monetization**:
  * Minted vast volume of coins across **all denominations and metals** (Gold, Silver, Copper).
  * **High Monetization**: Local markets operated on cash; substantial tax collected in cash (contrast with Guptas who minted mostly gold coins as store of wealth, resulting in lower trade monetization).
* **Export Portfolio**:
  * **Agricultural & Food**: Oil seeds (mustard, sesame / *til*).
  * **Spices & Mineral**: **Rock Salt / Pink Himalayan Salt** from **Mount Oromenus** (Salt Range, Pakistan; Chinese texts record salt as Kushanas' primary wealth source).
  * **Textiles**: **Shataka** (famous cotton textile of Mathura) and woolen textiles.
  * **Crafts & Luxury**: Perfumes (*Gandha* → *Gandhikas*), jewelry, ivory, iron implements (Jharkhand ore) and Taxila pottery/utensils.
  * **Raw Metals**: Gold, Silver, Copper, Tin and Lead extracted from Balochistan mines (Makran/Gwadar region).

## Imperial Administration, Titles & Cultural Innovations

* **Multicultural Administrative Architecture**:
  * Empire divided into **Satrapies** ruled by Satraps; each satrapy retained local traditional administration → **No uniform administrative system**.
  * **Joint Rule System**: Joint rule of **Father and Son** as co-kings; prevented fratricide / brother-killing typical of Central Asian dynasties.
* **Multicultural Titles**:
  * *Soter* (Greek = Savior).
  * *Kaiser* (Roman = Caesar / Emperor).
  * *Shah-in-Shah / Badshah* (Persian = King of Kings).
  * *Maharaja* (Indian = Great King).
  * *Devaputra* (Indian/Chinese inspired = Son of Gods).
  * **Vertical Script Alignment**: Coin inscriptions written vertically (Chinese linguistic influence).
* **Clothing & Lifestyle Innovations**:
  * Pre-Kushana: Single unstitched cloth (dhoti/lungi/vasti), no trousers, top cloth, slippers/barefoot.
  * Kushana Introductions:
    1. **Trousers**: Stitched lower garments (pajamas/jeans).
    2. **Kurta / Tunic**: Stitched upper garments with sleeve and neck openings.
    3. **Long Coats**: Front-open buttoned coats for cold climates.
    4. **Leather Boots & Shoes**: Replaced slippers and bare feet.
* **Cavalry & Military Innovations**:
  * **Reins (Lagam)**: Encased horse's mouth with rope passing between jaws for precise steering control.
  * **Stirrups (Rikab)**: Introduced hanging foot-rests allowing riders to place full foot and stand, providing superior balance and combat maneuverability (replaced old practice of tying toe-knots).
