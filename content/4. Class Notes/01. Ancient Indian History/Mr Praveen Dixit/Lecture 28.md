## Gupta Sculpture & School of Art

* **Material & Lineage**: Executed in **Buff-colored Sandstone**; direct upgrade & refinement over **Mathura School of Art**.
* **Key Visual Features**:
  * **Proportional Anatomy**: Proportional body ratio (face = 1/9th to 1/10th of body, inspired by classical proportion rules).
  * **Surface & Expression**: Smoother stone surfaces, heightened naturalism, serene & expressive faces, dynamic sense of movement.
  * **Curly Hair Representation**: Curly hair depicted as uniform **dots** → Symbolizes **108 Snails** sent by gods to cool Buddha's head during intense meditation.
* **Female Sculpture & Social Norms**:
  * **More Clothes, Less Jewelry**: Shift from Yakshini iconography (heavy jewelry, unclothed upper body) to draped figures (e.g. **Goddess Ganga**).
  * **Academic Interpretation**: Introduction of patriarchal rules requiring women's bodies to be covered vs. artistic evolution of spiritual purity.
* **Sarnath Sub-School of Art (4th – 6th Century AD)**:
  * **Dominant Sub-School**: Dominant genre of Gupta art; themes **exclusively Buddhist** (with minor Hindu motifs).
  * **Iconographic Identifiers**:
    * **Halo**: Intricately carved, highly **decorative halo (Prabhavali)** behind Buddha's head.
    * **Earlobes**: Over-elongated earlobes almost touching shoulders (Kshatriya prince jewelry mark).
    * **Drapery**: **Transparent drapery covering BOTH shoulders** → Cloth outline visible only along hem/end lines; transparent fold lines cut across knees and feet.
    * **Facial Expression**: Serene, meditative, eyes half-closed (inward contemplation).
* **Sculptural Naturalism & Dynamic Realism**:
  * **Bal Krishna Killing Keshi** (Sarnath): Child Krishna killing horse demon Keshi; depicts body tilt, muscular tension and realistic throat anatomy of dead horse.
  * **Dashavatara Temple Sculptures** (Deogarh):
    * **Bhima**: Holding heavy mace (*Gada*) → Feet positioned wide/outward to shift center of mass and balance weight.
    * **Yudhishthira**: Pulling dagger from sheath → Heel lifted off ground to absorb force.

## Mural Paintings: Techniques, Ajanta, Ellora & Bagh Caves

* **Mural vs. Fresco vs. Tempera (Technical Classification)**:
  * **Mural**: Any painting executed directly on a wall.
  * **Fresco**: Mural painted over a **limestone plaster** (*Choona*) surface:
    * **Wet Fresco (Fresco Buono)**: Painted while limestone plaster is wet → Colors merge into lime, highly durable/permanent, but colors lose vibrancy (whitish tone).
    * **Dry Fresco (Fresco Secco)**: Painted after limestone plaster dries → Colors highly vibrant, but less merged/durable.
  * **Tempera**: Painting executed over a **mud or cow-dung plaster** surface (common in Indian village murals).
* **Ajanta Caves Painting Style (Vakataka & Gupta Period)**:
  * **Technical Features**: Dry fresco on lime/mud plaster; **monochromatic color palette** (limited colors: red, green, yellow, brown); **red outlines** merging into body color (red outline = high budget, specialized outline painters).
  * **Facial & Anatomical Details**: **Almond-shaped elongated eyes**; joined eyebrows (*Bhruti*); frontal decorative head knots; lean but broad bodies.
  * **Multi-Racial Population Depiction**: Dark and light-complexioned figures painted side-by-side → **Indicates multi-racial population in ancient India** without caste/job hierarchy tied to skin color.
  * **Foreign Depictions**: Roman, Persian (Persian caps), Chinese and Egyptian figures depicted → **Proves vibrant long-distance maritime & overland trade along Western India**.
  * **Bodhisattva Representation**: Buddha and Bodhisattvas depicted in diverse body colors → Shows Buddha belongs to all races/regions, not exclusive to UP/Bihar.
* **Ellora Caves Paintings**:
  * Poorer survival rate; more colorful than Ajanta; heavy prevalence of **green and red** (signature style of Jain paintings, e.g., Lord Mahavira; also seen in **Sittanavasal** Pallava paintings); covers both **Hinduism & Jainism**.
* **Bagh Caves Paintings** (Dhar, MP):
  * Executed under Vakatakas/Guptas; broad bodies, thick lips, dark/African and Egyptian-like features; use of rare **Lapis Lazuli Blue** pigment making monochromatic tones look vibrant.

## Vesara Style Temple Architecture

* **Definition**: Hybrid architectural style formed by the **merger of Nagara (Northern) and Dravida (Southern) styles**.
* **Earliest Specimen**: Built by **Chalukya Dynasty** → **Virupaksha Temple at Pattadakal** (Bagalkot district, Karnataka).
* **Evolution & Pinnacle**:
  * Developed further by **Rashtrakutas** (**Kailashnath Temple at Ellora** - rock-cut monolithic Vesara).
  * Reached its **architectural pinnacle under Hoysalas** (Dwarasamudra / Halebidu; **Hoysaleswara Temple** - star-shaped *Stellata* plan, black soapstone/chloritic schist, intricate 3D carvings).
  * Post-Hoysala: Vijayanagara Empire preferred Dravida style over Vesara.

## Historiographical Debate: Gupta Age – Golden Age vs. Age of Decadence

| Dimension | Golden Age Arguments (Nationalist / Right-Wing) | Age of Decadence Arguments (Marxist / Left-Wing) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Travel Accounts** | Fa-Hien records peace, prosperity, free hospitals, no theft. | De-urbanization began; ancient trade cities declined. |
| **Economy & Land** | Massive agrarian expansion via state land grants. | Rise of **Feudalism (*Samantavada*)** via land grants; trade/highways declined. |
| **Art & Architecture** | Pinnacle of sculpture (Sarnath), painting (Ajanta) and Nagara temples. | Art became elite-sponsored; restricted to royal courts and urban guilds. |
| **Literature** | Diverse academic & religious literature; masterpieces of **Kalidasa**. | Literature became elite-centric through complex **Classical Sanskrit** (*Kavya*). |
| **Science & Tech** | Aryabhata (Math/Astronomy), Varahamihira, rustless Mehrauli Iron Pillar. | Science remained restricted to elite guilds; lack of widespread application. |
| **Law & Judiciary** | Dharmashastras formalised; **first clear demarcation of Civil & Criminal law**. | Excessive laws increased social rigidity and red-tapeism. |
| **Position of Women** | Rise of female deities (**Lakshmi, Durga, Ganga, Yamuna**) on coins. | Female deity worship used to enforce pure/pious (*Sati-Savitri*) patriarchal norms; **first inscriptional proof of Sati (510 AD Eran Inscription)**. |
| **Social Structure** | Flexible society enabling rapid scientific/cultural growth. | Institutionalized untouchability; birth of **5th Varna (Pancham Varna / Avarna / Antyaja)**. |
| **Religion** | Reached Classical status; template for all future eras. | Decline of Buddhism began; rising social exclusions. |

## 7th Century AD Geopolitical History & Regional Dynasties

### Dynastic Landscape
* **Vishnukundin Dynasty**: Capital at **Vengi** (Andhra Pradesh).
* **Pushyabhuti / Vardhana Dynasty**: Capital at **Sthanisvara** (Thanesar, Haryana).
  * King **Prabhakaravardhana** → Children: **Rajyavardhana** (elder son), **Harshavardhana** (younger son), **Rajyashri** (daughter).
* **Maukhari Dynasty**: Capital at **Kannauj** (Ganga-Yamuna Doab). King **Grahavarman** married Rajyashri.

### Tripartite Conflict & Treachery Drama
* **Alliance**: Gauda King **Shashanka** (Bengal) + **Malwa King** attacked Maukharis, killed Grahavarman and imprisoned Rajyashri.
* **Treachery**: Rajyavardhana marched to defend Kannauj, signed a peace treaty with Shashanka, but Shashanka **treacherously assassinated Rajyavardhana**.
* **Ascension of Harsha**: Teenager **Harshavardhana** (17–18 yrs) crowned king; merged Pushyabhuti and Maukhari kingdoms; shifted capital from **Sthanisvara to Kannauj**.
* **Rescue of Rajyashri**: Harsha tracked Rajyashri into Vindhyan forests, rescuing her minutes before she attempted Sati.
* **Conquest**: Harsha allied with **Bhaskaravarman** (Kamarupa / Assam) and Later Guptas → Defeated Gauda/Malwa and killed Shashanka.

### Battle of Narmada (618 AD) & Chalukya-Pallava Conflict
* **Battle of Narmada (618 AD)**: Harsha expanded south → Blocked by **Chalukya King Pulakeshin II** (Capital: Vatapi/Badami) → Pulakeshin II defeated Harsha at Narmada River, halting Harsha's southern expansion.
  * **Aihole Prashasti**: Authored by court poet **Ravikirti** in Sanskrit; records Pulakeshin II's victory over Harsha (Harsha's joy/elephants vanished like dew before the sun). Banabhatta's *Harshacharita* remains silent on this defeat.
* **Eastern Chalukyas (620 AD)**: Pulakeshin II conquered Vishnukundins and established the **Eastern Chalukya / Chalukyas of Vengi** branch in Andhra Pradesh.
* **Conflict with Pallavas**:
  * **630 AD**: Pulakeshin II defeated **Pallava King Mahendravarman I**.
    * *Mahendravarman I (600–630 AD)*: Great scholar-king; authored 2 Sanskrit plays: ***Mattavilasa Prahasana*** (satire on Kapalika/Pasupata tantric ascetics) and ***Bhagavadajjukayam*** (Bhakti play); held titles **Chitrakarapuli** (tiger among painters) and **Vichitrachitta** (curious mind).
  * **Battle of Vatapi (642 AD)**: Mahendravarman's son **Narasimhavarman I (Mahamalla)** defeated and killed Pulakeshin II; took title **Vatapikonda** (Conqueror of Vatapi).
    * *Narasimhavarman I*: Title *Mahamalla* (great wrestler); port city **Mamallapuram / Mahabalipuram** named after him.

### Literary Works of Harshavardhana
1. ***Ratnavali***: Love story of King Udayana and Ratnavali (Ceylon princess disguised as servant in Queen Vasavadatta's palace); Yaugandharayana's political intrigue.
2. ***Priyadarshika***: Love story of King Udayana and Priyadarshika.
3. ***Nagananda***: Buddhist-themed play; story of Prince **Jimutavahana** sacrificing his life to Garuda to save the Naga (snake) clan.

## Travel Account of Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang / Si-Yu-Ki)

* **Background**: Chinese Buddhist monk/scholar who visited India (630–645 AD) during Harsha's reign to study **Mahayana Buddhism**.
* **Text**: Authored ***Si-Yu-Ki*** (*Record of the Western Regions*).
* **Nomenclature**: Referred to India as **Shianju / Tianzhu** (Chinese phonetic rendering of *Sindhu*).
* **Four Contemporary Kings Recorded**:
  1. **Harshavardhana** (Kannauj)
  2. **Bhaskaravarman** (Kamarupa / Assam)
  3. **Pulakeshin II** (Vatapi Chalukya)
  4. **Narasimhavarman I** (Pallava of Kanchi)
* **Assemblies Attended**: **Kannauj Assembly** (honoring Mahayana Buddhism & Xuanzang) and **Prayag Assembly** (Kumbh / 5-yearly royal charity distribution).
* **Key Observations on India**:
  * **Cities**: Ancient Buddhist centers declining (Pataliputra, Kapilavastu, Vaishali); new cities emerging (Kannauj); cities unplanned, dirty, circular roads; houses built of mud, wood, cow dung and old bricks.
  * **Highways & Safety**: Highways deserted and unsafe due to trade decay; **Xuanzang himself was robbed twice in dense forests**.
  * **Society & Diet**: Prosperous citizens; finest textiles and jewelry; majority **vegetarian** (avoiding garlic, onion, meat, alcohol); **absence of Purdah system**; prevalence of female education; practice of **Sati** mentioned; **rigid caste system**.
  * **Religion**: **Puranic Hinduism dominant**, Buddhism declining; 21 religions/sects coexisting in harmony; voluntary conversion.
  * **Education System**: High respect for education; education received between ages 9 and 30 (sometimes lifelong); oral instruction in **Sanskrit** via debates.
  * **Harsha's Governance**: Harsha depicted as a devoted Mahayana Buddhist, hard-working, inspecting subjects regularly; light taxation (**1/6th rate = Shashta**); repeated offenders received capital punishment or limb mutilation; **Revenue divided into 4 parts**:
    1. Administration & State Expenses (1/4th)
    2. Salaries of Ministers & Officers (1/4th)
    3. Rewards for Scholars, Poets & Intellectuals (1/4th)
    4. Religious Charity & Donations (1/4th)
* **Geographical Concept of India**: Clear cultural and spatial concept of **Shianju** stretching from Assam (Kamarupa) in the East to Khyber/North-West and down to Tamil Nadu/Kerala in the South.
