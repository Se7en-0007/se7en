## Evolution of Sangam Polity & Administrative Structure

* **Early Sangam Age (Decentralized Base)**:
  * Polity highly **decentralized**.
  * Local regions dominated by **Velirs** (local chiefs).
  * **Velir Function**: Controlled villages, collected local revenue and retained majority of taxes for themselves.
* **Middle Sangam Age (Feudal Integration)**:
  * Army commanders (captains) of major kingdoms defeated **Velirs**.
  * **Outcome**: Velirs subordinated or eliminated. Subordinated Velirs converted into **feudal lords** of kingdoms.
  * **Direct Tax System**: Elected village assemblies (**Avvai** or **Manram**) bypassed local chiefs and began paying taxes directly to the central State.
* **King's Executive Machinery**:
  * **Aimperunkulu** = Council of **5 Ministers** (*Aim / Anju* = 5, *Peru* = Great, *Kulu* = Council / Family Heads).
  * **Enperayam** = Group of **8 Bureaucrats** (*En* = 8, *Perayam* = Bureaucracy).
  * **Lower Bureaucracy**: Majority drawn from families of landed farmers (**Vellalars**).
* **State Revenue & Nature of Warfare**:
  * Kingdoms warring in nature; **war tribute** formed a primary source of state wealth alongside agriculture and trade.
  * **Primary Triggers for War**: Cattle raids, territorial expansion and personal/dynastic revenge.
  * **Political Pretext**: Wars fought under pretext of liberating populations from the "tyranny" of neighboring kingdoms.

## Sangam Kings & Epigraphic Anchors

* **Megalithic Foundation**: Chiefs of Megalithic cultures = **Velirs** of Sangam literature.
* **Dynastic Corpus**: Over 400 kings mentioned in Sangam poems; only a few historically/epigraphically verified.
* **Famous Chola Kings**:
  * **Elara**: Conquered Sri Lanka (corroborated by Sri Lankan Buddhist chronicles).
  * **Karikala (Karikalan = "Black as Coal")**:
    * Reclaimed father's lost kingdom.
    * Extended northern conquest till Himalayas via military alliance with a northern "King with 100 ears" (**Satakarni / Satavahana**).
* **Famous Chera King**:
  * **Senguttuvan ("Red Chera" or "Good Chera")**: Highlighted as the principal Sangam Chera monarch in historical analysis.
* **Famous Pandya King**:
  * **Nedunjeliyan (Nedunjelian)**:
    * Associated with the **Mangulam Inscription** (Madurai, Tamil Nadu).
    * **Language**: Tamil | **Script**: **Tamil Brahmi**.
    * **Historiographical Epigraphy Debate**:
      * **Ashokan / Mauryan Brahmi** (North) vs. **Tamil Brahmi** (South).
      * Debated whether Tamil Brahmi represents an independent/earlier origin or a southern adaptation of Ashokan Brahmi.

## Position of Women In Sangam Society

- **HISTORICAL TRAJECTORY OF WOMEN'S STATUS IN INDIA**
	- Early Ages (Vedic / Sangam) → Higher Social Status & Participation
	- Middle / Late Classical Period → Gradual Decline in Status & Increasing Social Restrictions
	- 19th Century Reform Movements → Legal & Social Protection; Revival of Women's Education
	- 20th Century Service Sector Expansion → Economic Independence & Progress Towards Gender Equality

---

* **Relative Baseline**: Position of women in Sangam Age relatively better than in later classical/medieval times.
* **Social & Economic Participation**:
  * Women actively involved in social, economic and agrarian activities.
  * Female literacy encouraged; female child not denied education.
  * Majority practiced **monogamy**.
* **Prevalence of Love Marriages**:
  * Literature extensively mentions love marriages → Indicates **adult marriage**, **free consent of girls** and **unsegregated social intermingling** of males and females.
* **Status of Prostitutes (Kanigaiar)**:
  * Prostitution was a **legal, tax-paying profession**.
  * Prostitutes lived inside main cities (not segregated ghettos) → Indicates higher social status, protection from male middlemen and legal state recognition (e.g., **Madhavi** in *Cilappatikaram*, **Amrapali** in Vaishali, Vijayanagar tax system).
* **Female Literary Contribution**:
  * Out of ~350 known Sangam poets, **60 were women** (~1/6th of total corpus).
* **Negative Social Features**:
  * Ruling elites practiced **polygamy**.
  * Prevalence of **widow suicide (Sati)** and harsh, austere living conditions forced upon surviving widows.

## Religion, Cults & Sacred Worship

* **Brahmanical / Hindu Pantheon**: Worship of **Shiva**, **Vishnu** and **Indra**.
* **Heterodox Traditions**: Presence of **Jainism** (Mahavira) and **Buddhism** (Buddha).
* **Most Invoked Deity**: **Murugan / Shanmuga / Kartikeya / Subramanya**:
  * Identified as the younger son of Shiva in Southern tradition (elder son in Northern tradition).
* **Cult of Pious Women**:
  * **Kannagi Worship / Pattini Cult**: Deification and worship of virtuous wives who followed their husbands in death/Sati.
* **Cult of Heroic Warriors**:
  * **Nadukal / Veerkkal (Hero Stone Worship)**: Erection and ritual worship of carved stone pillars honoring warriors who died defending the community or king.

## Warrior Ethics & Martial Suicides (Avibali)

* **Code of Honor**: Warriors considered a natural death in bed dishonorable. If a soldier died naturally, his body was ritually cut with swords prior to cremation.
* **Back-Wound Stigma**: Receiving a wound on the back signified retreat/cowardice, triggering ritual suicide.
* **Avibali (Warrior Suicides)**:
  1. **Navakandam**: Suicide by cutting one's body into **9 parts** to offer to deities for state victory or royal honor (e.g., Ravana's 9-head sacrifice depicted at Ellora).
  2. **Arikandam**: Suicide by **severing one's own head** to present to gods (performed by royal bodyguards after a king's death to prove devotion and protect family honor).
  3. **Vatakkiruttal**: Suicide by **starvation facing North** (*Vataku* = North) undertaken out of absolute embarrassment (e.g., king/warrior wounded on the back). Refused to meet ancestors in afterlife; soul accepted wandering status.
  4. **Tungutalai**: Suicide by **tying hair/head to a bent bamboo pole** (*Tungu* = hanging, *Talai* = head) under tension; neck severed by self or relative, causing the head to swing up and spray blood over gathered spectators.

## Decline of Sangam Kingdoms & The Kalabhra Interregnum

* **3rd Century AD Economic Collapse**:
  * Decline of Sangam monarchies coincided with the collapse of the **Indian Ocean maritime trade network**.
  * Simultaneous decline of major trade-dependent global empires: **Roman Empire** (Europe), **Parthian Empire** (Iran), **Han Dynasty** (China), **Satavahana Dynasty** (Deccan) and **Kushana Empire** (North India).
  * Shift in literature: Late Sangam poems shift from royal praise to direct criticism of kings due to loss of trade revenue and royal patronage.
* **The Kalabhra Interregnum (4th – 6th Century AD)**:
  * **Invasion**: In 4th c. AD, a warlike hill tribe named **Kalabhras (Kalpiras)** overran Tamilakam, defeated Chera/Chola/Pandya kings, imprisoned rulers and expelled Brahmins.
  * **Historiographical Label**: Later orthodox Tamil texts labeled this period the **"Age of Darkness, Anarchy and Chaos"**.
  * **Religious Patronage**: Kalabhras were staunch patrons of **Jainism** and **Buddhism**.
  * **Termination (6th c. AD)**: Kalabhra rule overthrown by a grand alliance of **Pallavas, Pandyas, Chalukyas and Kadambas**.
  * **Historical Legacy**: Post-Kalabhra recovery under Pallavas sparked the **Southern Bhakti Movement** (Alvars & Nayanars targeting Jainism/Buddhism) and monumental rock-cut/structural temple architecture.

## Foreign Invasions: Indo-Greeks (2nd Century BC – 1st Century AD)

* **Post-Mauryan Geopolitical Genesis**:
  * 305 BC: Chandragupta Maurya defeated Seleucus Nicator → North Afghanistan became eastern boundary of Seleucid Empire.
  * Mid-3rd c. BC (~250 BC): **Bactria** (North Afghanistan) and **Parthia** (Iran) revolted against Seleucid rule.
  * Bactrian Greeks achieved independence under the **Euthydemic Dynasty** (*Euthydemus*).
* **Demetrius I**:
  * Son of Euthydemus; first Indo-Greek king to invade India during Mauryan decline.
  * Capital = **Kapisa (Kabul)**.
* **Menander I (Milinda) {160 BC – 130 BC}**:
  * **Early Life**: Born in Kabul; family killed in dynastic rivalry; fled to **Sagal**, married local ruler's daughter and inherited throne.
  * **Capital**: **Sagal / Sakala** (modern **Sialkot**, Pakistan).
  * **Conquests**: Conquered North India up to **Mathura** (**Yamuna River** as boundary); launched regular raids into Ganga-Yamuna plains against the **Shunga Dynasty** (Pushyamitra, Agnimitra, Vasumitra).
  * **Religious Conversion**: Converted to **Buddhism** after philosophical debates with Buddhist monk **Nagasena**.
  * **Milinda Panho ("Questions of Milinda")**:
    * Text recording dialogues between Menander and Nagasena.
    * **Language**: **Prakrit** (*Panha* = Prakrit for Sanskrit *Prashna*).
    * **Lineage of Nagasena**: Disciple of **Dharmaraksita** (Ashokan Dhamma Pracharak sent to NW India), who was a disciple of **Moggaliputta Tissa** (President of 3rd Buddhist Council).
  * **Nagasena's Chariot Analogy**:
    * Explains **Sunyavada (Emptiness)** and **Anatta (No-Soul)**.
    * A chariot is not a discrete entity; it is merely a conventional name for an assembly of wheels, axle and frame. Similarly, "Nagasena" is merely an empirical label for temporary physical/mental aggregates (*Skandhas*).

## Epistemological Parables (Reference)

* **Story of Nagasena & Chariot**:
  * **School**: **Buddhism** | **Doctrine**: **Sunyavada** (Emptiness) & **Anatta** (No-Self) → Entities lack intrinsic independent existence; composed of temporary arrangements.
* **Story of Seven Blind Men & Elephant**:
  * **School**: **Jainism** | **Doctrine**: **Anekantavada** (Many-Sidedness of Reality) → Absolute truth has multiple facets; partial human perception requires accommodating alternate viewpoints.
* **Story of the 10th Man**:
  * **School**: **Advaita Vedanta (Shankara)** | **Doctrine**: **Monism / Non-Duality** → Ignorance causes one to search externally for Ultimate Reality (*Brahma*), unaware that the seeker is inherently divine (*Aham Brahmasmi*).

## Antialcidas & Heliodorus Pillar

* **Antialcidas**: Indo-Greek king of Taxila.
* **Diplomatic Mission**: Sent ambassador **Heliodorus** to the court of Shunga King Bhagabhadra at **Vidisha** (MP).
* **Heliodorus / Garuda Pillar (Khamba Baba)**:
  * Monolithic pillar erected at Vidisha in devotion to **Bhagavata / Vasudeva (Vishnu)**.
  * Proves early adoption of **Vaishnavism / Bhagavatism** by Indo-Greeks.

## Resistance, Decline & Legacies of Indo-Greeks

* **Decline Drivers (1st Century BC)**:
  * Defeated from the **West** by **Scythians (Sakas)**.
  * Defeated from the **East** by Indian **Gana-Sanghas**:
    1. **Audumbaras** (Punjab).
    2. **Arjunayanas** (North Rajasthan).
    3. **Yaudheyas** (Haryana & West UP; worshippers of Vasudeva Krishna; ancestors of modern Yadavas).
* **Last Indo-Greek King**: **Strato III** (coins dated ~10 AD).

### Six Major Legacies of Indo-Greeks / Greeks in India

1. **Coinage Innovations**:
   * **Cast-Die Struck Method**: Melted metal poured into molds and stamped with terracotta seals while molten (replaced crude punch-marked technique).
   * **Portrait Style**: King's face/side-profile on obverse; deity on reverse.
   * **Inscriptional Detail**: Inscribed with king's name, titles, minting year, place, language and script.
   * **State-Struck Gold Currency**: First authority to issue official state-minted gold coins (*Denari* → Roman *Denarius* → Arabic *Dinar*; silver/copper *Dram* → Arabic *Dirham* → Indian *Dam / Damri*).
2. **Gandhara School of Art**: Introduced Greek realistic sculptural techniques (curly hair, facial expressions, heavy drapery folds) applied to Buddhist themes.
3. **Dramatic Arts (Theatre)**:
   * Introduced **Drama / Natak** written in dialogue format.
   * Introduced stage **Yavanika** (Curtain; derived from *Yavana* = Greek) and **Vidushaka** (Dedicated jester/clown for parallel lighthearted plots, heavily utilized by Kalidasa and modern Indian cinema).
4. **Astronomy & Astrology**:
   * Transmitted Greek astrology into India as **Hora Shastra** (*Horoscope*).
   * Standardized 7-day week, 24-hour day, 12-month solar division.
5. **Direct Maritime & Overland Trade**: Bypassed Persian middlemen, expanding direct Indian exports to Europe (*Age of Commerce*).
6. **Central Asian Spread of Buddhism**: Established Buddhist footholds in Afghanistan and Central Asia, initiating the **Silk Road expansion of Buddhism**.
