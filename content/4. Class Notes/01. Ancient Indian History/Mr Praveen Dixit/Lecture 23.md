## Kushana Numismatics & Coinage Architecture

* **Indo-Greek Lineage**: Kushana coins based on Indo-Greek cast die-struck portrait style.
* **Full-Body Depiction**: Unlike Indo-Greek coins (showing only royal face), Kushana coins depict **full body of the king** holding an offering over an altar/fire.
* **Royal Attire & Regalia**: King depicted wearing **trousers**, **long coats**, **boots** and holding a **trident (Trishul)** or **mace (Mugdhar)**.
* **Trident Motif**: Trident on coins includes a **plowshare motif** → Symbol of agricultural fertility & royal protection.
* **Rotated Om Symbol**: Contains an early **Om symbol** rotated 90 degrees → Originates from Brahmi script (~2200 years old).
* **Multicultural Deities**: Reverse side depicts diverse pantheon deities:
  * **Shaivism**: Lord Shiva (**Osho** / **Pashupati**) depicted with **4 heads**, **muscular body / abs**, holding **Trident**, **Damru** and accompanied by **Bull (Nandi)**, wearing a **dhoti**.
  * **Buddhism**: Lord Buddha labeled as **BODDO** in Greek script.
  * **Zoroastrianism**: Persian gods including **Ahura Mazda** (Fire God) on coins of **Huvishka**.
  * **Shaktism**: Goddess Parvati depicted as **Uma**.
* **Royal Facial Marks**: Coins of **Kanishka** and **Huvishka** feature a distinct **facial mark/scar on the cheek** → Symbol of battle wound or royal birthmark.
* **Gurjar Community Connection**:
  * Kushana coins feature a unique royal emblem: **4 vertical lines above a horseshoe with 2 side marks**.
  * Same symbol continues as the traditional emblem of the **Gurjar community** in North India.
  * Etymological gotra parallels: **Chechi** (Yuezhi), **Kasana** (Kushana), **Kapasia** (Kapisa / Vima Kadphises).
* **Coin Weight & Currency Terms**:
  * **Gold Coin**: **Denarii** (Greek) → **Dinar** (1 Dinar ≈ 7 grams).
  * **Copper Coin**: **Drachm** (Greek) → **Dram** → **Dam** → **Damri**.
  * **Silver / Thick Copper**: **Tetradrachm** (Attic weight standard).

## Gandhara School of Art (1st Century AD – 3rd Century AD)

* **Geography & Patrons**:
  * **Region**: Gandhara (NWFP, Taxila, Swat Valley, Peshawar, Afghanistan).
  * **Patrons**: **Kushanas** and **Western Satraps**.
  * **Type**: **Narrative Art** (depicts life events and story characters of Buddha).
* **First Human Form of Buddha**: **Gandhara School of Art was the FIRST to depict Lord Buddha in Human Form**.
* **Tripartite Aesthetic Inspirations**:

### Central Asian Features
* **Plain Un-decorated Halo**: Plain disk behind Buddha's head indicating divinity (*Halo* = shining solar disk).
* **Stone Type**: Executed 99% in **Grey Schist Stone**; extremely rare **Blue / Green Schist Stone** (e.g., 15-foot blue stone Buddha discovered in NWFP, Pakistan).

### Indian Features
* **Buddhist Themes**: Centered around **Mahayana Buddhism** and Bodhisattvas.
* **Head Knot (Jata / Ushnisha)**: Top-knot symbolizing spiritual enlightenment.
* **Forehead Mark (Tilak / Urna)**: Mark between eyebrows symbolizing inner vision.
* **Sad / Compassionate Expression**: Face is contemplative/sad → Reflects **Bodhisattva compassion** for the suffering of all worldly beings.
* **Broad Body & Proportion**: Buddha depicted with a **broad, larger body** compared to smaller surrounding followers.
* **Elongated Ear Lobes**: Symbolizes former life as a wealthy **Kshatriya prince** who wore heavy royal jewelry before renouncing the throne.
* **Standing Weight Shift**: Body weight rests on **1 foot with 1 knee visible** (*Stand-at-Ease posture*) → Represents physical fatigue from relentless travel and preaching to end human suffering.
* **Garment Holding**: Buddha holds the edge of his drapery with **one hand**.

### Greek or Greco-Roman Features
* **Curly Hair**: Wavy, curly hair locks inspired by Apollo.
* **Muscular Body**: Physical musculature inspired by Greek hero **Hercules**, **Zeus** and **Poseidon**.
* **Expressive Facial Features**: Detailed facial anatomy and realistic expressions.
* **Greco-Roman Motifs**: Decorative borders with **olive leaves**, **vine creepers (Kaper)** and European floral patterns alongside Indian Lotus and Swastika.
* **Heavy Drapery (Toga)**: Thick, opaque **Toga covering BOTH shoulders** with prominent heavy folds → Adapted for cold North-Western climate.

### Key Masterpieces & Examples
* **Taxila Stupa of Kanishka**: Monumental stupa complex at Taxila.
* **Bamiyan Buddha Statues**:
  * Located in Bamiyan Valley, Afghanistan (6th–7th Century AD).
  * Carved into sandstone cliffs in Gandhara style; destroyed in 2001 by Taliban.
* **Starving Buddha**:
  * Depicts Buddha in extreme emaciation (exposed ribs, sunken eyes).
  * **Philosophical Message**: Critique of extreme asceticism/starvation (*Santhara/Sallekhana*); illustrates that extreme self-torture yields no enlightenment, proving the necessity of the **Middle Path (Majjhima Patipada)**.

## Mathura School of Art (1st Century AD Onwards)

* **Geography & Material**:
  * **Center**: Mathura, Uttar Pradesh.
  * **Stone**: Executed exclusively in **Spotted Red Sandstone**.
* **Inspiration & Lineage**: Directly inspired by pre-existing **Mauryan Yaksha & Yakshini** monumental stone figures.
* **Key Visual Features**:
  * **Bulky / Fat Body Posture**: Robust, powerful physical build.
  * **Happy / Smiling Face**: Expression of **Bliss (Ananda)** and inner contentment upon ending suffering.
  * **Head Knot (Jata)**: Hair tied at the top, but **NO curly hair** (shaved or smooth hair).
  * **Carved Decorative Halo**: Intricately carved, highly decorated halo behind the head.
  * **Forehead Mark & Ear Lobes**: Presence of **Tilak/Urna** and **Elongated Ear Lobes**.
  * **Translucent Drapery (Uttariya / Samghati)**:
    * Drapery covers **ONLY the Left Shoulder** (Right shoulder left bare for working mobility).
    * Thin, translucent cloth with delicate carved lines → Body anatomy clearly visible beneath garment.
* **Inclusive Religious Spectrum**:
  * Depicts deities across **Buddhism**, **Jainism** and **Hinduism**.
  * Hindu deities depicted with weapons: **Kartikeya** (Spear), **Agni**, **Shiva**, **Vishnu**.
  * Features secular and domestic scenes (family life, female figures, Yakshinis with heavy necklaces).
* **Key Masterpieces & Discoveries**:
  * **Dated Kanishka Buddha (1905 Discovery)**: Discovered by **John Marshall** at Mathura; dated to reign of Kanishka; foundational piece for cataloging Kushana art.
  * **Bal Bodhisattva**: Early standing child-like Bodhisattva figure.
  * **Parkham Yaksha**: Manibhadra Yaksha sculpture.

## Mahayana Buddhism

**EVOLUTION OF BUDDHISM**

- Theravada / Hinayana → Emphasis on Individual Liberation (Self-Liberation)
  - Theravada → "Teaching of the Elders"; Emphasis on the Arhat Ideal
  - Hinayana → Historical Polemical Term Used by Mahayana Traditions; "Lesser Vehicle"

- Mahayana → Emphasis on Universal Liberation (Greater Vehicle)
  - Bodhisattva Ideal → Liberation of All Sentient Beings
  - Greater Vehicle → Path Towards Buddhahood for the Benefit of All Beings

### Socio-Historical Catalyst
* Developed in the **Post-Mauryan Age** to make Buddhism accessible to commoners and foreign converts (**Sakas, Kushanas, Greeks**).
* Reform targeted **2 Core Drawbacks** of original Theravada/Hinayana Buddhism:
  1. *Drawback 1*: Theravada made **Moksha/Nirvana via severe meditation** the sole goal; provided no prayers/rituals for **daily life needs** (health, wealth, job, progeny).
  2. *Drawback 2*: In Theravada, once a monk achieved Nirvana, he left the world permanently (*end of rebirth*), leaving **no enlightened guide** in the world to assist others.

### Eight Core Principles of Mahayana Buddhism
1. **Divine Nature of Buddha**:
   * Buddha is a **Divine Being / God**, not merely a human teacher.
   * His birth and physical death were **cosmic illusions (Lila)** shown to humanity.
   * (*Contrast*: Theravada holds Buddha was a human whose birth and death were real historical events).
2. **Worship & Deities for Daily Goals**:
   * Gods exist below Buddha; **both Buddha and deities are worshipped** via rituals and prayers to achieve daily worldly goals.
3. **Concept of Bodhisattva**:
   * **Bodhisattva** = Enlightened being who **suspends his own final Moksha**, voluntarily taking rebirth in the world to guide all living beings to liberation.
   * **Nirvana becomes a Social Goal** (*Universal Salvation*) rather than an individual selfish goal.
4. **Cultural Assimilation & Acculturation**:
   * Non-Buddhist deities and regional gods incorporated into Buddhism by declaring them as **Bodhisattvas** or protective spirits.
5. **Supernatural Powers & Esoteric Growth**:
   * Worship and meditation grant **supernatural and magical powers**.
   * Spurred the rise of **Vajrayana / Tantrayana / Mantrayana / Esoteric Buddhism** → Focus on rituals, spells and worship of **Goddess Tara** alongside Buddha.
1. **Illusionary Nature of the World**:
   * World is **Phenomenal / Illusion (Maya)** → Adapted from Upanishadic metaphysics.
7. **Sanskritization of Canonical Literature**:
   * Adopted **Sanskrit** (the elite/court language) for canonical texts, termed **Vipulya Sutras**.
   * (*Contrast*: Theravada composed texts in **Pali / Prakrit**).
8. **The "Greater Vehicle" Analogy**:
   * **Mahayana** = "Greater Vehicle" (a massive ferry carrying all beings across the sea of suffering).
   * **Hinayana** = "Lesser Vehicle" (a small, selfish individual life-boat).

### Geographic Spread
* **Theravada / Hinayana**: Spread to **Sri Lanka** (Ashoka's mission) and **Southeast Asia** (Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos).
* **Mahayana**: Spread along the **Silk Road** via Indo-Greeks and Kushanas into **Central Asia**, **China**, **Korea**, **Japan** and **Vietnam**.
* **Vajrayana / Tibetan Buddhism**:
  * Origin: **Pala Empire** of Bengal and Bihar (Nalanda, Vikramashila).
  * Spread to **Tibet, Nepal, Bhutan and Mongolia** in 8th Century AD by monk **Padmasambhava (Rinpoche)**.
  * Features: Monastic centers (**Gompas**), spiritual heads (**Dalai Lama**, **Karmapa**), **Prayer Cylinders/Wheels** and **Brass Singing Bowls**.

## Literature of The Age of Commerce (2nd Century BC – 3rd Century AD)

**LITERATURE SHIFT**

- Vedic / Mahajanapada Age → Vedic Literature (Samhitas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas & Upanishads)
- Post-Mauryan Age → Puranic Literature (Itihasa-Purana Tradition)

### Puranic Literature (Puranas)
* **Rise & Dominance**: Became the primary genre of Hindu literature in the post-Mauryan age.
* **Dual Objective**:
  1. To make complex Vedic mythology and morals accessible to commoners through engaging stories.
  2. To **legitimize royal dynasties and kings** by connecting their genealogies to divine origins (**Suryavansha**, **Chandravansha**, **Agnikula**).
* **Retrospective Prophecy (Future Tense)**: Written in **Future Tense** ("This king will come, he will rule...") to present historical facts as ancient divine predictions.
* **Pancha Lakshana (5 Canonical Contents of Puranas)**:
  1. **Sarga**: Story of the Creation of the Universe.
  2. **Pratisarga**: Story of the Destruction (**Pralaya**) and Re-creation of the Universe.
  3. **Vamsha**: Genealogies and lineages of gods, patriarchs and sages.
  4. **Manvantara**: Cosmic time cycles, Yugas (**Satya, Treta, Dvapara, Kali**) and cosmic epochs governed by Manus.
  5. **Vamshanucharita**: Historical deeds and chronological lists of royal dynasties.
* **Itihasa-Purana Tradition**: Synthesis of **History + Mythology**.
* **Authors & Bards**:
  * **Puranakars**: Intellectual authors/scholars in royal courts.
  * **Suta / Sauti**: Traditional bards, court narrators and folk singers who transmitted stories orally across generations.
* **Corpus**: **18 Mahapuranas** + **18 Upapuranas**.
  * *Example*: ***Bhavishya Purana*** records historical royal lineages down to **Prithviraj Chauhan**.

### Dharmashastras (Law Books)
* **Social Catalyst**: Foreign invasions (**Sakas, Kushanas, Greeks**) and rapid urbanization caused widespread breaking of traditional social norms.
* **Orthodox Response**: Orthodox Brahmins composed **Dharmashastras** (e.g., ***Manusmriti***) based on earlier **Dharmasutras** (law codes) to enforce social hierarchy, Varna purity and moral conduct.

### Avadana Literature
* **Buddhist Narrative Corpus**: Buddhist texts in Sanskrit (e.g., ***Divyavadana***, ***Mahavadana***) detailing noble deeds, moral parables and past lives of Buddha and Bodhisattvas.
