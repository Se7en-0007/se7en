## Geographical Zonation & Regional Economy

**SATAVAHANA EMPIRE**

1. **WESTERN ZONE (DECCAN PLATEAU / MAHARASHTRA)**
   - Soil & Crops → Black Basalt Soil; Cotton, Oilseeds, Sugarcane (Jaggery)
   - Capital → Pratisthan (Paithan, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar / Aurangabad, Maharashtra)
   - Architecture → Rock-Cut Caves in Black Basalt Rock
   - Exports → Oilseeds (Mustard, Til), Jaggery, Coconut, Spices, Textiles, Iron, Perfumes (Gandha → Gandhikas), Pearls & Shells

2. **EASTERN ZONE (DELTAIC ANDHRA PRADESH)**
   - Soil & Crops → Alluvial Soil of Krishna-Godavari Delta; Rice (Staple Crop)
   - Capital → Amaravati (Palnadu / Guntur District, Andhra Pradesh)
   - Architecture → Amaravati School of Art
   - Exports → Rice, Muslin / Malmal (Masulipatnam), Calico / Chhint (Calicut), Diamonds (Golconda / Kakatiya Mines), Teak (Rajahmundry), Sandalwood, Ivory (Dantapura Port on Mahanadi), Pearls (Hyderabad)

---

### Western Zone (Upper Deccan)
* **Core Geography**: **Black Soil Region** → Primary crop = **Cotton**.
* **Capital**: **Pratisthan** (Modern **Paithan**, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar / Aurangabad district, Maharashtra).
* **Architecture**: **Rock-Cut Cave Architecture** carved into **Black Basalt Rock**.
* **Export Categories & Commodities**:
  * **Food Grains & Spices**: **Oil seeds** (Mustard / *Sarson*, Sesame / *Til*), **Sugarcane** (processed & exported as thick **Jaggery** / *Gud*), **Coconut** (from Konkan), **Spices** (from Konkan & Malabar).
  * **Jewelry & Perfume**: Coastline export. Perfume = **Gandha** → Perfume sellers = **Gandhika** (Etymological anchor: Porbandar *Gandhi* family). **Pearls & Shells**.
  * **Textiles & Metallurgy**: Cotton fabrics & **Iron implements**.

### Eastern Zone (Lower Deccan)
* **Core Geography**: **Deltaic Region of Krishna-Godavari Rivers** → Primary crop = **Rice** (High yield → Agrarian surplus → Geopolitical value: Later targeted as **Northern Circars** by French & British).
* **Capital**: **Amaravati** (Modern **Palnadu / Guntur district**, Andhra Pradesh).
* **Architecture**: **Amaravati School of Art**.
* **Export Commodities & Ports**:
  * **Rice**: Staple food export.
  * **Textiles**:
    * **Muslin / Malmal**: Fine cotton fabric purchased by Europeans from **Masulipatnam / Machilipatnam Port** → Named **Muslin**.
    * **Calico / Chhint**: Printed/splashed (*Chhinta / Chhppa*) cotton fabric purchased by Europeans from **Calicut Port** → Named **Calico**.
  * **Diamonds**: **Golconda / Kakatiya Diamond Mines** (Primary global source of diamonds until 18th century).
  * **Timber & Perfumery**: **Teak Wood** (from **Rajahmundry**) & **Sandalwood** (used for perfumes).
  * **Ivory**: Sourced from elephant corridors → Exported via **Dantapura Port** at the mouth of **Mahanadi River** (*Danta* = Tooth/Ivory → Orissa's medieval **Gajapati Empire** / Elephant Lords).
  * **Pearls**: **Hyderabad** as historic pearl processing capital.

## Dynastic Political History & Key Rulers

- **CHRONOLOGY OF SATAVAHANA RULERS**
	- Simuka (3rd / 2nd Century BC) → Founder; Post-Mauryan Sovereignty; Pratisthan Core
	- Sri Satakarni (1st Century BC) → Naneghat Prasasti (Naganika); Hathigumpha Link; Control Over Both Coasts
	- Gautamiputra Satakarni (1st–2nd Century AD) → Nasik Prasasti (Gautami Balashri); Defeated Nahapana; Shalivahana Samvat (78 AD)
	- Vashishtiputra Satakarni (2nd Century AD) → Son-in-Law of Rudradaman I; Defeated Twice; Junagadh Inscription
	- Sri Yajna Satakarni (2nd Century AD) → Recaptured Western Territories; Donations at Nagarjunakonda; Last Strong Satavahana Ruler

### Simuka (3rd / 2nd Century BC)
* **Founder**: Established **Satavahana Kingdom**.
* **Sovereignty**: Declared independence during/after the decline of the **Mauryan Empire**.
* **Territory**: Controlled upper course of **Godavari River**; earliest coins discovered in/around **Pratisthan (Paithan)**.

### Sri Satakarni (1st Century BC)
* **Genealogy**: Son, grandson, or grand-nephew of Simuka.
* **Consort & Epigraph**: Wife **Queen Naganika / Nayanika** commissioned the **Naneghat Prasasti** (**Prakrit** language, **Brahmi** script).
* **Achievements**: Expanded kingdom into an empire controlling both Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal coastlines ("Horses drank water from three oceans").
* **Contemporaneous Anchor**: Mentioned in King **Kharavela's Hathigumpha Inscription**.
* **Title Anchor**: Illustrious ruler whose title (**Satakarni**) was adopted by subsequent Satavahana monarchs.

### Gautamiputra Satakarni (Late 1st or Early 2nd Century AD)
* **Mother & Epigraph**: **Gautami Balashri** commissioned the **Nasik Prasasti** (**Prakrit** language, **Brahmi** script).
* **Shaka-Satavahana Wars**: Contemporary and arch-rival of Western Kshatrapa (Saka) King **Nahapana** over Arabian Sea trade and Narmada-Tapi territories. Initiated a 2-century conflict.
* **Defeat of Nahapana**: Destroyed the **Kshaharata branch** of Western Kshatrapas; assumed title **Kshaharatak** (Eradicator of Kshaharata).
* **Counter-Struck Coins**: Over-stamped Nahapana's silver coins with his own royal seal over Nahapana's portrait as a mark of victory.
* **Shalivahana Samvat (78 AD)**: Founded a new calendar era (**Shalivahana Samvat**) in **78 AD** to commemorate victory (*Shalivahana* = personal name of Gautamiputra). Later renamed **Shaka Samvat** by Western Kshatrapas (Adopted as official national calendar of Republic of India; calculation: Present Year - 78 = Shaka Samvat Year).
* **Ugadi Festival**: Celebrated victory as the start of a new era (**Yugadi** = *Yuga* + *Adi*), evolving into **Ugadi**, the traditional Deccan New Year.

### Successors of Gautamiputra Satakarni
1. **Vashishtiputra Pulumavi (c. 90 – 120 AD)**: Expanded influence in eastern Andhra.
2. **Vashishtiputra Satakarni (c. 120 – 150 AD)**:
   * Contemporary, rival and **son-in-law** of Western Kshatrapa King **Rudradaman I**.
   * Defeated twice in battle by Rudradaman I, who spared his life due to familial matrimonial alliance.
   * **Source**: **Junagadh Prasasti of Rudradaman**.
3. **Sri Yajna Satakarni (c. 150 – 180 AD)**:
   * **Last Strong Satavahana Monarch**: Decisively defeated Western Kshatrapas and reconquered lost western territories.
   * **Monastic Patronage**: Donated a mountain/hill to Buddhist philosopher **Nagarjuna** → Named **Nagarjunakonda** (In 20th century, **Nagarjuna Sagar Dam** built on Krishna River surrounded this hill).

### Decline & 3rd Century AD Regional Successors
* **Upper Western Deccan**: **Abhiras / Abhiraj** (Shepherd-warriors; root of *Aheer* → Later **Seuna Yadavas of Devagiri/Daulatabad**).
* **Lower Western Deccan**: **Chutus** (Chutu Dynasty).
* **Andhra / Tamil Frontier**: **Pallavas**.
* **Orissa / Chhattisgarh**: **Ikshvakus** (Claimed descent from Rama → **Dakshina Kosala**).
* **Main Deccan Successors**: **Vakatakas** (Vakataka Dynasty).

## Name & Title Etymologies

* **Satavahana**: Meaning either "Rider of 7 Horses" (Sun God's chariot *Saptashva*) or "King possessing 100 Horses" (*Sata* = 100, *Vahana* = vehicle/horse).
* **Satakarni**: *Sata* (100) + *Karna* (Ears) = "King with 100 Ears" (Metaphor for 100 spies / complete imperial intelligence). In Sangam literature (*Akananuru* / Chola records), King **Karikalan** allied with a northern "King with 100 ears" (*Satakarni*) and Kharavela to defeat Magadha.
* **Matronymics**: Royal names like *Gautamiputra* ("Son of Gautami") or *Vashishtiputra* ("Son of Vashishti") honored maternal lineage, though dynastic succession remained patriarchal & polygamous.

## Satavahana Administration & Military Character

- **ADMINISTRATIVE HIERARCHY**
	- Empire → Rajan / Eka-Brahmana
	- District (Ahara) → Senapati / Maharathi (Military Generals in Civil Roles)
	- Village (Grama) → Gramika (Civil Headman) + Golmika (Military Unit Commander)

---

* **Varna Identity**: Satavahanas belonged to the **Brahmin Varna** → Assumed title **Eka-Brahmana** (Unmatched / Supreme Brahmin). Contrast: Western Kshatrapas claimed Kshatriya Varna.
* **Territorial Units**: **Ahara** (District, equivalent to Mauryan Vishaya/Ahara), **Vishaya**, **Grama** (Village).
* **Military Character of Governance**:
  * **Ahara (District) Heads**: Administered by **Senapati** or **Maharathi** (Military generals exercising civil administrative powers).
  * **Village Level Dual System**:
    1. **Gramika**: Civil village headman.
    2. **Golmika**: Commander of a military unit (**Golma** / army squad) stationed directly inside the village to maintain order, repel tribal raids and suppress local uprisings.
  * **Mahatalavara**: Chief of the network of watchmen patrolling highways and trade routes against tribal bandits.
  * **Cantonments (Katak / Kantak / Skandhavara)**: Military camps established across the empire:
    * **Dhanyakataka**: Cantonment near Amaravati.
    * **Amarkantak**: Cantonment near origin of Narmada & Mahanadi rivers.
    * **Jayaskandhavara**: Victorious military encampment.
  * **Uparakshita**: Specific administrative bureaucrat (*Amatya*) in charge of **cave construction, maintenance, food supplies and security (*Raksha*) for Buddhist monks** in mountain caves.
* **Institutional Comparison**:
  * *Terms Similar to Mauryan System*: **Vishaya**, **Ahara**, **Kumara**, **Amatya**.
  * *Terms Indicating Military Polity*: **Senapati**, **Maharathi**, **Golmika**, **Mahatalavara**, **Katak / Kantak / Skandhavara**.

## Satavahana Coinage

* **Stylistic Influence**: Adopted **Indo-Greek Style** (Cast Die-struck coins, portrait side-profile).
* **Primary Metal**: **Lead** (**Seesa** - black, fragile, leaves dark mark on stone; majority of Satavahana coins minted in lead). Other metals: Potin, Copper, Bronze, Silver (rare).
* **Key Features & Motifs**:
  * **Bilingual Inscriptions**: **Prakrit** language on one side, local **Dravidian/tribal language** on the reverse; **Brahmi** script.
  * **Royal Portraits**: Kings shown in side profile (one eye visible), wearing elaborate wigs/hair-knots.
  * **Dominant Motifs**: **Ship / Boat with double masts** (Signifies flourishing maritime & naval trade), **Elephant**, **Lion**, **Horseshoe / Pyramidal Hill symbol**, **Nastika-style Swastika**.
  * **Counter-Struck Coins**: Gautamiputra Satakarni's over-stamped coins on Nahapana's silver currency.

## Western Rock-Cut Cave Architecture & Water Management

- **CAVE ARCHITECTURE (2ND CENTURY BC – 10TH CENTURY AD)**
	- Location → Western Ghats along Dakshinapatha; Black Basalt River Gorges
	- Patronage → Satavahanas, Western Kshatrapas, Vakatakas, Guptas and Trade Guilds (Shrenis)
	- Primary Sites → Ajanta, Ellora, Karle, Bhaja, Junnar, Pandavleni (Nasik), Naneghat and Kanheri
	- Water Management System
	  - Outer Channels → Carved into Cliff Faces to Collect Rainwater
	  - Internal Channels → Routed Water Through Cave Walls for Filtration / Sedimentation
	  - Storage Cisterns (Podhi) → Underground Water Tanks Managed by Uparakshita

---

* **Spatial Location**: Western Ghats, along **Dakshinapatha** trade routes, carved into river gorges and vertical cliffs of **Black Basalt Rock**.
* **Patronage**: Satavahanas, Western Kshatrapas, Vakatakas, Guptas and **Trade Guilds** (*Shrenis*).
* **Primary Cave Complexes**: **Ajanta**, **Ellora**, **Karle**, **Bhaja**, **Junnar**, **Pandavleni (Nasik)**, **Naneghat**, **Elephanta**, **Kanheri**, **Bhut Lenga**, **Kishna Caves**.
* **Water Harvesting & Management System**:
  * **Outer Channels**: Carved on outer mountain walls above cave entrances to intercept and divert rainwater.
  * **Internal Channels & Sedimentation**: Channels routed water inside cave walls into specialized pots for **sedimentation** (settling mud/silt) before overflowing into main storage tanks.
  * **Storage Tanks (Podhi)**: Multiple rock-cut underground cisterns created for human consumption, ritual washing and pack animals. Constructed and maintained under the supervision of the **Uparakshita**.

## Amaravati School of Art (2nd Century BC – 3rd Century AD)

- **AMARAVATI SCHOOL OF ART**
	- Region → Lower Krishna-Godavari Basin (Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda, Bhattiprolu)
	- Patronage → Satavahanas & Ikshvakus
	- Material → White Marble / White Limestone Slabs
	- Religion → Buddhism
	- Distinctive Artistic Style
	  - Depiction of Buddha → Both Symbolic & Human Forms; Lean, Elongated Body
	  - Narrative Art → Jataka Stories & Life Events Carved on Relief Panels
	  - Composition → Crowded Panels; Dynamic Sense of Movement & Action
	  - Head-Knots → Frontally Decorative Crowns / Head-Knots
	  - Gender Depiction → Men & Women Depicted Together in Dignified Poses
	  - Influence → Inspired Pallava & Chola Bronze Sculptures, Including Nataraja

---

* **Geographical Region**: Lower Krishna-Godavari valley (Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda, Jaggayyapeta, Bhattiprolu).
* **Patronage**: **Satavahana Dynasty** & **Ikshvaku Dynasty**.
* **Material**: **White Marble / White Limestone** slabs decorating stupa walls.
* **Religious Affiliation**: Exclusively **Buddhism**.
* **Key Artistic Characteristics**:
  * **Narrative Focus**: Carved full narrative panels depicting Jataka tales and Buddha's life events.
  * **Buddha Depiction**: Depicted in **both Symbolic and Human Form** (Along with Gandhara and Mathura, Amaravati was among the earliest schools to depict Buddha as a human being).
  * **Physical Features**: **Lean, elongated, graceful bodies** (not fat/bulky), featuring **frontally decorative head-knots / crowns**.
  * **Dynamic Composition**: **Crowded panels**, intense **sense of movement, action and emotion** (dancing, battle, procession, worship).
  * **Gender Integration**: Men and women depicted together without social segregation; women portrayed in prominent, dignified social roles.
  * **Stupa Decoration**: Slabs decorating the **Anda** (mound), **Vedika** (railing) and **Torana** (gateways) were heavily carved with relief panels.
  * **Architectural Legacy**: Directly inspired **Pallava and Chola bronze sculptures** (lean body proportions, tall crowns, dynamic posture, e.g., Nataraja).

## Key Cultural & Philosophical Concepts

* **Mara**: The chief demon/adversary in Buddhism representing worldly temptation, desire and death, whom Buddha and all 28 Buddhas must defeat to attain supreme enlightenment.
* **Amrapali**: Celebrated courtesan (*Nagarvadhu*) of Vaishali who converted to Buddhism and donated a mango orchard (*Amravana*) to Buddha.
* **Anathapindika**: Wealthy merchant of Sravasti who purchased Jetavana park by covering the ground with gold coins and donated it to the Buddhist Sangha.
