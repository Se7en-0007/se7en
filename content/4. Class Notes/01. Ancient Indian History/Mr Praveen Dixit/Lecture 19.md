## Literature of The Satavahana Age

### Gaha Sattasai (Gatha Saptashati)
* **Author**: **King Hala** (Satavahana Dynasty ruler).
* **Language**: **Prakrit** (*Gaha* = Prakritized *Gatha* {story}; *Satta* = *Sapta* {700}; *Sai* = *Shati* {100}).
* **Structure & Style**: **700 love poems/stories** written in **first person** from perspective of **young girls**.
* **Societal Indicator**: **Vibrant, non-rigid, emotionally expressive society**.
* **Key Narrative Examples**:
  * Young married girl stung by a scorpion (*Vrichhika*), brought to a doctor who is her ex-lover, in front of her husband (emotional dilemma).
  * 15-year-old girl writing confidently to her 50-year-old lover ("don't fear your wife or children; let's meet").
* **Literary References**: Mentions Vatsyayana's ***Kamasutra*** (proves Kamasutra was known in Satavahana times as a symbol of past societal openness).

### Brihatkatha (Vad-Kaha / Badd-Kaha)
* **Author**: **Gunadhya** (Satavahana court).
* **Language**: **Prakrit** (Paishachi Prakrit).
* **Core Plot**: Legend of **Prince / King Udayan** & **Vasavadatta**:
  * Infant prince Udayan placed on terrace → carried away by eagle → dropped near tribal village → raised by childless tribal family → grew powerful → reclaimed throne → hunted alone in wilderness → captured by neighboring king → married princess **Vasavadatta**.
* **Status**: Original work **lost**; known entirely through later retellings.
* **Key Retellings (7 Famous Historical Adaptations)**:
  1. ***Katha Sarit Sagara*** (10th c. AD) → **Somadeva** (Sanskrit).
  2. ***Brihatkatha Manjari*** (11th c. AD) → **Kshemendra** (Sanskrit).
  3. Adaptations by **Bhasa** & **King Harshavardhana**.

### Katantra Vyakarana
* **Author**: **Sarvavarman** (Satavahana court).
* **Language**: **Sanskrit**.
* **Content**: **Sanskrit Grammar** treatise.

### Works of Acharya Nagarjuna (Satavahana Era)
* **Historical Context**: Contemporary of Satavahana King **Sri Yajna Satakarni** (2nd c. AD).
* **Affiliation**: **Mahayana Buddhism** monk/philosopher.
* **Language**: **Sanskrit** (Mahayana Buddhism adopted Sanskrit from 2nd c. AD onwards).
* **4 Major Works**:
  1. ***Madhyamaka Karika***: Commentary on **Madhyamaka** (Middle Path) philosophy.
  2. ***Prajnaparamita***: Commentary on **Sunyavada** (**Emptiness / Sunyata** philosophy); details features of Buddhahood.
  3. ***Sutrasamucchaya***: Anthology / compilation of commentaries on Buddhist stories (Jatakas & Avadanas).
  4. ***Rasaratnakara***: Foundational treatise on **Chemistry / Alchemy** (**Rasayana**).

## Sangam Age & Sangam Literature

### Origins & Chronology
* **Timeline**: **3rd Century BC onwards** → Rapid surge in literary output in Southern India.
* **Etymology**: **Sangam** = Assembly / Academy of Tamil poets.
* **Language**: **Tamil** (an older archaic form of Tamil).
* **Geographical Horizon**: **Tamilakam (Tamilagam)** = Cultural realm of Chola, Chera and Pandya kingdoms.

### Classification of Literature by Content Type
* **Narrative Literature**:
  * Focus = **Story / Plot**.
  * Moral message imparted **indirectly** through narrative events (e.g., Epics, Puranas).
* **Didactic Literature**:
  * Focus = **Direct Moral Message / Instruction**.
  * No story framework; explicit moral dictation (e.g., Tirukkural, Nitishastra).

### Classification by Style & Theme (Akam vs. Puram)
* **Akam (Agam) Themes**:
  * Focus = **Inner emotions**, subjective feelings, personal relationships.
  * Subject = **Love Stories**.
* **Puram Themes**:
  * Focus = **Outer actions**, objective heroics, physical warfare (*Puram* = Fort/Citadel; *Purandara* = Destroyer of forts).
  * Subject = **War Stories & Public Deeds**.

### Classification by Tinai (Landscapes & Flower Motifs)
* **Concept**: 5 geographical zones, each mapped to a specific emotion, landscape stage (river's journey) and regional flower (**Tinai**):
  1. **Kurinji** (Mountain) → Emotion: **Union of Lovers** | Flower: *Kurinji*.
  2. **Mullai** (Forest) → Emotion: **Patient Waiting** | Flower: *Mullai*.
  3. **Marudam** (Agricultural Plain) → Emotion: **Quarrel / Conflict** (driven by crop surplus) | Flower: *Marudam*.
  4. **Neithal** (Coastal / Sea) → Emotion: **Grief / Pain** | Flower: *Neithal*.
  5. **Palai** (Arid / Desert) → Emotion: **Separation** | Flower: *Palai*.

### The Three Tamizh Sangams
* **Patronage**: All 3 Sangams patronized by **Pandya Kings**.
* **1st Sangam**:
  * Location: **Ten-Madurai** (South Madurai).
  * Claimed Duration: 4,400 years (exaggerated).
  * Status: **100% Literature Lost**.
* **2nd Sangam**:
  * Location: **Kapatapuram**.
  * Claimed Duration: 3,700 years.
  * Status: **Only 1 work survived** (**Tolkappiyam**).
* **3rd Sangam**:
  * Location: **Madurai**.
  * Claimed Duration: 1,800 years.
  * Status: **Major corpus survived**.
* **Kumari Kandam Theory**: Ancient Tamil tradition of a submerged continent south of Kanyakumari (lost to sea-level rise / *Deluge*), matching 19th-century *Lemuria* concept prior to Plate Tectonics.

### Structural Corpus of Sangam Works
* **Tolkappiyam**:
  * Author: **Tolkappiyar**.
  * Topic: **Tamil Grammar** & Poetics (oldest surviving Tamil text; 2nd Sangam).
* **Pathinenmelkanakku (18 Major Works — Narrative)**:
  * **Ettuthokai** (Eight Anthologies): 8 works on **Akam (Love)** themes.
  * **Pattupattu** (Ten Idylls): 10 works on **Puram (War)** themes.
* **Pathinenkilkanakku (18 Minor Works — Didactic)**:
  * 18 moral/instructional works.
  * **Tirukkural**: Most famous work by **Tiruvalluvar** (1330 moral couplets; direct ethical maxims, e.g., "Bad habits are like a leak in a boat").
  * **Kanyakumari Memorials**: **Vivekananda Rock** (1970) & **Statue of Tiruvalluvar** (2000).

## The Five Great Epics of Tamil Literature (Aimperumkappiyam)

* **Unique Feature**: *All 5 Tamil Epics are named after items of women's jewelry!*

### 1. Cilappatikaram (The Anklet)
* **Author**: **Ilango Adigal** (Sangam Age).
* **Protagonists**: **Kovalan** (rich merchant), **Kannagi** (chaste wife), **Madhavi** (courtesan/dancer).
* **Plot Sequence**: Kovalan squanders wealth on Madhavi → returns penniless to faithful wife Kannagi → couple migrates from Puhar (Poompuhar) to **Madurai** → Kovalan takes Kannagi's gold anklet (*Cilambu*) to sell → falsely accused of stealing Queen's anklet → executed by Pandya King → Kannagi breaks remaining anklet (filled with rubies vs Queen's pearls), proving innocence → wrath burns Madurai.
* **Political Anchor (Sengol)**: Pandya King's scepter (**Sengol**) symbolizes righteous moral rule; breaks when injustice is committed.
* **Iconography**: **Kannagi Statue** at Marina Beach, Chennai.

### 2. Manimekalai
* **Author**: **Sithalai Sattanar** (Sangam Age).
* **Protagonist**: **Manimekalai** (daughter of Kovalan & Madhavi).
* **Plot Sequence**: Manimekalai seeks meaning of human suffering → pursued by Prince **Udayakumaran** → Prince killed by rival → Manimekalai adopts **Buddhism** and becomes a **Buddhist nun**.

### 3. Civaka Cintamani (Jivaka Chintamani)
* **Author**: **Tirutthakkadevar** (Post-Sangam Jain monk).
* **Plot Sequence**: Prince **Civak / Jivaka** born after father killed in palace coup → raised in tribal family → grows powerful, defeats usurpers, reclaims throne → marries multiple wives → ultimately renounces kingdom to become a **Jain monk**.

### 4. Kundalakesi
* **Author**: **Nathakuthanaar** (Post-Sangam Buddhist author).
* **Protagonists**: **Kundalakesi** ("Girl with curly hair", merchant's daughter) & **Kalan** (condemned criminal).
* **Plot Sequence**: Kundalakesi falls in love with criminal Kalan → father uses wealth to free Kalan → married → daily domestic quarrels → Kalan takes her to mountain peak to murder her → Kundalakesi tricks him (requests 3 circumambulations as last wish) → pushes Kalan off cliff → renounces world to become a **Buddhist nun**.

### 5. Valayapathi
* **Author**: Unknown (Post-Sangam).
* **Status**: **Work lost**; surviving fragments compiled in 19th c. by **Vaiyapuri Pillai**.

## Sangam Society, Polity & Economic Architecture

### Social Base (Kudi & Proto-Varna System)
* **Fundamental Unit**: **Kudi / Kuti** = Family / Joint Clan (equivalent to Vedic *Kula*).
* **Social Stratification**: No classical Varna system initially → **Proto-Varna System** (hereditary, endogamous professional guilds):
  1. **Arasar**: Kings & Warriors.
  2. **Anthanar**: Priests & Scholars.
  3. **Vanigar** (Vani / Vanik): Merchants & Traders.
  4. **Vellalar**: Rich Landowners / Wetland Farmers (*Velanmai*).
  5. **Karalar / Villar**: Rain-fed / Dryland Farmers.
  6. **Kadaisiyar & Ulavar**: Landless Agricultural Laborers.
  7. **Pariyars**: Artisans & Craftsmen.

### Agricultural Economy & Terminology
* **Primary Economy**: **Agriculture** (land owned communally by *Kudi* / family).
* **Landowner Suffix**: **-lar** in Tamil terminology indicates land ownership (e.g., *Vellalar*, *Karalar*).
* **Agricultural Tax**: **Kadamai** (KD combination root).
* **Folk & Classical Dance Links**:
  * Harvest dance on farm fields (**Kudiyattam**) → evolved into **Kathakali**.
  * **Mohiniyattam**: 2nd classical dance of Kerala (*Aattam* = Dance).

### Megalithic Foundation & Material Trade
* **Evolutionary Origin**: Sangam monarchies (**Chola, Chera, Pandya**) evolved directly out of coastal **Megalithic Cultures** (c. 1000 BC / 1st Millennium BC).
* **Megalithic Burial Types**: *Dolmen, Cist, Menhir, Capstone, Stone Circle* (large stone structures built over graves).
* **Lithic Tool Typology**:
  * **Microlith**: Small stone tools (1–4 cm) marking the end of Paleolithic and start of **Mesolithic Age**.
  * **Macrolith**: Large stone implements (e.g., hand-milling *Chakki*).
  * **Megalith**: Large stone burial architecture (NOT a tool).

### Maritime Commerce & Global Trade Network
* **Commodity Exports**: Textiles (Muslin, Calico), Spices (Black Pepper), Teak wood, Sandalwood, Ivory, Perfumes, Gold, Silver, Pearls, Shells, Beads.
* **Export Destinations**: China, Southeast Asia, Sri Lanka, Southeast Africa, West Asia, Roman Europe.
* **Monsoon Trade Expansion (1st Century AD)**: Discovery of monsoon trade winds (**Hippalus**) accelerated Indian Ocean maritime trade.
* **Roman Coin Hoards**: Massive finds of Roman gold/silver coins in **Karur** and **Madurai** → transformed into coin jewelry (**Kasumalai** / *Dollar*).
* **Polity Structure**: Constant inter-state warfare among Chola, Chera and Pandya kingdoms.
