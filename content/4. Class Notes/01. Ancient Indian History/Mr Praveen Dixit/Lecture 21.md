## Origins, Migration & Early History of The Scythians (Sakas)

* **Origin & Push Mechanics**:
  * **Native Habitat**: Central Asian nomads.
  * **2nd Century BC**: Pushed from East by **Kushanas** → Migrated southward → Pushed **Indo-Greeks** into NW India.
  * **Settlement in Sistan**: Resided in Iran-Balochistan region for ~2 centuries → Territory named **Shakistan** (modern **Sistan** in Iran/Afghanistan).
  * **1st Century BC**: Pushed from West by **Parthians (Pahlavas)** → Forced into India.

* **First Saka King in India**:
  * **Maues / Moga (92 BC – 60 BC)**: First Saka ruler in India; conquered up to Mathura region.
  * **Epigraphic Proof**: **Taxila Copper Plate Inscription** refers to him as **Maharaja Moga**.
  * **Toponymic Legends**: Local traditions tie his name to *Moga* (Punjab) and *Meo / Mewat* (Rajasthan/Haryana) (linguistic similarity, unproven historical link).

* **The 57 BC Saka Defeat & Vikram Samvat Era**:
  * **Ujjain Invasion (57 BC)**: Sakas invaded Ujjain → Defeated and expelled by regional ruler **King Vikramaditya**.
  * **Vikram Samvat (57 BC)**: Founded by Vikramaditya in 57 BC to mark his victory over the Sakas (*Current year calculation*: Gregorian Year + 57).
  * **Title Dynamics**: *Vikramaditya* became a supreme royal title in Indian history (held by over 25 kings, e.g., Chandragupta II).
  * **Folklore Origin**: The legendary **Vikram-Betal** (*Betal Pachisi / Baital Pachisi*) story originates in Gunadhya's Prakrit work ***Brihatkatha***.

## Saka Satrap System & Administrative Classification

* **Kushana Subordination**: By mid-1st c. AD, Saka kingdoms were defeated and subordinated by the **Kushana Empire** → Ruled as **Satraps**.
* **Satrap System Origin**:
  * **Achaemenid Persian Empire (6th c. BC)**: Originated the *Satrap* system of provincial governors / subordinate kings.
  * **Indianized / Prakritized Term**: **Chatrap** (Subordinate ruler holding another king's umbrella/flag) vs. **Chatrapati** (Sovereign monarch owning his own umbrella/flag, e.g., Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj).
* **Two Geopolitical Branches**:
  1. **Northern Satraps**: Capital at **Mathura**; Founder **Rajuvula**; patronized **Mathura School of Art**.
  2. **Western Satraps**: Capitals at **Bhrigukachha (Bharuch)** & **Ujjain**; patronized **Gandhara School of Art**.

## Western Satraps – Kshaharata Branch

* **Titles**: **Chatrap** and **Mahachatrap**.
* **Genealogy**: Founder **Abhiraka** (early 1st c. AD) → Son **Bhumaka** → Grandson **Nahapana**.

* **Reign of King Nahapana (1st / 2nd Century AD)**:
  * **Monastic Patronage**: Great patron of rock-cut **Chaitya and Vihara caves** along **Dakshinapatha** trade routes in the Western Ghats.
  * **Karla Caves**: Constructed the grand **Maha-Chaitya at Karla**, which became the benchmark for mature Chaitya architecture.

* **Trade Expansion & Saka-Satavahana Wars**:
  * **1st Century AD Trade Surge**: **Discovery of Monsoon Trade Winds** expanded Indian Ocean maritime commerce.
  * **Saka-Satavahana Conflict**: 2-century-long rivalry over Western Ghat passes, Arabian Sea ports and trade tax revenue.
  * **Classical Roman & Greek Sources**:
    * **Pliny the Elder**: Roman author of ***Natural History*** (1st c. AD).
    * ***Periplus of the Erythraean Sea***: Anonymous Greek travelogue (1st c. AD) describing Indian Ocean (*Erythraean Sea*) trade routes.
    * **Key Emporiums (*Market Towns*)**:
      * **Barygaza** (Sanskrit *Bhrigukachha* → British *Broach* → Modern *Bharuch* on mouth of Narmada River).
      * **Barbaricum** (Medieval *Thatta* → Modern *Karachi* on mouth of Indus River).

* **Fall of Kshaharata Branch**:
  * **Defeat by Gautamiputra Satakarni**: Satavahana King Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated Nahapana and destroyed the Kshaharata branch.
  * **Counter-Struck Coins**: Gautamiputra overstamped Nahapana's silver coins with his own emblem; assumed the victory title **Kshaharatakat** (*Ender of Kshaharata*).

## Western Satraps – Kardamaka Branch & Shaka Samvat

* **Titles**: **Mahachatrap**.
* **Founder**: **Chashtana** (relative of Nahapana); re-established Western Satrap rule in Gujarat and Malwa.

* **Shaka Samvat Era (78 AD)**:
  * **Origin**: Founded upon the coronation of Chashtana in **78 AD** (*Current year calculation*: Gregorian Year - 78).
  * **National Official Calendar**: In **1956/1957**, the **Calendar Reform Committee** recommended Shaka Samvat as the official civil calendar of the **Republic of India**.

* **Reign of Rudradaman I (2nd Century AD)**:
  * **Grandson of Chashtana**: Last and greatest ruler of the Kardamaka branch.
  * **Junagadh Prasasti / Girnar Inscription**:
    * **First Major Pure / Chaste Classical Sanskrit Inscription** in Indian history (shifting away from Prakrit).
    * Records the repair and reconstruction of **Sudarshana Lake** in Junagadh, Gujarat, executed without levying extra taxes or forced labor (*Visti*).
  * **Four Kings of Sudarshana Lake**:
    1. **Chandragupta Maurya** (Original construction by Governor Pushyagupta).
    2. **Ashoka** (Irrigation canals added by Governor Tushaspha).
    3. **Rudradaman I** (Reconstruction recorded in Junagadh Prasasti as *Mahachatrap*).
    4. **Skandagupta** (5th c. AD Gupta repairs by Governor Parnadatta/Chakrapalita).
  * **Court Culture & Science**: Indo-Greek scholar in Rudradaman's court popularized **Hora Shastra** (*Horoscopic Astrology*) in Western India.
  * **Satavahana Conflict**: Father-in-law and rival of Satavahana King **Vashishtiputra Pulumavi / Satakarni**; defeated Vashishtiputra twice but spared his life due to marital ties (recorded in Junagadh Prasasti).

* **End of Western Satraps**:
  * Continued ruling Gujarat as local subordinates until early 5th c. AD.
  * **Gupta Conquest**: Entirely ended by Gupta Emperor **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)**.

## Epigraphic Transition & The Phase of Sanskritization

* **Chronological Epigraphic Shift**:
  * **Pre-2nd Century AD**: Inscriptions composed in **Prakrit** (Ashokan Edicts, Heliodorus Pillar, Hathigumpha Inscription, Naneghat Prasasti, Nasik Prasasti).
  * **Post-2nd Century AD**: Inscriptions composed in **Sanskrit** (Junagadh Prasasti, Prayag Prasasti, Mehrauli Iron Pillar, Bhitari Inscription, Mandsaur Prasasti, Banskhera Inscription).

* **Causal Mechanics of Sanskritization**:
  * **Foreign Influx & Social Friction**: Rapid immigration of foreign tribes (Indo-Greeks, Sakas, Kushanas) + urbanization → Breaking of traditional social norms.
  * **Dharmashastra Reaction**: Orthodox Brahmins composed law books (e.g., ***Manusmriti***) to codify social order.
  * **Social Categorization**: Outsiders labeled either **Mleccha** (*unclean/impure*) or **Vratya Kshatriya** (*demoted/semi-pure Kshatriyas*).
  * **Adoption of Sanskrit**: To gain social legitimacy and elite status, foreign rulers adopted **Sanskrit** (*language of scholars and court elites*) in royal inscriptions, courts and coins.
  * **Buddhist Adoption**: Mahayana Buddhism adopted Sanskrit (e.g., **Asvaghosha**, **Vasumitra**, **Acharya Nagarjuna**) to compete for royal patronage.

* **Sanskritization Concept (M.N. Srinivas)**: Social process wherein non-elite groups, tribes, or foreign rulers adopt the customs, rituals and language of upper castes to elevate their social hierarchy.

## Structural Evolution: Vedic vs. Classical Sanskrit

| Feature | Vedic Sanskrit | Classical / Laukika Sanskrit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Period of Origin** | Early Vedic Age (~1200 BC) | Post-2nd Century AD (Standardized by Panini/Patanjali) |
| **Structural Design** | Simple, fluid sentence structure | Complex, highly stylized structure |
| **Vocabulary & Grammar** | Single uncompounded words, natural grammar | Heavy compound words (*Samasa*), tedious grammar |
| **Literary Ornamentation** | Direct, functional, unadorned | Heavy ornamentation (*Alankara*), double meanings |
| **Usage Domain** | Ancient Vedic hymns (*Samhitas*, *Brahmanas*) | Court poetry, royal inscriptions (*Prasastis*), Classical drama |

## Historical Linguistic Evolution Tree

**EVOLUTION OF INDIAN LANGUAGES**

Vedic Sanskrit
  - Classical / Laukika Sanskrit → Elite, Epigraphic & Court Language
  - Prakrit → Natural / Spoken Languages
    - Pali → Buddhist Canon
    - Dramatic / Literary Prakrit
      - Apabhramsha → Regional Transitional Dialects
        - Kauravi → Punjabi, Haryanvi, Khari Boli
        - Magadhi → Bhojpuri, Magahi, Angika, Assamese, Bengali, Odia
        - Maharashtri → Marathi, Gujarati
    - Rural Local Dialects → Regional / Tribal Vernaculars
