## Epigraphy & Inscriptional Classification
* **Epigraphy** = Study of inscriptions.
* **Numismatics** = Study of coinage.
* **Archaeology** = Study of material remains.
* **Classification Criteria**: Minor vs. Major distinction defined by later epigraphists/historians based on **descriptive nature/role**, NOT physical size or Ashoka's explicit labeling.

### 1. Minor Rock Edicts
* **Period**: Inscribed in **early years** of Ashoka's reign.
* **Core Focus**: Personal devotion and association with **Buddhism**.
* **Medium**: Inscribed on natural rocks/stones → **Rock Edicts**.
* **Nature**: Non-descriptive → **Minor Edicts**.
* **Location**: Placed on **highways & trade routes** (read aloud by officers to travelers/traders).

### 2. Major Rock Edicts
* **Period**: Inscribed in **middle years** of reign.
* **Core Focus**: Message to subjects/people on living a **moral and righteous life**.
* **Medium**: Inscribed on natural rock faces / large stone pieces.
* **Nature**: Detailed & descriptive → **Major Edicts**.
* **Location**: Placed on **highways & trade routes** (Major Rock Edict 7 instructs state officers to read edicts aloud to the public).

### 3. Pillar Edicts
* **Period**: Inscribed in **later years** of reign.
* **Core Focus**: Message to **future kings / successors** on how to rule the empire and govern subjects.
* **Medium**: Inscribed on free-standing **monolithic stone pillars** (7 pillars in total; some lack inscriptions).
* **Nature**: Both descriptive & non-descriptive → Epigraphists categorize as **Major Pillar Edicts** and **Minor Pillar Edicts**.
* **Location**: Placed at **8 sacred pilgrimage centers of Buddhism**.
* **Royal Nomenclature**: Name "Ashoka" appears in only 2-3 places (e.g., Maski, Gurjaras); elsewhere written as **Devanampiya** ("Beloved of Deities") or **Piyadasi** ("Of Gracious Appearance").

## Languages & Scripts of Ashokan Inscriptions

**ASHOKAN INSCRIPTIONAL CORPUS (≈90%)**
- Language → Prakrit
- Script → Brahmi

---

* **Single-Word Default Answer**: **Prakrit** (Language) & **Brahmi** (Script).
* **Language vs. Script Rule**:
  * **Language** = Spoken vocal communication.
  * **Script** = Written visual symbols representing language.

### Scripts & Languages
1. **Brahmi Script**:
   * Earliest deciphered readable script developed in India (Harappan script is older but undeciphered).
   * Developed c. **7th Century BC**.
   * Deciphered by **James Prinsep in 1830s**.
   * Direction: Written **Left-to-Right** (like Devanagari & Roman).
2. **Kharosthi Script**:
   * 2nd deciphered readable script developed in India.
   * Developed c. **5th / 4th Century BC** in NW India (under Persian Achaemenid Empire influence).
   * Formed via fusion of **Brahmi** and **Aramaic** scripts.
   * Direction: Written **Right-to-Left** (like Urdu/Persian/Arabic).
   * Usage: NW India / Pakistan / Afghanistan regions (e.g., Shahbazgarhi & Mansehra inscriptions).
3. **Aramaic**:
   * Language = **Aramaic**, Script = **Aramaic** (West Asian origin). Used in NW / Afghanistan frontier.
4. **Greek**:
   * Language = **Greek**, Script = **Greek** (Alpha, Beta, Gamma). Used in NW / Afghanistan frontier (e.g., Kandahar bilingual edict).
* **Sanskrit**: Used occasionally in Brahmi/Kharosthi scripts.

## Edicts vs. Rocks & "Separate Edicts" Concept
* **Edict = Specific article / textual point / message** (NOT the physical rock).
* **14 Major Rock Edicts**: Represents 14 distinct messages/articles, NOT 14 rock locations (same 14 edicts inscribed on multiple rocks across regions).

### Separate Edicts (Localized Message Substitution)
* **Definition**: Omission of a standard global edict message at a specific site, replaced by a localized alternate message.
* **Case Study: 13th Major Rock Edict**:
  * **Standard 13th Edict**: Details Ashoka's remorse over slaughter in **Kalinga War** (261 BC) and shift from *Bherighosha* to *Dhammaghosha*.
  * **Kalinga Region Exception (Dhauli & Jaugada)**: 13th Edict message **omitted** inside Kalinga territory.
  * **Replacement**: Substituted by **Separate Rock Edict 1 & 2** instructing state officers to treat Kalinga people with mercy and urging local population to cooperate with state authorities.
  * **Causal Reasoning**: Ashoka avoided constantly reminding conquered Kalinga citizens of their military defeat and humiliation on public rocks.
  * **Material Reality**: Separate Edicts are part of Major/Minor rock edicts inscribed on the same rock/pillar surface, NOT on separate physical materials.

## Ashokan Dhamma Policy: Ideology & Constituents

**ASHOKAN DHAMMA**
- Rule by Morality → Spread of Morality
- Ethical Code (Way of Life) → Emphasis on Ethical Conduct Over Religious Ritualism
- Rooted in Buddhist Philosophy → Expressed Through Universal Human Values

---

* **Administrative Ideology**: **Dhamma Policy** = State governance through moral persuasion to foster universal righteousness.
* **Nature**: Social and ethical code of conduct; NOT a new religion or ritual imposition.
* **Welfare Extension**: Covers both **human welfare** and **animal welfare** (banning animal sacrifice, establishing hospitals/dispensaries for humans & animals).
* **Key Virtues Enjoined**:
  * Absence of negative traits, social harmony, religious tolerance across all sects, equal respect to elders, gentle treatment of slaves/servants (*Dasas* & *Karmakaras*).

### Constituents of Dhamma (Ashokan Inscriptional Vocabulary)
1. **Satya** = Truthfulness (Aramaic rendering: *QSYT* / *QSY* / *T* = Truthful/Lawful).
2. **Ahimsa** = Non-injury / Non-violence to living beings.
3. **Dana** = Charity / Almsgiving.
4. **Shauchaya** = Inner purity / Cleanliness.
5. **Sadhave** = Goodness / Virtue.
6. **Madhave** = Gentleness / Sweetness in speech & conduct.
7. **Apasinave** = Freedom from evil / Sinlessness.
8. **Daya** = Mercy / Compassion (Greek rendering: *Eusebeia* = Piety/Compassion).
* **Relevance**: Universal virtues independent of time and geographical context; if applied today, eliminates social conflicts and personal distress.

## Mauryan & Contemporary Literature

### 1. Secular & Statecraft Literature
* **Indica**: Authored by **Megasthenes** (Greek ambassador of Seleucus Nicator); Language = **Greek**.
* **The Four Sanskrit Works Attributed to Kautilya / Chanakya**:
  * *Modern Historiographical Synthesis*: 4 historical names represent the same individual (**Kautilya**, **Chanakya**, **Vishnugupta**, **Shanaka**).
  1. ***Arthashastra***:
     * Author: **Kautilya**.
     * Structure: **15 Adhikaranas** (Chapters).
     * Content: Political Economy, Statecraft, Bureaucracy, Espionage, & Law.
  2. ***Niti Shastra / Chanakya Niti***:
     * Author: **Chanakya**.
     * Content: Human behavior, realpolitik, pragmatic self-defense and practical survival tactics (e.g., non-venomous snake pretending to possess venom to survive).
  3. ***Chanakya Shataka***:
     * Author: **Chanakya**.
     * Structure: **100 Verses** (*Shataka* = 100).
     * Content: Ethical poetry.
  4. ***Panchatantra***:
     * Author: **Vishnu Sharma** (In *Mudrarakshasa*, Chanakya is named *Vishnugupta*).
     * Structure: **5 Tantras** (Chapters):
       1. *Mitra Bheda* (Sowing discord among allies).
       2. *Mitra Samprapti* (Gaining allies).
       3. *Kakulokiyam* (War and peace / when to fight or negotiate; e.g., Crows vs. Owls story).
       4. *Labdha Pranasham* (Loss of hard-earned gains through foolishness).
       5. *Apariksita Karakam* (Disastrous outcomes of rash actions).
     * Content: Statecraft, diplomacy, & moral wisdom via animal fables.
  5. ***Medical Treatise by Shanaka***:
     * Recorded by 9th/10th-century Arab travelers **Al-Biruni** & **Sulaiman**.
     * Phonetic Shift: Arab translators converted CH → SH → *Shanaka* = *Chanaka* / *Chanakya*. Content = Medicine.

### 2. Buddhist Canonical Literature
* ***Kathavatthu***: Authored by **Moggaliputta Tissa** during 3rd Buddhist Council (Pataliputra, c. 250 BC); Language = **Pali** (a refined form of Prakrit); Content = Refutation of heretical viewpoints & points of controversy; added as 7th book to **Abhidhamma Pitaka**.

### 3. Jain Canonical History & Literature

**JAIN CANONICAL STRUCTURE (46 AGAMAS)**
- Core Central Corpus → 12 Angas (Anga Agamas)
- Supplementary Corpus → 34 Agamas
- Total → 46 Agamas

---

* **Tirthankara & Lineage Hierarchy**:
  * **24 Tirthankaras**: 1st = **Rishabhdev / Adinath** (Sons: **Bahubali** at Shravanabelagola, **Bharat**; Daughter: **Brahmi** → origin of Brahmi script); 23rd = **Parshvanatha**; 24th = **Vardhamana Mahavira** (*Vardhamana* = Ever-growing).
  * **11 Gandharas**: Original 11 direct disciples of Mahavira (*Gandhara* = Those who chant/preach teachings). 10 died in Mahavira's lifetime; only **1 survived** = **Arya Sudharma** (last Gandhara).
  * **Thera** = Supreme administrative head of Jain monastic community after last Tirthankara.
    * **1st Thera**: **Arya Sudharma**.
    * **6th Thera**: **Bhadrabahu** (Last Thera of unified, undivided Jainism).
  * **12 Angas**: Compiled by 11 Gandharas containing core teachings of 24 Tirthankaras. Remaining 34 Agamas compiled by later monks.

#### First Jain Council (c. 300 – 280 BC)
* **Location**: **Pataliputra** (Patna, Bihar).
* **President**: **Sthulabhadra** (Sthulabahu).
* **Patron**: No specific royal patron recorded (Bindusara uninvolved; Chandragupta Maurya had already abdicated and migrated South with Bhadrabahu due to a predicted 12-year famine).
* **Objective**: Re-collect and re-compile Jain canonical texts following the 12-year famine in Magadha.
* **Outcomes**:
  1. Recompiled **45 Agamas** (**12th Anga lost**, named ***Drishtivada*** / ***Ditthivada*** in Prakrit).
  2. **First Schism / Split in Jainism**:
     * **Svetambaras** (White-Clad): Led by Sthulabhadra; held that 45 Agamas survived (12th Anga lost); accepted white garments (Parshvanatha wore white; Mahavira did not forbid clothes).
     * **Digambaras** (Sky-Clad / Nude): Led by Bhadrabahu's tradition; held that all **46 original Agamas were completely lost** (only core summaries survived); enforced total nudity as absolute non-possession (*Aparigraha*).

#### Mauryan-Era Jain Texts
1. **Compilation of 45 Agamas**: Finalized at First Jain Council.
2. ***Kalpasutra***: Authored by **Bhadrabahu**; Content = Biographies of the 24 Tirthankaras.
3. ***Bhadrabahu Samhita***: Authored by **Bhadrabahu**; Content = Jain Astrology & Cosmology.

## Mauryan Art & Architecture

### 1. Mauryan Painting
* **Archaeological Status**: **NO paintings of the Mauryan Age have been discovered so far**.
* **Earliest Historical Paintings**: **Ajanta Paintings** (Post-Mauryan Age, c. 2nd Century BC onwards).
  * *Distinction*: Prehistoric rock art at Bhimbetka belongs to *Pre-history*; Ajanta represents earliest *Historical* painting.
* **Stylistic Implication**: High artistic sophistication of Ajanta proves prior painting traditions existed during Mauryan times on **perishable media** (wood, cloth, palm leaves) or await archaeological discovery.
* **Literary References**: Textual accounts record paintings under Ashoka (e.g., a painting depicting Buddha bowing to Mahavira enraged Ashoka, leading to a decree offering 1 gold coin per Jain monk's head; 16,000 monks killed in 1 day before Ashoka's brother **Vitashoka / Vitasoka** was mistakenly executed, prompting Ashoka to halt the massacre).

### 2. Cave Architecture: Evolution & Structural Mechanics

**EVOLUTION OF ROCK-CUT CAVES**
- Saptaparni Cave (Rajgir) → Natural Cave; Venue of the 1st Buddhist Council
- Barabar & Nagarjuni Caves (Gaya) → Earliest Man-Made Rock-Cut Caves in India; Mauryan Period

---

* **Natural vs. Man-Made**: Natural caves (e.g., Saptaparni) do not constitute cave *architecture*. Cave *architecture* requires **man-made excavation & structural shaping**.
* **Mauryan Cave Engineering**: Carved out of massive solid granite rock boulders.
  * Entry carved into precise rectangular doorway shapes.
  * Interior walls smoothed and polished to a mirror-like shine (**Mauryan Polish**).

#### Lomas Rishi Cave (Barabar Complex, Gaya)
* **Sectarian Dedication**: Donated to **Ajivika Sect**.
* **Typology**: **Chaitya Cave** (Prayer / Meditation Hall).
* **Architectural Highlight**: Famous for its **Rock-Cut Facade Arch** (Front entrance decorative wall).
* **Structural Elements of Facade Arch**:
  * Rectangular doorway entrance.
  * Inner decorative arch & outer arch featuring carved teeth/petal patterns (**Chaitya Arch**).
  * Top crowning element (**Finial**).
  * Flanking pillar-like structural supports.
  * Carved elephant frieze in relief depicting devotion.
* **Legacy**: Outer arch style became the signature **Chaitya Arch** replicated across all post-Mauryan Buddhist/Jain rock-cut Chaityas (e.g., Bhaja, Bedse, Karle, Kanheri, Nasik, Ajanta).
* **Persian Parallel**: Contemporary **Lycian Tombs of Persia (Iran)** feature identical sculpted arches, suggesting Achaemenid Persian artistic influence on Mauryan rock-cut forms.

#### Barabar & Nagarjuni Cave Complexes Overview
* **Barabar Caves (Ashoka's Donations)**: 4 Caves - **Sudama**, **Lomas Rishi**, **Karna Chaupar**, **Visvakarma**.
* **Nagarjuni Caves (Dasharatha Maurya's Donations)**: 3 Caves - **Gopi**, **Vapi**, **Vadathika** (Dasharatha was Ashoka's grandson).
* **Total Mauryan Ajivika Caves** = **7 Caves**.
* **Architectural Uniqueness**: Only **Lomas Rishi Cave** features the sculpted arch facade; all other 6 caves feature plain, unadorned rectangular entryways.
* **Interior Polishing Technique**: **Mauryan Polish** achieved manually by prolonged rubbing of stone blocks, leather and sand against granite interiors.

#### Mauryan vs. Post-Mauryan Cave Architecture
| Architectural Attribute | Mauryan Cave Architecture | Post-Mauryan Cave Architecture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exterior Facade** | Plain & unadorned (except Lomas Rishi) | Heavily decorated with sculpted pillars, relief panels, & arches |
| **Interior Decoration** | Mirror-polished smooth walls (**Mauryan Polish**); no murals | Elaborate relief sculptures, carved pillars, & **Murals** (wall paintings) |
| **Sectarian Association** | Primarily **Ajivika Sect** | **Buddhist** & **Jain** Sects |
| **Structural Elements** | Single or double simple chamber | Multi-chambered **Chaityas** (with internal Stupa) & **Viharas** (monk cells) |

#### True Arch vs. Rock-Cut Arch
* **Rock-Cut Arch (Mauryan / Lomas Rishi)**: Sculpted directly into a solid rock boulder; does NOT support its own weight or rely on structural mechanics.
* **True Arch (Free-Standing Arch)**: Built with independent stone voussoirs and a central **Keystone** to hold structural weight; appeared in India in the **13th Century AD** (Delhi Sultanate period).
