## Shravanabelagola & Chandragiri Hills
* **Geographical Location**: **Chandragiri Hills**, **Hassan District**, **Karnataka**.
* **10th Century AD Development**: **Western Ganga Dynasty** constructed a colossal monolithic stone statue of **Bahubali / Gommateshwara**.
* **Genealogy of Bahubali**:
  * Son of **Rishabhdev (Adinath)** = 1st Jain Tirthankara.
  * **Children of Rishabhdev**:
    * **Son 1: Bahubali / Gommateshwara** → Colossal statue at Shravanabelagola.
    * **Son 2: Bharat** → Name *Bharat* (India) attributed to him in Jain tradition.
    * **Daughter: Brahmi** → Taught writing by Rishabhdev → Origin of **Brahmi script** in Jain tradition.
* **Mahamastakabhisheka**:
  * Grand Jain ritual performed once every **12 years** on Gommateshwara statue.
  * Pouring of 5 sacred substances over the head of Bahubali.
  * **Timeline**: Last performed in **2018** → Next scheduled in **2030**.
* **Basti / Basadi**:
  * **Basti / Basadi** = Local / rural term for **Jain Temple**.
  * **Chandragupta Basti**:
    * Built by **Western Ganga Dynasty** at the site where **Chandragupta Maurya** committed ritual starvation.
    * **Sallekhana** (Digambara term) / **Santhara** (Svetambara term) = Ritual death by starvation.
    * Committed before the Svetambara–Digambara schism.
  * **Monk Companion**: Migrated southwards with Jain monk **Bhadrabahu** (depicted on Bhadrabahu panel at Shravanabelagola).
* **Architectural Style**:
  * **Chandragupta Basti** = **Dravida (Southern Indian) Style** → Features pyramidal tower (**Vimana**), large top stone and **Kalasha** (vs. Northern **Nagara Style** → Curvilinear **Shikhara**, **Amalaka** disc and **Kalasha**).

## Reign of Bindusara (298 BC – 273 BC / 268 BC)
* **Lineage**: Son and successor of **Chandragupta Maurya**.
* **Nomenclature & Titles**:
  * **Greek Name**: **Amitrochates / Amikokottos** (in Greek texts).
  * **Sanskrit Title**: **Amitraghataka / Amitraghatam** = "Slayer / Killer of Enemies" (*Amitra* = Enemy, *Ghataka* = Slayer).
* **Southern Territorial Expansion**:
  * Expanded Mauryan Empire southwards till **Lower Deccan**.
  * *Historiographical Note*: Some historians hold that southern expansion was initiated/completed by Chandragupta Maurya.
* **Sangam Literature Evidence**:
  * Mauryans recorded as **Moriyar** in Sangam Tamil literature (detailing southern campaign).
  * Pre-Mauryan oppression by **Nanda Dynasty** recorded in Sangam text ***Akananuru***.
* **Religious Affiliation**:
  * Follower of **Ajivika Sect**.
  * Disciple of Ajivika monk **Pingalavatsa** (visited court; predicted Ashoka's future greatness → disliked by Bindusara & other sons).

### Ajivika Sect
* **Founder**: **Nandavatsa (Nanda Vacha)**.
* **Main Proponent**: **Makkhali Gosala (Makkhali Ghoshal)**.
* **Core Philosophy**: **Absolute Predestination / Fatalism (Niyati / Niyativada)**.
  * Rejects free will; everything in cosmos is pre-determined and pre-destined.
  * Human thoughts, actions and effort are pre-determined by universal natural laws.
  * Performing Karma is pointless as human effort itself is pre-determined.
* **Literature & Historical Reconstitution**:
  * Ajivika original literature did NOT survive.
  * Doctrines preserved strictly through hostile critiques in Jain and Buddhist texts.
* **Derogatory Rival Terms**:
  * **Ajivika** = "As good as dead" (mocking refusal to exert human will for survival).
  * **Shudra Sanyasi** = Monks devoid of wealth (*Shudra* = "No wealth", same etymological root as *Daridra* → *Dalid* → *Dalit* = wealthless; contrast with Rigvedic *Dravida / Dravina* = wealth).

### Foreign Diplomatic Relations & Travelers
* **Greek Ambassadors at Bindusara's Court**:
  1. **Deimachus** (Sent by Seleucid King Antiochus I).
  2. **Dionysius** (Sent by Ptolemaic Egyptian King Ptolemy II Philadelphus).
* **Greek Traveler**: **Lambulus** visited India during Bindusara's reign (work now lost).

## Mauryan Civil War & Succession (273 BC – 268 BC)
* Succession war fought among sons of Bindusara.
* **Ashoka** emerged victorious by killing all brothers except one:
  * **Vishtashoka (Vitasoka)** (in Northern Indian texts) / **Tissa** (in Sri Lankan / Southern texts).
  * Spared and later guided by Ashoka to become a Buddhist monk.

## Reign of Ashoka (268 BC – 232 BC)

### Kalinga War (261 BC / 8th Regnal Year)
* Ashoka launched invasion and conquered **Kalinga** after a brutal, bloody war.
* Marked the **Territorial Peak** of the Mauryan Empire.

### Post-War Transformation & Dhamma Policy
* Buddhist texts record conversion to Buddhism and renunciation of warfare after Kalinga.
* **Linguistic Note**: *Dhamma* = Prakrit form of Sanskrit *Dharma* (dropping 'r' sound: *Dharma* → *Dhamma*, *Sapta* → *Sat*, *Ashta* → *At*).

### 5 Core Policy Transformations
1. **Chandashoka** (Violent & Cruel Ashoka) → **Dhammashoka** (Moral & Benevolent Ashoka).
2. **Bherighosha** (Military threat / war trumpet sound) → **Dhammaghosha** (Moral persuasion & righteous conduct).
3. **Digvijaya** (Military conquest of 10 directions; *Dig* = direction, 10 directions each with a *Digpala* god on a *Diggaja* elephant) → **Dhammavijaya** (Moral victory by forming peaceful alliances).
4. **Appointment of Dhamma Mahamattas**: Special cadre of state officers appointed to enforce public morality and welfare.
5. **Dhamma Pracharaks**: Foreign moral ambassadors sent to propagate Dhamma:
   * Sent son **Mahendra** and daughter **Sanghamitra** to **Sri Lanka**.

### Critical Historiographical Revision
* **Minor Rock Edicts** prove Ashoka was ALREADY a Buddhist prior to Kalinga War / during coronation.
* Transition from *Chandashoka* to *Dhammashoka* represents a religious exaggeration to glorify conversion.

## Third Buddhist Council (c. 250 BC)
* **Date**: c. **250 BC**.
* **Location**: **Pataliputra**.
* **Patron**: **Ashoka**.
* **President**: **Moggaliputta Tissa**.
* **Primary Objective**: To end **sectarianism / schism** in Buddhism (*Religion* = fundamental divergence vs. *Sect* = interpretive difference within shared fundamentals).

### Background: Buddhist Canon & Early Councils
* **Tripitaka (Three Baskets of Canon)**:
  1. **Sutta Pitaka**: Direct messages & formulas (*Sutra*) of Buddha. Divided into 5 **Nikayas**:
     * *Digha Nikaya* (Long)
     * *Majjhima Nikaya* (Middle)
     * *Khuddaka Nikaya* (Minor; contains **Jatakas** = past-life stories of Buddha)
     * *Anguttara Nikaya* (Numbered)
     * *Samyutta Nikaya* (Connected / Miscellaneous)
  2. **Vinaya Pitaka**: Monastic rules for **Bhikkhus** (monks) & **Bhikkhunis** (nuns); lay followers = **Upasakas**.
  3. **Abhidhamma Pitaka**: Higher philosophy of Buddhism (*Abhi* = higher form, e.g., *Abhijit* = glorious victory).
* **First Buddhist Council (483 BC)**:
  * Location: **Saptaparni Cave, Rajagriha**.
  * Patron: **Ajatashatru** | President: **Mahakashyapa**.
  * Outcome: Compiled **Sutta Pitaka** and **Vinaya Pitaka**.
* **Second Buddhist Council (383 BC – 100 Years Later)**:
  * Location: **Vaishali**.
  * Patron: **Kalasoka (Kakavarna)** | President: **Sabakami**.
  * Cause: Disputes over monastic relaxation in **Vinaya Pitaka** (salt storage, comfort, gold/silver acceptance).
  * Outcome: **First Major Schism**:
    1. **Sthaviravadins (Theravadins)**: Conservative/rigid (*Sthira* = stable), held Buddha as a human (*Thera* = elder monk).
    2. **Mahasanghikas**: Liberal, formed larger group (*Mahasangha*), held Buddha as divine.
    * Evolution by 2nd century AD: Sthaviravadins → Hinayana basis; Mahasanghikas → **Mahayana**.

### Outcomes of Third Council
1. **Failure to Eliminate Sectarianism**: Sectarian divisions persisted (Ashoka personally expelled 6,000 heretical monks on Day 1).
2. **Compilation of Kathavatthu**: Moggaliputta Tissa authored the 7th chapter ***Kathavatthu*** ("Points of Controversy"), systematically documenting debate points in Buddhist philosophy. Integrated into **Abhidhamma Pitaka**, establishing its final present form.

## Ashoka's Religious Mentors & Cave Donations
* **Initiation Teacher**: ***Divyavadana*** names monk **Upagupta** as Ashoka's spiritual teacher (scholars note Upagupta and Moggaliputta Tissa may be identical or variant names).
* **Barabar Caves Donation**:
  * Ashoka donated a complex of **4 rock-cut caves** to **Ajivika monks** at **Gaya, Bihar** (includes Sudama Cave, Lomas Rishi Cave).
* **Dasharatha Maurya (Grandson of Ashoka)**:
  * Donated a complex of **3 rock-cut caves** to **Ajivika monks** at **Nagarjuni Hills, Gaya, Bihar** (Nagarjuni Caves).
  * Combined Barabar (4) + Nagarjuni (3) = **7 Ajivika Caves**.
  * *Significance*: Demonstrates state religious pluralism (Chandragupta = Jain, Bindusara = Ajivika, Ashoka = Buddhist, Dasharatha = Ajivika).
  * *Deccan Loss*: During Dasharatha's reign, Mauryans lost Deccan territories to the **Andhra / Satavahana Dynasty** (formerly Mauryan subordinates).

## Later Mauryan Kings & Fall of Dynasty
* **Samprati Maurya**: Grandson of Ashoka (son of Kunala). Follower of **Jainism**. Re-conquered western territories.
* **Decline**: Series of short-reigned, weak successors with conflicting lists across sources.
* **Brihadratha Maurya**: Last Mauryan Emperor.
  * Assassinated in **185 BC** by his military commander-in-chief (**Senapati**), **Pushyamitra Shunga**, during a military parade.
  * Pushyamitra Shunga established the **Shunga Dynasty**.

## Megasthenes' Indica vs. Tesias' Indica

### Tesias' Indica (c. 5th Century BC – 100 Years Before Megasthenes)
* **Author**: Tesias, Greek court physician in Achaemenid Persia. Never visited India.
* **Sources**: Based on hearsay and accounts of Indian traders/travelers.
* **Rejection**: Dismissed by contemporary Greeks (Arrian called him an "inexperienced liar") for absurd fabrications (dog-headed barking humans, one-legged *Monosceli* using foot as umbrella, chest-faced humans, *Manticore / Matakhor* lion with human head & scorpion tail).

### Megasthenes' Indica
* **Author**: Ambassador sent by **Seleucus Nicator** to court of **Sandrokottos (Chandragupta Maurya)**.
* **Key Observations & Accounts**:
  1. **Wealth of India**: Agrarian surplus engine; majority population = farmers.
  2. **Dominance of Life-Cycle Rituals**: Ceremonies for every stage (pre-birth, 6th day, 5th month, head shaving, marriage, pre-exam/post-result traditions).
  3. **Large Population Density**.
  4. **High Moral Character & Business Honesty**: Trade conducted without written contracts/agreements; unaware of formal written scripts in daily commerce.
  5. **Status of Women**: Good position; example: Chandragupta Maurya maintained **500 female bodyguards** (protecting king against poisoning, *Vishkanyas* and internal court conspiracies).
  6. **Strong Military Organization**.
  7. **Absence of Slavery in India**:
     * *Contrast with Kautilya's Arthashastra*: Arthashastra details an entire chapter on **Dasa** (slaves).
     * *Historiographical Synthesis*: Both accounts are accurate—Indian *Dasas* possessed legal protections, property rights and humane treatment, unlike European chattel slavery. To a Greek used to brutal chattel slavery, Indian *Dasas* appeared as free employees.
  8. **Capital Pataliputra (Palibothra)**: Large, planned city; primary construction material was **WOOD**.
     * *Archaeological Proof*: Confirmed in **1913** when **ASI** excavated massive Mauryan wooden palace platforms and monolithic Chunar sandstone pillars at **Kumrahar (Patna, Bihar)**, funded by Ratanji Tata.
  9. **Units of Measurement**:
     * Length: **Stadia** (e.g., 10 stadia).
     * Weight: **Talent**.
  10. **Seven-Fold Division of Indian Society**:
      * Replaced traditional 4-fold Varna system with a **7-fold endogamous and hereditary division** (modeled on Herodotus' 7-fold classification of Egypt):
      * Each division termed a **Genos** (endogamous & hereditary).
      * **7 Classes (Genos)**: Philosophers, Farmers, Herdsmen/Pastoralists, Artisans/Craftsmen, Military/Soldiers, Inspectors/Spies, Magistrates/Councillors.
