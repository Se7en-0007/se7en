## Commemorative Architecture & Roots of Stupa
* **Universal Practice**: Memorials erected across all cultures to respect the dead (Cremation/Burial → Taj Mahal, Humayun's Tomb, Pyramids, Rajghat / Samadhi Sthal).
* **Megalithic Roots**: Pre-historic practice of placing large stones (*Megaliths*) to honor the dead.
* **Stupa Definition**: Architecture raised atop the **relics** (*Dhatu* - unburnt bones, teeth, collarbones) of **Buddha** or **Buddhist monks**.
* **Origin & Symbolism**:
  * Derived from pre-Buddhist megalithic earthen mounds.
  * **Buddhist Iconography**: Stupa represents a **Water Bubble** → Symbol of **death** & **impermanence of life** (*Anicca* / transient existence).

## Buddhist & Comparative Iconography
* **Iconography Definition**: Visual symbols used to identify divine/historical figures (e.g., Krishna = Flute + Peacock Feather; Shiva = Third Eye / *Trinetra*).

### Life Events & Symbols of Gautama Buddha
* **Arrival of Buddha**: **White Elephant** (e.g., Lomas Rishi cave frieze).
* **Birth of Buddha**: **Bull** (universal symbol of fertility across cultures, e.g., Nandi in Shaivism) & **Lotus** (*Padma* → Meditation pose *Padmasana*; Ram called Padma in Jain Ramayana).
* **Renunciation (Mahabhinishkramana)**: **Horse** (*Ashwa* - Siddhartha departed on his horse Kanthaka).
* **Meditation & Enlightenment (Nirvana)**: **Bodhi Tree** & **Chaitya Arch**.
* **First Sermon (Dharmachakra Pravartana at Sarnath)**: **Chakra / Wheel** (24-spoke wheel of law).
* **Death / Liberation (Mahaparinirvana)**: **Bubble** / **Stupa**.
* **Buddha Himself**: **Lion** (*Simha*).

### Cross-Religious
* **Jainism**:
  * **1st Tirthankara (Rishabhdev / Adinath)**: **Bull** (*Vrishabha*).
  * **24th Tirthankara (Mahavira)**: **Lion** (*Simha*).
* **Hinduism**:
  * **4th Avatar of Vishnu (Narasimha)**: **Lion**.
  * **Goddess Durga / Chandi**: **Lion** (depicted as vehicle on majority of Gupta gold coins).

## Structural Anatomy & Philosophy of Stupa (Sanchi Maha Stupa / Stupa No. 1)
* **Relic**: Buried underground below the central axis.
* **Anda**: Hemispherical earthen/stone mound → Symbolizes **Bubble** = Impermanence of life & absolute reality of death.
* **Harmika**: Square enclosed railing atop Anda → Symbolizes **Life** (life rests upon the ultimate truth of death/impermanence).
* **Yasti**: Central vertical stone mast passing through Harmika → Symbolizes **cosmic axis / connection between this life and next life** (transfer of Karma).
* **Chhatra (Three Umbrella Shades)**: Three stacked umbrellas atop Yasti → Symbolizes **Triratna of Buddhism** (**Buddha**, **Dhamma**, **Sangha**; life lived under 3 shelters).
* **Later Structural Additions (Shunga Period - 1st Century BC)**:
  * **Upper Railing** (around terrace / *Medhi*).
  * **Lower Railing** (ground circumambulatory path / *Pradakshina Patha*).
  * **Toranas (4 Ceremonial Gateways)**: Facing North, South, East, West.
* **Construction**: Stone brick core coated with plaster (now lost).

## Monastic Architecture: Shramana, Vihara & Chaitya
* **Shramana vs. Brahmana**:
  * **Shramana / Shramanic Sects**: Ascetic traditions (**Buddhism**, **Jainism**, **Ajivika**) based on individual effort (*Shrama*), meditation and renunciation.
  * **Brahmana**: Priest-led, ritualistic, Vedic tradition.
* **Vihara**: Monastic residence/resthouse for monks.
  * Monks wander 8 months/year; reside in Viharas during **4 monsoon months** (**Vasa** in Buddhism; **Chaumasa / Chaumas** in Jainism).
  * **Architecture**: No fixed shape (caves, houses, or whole regions; e.g., **Bihar State** named after density of Viharas).
* **Chaitya**: Monastic prayer hall / meditation chamber.
  * Derived from **Chitta** (mind / inner consciousness / soul beyond body).
  * **Signature Architectural Features**:
    1. **Chaitya Arch** (horseshoe-shaped outer arch with teeth/petals) on facade.
    2. **Stupa inside Chaitya** (monks perform circumambulation / meditation around interior stupa).

## Imperial Pillars of Ashoka (Dhamma Stambha)

### Achaemenid Persian vs. Mauryan Pillars

| Feature | Achaemenid Persian Pillars (Darius I) | Ashokan Imperial Pillars |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structure** | Constructed of joined stone segments | **Monolithic** (carved from single Chunar sandstone block) |
| **Location / Purpose** | Part of royal palace halls (e.g., Persepolis) | Independent free-standing pillars on trade routes / holy sites |
| **Inscriptions** | Royal administrative decrees | Edicts of **Dhamma** and moral governance |
| **Lotus Base** | Inverted Lotus / Bell shape | **Inverted Lotus** (more ornate / bell-shaped) |
| **Abacus** | Plain stone block | Circular / Rectangular disk with carved animals and **Chakras** |
| **Capital** | Back-to-back joined twin animal figures (e.g., bulls/unicorns) | 4 back-to-back Lions or single Animal (Lion, Bull, Elephant) |
| **Top Element** | None | **Dhammachakra** (Wheel of Law) mounted on socket |
| **Polish** | High surface polish | **Mauryan High-Gloss Surface Polish** |

### Prominent Ashokan Pillars & Capitals
* **Sarnath Lion Capital**:
  * **Capital**: 4 back-to-back Lions (representing Buddha & spread of Dhamma in 4 directions).
  * **Abacus**: Circular disk with 4 animals (**Elephant** = Arrival, **Bull** = Birth, **Horse** = Renunciation, **Lion** = Buddha) separated by 4 **24-spoke Chakras**.
  * **Top**: Originally held a massive **Dhammachakra** (24-spoke wheel).
* **Rampurva Pillar** (West Champaran, Bihar): **Bull Capital** (presently housed at Rashtrapati Bhavan, New Delhi).
* **Sankissa / Sankisa Pillar** (Farrukhabad, UP): **Elephant Capital** (trunk broken).
* **Vaishali Pillar** (Bihar): Single **Lion Capital**, uncarved abacus, no Mauryan polish.
* **Sanchi Pillar** (MP): 4 Lions, abacus carved with **Swans / Geese** (*Hamsa*), no polish.

## National & Judicial Emblems of India

### 1. National Emblem of Republic of India
* **Source**: Adopted from Abacus & Lion Capital of **Sarnath Ashokan Pillar**.
* **Motto**: **Satyameva Jayate** ("Truth Alone Triumphs").
* **Source Text**: ***Mundaka Upanishad***.

### 2. Emblem of The Supreme Court of India
* **Components**: Abacus, Lion Capital and top **Dhammachakra** of Sarnath Pillar.
* **Motto**: **Yato Dharmastato Jayah** ("Where there is Law / Righteousness, there is Victory").
* **Source Text**: ***Mahabharata*** (Lord Krishna's statement to Karna).

## Yaksha & Yakshini Sculptures

### Concept & Cult
* **Identity**: Pre-Vedic nature spirits and guardian deities of local wealth (orchards, ponds, lakes, soil, trees).
* **King of Yakshas**: **Kubera** (Chief attendant in Hinduism = Manibhadra).
* **Cross-Religious Integration**: Incorporated into Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism as minor protector deities.

### 5 Key Style Features of Mauryan Yaksha/Yakshini Sculptures
1. **Fat Bodies**: Portrayed with rounded bellies (ancient indicator of prosperity & wealth).
2. **Heavy Jewelry**: Adorned with elaborate necklaces, armlets, anklets and headbands.
3. **Happy / Smiling Faces**: Auspicious, smiling expressions (gods must reflect joy and abundance).
4. **Unclothed Upper Body**: Females depicted without garments on upper torso (artistic convention & social reality of early India, standard until Gupta Age *Shilpa Shastras*).
5. **Mauryan High-Gloss Polish**: Distinctive lustrous surface finish.

### Major Specimens
* **Didarganj Yakshini**:
  * **Discovery**: Uncovered in **1917** at Didarganj near Patna, Bihar by washermen; housed in **Patna Museum**.
  * **Iconography**: Life-sized (5.5 ft) polished sandstone statue; smiling face, heavy jewelry, headband with central forehead gem, holding **Chauri** (fly-whisk) in right hand (attending King Kubera), left hand broken.
  * **Continuity**: Direct stylistic & iconographic link to IVC Mother Goddess figures.
* **Parkham Yaksha**:
  * **Discovery**: Parkham near Mathura; dated 2nd Century BC; represents **Manibhadra Yaksha**.
  * **Significance**: Transitional link between Mauryan sculpture and **Mathura School of Art**.
* **Modern Depiction**: Monumental Yaksha and Yakshini statues flanking entrance of **Reserve Bank of India (RBI), New Delhi** (carved by Ramkinkar Baij in 1960s to guard national wealth/forex).

## Post-Mauryan Period: Shunga Dynasty (185 BC – 73 BC)

### Rise of Pushyamitra Shunga
* **Origin**: Military commander (*Senapati*) of the last Mauryan king **Brihadratha**.
* **Usurpation (185 BC)**: Assassinated Brihadratha during a military parade.
* **Varna**: Brahmin varna.
* **Religious & Cultural Shift**: Replaced Mauryan patronage of Shramana sects with **Brahmanism / Hinduism**.
* **Language & Script Policy**:
  * Replaced Prakrit with **Sanskrit** as the official court and inscriptional language.
  * Retained **Brahmi script**.
* **Capitals**: Primary capital shifted from **Pataliputra to Ayodhya**; secondary capital established at **Vidisha**.

### Inscriptional & Literary Evidence
* **Ayodhya Prashasti of Dhandeva**:
  * Commissioned by later Shunga king Dhandeva; written in **Sanskrit language** and **Brahmi script**.
  * Records Pushyamitra Shunga performing **2 Ashvamedha Yajnas** (horse sacrifices).
* **Rishi Patanjali**:
  * Served as chief priest at Pushyamitra's coronation (*Rajyabhisheka*).
  * Authored ***Mahabhasya*** (authoritative commentary on Sanskrit grammar).

### The Grammar Trilogy (Vyakarana Trayi)

**Panini (Ashtadhyayi) → Katyayana (Varttikakara) → Patanjali (Mahabhashya)**

1. ***Ashtadhyayi*** by **Panini** (Foundational Sanskrit grammar).
2. ***Vartikakar*** by **Katyayana** (Critical notes/annotations on Ashtadhyayi).
3. ***Mahabhasya*** by **Patanjali** (Elaborate, authoritative commentary).

### Commentary Terminology (*Katha* & Exegesis)
* **Bhasya**: Most elaborate, authoritative commentary (e.g., *Mahabhasya*).
* **Vritti / Tika / Tippani / Akhyana**: Progressively shorter or simple explanatory notes.
* **Nibandha**: Thematic commentary synthesizing material across multiple books (e.g., UPSC Essay = *Nibandha*).

### Foreign Relations & Historiographical Debates
* **Indo-Greek Conflict**: Pushyamitra fought and repulsed Indo-Greek invasions led by **Menander I (Milinda)**.
* **Historiographical Perspectives**:
  * **Left-Wing View (Rahul Sankrityayan et al.)**: Pushyamitra viewed as an anti-Buddhist persecutor who destroyed monasteries, imposed orthodox Brahminical hierarchy and codified *Manu Smriti*.
  * **Right-Wing View**: Pushyamitra viewed as a national savior who ended Mauryan demilitarization/pacifism, repulsed foreign Indo-Greek invaders and restored national defense.
