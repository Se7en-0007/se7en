## Evolution & Architecture of Indian Coinage

- **Chronological Coin Lineage**:
  - **Pre-Greek / Mahajanapada & Maurya**: **Punch-Marked Coins (PMC)** (irregular cut strips, single-side stamped).
  - **Indo-Greeks**: Introduced **Cast Die-Struck** & **Portrait Style** coins.
  - **Kushanas**: Perfected coins based on Indo-Greek models.
  - **Guptas**: **Pinnacle of Numismatic Art in India** (further perfected Kushana style).
  - **Early Medieval**: **Imitations / Copies of Gupta Coins** (decline in artistic detail & uniformity).
  - **Medieval / Islamic**: **Calligraphic Coins** (devoid of human/animal figures, Quranic verses inscribed; later banned by **Aurangzeb** to prevent holy verses being handled during trade/alcohol purchases).

- **Artistic & Iconographic Spectrum of Gupta Coins**:
  - **King & Deity Depictions**: Most diverse styles, postures and royal activities:
    - **King with Queen**: Chandragupta I & Kumaradevi.
    - **Musical Mastery**: Samudragupta playing **Veena**.
    - **Non-Imperial Postures**: King sitting in relaxed posture / taking steam via pipe.
    - **Altar Offerings**: King standing at **Havan Altar** making an offering (derived from Kushanas).
    - **Royal Hunting**: Hunting/killing **Lion**, **Rhinoceros** (Rhino-killing coin = symbol of conquering **Eastern India / Assam / Kaziranga**), **Tiger**, **Parrot**, **Riding Elephant**, **Riding Horse**.
    - **State Events**: Scenes of **Coronation** & **Renunciation**.
    - **Ashvamedha Coins**: First minted by **Samudragupta** to commemorate imperial conquest; followed by subsequent Gupta rulers.
  - **Artistic Techniques & Aesthetics**:
    - **Anatomy & Proportion**: Precise human and animal anatomy; sense of physical weight, muscle definition and body proportion.
    - **Sense of Movement**: Dynamic postures (e.g. Queen's turning stance with lifted heel; King taken aback; horse leg flexion & hooves).
    - **Expressive Faces**: Facial expressions depicting pride, love, devotion and imperial dignity.
    - **Projection & Relief**: 3D depth and relief carving on small metal surfaces.
  - **Rise of Female Deities (Goddess Cult)**:
    - **Goddess Lakshmi**: Seated/standing on **Lotus**, depicted in **Varada Mudra** with gold coins showering from her hand (*template copied by modern Indian gold coins & poster art*).
    - **Goddess Durga**: Seated naturally atop a **Lion**.
    - **Other Deities**: **Ganga**, **Yamuna**, **Kartikeya** seated on a Peacock.
  - **Nuanced Historical Analysis (Goddess Worship vs. Status of Women)**:
    - Female worship is non-binary and context-dependent:
      - *Negative Aspect*: Used to enforce an idealized **Pure & Pious Image** (*Sati-Savitri* ideal), restricting women's real-world social mobility.
      - *Positive Aspect*: Represents women as the ultimate source of power (**Shakti** / Durga / Kamakhya) and spiritual equality in the pantheon.
      - *Analytical Rule*: Female worship alone does not prove high or low social status; must be cross-verified with economic independence, legal rights and social autonomy.

- **Gupta Coinage Denominations & Metals**:
  - **Gold Coin**: **Dinar** (majority of Gupta coinage; high purity in early reign, alloyed in late reign).
  - **Silver Coin**: **Rupaka** (introduced after conquering Western Kshatrapas in Gujarat).
  - **Copper Coin**: **Dram** / **Dam** (extremely rare in Gupta era; used by commoners; root of *Dram / Damri* & *Paisa*).

## Gupta Imperial Administration & Feudal Polity

- **Royal Titles & Rising Divinity**:
  - **Mauryan Kings**: *Raja*, *Devanampiya* (Beloved of Gods).
  - **Kushana Kings**: *Maharaja*, *Devaputra* (Son of God).
  - **Gupta Kings**: **Maharajadhiraja** (King of Kings), **Devadhideva** (God of Gods), **Param Devata** (Great God), **Param Bhattaraka** (Greatest Warrior), **Param Bhagavata** (Greatest Devotee of Vishnu), **Parameshwara** (God of Gods).
  - **Causal Mechanism**: Defeating subordinate kings who already held titles like *Devanampiya* or *Devaputra* forced conquerors to adopt increasingly elevated divine titles → **Direct indicator of rising Feudalism (*Samantavada*)** and the presence of autonomous vassal kings.

- **Administrative Hierarchy & Territorial Divisions**:
  - **Rashtra (Empire)** → Headed by **Emperor (Samrat / Maharajadhiraja)**.
  - **Bhukti (Province)** → Headed by **Uparika** (Provincial Governor).
    - *Etymology*: Root of *Bhukti* = *Bhoga* / *Bhogika* = Feudal Land.
    - *Feudal Marker*: Majority of *Uparikas* held the royal title **Maharaja**, indicating they were autonomous subordinate kings acting as provincial governors.
    - *Tax*: **Uparikara** (additional levy / tax on manufacturing).
  - **Vishaya (District)** → Headed by **Vishayapati**, assisted by **Ayukta** (evolved from Mauryan *Yukta*).
    - *Territorial Shift*: What functioned as a province (*Vishaya*) under earlier dynasties became a district under the larger Gupta Empire as new administrative layers were superimposed.
  - **Sub-District & Local Administrative Units**:
    - **Vithi**: Administrative unit below Vishaya in Eastern India/Bengal (mentioned in copper plates); headed by **Vithi Mehtar**.
    - **Paethaka** / **Santaka**: Group of villages.
    - **Ashtakuladhikarana**: Local board governing 8 villages/clans; headed by **Ashtakuladhikari** (*Kula* = family/clan; Indian villages are historically overgrown single-caste families).
  - **Grama (Village)** → Headed by **Gramika** (Elected village headman).
    - **Patta**: Land record written on cloth.
    - **Pattalika**: Officer issuing and maintaining land records (*Patta*). Evolved into medieval/modern titles: **Patil**, **Patel**, **Patnayak**, **Patwari** (office), **Lekhpal** (from *Lekhak*).
    - **Downward Mobility of Imperial Titles**: Mauryan central titles demoted down the hierarchy over time (e.g. Mauryan *Mahamatra* → Gupta village elder **Mahattara** → *Mehta / Mahto / Mehtar*; Mauryan *Gopa* / *Subedar* similarly pushed down).

- **Nagara (City Administration)**:
  - Headed by **Nagarapati** (Elected city head), assisted by **Nagara Sabha** (City Council).
  - **Key City Officers**:
    - **Dandika**: City Tax Collector (despite *Danda* meaning judicial/punishment; exercised tax collection with summary judicial enforcement, analogous to a train magistrate).
    - **Parapala**: Head of City/Fort Defense Wall (**Perakota** = defense wall; city gates = **Pol**, e.g., *Chandpol*, *Hathipol*).
    - **Nagara Shreshtin** / **Nagara Seth**: Head of City Guilds (*Shreshtin* → *Seth*); wealthy merchants directly incorporated into city governance (*positive*: represents merchant interests; *negative*: elitist and eventually hereditary).

- **Central Bureaucracy & Departmental Officers**:
  - **Mahapratihara**: Head of Fort Gates & Royal Palace Security (Emperor's most trusted security officer; e.g. Gurjar Pratiharas claimed lineage from Lakshmana, the *Pratihara* / gatekeeper for Rama).
  - **Dutaka**: Officer in charge of **Land Grants** (records, verifies and executes royal land grant deeds).
  - **Sandhivigrahika**: Minister of War & Peace (Foreign Minister / Chief Diplomat; granted 100% diplomatic immunity).
  - **Mahabaladhikrita**: Commander-in-Chief / Head of Army (Classical Sanskrit term; vs. Mauryan *Senani*).
  - **Ranabhandagarika**: Head of Military Supply & Armory (*Rana* = war, *Bhandagara* = storehouse; vs. Mauryan *Goadhyaksha*).
  - **Narapati**: Head of Infantry (*Nara* = foot soldiers; vs. Mauryan *Padadhyaksha*).
  - **Ashvapati**: Head of Cavalry (*Ashva* = horse; vs. Mauryan *Ashvadhyaksha*).
  - **Pilupati**: Head of Elephants (*Pilu* = elephant; vs. Mauryan *Hastyadhyaksha*).
  - **Adhikarana**: Central Office / Department; **Pradhikarana**: Regional Branch Office.

## Architecture & Evolution of Guilds (Shreni)

- **Definition & Origins**:
  - Autonomous organization of families, traders, or artisans engaged in the same profession or economic activity.
  - Existed continuously from the **Later Vedic Age** through Ancient & Medieval periods.

- **Internal Structure & Guild Autonomy**:
  - Formulated internal rules, regulations and quality standards; elected a guild head (**Shreshtin / Pramukha**) from among themselves.
  - **Enforcement & Outcasting**: Violation of guild rules resulted in social boycott and expulsion (**Hukka-Pani Band** / *Outcast*; e.g. Mahatma Gandhi was outcasted by his guild for crossing the ocean to study law in England).
  - **Economic Autonomy**: Set production volume, pricing, sales channels and internal wages. Paid regular taxes to the state and adhered to basic state laws.
  - **Transformation into Caste**: Practice of **Endogamy** (marrying strictly within the guild) led to the crystallization of guilds into **Jati / Caste**.

- **Guild Terminology**:
  - **Shreni**: Primary term for Guild / Profession Category (*Shreshtin* = Guild Head → *Seth*).
  - **Puga**: Guild team / group walking together (*Pugai* = team formation).
  - **Sangha**: Association / Union of equals.
  - **Nigama**: Commercial Guild Department or Market Branch.
  - **Vrata**: Guild bound by shared commitment and internal laws (*Vrata* = commitment/duty).

- **Guild Head Titles**:
  - **Shreshtin** (→ *Seth*), **Pramukha** (Head/Forehead), **Prathama** (First), **Jyeshtaka** (Elder).
  - **Sarthavaha**: Head of a **Long-Distance Trade Guild** (Root = *Sarathi* = Guide / Caravan Leader; e.g. Krishna as Sarathi elevated the term's prestige).

## Market Typologies & Historical Nomenclature

- **Mandapika** (Gupta Age): Central enclosed plot in a city where merchants and buyers congregate (*Mandapa* = pavilion) → Root of modern **Mandi** (e.g. *Sabzi Mandi*).
- **Nigama** (Mahajanapada Age): Market located directly within the residential colony/quarter of a guild.
- **Patabhedana** (Mahajanapada Age): Wholesale transit market along highways or river ports where sealed cloth bundles (*Pata* = cloth lock/gate, *Bhedana* = breaking) were opened, breaking wax seals onto the ground.
- **Angadi** (Sangam Age): Ancient Tamil term for market.
- **Ahata** (Early Medieval Age): Vacant plot designated for a weekly periodic market (e.g. Monday market) → Root of **Haat** / **Paet** / **Peth** / **Pat** (e.g., *Somwar Peth* in Pune, *Begumpet* in Hyderabad, *Chitpet* in Chennai).
- **Ganj** (Early Medieval / Xuanzang at Nalanda): Grain market center / commercial square.
- **Foreign Terms**: **Bazar** (Persian), **Emporium** (Greek), **Plaza / Place** (Spanish/German/English → *Connaught Place*, *DLF Plaza*).

## Land Classifications & Agricultural Units

- **Three Major Types of Land Ownership**:
  1. **Crown Land / State-Owned**:
     - *Mauryan*: **Sita** / **Sitaland**.
     - *Satavahana / Prakrit*: **Rajanya Kshetra** / **Rajam Khetam**.
     - *Medieval*: **Khalisa** (from *Khalis* = pure; state-administered land).
  2. **Feudal / Landlord-Owned**:
     - *Gupta*: **Bhoga** / **Bhogika**.
     - *Medieval*: **Jagir**.
  3. **Private / Farmer-Owned Land**:
     - **Kshetra**: Fertile, cultivated land (→ *Khet*).
     - **Khila**: Infertile, uncultivated, or empty land (→ *Khali*).
       - *Aprahata*: Uncultivated forest/scrubland yielding zero tax (*Shunya Pratikara*).
       - *Adhastambha*: Infertile scrubland covered in thorny half-pillar bushes.
       - *Khalihana*: Empty infertile plot adjacent to farm fields used for storing and threshing harvested grain (→ *Khali* / *Khaliher*).
     - **Urvara**: Vedic term for fertile soil vs. **Usara**: Saline / infertile soil.

- **Units of Land Measurement**:
  - **Adhavapa**: Area needed to sow half a handful of seeds (*Adha* = half, *Vapa* = sowing/digging).
  - **Kullyavapa**: Area needed to sow a full handful / total seed quantity (*Kulla* = total).
  - **Dronavapa**: Area needed to sow a leaf-bowlful of seeds (*Drona* = leaf bowl → *Dona*; Dronacharya born in a pot/bowl).
  - **Nivartana**: Standardized land area measurement unit (e.g., 210 hands × 210 hands).
  - **Avartana**: Unit of volume measurement (→ *Bartan* = utensil/pot).

## Evolution of Irrigation Technology

- **Ghati Yantra**: Manual irrigation device. Small clay pots (*Ghati* = pot → *Ghada*, *Ghat*, *Panaghat*) tied to a circular rope, lowered into a well via a pulley and manually tilted over a drainage slope (*Ghatiya* = fragile like a clay pot).
- **Araghatta / Rahat**: Water wheel mechanism. Large wooden wheel (*Ratha* = wheel + *Ghati* = pot → *Araghatta* / *Rahat*) with pots mounted along its rim, rotated in shallow water.
- **Ghatimala**: Long belt of pots attached to an Araghatta, extending deep into underground water tables (20+ feet below ground level).
- **Worm Gear (Delhi Sultanate - 13th Century AD)**: Introduction of 90-degree right-angle gears (**Worm Gear**), connecting the Araghatta/Ghatimala to an animal harness, converting horizontal animal walking into vertical wheel rotation → **Animal-driven automated irrigation**.
