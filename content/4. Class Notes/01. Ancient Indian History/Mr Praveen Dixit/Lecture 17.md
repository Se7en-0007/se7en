## Debate On Pushyamitra Shunga As Persecutor of Buddhism

### Arguments Supporting Persecution Theory
* **Buddhist Textual Evidence**: ***Divyavadana*** (1st/2nd c. AD) explicitly narrates events of Pushyamitra persecuting Buddhist monks and destroying monasteries.
* **Geopolitical Rivalry**: Shungas were rivals of **Indo-Greeks**, who were active patrons of Buddhism → Political conflict escalated into religious persecution.
* **Regional Decline of Buddhism**: Relative decline of Buddhism in Magadha during Post-Mauryan age attributed directly to Shunga state persecution.
* **Brahmanical Resurgence**: Hinduism/Brahmanism regained royal patronage after 2 centuries of Mauryan favor to Buddhism/Jainism → Revenge for past marginalization.

### Arguments Against Persecution Theory
* **Textual Bias**: ***Divyavadana*** is a Buddhist text → Obvious sectarian bias presenting rival patrons as villains.
* **Factual Inconsistencies**: ***Divyavadana*** confuses basic genealogy, naming Pushyamitra as the *last Mauryan king* who persecuted Buddhism to outshine Ashoka's glory → Unreliable source.
* **Survival of Ashokan Monuments**: All major Ashokan pillars and stupas survived the Shunga period intact (recorded later by Chinese pilgrims Faxian & Xuanzang) → Contradicts state-sponsored destruction.
* **Flourishing Buddhist Art**: Buddhist art centers expanded during Shunga reign → **Bharhut** prospered in early Shunga era; **Sanchi** railings and gateways added in later Shunga/Satavahana era.

## Later Shunga Monarchs & Epigraphic Anchors

### Agnimitra (c. 150 – 140 BC)
* **Son & Successor of Pushyamitra Shunga**.
* **Military Conflict**: Contemporary & rival of Indo-Greek king **Menander (Milinda)**; his son **Vasumitra** also fought Menander.
* **Literary Anchor**: Main protagonist of Kalidasa's 4th/5th c. AD Sanskrit play ***Malavikagnimitram*** → Focuses on the love story of King Agnimitra and his maidservant **Malavika**.

### Bhagabhadra & Heliodorus Pillar (c. 110 – 100 BC)
* **Regional Capital**: **Vidisha** (Besnagar, MP).
* **Diplomatic Mission**: Received Indo-Greek ambassador **Heliodorus** (sent by King **Antialcidas** of Taxila).
* **Heliodorus Pillar (Garuda Pillar / Khambha Baba)**:
  * **Dedicatory Deity**: Erected in devotion to God **Vasudeva** (earliest epigraphic proof of Bhagavata/Vaishnava cult).
  * **Capital**: Originally topped by a **Garuda** (bird vehicle of Vishnu) sculpture; top socket remains on abacus.
  * **Regional Name**: Locally called **Khambha Baba**.
  * **Language & Script**: **Prakrit** language, **Brahmi** script.
  * **Historical Significance**: Pushes formal Bhagavata Vaishnavism back to 2nd c. BC.

### Devabhuti (ended 72 BC)
* **Last Shunga King**.
* **Assassination**: Killed by his Brahmin minister **Vasudeva Kanva** in 72 BC → End of Shunga Dynasty.

## Kanva Dynasty (72 BC – 27 BC)

* **Vasudeva Kanva**: Founder (72 BC); minister who overthrown Devabhuti.
* **Susharman**: Last Kanva king (ended 27 BC).
* **Purana Chronology (*Vishnu Purana*)**:
  * Mauryas (137/136 yrs) → Shungas (112/133 yrs) → Kanvas (45 yrs).
  * Timeline anchored to Alexander's invasion (326 BC) + 5 yrs = 321 BC Chandragupta Maurya → 185 BC Shunga → 72 BC Kanva → 27 BC Kanva collapse.
* **Fall of Kanvas**: Ended either by **Mitra Dynasty** (ruling Kaushambi) or by **Andhra / Satavahana Dynasty**.

## Kaushambi: Strategic Market Node & Confluence

* **Geographic Confluence**: Situated at confluence of **Ganga and Yamuna** rivers (near modern Prayagraj).
* **Highway Junction**: Convergence point where **Uttarapatha meets Dakshinapatha**.
* **Capital**: Capital of **Vatsa Mahajanapada** (branch of Kuru dynasty of Hastinapur; relocated to Kaushambi after floods in Hastinapur).
* **Evolution**: Commercial hub faded over time while religious bathing ritual survived at Prayagraj (Sangam).

## Bharhut School of Art (2nd Century BC)

* **Time & Location**: Early Shunga period (2nd c. BC); **Satna District, MP** (discovered by Alexander Cunningham; fragments preserved in Kolkata Indian Museum; nothing in-situ).
* **Patronage**: Shunga Dynasty.
* **Medium**: Flawless **Dark Red Sandstone** (darker than Mathura sandstone).
* **Genre**: **Narrative Art** (direct visual storytelling vs. Mauryan symbolic art).
* **9 Core Features**:
  1. **No Anthropomorphic Buddha**: Buddha depicted ONLY via symbols (Bodhi tree, Chaitya arch, Wheel/Chakra, Footprints/Paduka).
  2. **Head Knots (Jata)**: Figures and monks depicted with long tied head knots.
  3. **Lean Monastic Bodies**: Monks shown with lean, disciplined physiques.
  4. **Unclothed Upper Body**: Female sculptures (e.g. Queen Maya Devi) unclothed above waist (standard pre-Gupta artistic convention; no objectification).
  5. **Shallow Carving**: Early chisel technique resulting in shallow depth (~mm/inch).
  6. **Overcrowded Panels**: Panels densely packed with figures; zero vacant space.
  7. **Descriptive Captions**: Inscriptional captions in Brahmi script / Prakrit language explaining scenes.
  8. **Awkward Bends**: Lack of 3D projection leads to 90° bent hands and 180° flat feet postures.
  9. **Continuous Narration**: Multiple chronological scenes depicted within a single panel.
* **Major Depicted Themes & Jatakas**:
  * **Arrival of Buddha**: White elephant entering Queen Maya Devi's room in her dream.
  * **First Sermon**: Deer park in Sarnath; 5 disciples, hunter, river crossing; Buddha represented symbolically.
  * **Anathapindika's Donation**: Merchant buying Jetavana park by covering ground with gold coins and donating village to Buddha.
  * **Mahakapi Jataka**: Buddha as monkey king forming a body-bridge between two trees to save fellow monkeys from hunters.
  * **Temiya Jataka**: Mute-pretending prince Temiya giving a sermon when about to be buried; converting his father.
  * **4 Buddhist Miracles**:
    1. *Sankissa / Tusita Heaven*: Ladder showing Buddha's ascent to preach to mother Maya Devi and descent (feet with Chakra on ladder).
    2. *Vaishali*: Wild monkeys offering bowl of honey to Buddha.
    3. *Rajgir*: Taming enraged elephant Nalagiri released by Devadatta via hand gesture.
    4. *Sravasti*: Twin Miracle (Yamakapratiharya).
  * **Yaksha & Yakshini**: Minor guardian deities (Kubera, Erapato Nagaraja, Goddess Lakshmi with coin-pouring elephants).
  * **Greek (Yavana) Influence**: Gateways/Toranas built with deeper carvings; Greek figure depicted with curly hair, chest-tied cape, skirt and shield.

## Sanchi School of Art (Sanchi Stupa No. 1 – Maha Stupa)

* **Time & Location**: Later Shunga & Satavahana period (2nd c. BC – 1st c. BC); **Raisen District, MP**.
* **Medium**: **Yellow-White Sandstone** (appears yellow in evening light, white in morning light).
* **Patronage**: Shunga Dynasty & Satavahana Dynasty (Toranas/Gateways added by Satavahanas).
* **Key Improvements over Bharhut**:
  1. **No Anthropomorphic Buddha**: Retains strict symbolic depiction of Buddha.
  2. **Broad & Powerful Bodies**: Figures depicted broader and stronger (reflecting Kshatriya royal prince heritage; proving voluntary renunciation).
  3. **Deeper Carvings**: High relief carving creating 3D depth.
  4. **No Captions**: Lacks explanatory captions (unlike Bharhut).
  5. **Natural Postures**: Eliminates awkward 90° bends in limbs.
  6. **Decorative Head Knots**: Head knots decorated with ribbons.
  7. **Votive Inscriptions**: Inscriptions record individual lay donations.
* **Anatomy of Sanchi Stupa**:
  * **Anda**: Hemispherical mound (symbolizing water bubble = death/impermanence).
  * **Harmika**: Square enclosed railing atop Anda.
  * **Yasti**: Central vertical mast.
  * **Chhatra**: 3 umbrellas (Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha).
  * **Upper Pradakshina Patha**: Elevated circumambulatory path with upper railing.
  * **Lower Pradakshina Patha (Vedika)**: Ground railing (minimalist carvings).
  * **Medhi**: Terrace platform between Vedika and Anda.
  * **Toranas (Gateways)**: 4 heavily carved gateways (North, South, East, West); 2 pillars + 3 horizontal beams; beam ends feature 3 circles representing Tripitaka; bracket angles feature **Shalabhanjika Yakshini** (fertility goddess holding tree branch).

## Mahameghavahana Dynasty (Kalinga Region)

* **Region**: Kalinga (modern Odisha & N. Andhra Pradesh) in Post-Mauryan era.
* **Religious Affiliation**: **Jainism**.
* **Udayagiri & Khandagiri Caves (Bhubaneswar, Odisha)**:
  * 113 rock-cut Jain caves built; 33 discovered so far.
  * *Key Caves*:
    * **Ganesh Gumpha**: Elephant figures outside.
    * **Jai-Vijaya Gumpha**: Two warrior figures outside.
    * **Patalapuri Gumpha**: Underground cave.
    * **Rani Gumpha**: Sculptures of jeweled women outside.
    * **Swargapuri & Mankapuri Gumpha**: Double-story cave (single opening, two internal levels).
    * **Hathi Gumpha**: Elephant figure outside; contains royal inscription.
* **Hathi Gumpha Inscription**:
  * **Commissioner**: **King Kharavela** (1st Century BC).
  * **Language & Script**: **Prakrit** language, **Brahmi** script.
  * **Contents**:
    * Kharavela's charities, irrigation projects and military campaigns.
    * Conquest of Deccan against Satavahana king **Sri Satakarni**.
    * Military expedition against **Sangam Kingdoms** (Chola, Pandya, Chera).
    * Defeat of **Magadha**: Reclaimed the **idols of Jina (Jain Tirthankara)** taken from Kalinga to Magadha during past Nanda/Mauryan wars.
  * **Etymology**: **Jina** = Conqueror of self (root word of *Jainism* & greeting *Jay Jinendra*).

## Satavahana Dynasty: Background & Early Evolution

* **Origins**: Began as vassals/sub-rulers of Mauryan Empire in Deccan; declared independence after Mauryan collapse; retained Mauryan administrative terms.
* **Tribal Background & Legitimacy**:
  * Recent tribal origin (egalitarian, family-centric, land as non-private property).
  * Invited **Brahmins from North India** to elevate royal status via Vedic rituals (**Rajasuya**, **Ashvamedha**, **Vajapeya**, **Hiranyagarbha**).
  * Instituted **Tax-Free Land Grants** (*Agrahara* / *Brahmadeya*) to Brahmins and monks with immunity from state interference → **Birth of Land Grants and Feudalism in India**.
  * Educational institutions established by Brahmins/monks called **Ghatika**.
* **Socio-Cultural Expansion**:
  * Transmitted Northern religions (Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism) to Deccan and Sangam South.
  * Transmitted Northern urbanization features: metallic coinage, script, planned towns (aligned houses, straight roads), soak pits and **Terracotta Ringwells** (sanitation/drainage wells since IVC/Hastinapur).
  * Matrilineal influence: Kings adopted matronymics (mother's name, e.g. Gautmiputra Satakarni).
* **Economy & Maritime Trade**:
  * Hub for Indian Ocean maritime trade across Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.
  * Issued coins featuring **Boats / Ships** (signifying naval trade).
  * Benefited from 1st Century AD Discovery of **Monsoon Trade Winds**.
  * Administrative division into 2 zones: **Western Zone** and **Eastern Zone**.
