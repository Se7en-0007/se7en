## Avadana Literature (Buddhist Literature of Noble Karma)

- **Definition**: **Avadana** = Literature describing noble/good deeds (**Noble Karma**) of Buddhist followers and the gathering of **Punya** (Merit).
- **Concept of Punya (Merit) vs. Karma**:
  - **Theory of Karma**: Unrelated good happens as a consequence of good deeds (e.g., helping a poor person → getting higher exam marks).
  - **Linguistic Reality**: No direct English equivalent for *Punya* (approximated as "merit" or "virtue"; *Pap* = Sin).
- **Divyavadana (Divine Narratives)**:
  - Collection of divine narratives of noble karma by Buddhist followers.
  - **Predictive / Prophetic Style**: Written in **future tense** for divine legitimacy (e.g., Buddha predicts a child giving him food will be reborn 200 years later as King Ashoka).
- **Ashokavadana**:
  - Part of *Divyavadana*; details Ashoka's noble deeds and building of 84,000 stupas.
  - Identifies **Pushyamitra Shunga** as the villain who destroyed Ashoka's stupas and Buddhist monuments.
- **Avadanasataka**: Collection of **100 stories** of noble karma by Buddhist followers.
- **Distinction: Jataka vs. Avadana**:
  - **Jataka** = Stories of **past lives of Gautama Buddha**.
  - **Avadana** = Stories of **followers / disciples of Buddhism** (Ashoka, Menander, Kanishka).

## Mahavastu, Ananda & Nidana Katha

- **Mahavastu**:
  - **Etymology**: *Vastu / Vastu Shastra* = Living space / Life / Living conditions (e.g., *Kapilavastu* = living place of Kapila Muni).
  - **Nature**: Great commentaries on Buddhist narratives (**Jataka tales & Avadana tales**).
  - **Mahayana Rationale**: Explains present worldly relationships via karmic connections across thousands of past lives.
- **Characterization of Ananda**:
  - Relative and chief personal attendant of Gautama Buddha.
  - Depicted with human emotional vulnerabilities and dilemmas (wept at Buddha's deathbed; scolded by Buddha for forgetting the Middle Path and impermanence).
  - **1st Buddhist Council**: Late due to sleeping; Mahakashyapa proposed banishment, but admitted because only Ananda knew Buddha's informal/unofficial personal teachings.
- **Nidana Katha**:
  - **Nidana** = Root cause / Culmination narrative occurring at the end.
  - **Mechanism**: A simple initial childhood event is dramatically exaggerated to explain all future major achievements (analogous to *Vrat Katha* or exaggerated motivational anecdotes).

## Academic Literature & Prosody (3rd c. BC – 5th c. AD)

- **Mahabhasya**: Authored by **Rishi Patanjali**; core treatise on Sanskrit grammar.
- **Chhandasutra (Work on Meters / Prosody)**:
  - Authored by **Pingala**.
  - **Subject**: Study of meters (**Chhanda** / poetic rhythms).
  - **Meter vs. Song**:
    - **Meter / Poem**: Literary structure based on strict syllabic rhythm and rules.
    - **Song**: Vocal sound modulation by singer (*Tarana*, *Alaap*, *Raga*); independent of literary meters.
  - **Earliest Binary Mathematics**: Pingala's table comparing rhyming/non-rhyming syllable structures using binary classifications (1/0 notation).

## Brihattrayee of Ayurveda (Three Great Medical Classics)

- **Comparison**: **Vyakarana Trayi** (Grammar Trilogy) = *Ashtadhyayi* (Panini), *Vartikakar* (Katyayana), *Mahabhasya* (Patanjali).
- **Brihattrayee of Ayurveda** (Three Medical Masters):
  1. **Charaka Samhita**:
     - Author: **Charaka**.
     - Focus: **Kaya Chikitsa** (Internal Medicine).
     - Content: Theories on human body, disease causation, symptoms, diagnosis, cures; comprehensive catalog of plants, herbs, rivers, lakes, botany and ethnic groups → **Describes the Human & Physical Geography of Ancient India**.
  2. **Sushruta Samhita**:
     - Author: **Sushruta**.
     - Focus: **Shalya Chikitsa** (Surgery & Surgical Procedures).
     - Content: Derived from *Charaka Samhita*; applies a surgical perspective (surgical instruments, training, procedures).
  3. **Vagbhata Samhita**:
     - Author: **Vagbhata** (comprises 2 works: **Ashtanga Hridaya** & **Ashtanga Samhita**).
     - Focus: **Kaya Chikitsa** (Internal Medicine).
     - Content: Investigates *Charaka Samhita* themes; details the core principles of **Tridosha** (**Vata**, **Pitta**, **Kapha**).
- **Scientific Methodology & R&D**: Evolution of science relies on continuous Research & Development (R&D) across centuries.

## Kavya Literature (Post-Mauryan Dominant Sanskrit Genre)

- **Kavya** = Supreme poetic genre; benchmark of literary excellence in any language (e.g., *Ramcharitmanas* in Chaupai meter, *Mahabharata* with 188,000 shlokas).
- **Structural Features**:
  - Authored in highly refined Sanskrit utilizing **metaphors**, **similes** and **hyperboles**.
  - Precise use of meters reflects intense competition among court poets during the **Age of Sanskritization**.
  - Primarily **Court Epics** featuring **Royal Characters** with **Happy Endings** (mandated by Bharata Muni's ***Natyashastra***).
  - **Social Function**: Legitimizes royal blood and divine right of kingship.

## Works of Mahakavi Bhasa (13 Post-Mauryan Plays)

- **Chronology**: Authored ~2nd c. BC (Post-Mauryan era). Corpus of **13 Plays**:
  1. **Madhyama Vyayoga**: Story of the reunion of **Bhima** and his son **Ghatotkacha** (*Madhyama* = middle son of Pandavas).
  2. **Duta Vakya**: **Krishna** attending Duryodhana's court as a messenger (*Duta*) offering 5 villages to avert war.
  3. **Pancharatra**: Duryodhana donating half his kingdom to Pandavas after 5 nights, preventing war (**Alternate ending to Mahabharata**).
  4. **Duta Ghatotkacha**: **Ghatotkacha** delivering Arjuna's oath to the Kaurava camp after Abhimanyu's death.
  5. **Karna Bharam**: **Karna's** dilemma over donating his armor/earrings (*Kavacha-Kundala*) to save his honor vs. retaining them to save his life.
  6. **Urubhangam**: Events after the breaking of Duryodhana's thighs (*Uru* = thigh; *Bhanga* = breaking); features Krishna's curse to Ashwatthama and Gandhari's curse to Krishna.
  7. **Pratima Nataka**: Rama's life from exile to Ravana's defeat with creative plot variations (e.g., Golden Deer hunt sought for father's *Shraddha* ritual; Bharata meeting Rama near Lanka).
  8. **Pratijna Yaugandharayana**: Minister **Yaugandharayana's** oath (*Pratijna*) to free King **Udayana** from Avanti's captivity and marry him to Princess **Vasavadatta** (based on Gunadhya's *Brihatkatha*).
  9. **Svapnavasavadattam**: King **Udayana's** dream (*Svapna*) of **Vasavadatta** after rumors of her death in a fire (staged for a political alliance).
  10. **Charudatta**: Romantic drama between poor Brahmin merchant **Charudatta**, wealthy courtesan **Vasantasena** and Samsthanaka (king's brother-in-law). Vasantasena leaves jewelry in Charudatta's child's clay cart.

## Shudraka & Ashvaghosha

- **Shudraka (1st c. AD)**: Rewrote Bhasa's *Charudatta* into the famous play ***Mricchakatika*** ("The Little Clay Cart").
- **Kavya of Ashvaghosha** (Court Poet of Kanishka):
  1. **Buddhacharitam**: Biography of Gautama Buddha.
  2. **Saundaranandam**: Story of **Nanda** (half-brother of Buddha) and his wife **Saundarya**; Nanda's struggles with worldly attachment during meditation and ultimate attainment of Nirvana (Mahayana message on overcoming distractions).
  3. **Sariputra Prakarana**: Story of **Sariputra** (disciple of Buddha) attaining Nirvana by finding the right guide (**Bodhisattva**).

## Emergence & Feudal Origins of the Gupta Empire (3rd c. AD)

- **3rd Century AD Geopolitical Landscape**:
  - Dominant alliance in North/Central India = **Naga-Vakataka Alliance** (Nagas replaced Kushanas; Vakatakas replaced Satavahanas).
  - Guptas emerged as a minor power in **Eastern UP** (Varanasi/Allahabad/Prayagraj region).
- **Feudal Origins (Samantas under Kushanas)**:
  - Guptas began as **Feudal Lords (Samantas)** under the **Kushana Empire**.
  - **Evidence**:
    - *Coinage*: Similarities in gold coins (*Dinara*, ~7g weight, posture, spear, offering over altar).
    - *Titles*: Kushanas = *Maharaja*; Guptas = *Maharaja* → *Maharajadhiraja*.
    - *Literature & Art*: Kalidasa echoing Ashvaghosha; continuation of **Mathura-Gupta Red Sandstone Architecture**.
- **Early Non-Sovereign Kings**:
  1. **Sri Gupta** (~40 years): Founder of Gupta dynasty; non-sovereign king (title **Maharaja**; no coins minted).
  2. **Ghatotkacha** (~40 years): Son of Sri Gupta; non-sovereign king (title **Maharaja**; no coins minted). Arranged marriage of his son **Chandragupta I** to Licchavi princess **Kumaradevi**.

## Reign of Chandragupta I (319 AD Onwards)

- **First Sovereign King**: Issued coins and adopted sovereign title **Maharajadhiraja** ("King of Kings").
- **Gupta Samvat (Gupta Era)**: Founded in **319 AD** to mark his coronation.
- **Licchavi Matrimonial Alliance**:
  - Married Licchavi princess **Kumaradevi**.
  - Primary catalyst for claiming sovereignty.
  - Depicted alongside Queen Kumaradevi on gold coins (**King-and-Queen Type**); reverse features Goddess seated on a Lion.
  - Son Samudragupta proudly adopted the title **Licchavi-Dauhitra** (Grandson of the Licchavis).
- **Kaumudi Mahotsava**:
  - Sanskrit play authored by female dramatist **Vajjika** (or *Kishorika*); manuscript discovered in Kerala in 1914.
  - Describes early historical events of Chandragupta I's reign.

---

## Reign of Samudragupta & The Prayag Prasasti

- **Parentage**: Son of Chandragupta I and Licchavi princess Kumaradevi.
- **Imperial Expansion**: Series of military conquests from Satluj to Kaveri (Punjab to Tamil Nadu), transforming a small kingdom into an empire.
- **Prayag Prasasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription)**:
  - **Author**: **Harisena** (Court poet, Sanskrit scholar, held 3 high offices: **Sandhivigrahika** {Foreign Minister}, **Kumaramatya** and **Mahadandanayaka** {Chief Justice}).
  - **Language**: Classical Sanskrit.
  - **Script**: **Gupta Script** (Late Brahmi / Early Nagari → evolved into Nagari → Devnagari).
  - **Three Phases of Conquest**:
    1. *First Northern Invasion*: Nepal to Western UP.
    2. *Southern Invasion*: Eastern Bihar to Northern Tamil Nadu via the **Eastern Coast** (flat coastal plain enabling troop movement, unlike dense Western Ghats; route later taken by Rajendra Chola).
    3. *Second Northern Invasion*: Conquered up to the Satluj River.
- **Allahabad Pillar Chronology (3 Inscriptions on 1 Pillar)**:
  1. **Ashoka** (Prakrit in Ashokan Brahmi).
  2. **Samudragupta** (Sanskrit Prayag Prasasti in Gupta script).
  3. **Jahangir** (Persian inscription carved atop Ashokan edicts).
- **Titles & Cultural Traits**:
  - Title: **Dharanibandha** ("Unifier of Earth").
  - Title: **Licchavi-Dauhitra**.
  - **Veena-Player Coin Type**: Depicted seated on a couch playing the **Veena** (string instrument), demonstrating personal artistic accomplishment.

## Reign of Ramagupta & Devichandraguptam

- **Ramagupta**: Elder son of Samudragupta.
- **Devichandraguptam** (Play by **Vishakhadatta**):
  - Ramagupta attacked Sakas (Scythians) but was trapped; Saka king demanded surrender of Queen **Dhruvadevi** in exchange for peace.
  - Ramagupta agreed; younger brother **Chandragupta** opposed this dishonor.
  - Chandragupta disguised himself as Queen Dhruvadevi (*Devi Chandragupta*), entered the Saka camp and assassinated the Saka king.
  - Later, Chandragupta killed Ramagupta (who plotted against him out of jealousy) and married Queen **Dhruvadevi**.

---

## Emergence of Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya - 380 AD – 414 AD)

- Assumed the throne, adopted the title **Vikramaditya** and further strengthened the Gupta Empire through matrimonial alliances and conquests.
