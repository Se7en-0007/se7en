## Concept of Gupta Age as The Classical Age

- **Definition of "Classical Age"**: A period where art, culture, literature and science reach such a high benchmark of development that it becomes the foundational inspiration, standard and template for all subsequent generations.
- **Cultural Analogies**:
  - *Music*: Identification of the 7 basic musical notes (**Swaras / Octave**) upon which all later songs, ragas and genres are constructed.
  - *Dance & Cinema*: Master forms (*Bhava, Bhangima, Samabhanga*) copied by later artists.
  - *Sports*: Flawless textbook technique ("Classical batting").
- **Gupta Standardizations Copied in Modern Era**:
  - **Literature**: Classical Sanskrit works of **Kalidasa**.
  - **Temple Architecture**: **Shikhara-Mandapa** temple design.
  - **Royal Honors**: **Navaratna** ("Nine Gems" of Chandragupta II) → **Bharat Ratna** (highest civilian honor of India).
  - **Scientific Heritage**: **Aryabhata** (Gupta mathematician) → 1st Indian satellite sent into space named **Aryabhata**.
  - **Linguistic Reverence**: Classical **Sanskrit** recognized as the highest form of language in Northern India.

## Literature of The Gupta Age

### Puranic Literature
- **Finalization**: Majority of Puranas written or finalized during the Gupta Age.
- **Modern Continuity**: Forms the bedrock of Hindu mythology, Bhakti Movement literature and modern mythological films/TV.
- **Evolution of Mythology (Vedic vs. Puranic)**:
  - *Rigvedic Phase*: Nomadic tribal society personified natural forces (**Indra**, Mitra, Vayu, Varuna); Indra = supreme war god; **Vishnu** = minor deity (**Upendra**, younger brother of Indra).
  - *Puranic Phase*: Settled agricultural society worshipped all-powerful Trinity (**Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva**); Indra depicted as egoistic/degraded, seeking help from Vishnu or Shiva when defeated by demons.
- **Evolution Spectrum of Worship**:
  - **Nomadic Phase** → Personified Natural Forces (Pagan).
  - **Settled Agrarian Phase** → All-Powerful Gods / Trinity (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva).
  - **Advanced Developed Phase** → Pure Devotion without Rituals (**Bhakti**).
  - **Rational Phase** → Rationalism & Cultural Religion.

### Dharmashastras (Law Books)
- **Gupta Codification**: Majority composed in Gupta Age (e.g., ***Vishnu Smriti***).
- **Lineage**: Based on earlier **Dharmasutras** (law codes of Later Vedic/Mahajanapada Age: ***Baudhayana Sutra***, ***Vashistha Sutra***, ***Gautama Sutra***, ***Apastamba Sutra***).
- **Medieval Commentaries & Schools of Hindu Jurisprudence**:
  1. ***Dayabhaga***: Authored by **Jimutavahana** (commentary on *Manusmriti*; prevalent in Bengal).
  2. ***Mitakshara***: Authored by **Vijnaneshwara** (commentary on *Yajnavalkya Smriti*).
  - *Modern Impact*: Formed the basis for 20th-century **Hindu Personal Law Code**.
- **English Translation (1770)**: **Nathaniel Brassey Halhed** translated *Manusmriti* into English under the title ***A Code of Gentoo Laws*** (*Gentoo* = Portuguese/Spanish transliteration for *Hindu*).

### Kavya Literature of Kalidasa
- **Language**: Classical Sanskrit.
- **Three Dramatic Plays**:
  1. ***Malavikagnimitram***: Love story of King **Agnimitra** (**Shunga Dynasty**) and maidservant **Malavika**.
  2. ***Abhijñana Shakuntalam***: Story of King **Dushyanta** forgetting and remembering his wife **Shakuntala**.
  3. ***Vikramorvashiyam***: Tragic love story of celestial nymph (**Apsara**) **Urvashi** and earthly King **Pururava** (ancestor of Pandavas/Kauravas; Pururava addressed as *Vikram* in honor of patron **Chandragupta II Vikramaditya**).
- **Four Poetic Works**:
  1. ***Raghuvamsham***: Epic story of Lord Rama's family, focusing on ancestors (**Raghu**, **Dilipa**, **Aja**, **Dasharatha**).
  2. ***Meghadutam***: Story of a **Yaksha** sending a message of love to his wife via a **Cloud** (*Megha*) as messenger; describes the physical/geographical beauty of Indian regions.
  3. ***Ritu Samhara***: Describes the **6 Indian seasons** and their emotional/environmental effects on different sections of people.
  4. ***Kumarasambhavam***: Story of the birth of Lord **Kartikeya** (**Kumar**) and Parvati's efforts to persuade Shiva (named in honor of Prince **Kumaragupta I**).

### Works of King Bhartrihari (Raja Bhartri of Ujjain)
- Brother of Vikramaditya; renounced kingdom after the yellow fruit test by Guru **Gorakhnath**.
- ***Shataka Traya*** **(Three Hundred-Verse Treatises)**:
  1. ***Shringara Shataka***: 100 verses on love, passion and beauty.
  2. ***Niti Shataka***: 100 verses on ethics, diplomacy and governance.
  3. ***Vairagya Shataka***: 100 verses on renunciation, detachment and monasticism.
- **Grammar Treatise**: ***Vakyapadiya*** (also called ***Trikandi*** - 3 chapters/Kandas on Sanskrit syntax and sentence structure).

## Academic & Scientific Literature of The Gupta Age

### Medical Literature (Ayurveda)
- ***Nighantu***: Authored by **Dhanvantari** (Navaratna of Chandragupta II; treatise on medicinal herbs and glands).
- ***Bower Manuscript***: 20th-century discovery from Tibet written on leather parchment; authored by **Yashomitra**; named after Officer Bower; Gupta-era medical text.

### Political Economy & Statecraft
- ***Nitisara***: Authored by **Kamandaka** (**Kamandakiya Nitisara**); directly based on Kautilya's ***Arthashastra*** in theme, style and content.

### Grammatical Treatises & Lexicons
- ***Amarakosha***: Authored by **Amarasimha** (Navaratna; premier Sanskrit dictionary/thesaurus).
- ***Prakrita Prakasha***: Authored by **Vararuchi** (Navaratna; Prakrit grammar).

### Mathematics & Astronomy
- **Varahamihira**: Authored ***Pancha Siddhantika*** (5 astronomical schools), ***Brihat Samhita*** (encyclopedia), ***Brihat Jataka***, ***Laghu Jataka*** and ***Yoga Yatra***.
- **Brahmagupta**: Authored ***Brahmasphuta Siddhanta*** and ***Khandakhadyaka***. First to conceptualize gravity (**Guru-tva**): everything moves towards Earth.
- **Bhaskaracharya I**: Authored ***Aryabhatiyabhashya*** (commentary on Aryabhata), ***Mahabhaskariya*** and ***Laghubhaskariya***.
- **Aryabhata**: Authored ***Aryabhatiya*** and ***Surya Siddhanta***.
- **Global Transmission of Indian Mathematics**:
  - **772 AD (8th Century)**: Arab invasion of Sindh → Indian mathematician **Kanak** taken to Baghdad → introduced Indian numerals (0–9) and mathematics to Arabia (**Hindu Numerals** / *Al-Raqam Al-Hindu*).
  - **Arab Commentaries**: **Al-Khwarizmi** wrote ***Hisab al-Sind-Hind*** and ***Al-Jabr wa-al-Muqabala*** (Algebra, commentary on Indian *Bijaganita*).
  - **11th Century Transmission to Europe**: Introduced by Arabs to Europe → Europeans named them **Arabic Numerals** (*Algorithm* derived from *Al-Khwarizmi*; *Algebra* derived from *Al-Jabr*).
  - *Mathematical Significance*: Invention of **Zero** gave cyclicity to numbers, allowing base-10 rotation, trigonometric sine/cosine waves and calculus foundations.

## Metallurgy of The Gupta Age

- **Gold Metallurgy**: High-purity **Dinara** gold coins with intricate numismatic relief.
- **Silver Metallurgy**: **Rupaka** silver coins and detailed silver plaques.
- **Copper Metallurgy**: Large copper-plate land-grant inscriptions (**Tamra-Patra**).
- **Iron Metallurgy (Rustless Iron Technology)**:
  - **Mehrauli Iron Pillar** (Delhi): 7 meters high, monolithic rustless iron pillar of Chandragupta II Vikramaditya.
  - **Dhar Iron Pillar** (Dhar, MP): 14 meters high, monolithic rustless iron pillar of **Bhoja Parmar** (broken into 3 parts; stands in open sky without rusting).
- **Bronze Metallurgy (Lost-Wax Technique / Dhokra Art)**:
  - Lineage: **Lost-Wax Technique** (*Dhokra Art*) continuous since IVC (*Dancing Girl*).
  - **Sultanganj Colossal Buddha**: 2.5 meters high, 600 kg bronze sculpture discovered at Sultanganj (Bhagalpur, Bihar); currently housed in **Birmingham Museum & Art Gallery, UK**.
  - **Mirpur Khas Bronze Brahma**: 1 meter high, 100 kg bronze sculpture discovered at Mirpur Khas (Sindh, Pakistan); currently housed in **Sindh Museum, Pakistan**.
  - **Akota Hoard**: 68 Jain bronze sculptures discovered at Akota (Vadodara, Gujarat).

## Temple Architecture of The Gupta Age

### Pre-Gupta Temple Plans vs. Architecture
- **Building Plan** = Layout on paper/ground. **Architecture** = Structural construction built above ground.
- **Three Pre-Gupta Temple Plans**:
  1. **Sandhara Plan**: Garbhagriha surrounded by an enclosed circumambulatory path (**Pradakshina Patha**).
  2. **Nirandhara Plan**: Garbhagriha without a separate Pradakshina Patha.
  3. **Sarvatobhadra Plan**: Garbhagriha with entry doors on all **4 sides**.

### Gupta Innovation: Panchayatan Temple Plan
- **Layout**: Central main shrine for primary deity surrounded by **4 subsidiary shrines** at 4 corners for minor gods.
- **Socio-Political Symbolism**: Direct architectural assimilation of **Feudalism (*Samantavada*)** — subordinate feudal lords surrounding the king mirrored by minor shrines surrounding the main god.
- **Earliest Surviving Specimen**: **Dashavatara Temple** at Deogarh (Lalitpur, UP / MP border).

### Shikhara-Mandapa Style (Nagara Temple Architecture)
- First developed during the Gupta Age; became the signature style of North Indian temples.
- **Anatomy of a Nagara Temple**:
  - **Adhisthana / Jagati**: Raised stone platform / basement.
  - **Garbhagriha**: Sanctum sanctorum housing the main deity.
  - **Shikhara**: Curvilinear tower rising over Garbhagriha.
  - **Urushringa**: Subsidiary smaller shikharas attached to the main shikhara.
  - **Amalaka**: Large stone ribbed disc atop Shikhara.
  - **Kalasha / Amarakalasha**: Sacred pitcher/pot atop Amalaka.
  - **Dhvaja**: Sacred flag atop Kalasha.
  - **Antarala (Vestibule)**: Intermediate connecting passage between Garbhagriha and Mandapa.
  - **Mahamandapa & Ardhamandapa**: Outer pillared halls / entrance porches.
- **Earliest Surviving Specimen**: **Bhitargaon Temple** (Kanpur, UP; 4th–5th c. AD; **baked brick temple**; built on a *Triratha* plan).
- **Other Notable Gupta-Era Temples**:
  - **Mahabodhi Temple** (Bodh Gaya, Bihar; 5th–6th c. AD; Buddhist temple with tall pyramidal shikhara).
  - **Pateni Devi Temple** (Satna, MP; Jain flat-roof temple style).

## Introduction To Dravida Style Temple Architecture

- **Origins**: Developed under the **Pallava Kingdom** (6th–7th c. AD) at Mamallapuram / Mahabalipuram (**Shore Temples**, **Pancha Ratha / Pancha Pandava Temples**).
- **Imperial Perfection**: Perfected under the **Chola Dynasty** (e.g., **Brihadeshwara Temple** at Thanjavur).
- **Anatomy of a Dravida Temple**:
  - **Sri Kovil**: Garbhagriha (Sanctum sanctorum).
  - **Kutpalam**: Enclosed Pradakshina Patha.
  - **Vimana**: Stepped pyramidal tower rising over Sri Kovil (unlike curvilinear Nagara Shikhara).
  - **Shikhara**: Monolithic capstone/dome atop Vimana.
  - **Kalasha**: Metal pot atop Shikhara.
  - **Mandapas**: Mahamandapa, Ardhamandapa, Vahana Mandapa, Nandi Mandapa.
  - **Balipeetham**: Sacrificial platform.
  - **Dhvajastambha**: Free-standing metal-plated flagstaff outside the main temple.
  - **Gopuram**: Monumental, highly decorated gateway tower at complex entrance.
