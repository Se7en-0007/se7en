## Next Series of Impacts of Climate Change

    - **Climatic Outcome**: Shift in seasonal patterns, altered rainfall, severe droughts, and **weather extremities** (intensified, severe rainfall events & prolonged droughts).

## Desert Blooming
- **Location / Phenomenon**: **Atacama Desert** (**South America** - driest place on Earth).
- **Geographical Desert Definition**: Annual rainfall < **25 cm**.
  - **Atacama Baseline**: Annual rainfall < **7 cm** (hyper-arid).
- **Desert Blooming = Definition**: Sudden, explosive growth & flowering of dormant plants across dry desert mountains.
- **Causal Mechanism**:
  - Climate change → **El Niño frequency ↑** over Peruvian coast → Monsoonal winds diverted toward Atacama → Rainfall ↑ beyond normal thresholds.
  - Dormant plant seeds/leaves present in soil + increased water availability + high atmospheric $CO_2$ + abundant sunlight → Rapid photosynthesis & biomass production → Desert transformed into flowering landscape.
- **$CO_2$ Fertilization Effect**:
  - **Definition**: Increase in photosynthetic activity / photosynthesis rate of green plants caused by elevated atmospheric $CO_2$ concentration, *provided water and sunlight are sufficiently available*.
- **Homeostasis in Nature**:
  - **Homeostasis = Definition**: Automatic physiological or environmental self-regulating mechanism that absorbs instabilities and maintains internal equilibrium, neutrality, or normalcy.
  - **Human Body Examples**:
    - **Blood Sugar Regulation**: Sugar intake → Pallet/brain signal → Pituitary signal → Pancreas secretes **insulin** → Blood sugar normalized (disruption = diabetes).
    - **Thermal Regulation**: Hot/humid weather → Wet bulb temperature ↑ → Body secretes **sweat** → Evaporative cooling restores normal body temperature.
    - **Stress/Sport Response**: Physical activity → **Adrenal glands** (above kidneys) secrete **adrenaline** → Heartbeat ↑, blood pumping ↑, faster reaction rate.
  - **Nature's Environmental Homeostasis**:
    - Baseline atmospheric $CO_2$ concentration = **0.03%**.
    - Sudden atmospheric $CO_2$ rise → Plants naturally increase photosynthetic rate & biomass production to capture excess $CO_2$ (when water + sunlight are non-limiting).
    - **Oxygen Limits**: Excess atmospheric $O_2$ concentration → Respiration rate ↑, metabolic rate ↑, life expectancy ↓, forest fire frequency/intensity ↑.

## Melting of Permafrost
- **Permafrost = Definition**: Soil, sediment, or rock permanently frozen at or below 0°C for at least **2 consecutive years**.
- **Case Study - Batagaika Crater** (**Siberia, Russia**):
  - Largest permafrost meltdown crater on Earth ("gateway to the underworld").
  - Satellite expansion timeline along Siberian ridge: **1991** (small) → **1999** (0.55 km expansion) → **2005** → **2010** → **2014** → **2016** (massively elongated).
  - Geomorphology: Exposed clay contours form distinct **terraces** as ice melts out from soil layers.
- **Consequences / Impacts of Permafrost Melting**:
  - **1. Greenhouse Gas Release**:
    - Organic matter preserved/frozen for thousands of years exposed to oxygen → Anaerobic & aerobic decomposition → **$CO_2$** & trapped **Methane ($CH_4$)** released.
    - Methane impact: $CH_4$ is at least **25x** more lethal / potent as a GHG than $CO_2$.
  - **2. Disease & Pandemic Risk**:
    - Ancient dormant microscopic pathogens (viruses & bacteria) released from thawing ice.
    - Viruses remain dormant outside host cells; exposure to modern organisms/humans lacking historical immunity, vaccines, or antiviral treatments → Risk of novel pandemics.
  - **3. Ecosystem & Wetland Disruption**:
    - Thawing permafrost creates new wetlands in previously frozen landscapes, altering regional hydrology.
    - **Case Study - Siberian Crane**:
      - Migratory bird flying from North/Central Asia to Africa, historically stopping in India at **Keoladeo Ghana National Park / Bharatpur** (**Rajasthan**).
      - **Zero Sighting**: Siberian Cranes completely stopped visiting India since **2004**.
      - **Reason**: Milder Siberian winters reduce migration urge + warming Indian winter temperatures.

## Methane Cycle & Sources
- **Primary Mechanism**: Anaerobic decomposition (breakdown of organic matter in absence of $O_2$).
- **Key Methane ($CH_4$) Sources**:
  - **Landfills**: Anaerobic waste decay.
  - **Wetlands & Rice Paddy Fields**: Natural organic decay under inundated conditions.
  - **Fossil Fuel Emissions**: Natural gas extraction, processing & supply chain fugitive leakage.
  - **Enteric Fermentation**: Digestive process in ruminant livestock / cattle.
  - **Peat Degradation & Zombie Fires**: Arctic peatlands with un-decomposed organic matter undergoing subsurface anaerobic combustion/decay.
  - **Permafrost Thaw**: Release of ancient trapped $CH_4$.
  - **Biomass Burning**: Area fires causing incomplete combustion of organic material.
  - **Termite Digestion**: Micro-decomposers digesting cellulose in anaerobic gut conditions.
  - **Clathrates & Hydrates**: Deep-ocean mineral deposits of organic matter + water + clay under anaerobic conditions on ocean beds; breakdown releases massive trapped $CH_4$.
- **Bacterial Roles in Methane Cycle**:
  - **Methanogenic Bacteria**: Generate $CH_4$ during anaerobic decomposition.
  - **Methanotrophic Bacteria**: Consume / absorb and break down $CH_4$ for food and nutrition (acts as a $CH_4$ sink).

## Detailed Breakdown 1: Loss of Plankton
- **Plankton = Definition**:
  - Aquatic or marine organisms (found in freshwater & ocean ecosystems).
  - Do not actively swim; float on water surface = **Passive Drifters** (drift via ocean currents and waves).
  - Serve as **primary producers** of the aquatic/marine food chain and food web.
- **Classification of Plankton**:
  - **A. Phytoplankton** (Plant-like Plankton):
    - Autotrophic / photosynthetic organisms.
    - Examples: **Algae**, **Cyanobacteria**, **Diatoms**, **Dinoflagellates**, **Euglena**.
    - **Bioluminescence / Blue Tide**:
      - Phenomenon where Diatoms and Dinoflagellates emit blue light when threatened or attracting prey/predators.
      - Example: Blue tide observed along **Mumbai beaches**.
    - **Flagellum Structure**:
      - Thread-like longitudinal extension enabling short-distance maneuvering/movement.
      - Human cellular equivalent: **Sperm cell** is the only human cell with a flagellum.
    - **Trophic Classification**:
      - **Autotrophs**: Produce food via photosynthesis using chlorophyll (Diatoms, Algae).
      - **Mixotrophs**: Autotrophic (photosynthesis in sunlight) + Heterotrophic (feeding on other organisms/own species in darkness/absence of sunlight).
      - Examples of Mixotrophic Plankton: **Dinoflagellates**, **Euglena**.
  - **B. Zooplankton** (Animal-like Plankton):
    - Heterotrophic organisms; feed on phytoplankton (Primary Consumers in marine food chain).
    - Size: Microscopic to thumbnail-sized.
    - Examples: **Krills**, **Daphnia**, **Foraminifera**, **Copepods**, **Amphipods**.
- **Loss Mechanism & IPCC 2023 Synthesis Report**:
  - **Report Data**: Published **March 2023** by IPCC (**Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change**).
  - **Marine Heatwave = Definition**: Sea surface temperature rising > **3°C** above historical regional averages for prolonged periods.
  - **Regional Marine Heatwave Hotspots**:
    - **Western Australia**: +3°C above average for **60 consecutive days**.
    - **Mediterranean Sea**: +4°C above average for **30 consecutive days**.
    - **Northwest Atlantic Ocean** (2012): +2.5°C above average for **56 days**.
    - **The Blob** (South Alaska / West Coast of US & Canada): +2.5°C above average for **226 consecutive days** (near-permanent warm water anomaly).
  - **Indian Ocean & Tropical Storm Correlation**:
    - Indian Ocean Sea Surface Temperature (SST) routinely exceeds **27.8°C** (threshold for tropical cyclone formation).
    - Tropical storm frequency in Indian tropical waters increased by **3.1x** since **1970**.
  - **Plankton Loss Impact**:
    - Marine heatwaves → Phytoplankton productivity ↓ → Zooplankton populations decline (up to **30% reduction**) → Aquatic food chain collapse → Mass migration of pelagic megafauna (e.g., sharks).

## Detailed Breakdown 2: Ocean Acidification

### Acid Fundamentals
- **Acid = Definition**: Chemical substance that donates / releases **$H^+$ (hydrogen ions)** when dissolved in an aqueous solution (water).
- **Behavior of Common Acids in Water**:
  - **Hydrochloric Acid**: $HCl \rightarrow H^+ + Cl^-$ (naturally present in human stomach).
  - **Sulfuric Acid**: $H_2SO_4 \rightarrow 2H^+ + SO_4^{2-}$.
  - **Nitric Acid**: $HNO_3 \rightarrow H^+ + NO_3^-$.
  - **Carbonic Acid**: $H_2CO_3 \rightarrow H^+ + HCO_3^-$ (bicarbonate ion).
- **Physical States of Acids**:
  - **Solid**: Sulfonic acid.
  - **Liquid**: $HCl$, $H_2SO_4$, $HNO_3$.
  - **Gas**: $CO_2$ (water-soluble acidic gas, heavier than air), $SO_3$.
- **General Properties**: Sour taste, corrosive, leaves dry/burning impression on tissue.

### pH Scale (Power of Hydrogen)
- **pH Scale = Definition**: Logarithmic scale measuring the strength of an acid or base (range: **0 to 14**).
  - **pH = 7.0**: Neutral.
  - **pH < 7.0**: Acidic (0 = strongest acid).
  - **pH > 7.0**: Basic / Alkaline (14 = strongest base; bases absorb $H^+$ ions, bitter taste, soapy feel).
- **Key pH Benchmarks**:
  - **Milk**: ~**6.5 – 6.6** (slightly acidic).
  - **Human Blood**: **7.4** (slightly basic).
  - **Saliva**: ~**6.8** (slightly acidic).
  - **Pure Distilled Water**: **7.0** (neutral).
  - **Normal Rain**: **5.6 – 6.9** (slightly acidic due to atmospheric $CO_2$ dissolving into $H_2CO_3$).
  - **Acid Rain**: **pH < 5.6** (caused by $SO_2 \rightarrow SO_3 + H_2O \rightarrow H_2SO_4$ and $NO_x \rightarrow HNO_3$).
  - **Ocean Water**: Historical baseline ~**8.5** (basic/alkaline). Recently declining to **8.3, 8.2, 8.1** due to anthropogenic acidification.

### Ocean Depth & Zonation
- **Depth Zonation (Light Penetration)**:
  - Global average ocean depth = **3,500 m** (deepest point = Mariana Trench ~11,000 m).
  - **Photic Zone**: Top **0 – 200 m** layer where sunlight penetrates.
  - **Aphotic Zone** (Abyssal / Profundal zone): Below **200 m** to ocean floor (complete darkness).
- **Temperature Zonation**:
  - **Epilimnion** (warm surface layer) → **Thermocline / Metalimnion** (rapid temperature drop layer) → **Hypolimnion** (cold deep layer, average **4°C** at ocean bottom).

### Solubility Zonation & Saturation Horizon
- **Solute vs. Solvent**:
  - Solute = Dissolved substance (e.g., salt, sugar).
  - Solvent = Dissolving medium (e.g., water, milk).
- **Solution Types**:
  - **Saturated Solution**: Holds maximum possible solute at a given temperature.
  - **Oversaturated Solution**: Holds more solute than its natural saturation point at a given temperature.
  - **Undersaturated Solution**: Capable of dissolving additional solute.
- **Ocean Zonation by Solubility**:
  - **Surface Water**: **Oversaturated** (higher temperature, lower water density, continuous solute influx from rivers/human activities).
  - **Bottom Water**: **Undersaturated** (average temperature **4°C**, maximum water density).
  - **Saturation Horizon**: Imaginary boundary layer separating upper oversaturated waters from deep undersaturated waters.

### Key Solute & Calcification Mechanism
- **Critical Solute**: **Carbonate ions ($CO_3^{2-})$**.
  - Surface Oversaturated Waters = High $CO_3^{2-}$ concentration.
  - Deep Undersaturated Waters = Low $CO_3^{2-}$ concentration.
- **Calcification = Definition**:
  - Biological process where **calcifying organisms** (soft-bodied animals) absorb $Ca^{2+}$ and $CO_3^{2-}$ ions using enzymatic pathways to synthesize **Calcium Carbonate ($CaCO_3$)** exoskeletons/shells for protection.
  - **Calcifying Organisms Examples**: Oysters, Snails, Corals, **Spongula** (freshwater sponge), **Uplectella** (Venus' flower basket - Porifera).
  - **Rule**: Oversaturated surface waters support **calcification**; undersaturated deep waters cause **dissolution**.

### Ocean Acidification Chemical Cascade & Impact
- **Chemical Reactions**:
  - Atmospheric $CO_2 \uparrow \rightarrow$ Dissolves into ocean surface water $\rightarrow$ Forms Carbonic Acid ($H_2CO_3$).
  - **Reaction 1**: $CO_2 + H_2O \rightarrow H_2CO_3$
  - **Reaction 2**: $H_2CO_3 \rightarrow H^+ + HCO_3^-$ (Bicarbonate ion).
- **Disruption Mechanism**:
  - Released $H^+$ ions strongly attract double-negative Carbonate ions ($CO_3^{2-}$):
    - $H^+ + CO_3^{2-} \rightarrow HCO_3^-$ (Bicarbonate ion).
- **Ecological Consequences**:
  - Bicarbonate ion ($HCO_3^-$) concentration **increases ($\uparrow$)**.
  - Free Carbonate ion ($CO_3^{2-}$) concentration **declines ($\downarrow$)**.
  - Calcifying organisms require free $CO_3^{2-}$ (cannot utilize $HCO_3^-$).
  - Carbonate depletion $\rightarrow$ Calcification slows down $\rightarrow$ Shells/exoskeletons thin, weaken, and dissolve $\rightarrow$ Widespread mortality of oysters, corals, and snails $\rightarrow$ Marine food web degradation.
