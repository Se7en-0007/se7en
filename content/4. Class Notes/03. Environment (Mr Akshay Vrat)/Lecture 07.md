## Biotic Interactions (Continuation)

### Competition (- / -)
- **Definition**: Interaction where **both interacting species are harmed (-/-)**.
- **Root Cause**: Scarcity / **limited availability of vital resources**.
- **Factors Driving Competition**:
  - **Food**: Limited food availability.
  - **Water**: Restricted water access.
  - **Space & Dominance**: Territory marking via urine / droppings for hunting, habitat, and survival bounds.
  - **Reproduction**: Males competing for mating partners (**Emperor penguins**, **turtles**, **snakes**).
- **Classifications**:
  - **Interspecific Competition**: Competition between **different species** (*e.g.*, **Lion vs. Cheetah** in grasslands).
  - **Intraspecific Competition**: Competition within the **same species** (*e.g.*, **UPSC CSE aspirants** competing for fixed seats; **Lions**: male lion kills cheetah cubs or lion cubs of another pride to eliminate future resource competition and ensure survival of own pride).
- **Competitive Release**: Moment when competition between species is eliminated.
  - **Mechanism 1 (Adaptation)**: Species adapt to bypass direct conflict (*e.g.*, **Flamingos** vs. **Local Wetland Fishes** in Gujarat/Maharashtra: Flamingos hunt during day → Local fishes adapt to hunt at night).
  - **Mechanism 2 (Elimination / Migration)**: One competing species dies out or migrates out of the dominant species' territory.
- **Human Resource Competition**:
  - **Food**: Minimal direct competition due to agricultural revolutions & high-yield commercial farming (*e.g.*, broiler chicken).
  - **Water**: Severe ongoing competition (*e.g.*, urban slum water distribution); **NITI Aayog Report**: **~1/3rd** of Indians lack safe drinking water → Projected to reach **50%** by **2050** without intervention.

### Amensalism (- / 0)
- **Definition**: Type of interaction where **one species is harmed (-)** and the **other species remains unaffected (0)**.
- **Examples**:
  - **Black Walnut (*Juglans nigra*)**: Roots secretes chemical **Juglone** → inhibits growth of all surrounding plants (**Black Walnut = 0**, **Surrounding Plants = -**).
  - **Indian Plants (Neem, Pearl Millet / Bajra, Sorghum, Barley / Jau)**: Roots/leaves secrete **Caffeic acid** and **Ferulic acid** → inhibits surrounding plant growth.
- **Terminology & Concepts**:
  - **Semiochemicals**: Umbrella term for chemical substances secreted by organisms that trigger specific behavioral or physiological responses in another organism.
    - *Examples*: **Ant trail pheromones** (guided by colony queen semiochemicals), **Dog territory marking** (urine semiochemicals), **Warning pheromones** (threat signaling), **Termite Queen Infertilizing Chemicals** (semiochemical application causing queen infertility → entire colony collapses within 1 week).
  - **Allelopathy**: Phenomenon/relationship where plants release chemical compounds that inhibit the growth/germination of neighboring plants.
  - **Allelochemicals**: The specific chemical compounds involved in allelopathy (*e.g.*, **Juglone**, **Caffeic acid**, **Ferulic acid**); a specialized sub-category of semiochemicals.
  - **Agricultural Significance (UPSC PYQ)**: Allelochemicals function as eco-friendly **Bio-weedicides** / **Bio-herbicides** in irrigated Indian fields; non-toxic to humans (human liver & kidneys easily excrete/metabolize them).
- **Antibiotics (- / 0)**:
  - **First Antibiotic**: **Penicillin** (discovered by **Alexander Fleming** in **1928**; observed in a petri dish with fungal growth killing bacterial colonies; documented in book *Serendipity*).
  - **Fungal Source**: ***Penicillium notatum***.
  - **Mechanism**: Fungal extracts kill bacteria → **Fungi = unaffected (0)**, **Bacteria = harmed (-)**.

### Predation (+ / -)
- **Definition**: Relationship between **Predator (+)** (benefited) and **Prey (-)** (harmed).
- **Scope**: Includes herbivores consuming plants (plant = prey, herbivore = predator).

---

### Parasitism (+ / -)
- **Definition & Characteristics**:
  - **Primitive Organisms**: Lack complex organ systems (respiratory, circulatory, digestive, endocrine); absorb pre-digested nutrients directly from host.
  - **Single Host Attachment**: Cling / stick to a single host throughout their life cycle.
  - **Co-evolution**: Evolve in tandem with adaptations of the host.
  - **Rarely Kills Host**: Host death results in parasite death.
  - **May Benefit Host**:
    - ***E. coli***: Parasitic bacteria in large intestine of humans & cattle → aids in digesting **cellulose** (complex carbohydrate in plant cell walls).
    - **Protozoa**: Mutualistic protozoans in bovine digestive tract for cellulose breakdown.
- **Types of Parasites**:
  - **Endoparasites (Internal)**: Live inside host body.
    - *Examples*: ***Plasmodium vivax*** & ***Plasmodium falciparum*** (malarial parasites), **Liver fluke**, **Tapeworm**, ***Ascaris***.
  - **Ectoparasites (External)**: Live outside host body.
    - *Examples*: **Leech**, **Lice** (*Note: Mosquito is NOT a parasite as it does not cling/stay attached to host*).
  - **Brood Parasites**: Parasite relies on host to raise its offspring.
    - *Example*: **Cuckoo / Koel bird** lays eggs in **Crow** nest after discarding crow eggs → Crow rears cuckoo chicks.

## Climate Change & Carbon Sequestration

### Climate Change Context Recap
- Driven by Greenhouse Effect; **$CO_2$ = 75%** of total GHG volume.
- Global mean temperature risen **+1.1°C to +1.2°C** above pre-industrial levels.
- In **2024**, **80 days** recorded average temperatures exceeding the **+1.5°C** threshold.

---

### Carbon Sequestration
- **Definition**: Technologies/processes used to capture excessive atmospheric $CO_2$ and store it in a carbon sink for an **indefinite period of time**.
- **Carbon Reversal**: Risk of captured carbon escaping back into the atmosphere (*e.g.*, forest fires / biomass burning); indefinite storage prevents carbon reversal.
- **CCUS Framework**: **C**arbon **C**apture, **U**tilization, and **S**torage.
- **Carbon Capture & Utilization (CCU) Examples**:
  - **Air Ink**: Upcycled printer/pen ink synthesized from captured atmospheric carbon emissions (pioneered by brand Air Ink, operational since 2019).
  - **$CO_2$ / Carbon Tiles**: Sustainable construction tiles synthesized from captured atmospheric $CO_2$ pollution.
  - **$CO_2$ Lab-Grown Diamonds**: Pure carbon gemstone jewelry synthesized from atmospheric $CO_2$.

---

### Carbon Capture Methods

#### 1. Point Source Capture
- **Definition**: Capturing $CO_2$ directly at the actual point of emission (factory stacks, vehicle exhausts, cement kilns).
- Monitoring: Easy to monitor via stoichiometric mass balance calculations (100 tons cement → known CaCO₃ → known CO₂ output).
- **Chemical Reactions**:
  - Lime: CaO + CO₂ → CaCO₃ (Calcium carbonate).
  - Caustic Soda: 2 NaOH + CO₂ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O (Sodium carbonate).
  - Basic Metallic Oxides: Metallic oxide + CO₂ → Metal carbonate (e.g., Minerals: Feldspar, Rocks: Basalt rich in CaO & MgO).

#### 2. Direct Air Capture (DAC)
- **Definition**: Capturing $CO_2$ directly from ambient atmosphere anywhere.
- **Monitoring**: **Difficult to monitor** efficacy/utility in short term.
- **Key Global Projects (Climbworks - Iceland & Switzerland)**:
  - **Project CarbFix (2017)**: Feasibility demonstration of DAC technology.
  - **Project Orca (2021)**: Initiative to build world's largest DAC plant in Iceland.
  - **Mammoth Plant**: Named in 2022; operational in **2024** in **Iceland**. World's largest operational DAC facility.

---

### Green Buildings & Urban Heat Island (UHI)
- **Urban Heat Island (UHI)**: Urban areas significantly warmer than surrounding rural areas due to dark concrete/RCC heat absorption, reduced shade/vegetation, and waste heat from HVAC/vehicles.
- **UHI Reduction Strategies**: Light-colored reflective surfaces (high albedo), energy-efficient HVAC, optimal building geometry for natural ventilation & light.
- **Case Study 1 (Singapore)**: Sustainable building design utilizing light reflective materials, energy efficiency, and integrated green coverage.
- **Case Study 2 (Indra Paryavaran Bhawan - New Delhi)**:
  - Official headquarters of **MoEFCC** (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change).
  - India's **1st 5-star rated Net Zero Green Building**.
  - **70% natural lighting**, central courtyard garden for microclimate cooling, slanted basement geometry for **100% rainwater harvesting**, rooftop solar PV array.
- **Building Codes in India**:
  - **ECBC (2017)**: Energy Conservation Building Code (MoHUA + TERI University).
  - **ECSBC (2022)**: Renamed to Energy Conservation and Sustainable Building Code (includes alternative eco-friendly materials like rammed earth/sand).

---

### Carbon Sinks & Classifications
- **Definition**: Sites/locations storing carbon or carbon compounds for an indefinite period.
- **Natural Carbon Sinks (Global Proportion Distribution)**:
  1. **Oceans (~70%)**: Largest natural sink ($CO_3^{2-}$, $HCO_3^-$, $CaCO_3$ calcifiers, phytoplankton/marine biomass).
  2. **Soil (~15%)**: Soil organic matter, **Peat** (undecomposed organic matter).
  3. **Wetlands (~12%)**: Lakes, rivers, marshes, swamps, mangroves, corals.
  4. **Fossil Fuel Deposits (~2%)**: Underground coal, oil, gas reserves (released fossil fuel is <1% of total subterranean reserves; India has coal reserves for ~280 years).
  5. **Atmosphere (<1%)**: Atmospheric $CO_2$ gas.

#### Color-Coded Classification of Natural Carbon
- **Green Carbon**: Carbon captured via photosynthesis by terrestrial plants (forests, grasslands, terrestrial vegetation).
- **Blue Carbon**: Carbon captured and stored by coastal/marine ecosystems (mangroves, coral reefs, kelp underwater forests, seagrass, seaweeds, tidal marshes, aquatic fauna/flora).
- **Teal Carbon**: Carbon captured and stored specifically in wetland ecosystems (lakes, freshwater marshes, swamps).
