## Theories of Decline of Indus Valley Civilization

### 1. Aryan Invasion Theory
- **Proponent** → **Mortimer Wheeler** (1940s)
- **Premise** → **IVC destroyed by Aryan invasion** led by **Indra**
  - **Rigveda** → **Indra** = **Purandara** (**Destroyer / Breaker of cities / forts**)
  - **Pur** = Cities / Fortified centers of **IVC**
  - **Dasa** & **Dasyu** = Enslaved natives / bandits = **IVC inhabitants**
  - **Harupiya** = Site of major battle in **Rigveda** = **Harappa**
  - **Mlechha** (Vedic term for unclean / impure outsiders) ≈ **Meluhha** (Mesopotamian name for IVC)
- **Historiographical Status** → **Largely Rejected**
  - **1,200+ IVC cities** across **10 lakh sq. km** → Required massive warfare, but **no large-scale skeletal trauma / injured dead bodies** found
  - **No physical evidence of city destruction** by battle
  - **Literary exaggeration** in epic / religious texts (e.g., **Mahabharata** claims **18 Akshauhini armies** = **~36 lakh soldiers**; unbacked by archaeology)
  - **Unbacked by archaeological data**

---

### 2. Flood Theory
- **Evidence** → **Thick silt deposits** between settlement layers at **Mohenjodaro**
- **Hypothesis** → Massive catastrophic floods destroyed cities
- **Rejection Reason** → Formulated when only **4–10 cities** discovered (1930s–40s). Universal flooding across **10 lakh sq. km** (larger than modern Pakistan: **8 lakh sq. km**) geographically impossible and archaeologically unevidenced

---

### 3. Tectonic Flood Theory (Raikes Hypothesis)
- **Proponent** → **R. L. Raikes**
- **Premise** → **Tectonic uplift** at mouth of **Indus River** → Blocked river discharge into ocean → **Upstream water swelling** → Drowning of cities
- **Rejection Reason** → Geomorphologically invalid; rivers cut through tectonic uplifts or meander around obstacles. Upstream swelling of major river systems over hundreds of kilometers cannot occur without localized dam formation/pooling

---

### 4. Trade Decline Theory
- **Premise** (1940s) → **Decline in overseas trade** with **Mesopotamia** and **Egypt** → Economic breakdown of IVC
- **Rejection Reason** → IVC economy was overwhelmingly driven by **internal domestic trade** and **indigenous agricultural production**

---

### 5. Eastward Shifting of Monsoons
- **Premise** → **c. 1900 BC** monsoons shifted eastward → Forced abandonment of urban centers
- **Counter-Evidence** → 
  - Late Harappan migration occurred **both Eastward & Southward**
  - Agrarian settlements in **Ganga-Yamuna Doab** appeared **~1,000 years post-IVC decline**, using distinct agricultural practices

---

### 6. Change in River Course
- **Premise** → Sudden shifts in major river courses forced city abandonment
- **Counter-Evidence** → No evidence of synchronized river course changes across all rivers **c. 1900 BC**. Harappans routinely adapted to river course shifts throughout Mature Phase

---

### 7. Increasing Aridity & Saraswati River Drying
- **Premise** → Decline in rainfall → Rising aridity → **Saraswati River** (**Ghaggar-Hakra**) dried up
  - **Hydrological context** → **Satluj** and **Yamuna** were originally tributaries of **Saraswati/Ghaggar-Hakra** → Tectonic/geomorphological shifts diverted **Satluj → Indus** and **Yamuna → Ganga**
- **Counter-Evidence** → Hydrological shifts & river drying occurred **thousands of years prior to 1900 BC**. IVC thrived while Saraswati was already undergoing gradual desiccation → Timeline mismatch

---

### 8. Ecological Factors (Most Accepted Theory)
- **Proponent** → **Walter Fairservis**
- **Causal Chain** →
  - **Harappan urban centers** dependent on surrounding agrarian villages for food supply
  - **Millennia of continuous single-crop cultivation + overgrazing** → Severe soil fertility decline + degradation of pastures
  - **Small cultures** adapt via **shifting cultivation**; impossible for dense IVC urban population
  - **Lack of technical innovation** in agriculture (no historical equivalent of modern **Green Revolution**)
  - **Gradual rising aridity** compounded environmental stress
  - **Result** → Out-migration of population in search of fertile lands towards 3 main zones:
    1. **Punjab & Haryana**
    2. **Ganga-Yamuna Doab**
    3. **Gujarat** (**Rangpur Culture**, **Prabhas Culture**)

## Features of Late Harappan Phase (c. 1900 BC Onwards)

- **Historiographical Shift** → Pre-1960s **"Abrupt End"** models replaced by **"Gradual Ruralization / De-urbanization"** models
- **Urban Decay Markers** →
  - **Unplanned construction** → Houses built in middle of former public streets
  - **Recycling of building material** → Old bricks reused for crude new structures
  - **Slum-like settlements** → Breakdown of municipal governance, **loss of central drainage, sewage systems & wide streets**
  - **Public architecture abandonment** → **Great Bath** & **Great Granary** fell into complete disuse
  - **City shrinkages** → Major urban centers converted into small rural settlements
    - *Example*: **Harappa** replaced by **Cemetery H Culture** (**Cemetery H** = burials in H-shaped arrangement / decorated urns)
- **Disappearance of Standardized Harappan Material Culture** →
  - **Uniform binary/decimal weights** ↓ → Disappeared
  - **Harappan Script** ↓ → Disappeared
  - **Standardized Harappan Pottery** ↓ → Disappeared
  - **1:2:4 Brick Ratios** ↓ → Disappeared
  - **Bead manufacturing & Seal production** ↓ → Disappeared
  - **Mesopotamian texts** → Ceased mentioning **Meluhha** post-**1900 BC**
- **Population Redistribution** → Demographic expansion in **Punjab**, **Haryana**, **Ganga-Yamuna Doab** and **Gujarat**
- **Socio-Economic Mechanism of De-urbanization** →
  - **Loss of agrarian surplus** → Collapse of trade wealth → Inability to maintain public infrastructure (fortifications, municipal sanitation) → Urban features disappeared, rural population survived

## Legacies of Indus Valley Civilization in Modern Indian Culture

- **Urban Legacy** → **Zero continuity** (Citadels, baked brick grids, municipal drainage ended; 2nd Urbanisation developed independently without citadels/fortified grids)
- **Cultural & Religious Continuities** →

| Domain | Harappan Antecedent | Modern / Historical Indian Practice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Numerology & Measurement** | Base-4 & Base-16 weight / counting system | **4 Varnas**, **4 Dhamas**, **4 Ashramas**, **16 Samskaras**, **16 Aanas** (= 1 Rupee), **4 Paisa** (= 1 Aana) |
| **Attire** | Unstitched single cloth wrapping | **Dhoti** / unstitched drapes for men & women |
| **Headgear** | Cloth tied around head | **Pagdi** / turban tradition |
| **Architecture** | Domestic units built around central courtyard | Traditional Indian **Courtyard Houses** (*Angan*) |
| **Shakti Worship** | Terracotta Mother Goddess figurines | **Goddess / Female deity worship** |
| **Shaivism** | **Pashupati Seal**, **Linga** stones, **Trishula** depictions | **Proto-Shiva**, **Pashupati** (Lord of Animals), **Trident**, **Linga & Yoni worship** |
| **Sacred Symbols** | **Swastika** seals | **Swastika** as primary sacred auspicious symbol |
| **Body Art / Ritual Marks** | Seals with forehead marks | **Tilak** & **Bindi** |
| **Ornaments** | Full-arm bangles (**Dancing Girl**) | **Bangle wearing** (especially North-West India post-marriage) |
| **Rituals & Water** | **Kalibangan** & **Lothal** fire altars; **Great Bath** | **Fire Altars** (*Yajna Kunda*); **Ritual bathing before worship** |
| **Nature Worship** | Pipal leaf motifs, animal seals | **Pipal tree** (*Ficus religiosa*) & **Animal veneration** |
| **Spiritual Practices** | Seated meditative postures on seals | **Yogic postures** (*Asanas*) |
| **Metallurgy** | **Lost-Wax Technique** (*Cire Perdue*) | Continuous **Lost-Wax bronze casting** tradition |

## Artifacts & Art Forms of Indus Valley Civilization

### 1. Stone Sculptures
- **Male Torso (Harappa)** → **Red Sandstone**
  - Unique feature → Socket holes in neck and shoulders for **detachable head, arms and feet**
  - Function → Probable **clothing dummy / mannequin** for textile display
- **Male Dancer (Harappa)** → **Grey Sandstone**
  - Features → Sockets for limbs; dynamic posture conveying **sense of movement**
- **Priest-King / Bearded Nobleman (Mohenjodaro)** → **Steatite (Yellow-White Soft Stone)**
  - Features → Draped in shawl over **left shoulder only** (precursor to **Mathura School of Art** drapery), **trefoil patterns** on shawl, well-groomed beard, half-closed meditative eyes, wearing **armband** and **headband**

### 2. Metal Sculptures (Bronze / Copper)
- **Dancing Girl (Mohenjodaro)** → **Bronze** (Lost-Wax Technique)
  - Features → **Tribhanga pose**, right hand on hip, **left arm completely covered in bangles**, wearing necklace
- **Bronze Bull (Mohenjodaro)** → **Bronze**
- **Daimabad Hoard (Daimabad, Maharashtra)** → **Bronze**
  - Artifacts → Figures of **Rhinoceros**, **Buffalo**, **Elephant** and a **Chariot / Cart** driven by a standing man
  - Technical note → Solid wheels (**no spokes**); chemical analysis showing **arsenic bronze** sparked debate over whether hoard is Late Harappan or post-Gupta intrusion

### 3. Seals
- **Shapes** → Square, rectangular, circular, octagonal, cylindrical (cylindrical seals indicate Mesopotamian trade contact/influence)
- **Materials** → **Steatite** (predominant), terracotta, copper, bronze, clay
- **Structural Features** → Perforated **boss** (knob/projection on reverse side) for stringing / holding; carved in **intaglio** (mirror-image impression)
- **Functions** → Identity cards, commercial cargo sealing (stamping wet clay/wax), trade branding, financial receipt tokens, amulets, tribal markers
- **Major Examples** → **Pashupati Seal** (Mohenjodaro), **Brahmani Bull Seal**

### 4. Bead Industry
- **Status** → **Largest manufacturing industry** of IVC; workshop centers found at **Chanhudaro** and **Lothal**
- **Materials** → Carnelian, lapis lazuli, jasper, agate, steatite, quartz, copper, bronze, gold, silver, terracotta
- **Shapes** → Cylindrical, spherical, barrel-shaped, segmented, disc-shaped, oblong

## Contemporaneous Chalcolithic Cultures

### 3rd Millennium BC (Contemporary To Mature Harappan)

| Culture | Geographic Location | Key Sites / Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ahar Culture** (Banas Culture) | South Rajasthan | **Ahar**, **Balathal**, **Gilund**; copper smelting, absence of micro-blades |
| **OCP Culture** (Ochre Coloured Pottery) | Ganga-Yamuna Doab | **Bithoor** (Kanpur, UP); associated with **Copper Hoards** |
| **Ganeshwar-Jodhpura Culture** | North Rajasthan (Alwar/Sikar) | **Ganeshwar**; major copper supplier to Mature Harappan sites |
| **Kayatha Culture** | Central India (Chambal / Kshipra River near Ujjain) | **Kayatha**; sturdy red-slipped ware with chocolate-colored designs |
| **Savalda Culture** | Narmada-Tapi interfluve (Maharashtra/MP) | Pre-Jorwe Chalcolithic horizon |

### 2nd Millennium BC (Contemporary To Late Harappan)

| Culture | Geographic Location | Key Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prabhas Culture** | Coastal Gujarat (Somnath) | Late Harappan affiliated regional culture |
| **Rangpur Culture** | Gujarat | Late Harappan lustrous red ware phase |
| **Jorwe Culture** | Maharashtra (Godavari, Bhima rivers) | **Daimabad**, **Inamgaon**; circular huts (post-1700 BC), painted black-on-red pottery |
| **Malwa Culture** | Central India (North of Narmada) | **Navdatoli**; fine ceramic tradition, rich painted motifs |

- **Sociological Marker** → 2nd Millennium BC cultures show **drastic drop in bead production & long-distance trade contacts**, reflecting total disconnection from Harappan urban networks post-collapse

## Emergence of The Vedic Age (c. 1500 – 600 BC)

### Chronology & Terminology
- **"Dark Period"** (c. 1900 – 1500 BC) → Gap between IVC urban decline and Vedic literary emergence (lack of unified archaeological-literary link)
- **Dating** → **Max Müller** = c. 1200 BC; **Modern Consensus** = **c. 1500 – 600 BC**
- **Vedic People** → Nomadic/semi-nomadic pastoralists, speakers of **Vedic Sanskrit**, authors of **Vedic Hymns**
- **Core Geography** → **Saptasindhu** (**Land of Seven Rivers**; central axis = **Saraswati River**)

---

### Theories of Aryan Origin

#### 1. Eurasian Origin Theory (Most Accepted Academic Framework)
- **Premise** → Shared flora, fauna and environmental markers in early Rigvedic hymns match **Eurasian Steppes** (Southern Russia / Caucasus Mountains)
- **Key Historical & Archaeological Evidence** →
  1. **Boghazköi Inscription** (c. 14th–15th Century BC, Anatolia / Turkey):
     - Record of peace treaty between **Hittite** and **Mitanni** tribes
     - Invokes Rigvedic deities as oath witnesses: **Indra**, **Mitra**, **Varuna** and **Nasatyas / Ashwins**
  2. **Kikkuli’s Horse Manual** (c. 15th Century BC, Hittite tablet, Iraq):
     - Horse-training treatise by **Kikkuli the Mitannian**
     - Uses numerals identical to Sanskrit: **Aika** (1), **Tera** (3), **Panza** (5), **Satta** (7)
  3. **Linguistic Affinity** (**Rigvedic Sanskrit** vs. **Zand Avesta** / Old Persian):
     - Systematic phonetic shifts: Sanskrit **S** → Avestan **H** (*Sapta* → *Hapta*, *Sindhu* → *Hindu*)
     - Shared theological lexicon: Sanskrit **Asura** = Avestan **Ahura**; Sanskrit **Deva** = Avestan **Daeva**

#### 2. Sub-Theories of Entry Into India

##### A. Aryan Invasion Theory (AIT)
- **Proponents** → **Max Müller**, **Mortimer Wheeler**
- **Premise** → Military invasion by hostile Aryan tribes who subjugated native IVC inhabitants (**Dasas/Dasyus**), destroyed cities and drove **Dravidians** southwards
- **Status** → **Completely Rejected** by modern scholarship due to zero evidence of military destruction or racial replacement

##### B. Aryan Migration Theory (AMT)
- **Consensus Status** → **Widely Accepted**
- **Premise** → **Gradual migration in successive waves** over 1,000+ years (c. 1500 BC onwards)
- **Mechanism** → Small pastoral groups entering through North-West passes, peacefully integrating and intermarrying with local populations → **Bilingualism & mutual cultural exchange**
- **Definition** → **"Aryan" = Linguistic / Cultural identity**, NOT a biological race
- **Non-Aryan Contributions** → Ingestion of indigenous folk traditions into later texts (e.g., **Atharvaveda**)

#### 3. Arctic Origin Theory
- **Proponent** → **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (*The Arctic Home in the Vedas*, 1903)
- **Premise** → Rigvedic hymns describing **6-month long days and 6-month long nights** indicate ancestral origin in the **Polar / Arctic region**

#### 4. Out of India Theory (OIT) / Indigenous Origin Theory
- **Premise** → Aryans were **indigenous to Saptasindhu** (India) and migrated outwards to Iran and Europe
- **Arguments** →
  - **Boghazköi** and **Kikkuli** terms represent **later Prakrit distortions** exported from India
  - Avestan **Ahura/Deva** flip reflects later Vedic schism
  - Rigvedic geography, flora, fauna and perennial rivers match North-West India exclusively

---

### Periodization & Textual Corpus of The Vedic Age

- **1. Early Vedic Period (Rigvedic Age, c. 1500 – 1000 BC)** →
  - **Primary Text** → **Rigveda Samhita** (1,028 hymns; 10 Mandalas)
- **2. Later Vedic Period (c. 1000 – 600 BC)** →
  - **Later Samhitas** → **Samaveda** (Chants/Melodies), **Yajurveda** (Sacrificial formulas), **Atharvaveda** (Magical spells/charms/folk traditions)
  - **Brahmanas** → Prose manuals on sacrificial rituals
  - **Aranyakas** → "Forest Books" (Philosophical transition for ascetics/vanaprastha)
  - **Upanishads** → Philosophical treatises exploring **Ontology** (*Atman*, *Brahman*, existence)
