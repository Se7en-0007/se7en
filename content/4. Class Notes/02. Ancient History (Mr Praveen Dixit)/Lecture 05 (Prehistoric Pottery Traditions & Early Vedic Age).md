## Archaeological Pottery Sequences of Northern India

### Regional & Chronological Succession
- **OCP (Ochre Coloured Pottery)**:
  - **Geography**: Upper Ganga-Yamuna Plains (Punjab, Haryana to Kanpur/Lucknow; site: **Nuh**).
  - **Timeline**: **2800 BC – 1200 BC** (Late 2nd Millennium BC).
  - **Material Culture**: Associated with **Copper Hoards** (**Copper Hoard Culture**).
- **BRW (Black and Red Ware)**:
  - **Geography**: Middle Ganga Plains (**Allahabad to Bhagalpur / Bihar**).
  - **Timeline**: **1500 BC – 900 BC**.
  - **Technique**: Inverted firing → Overburning → Interior/rim **Black**, exterior **Red**.
  - **Cultural Attribution**: Non-Aryan indigenous / local tribal culture.
- **PGW (Painted Grey Ware)**:
  - **Geography**: Upper Ganga Plains → expanded eastward into Middle Ganga (BRW area) by **900 BC**.
  - **Timeline**: **1200 BC – 600 BC**.
  - **Technique**: Deluxe / elite pottery (only **5%** of total ceramics in PGW sites; remainder red/BRW ware). High quality, fine grey clay with black geometric designs; repaired with copper wire/wood if cracked.
  - **Vedic Identity**: **1949 → B.B. Lal (Hastinapur excavation)** → Proved **PGW = Signature Pottery of Vedic Aryans**.
  - **Technological Shift**: **~900 BC** → PGW culture introduced **Iron** usage.
- **NBPW (Northern Black Polished Ware)**:
  - **Timeline**: **600 BC – 100 AD**.
  - **Extent**: **Taxila** (Northwest) to **Deccan** (**Asmaka**) and **Bengal** to **Sindh**.
  - **Significance**: Characteristic pottery of **Second Urbanization** & **Mahajanapada / Mauryan Period**.

### Historical & Archaeological Debates
- **PGW Expansion (900 BC)**:
  - **PGW expansion Eastward** → Displacement/absorption of **BRW local tribes** → Archeological marker of **Vedic Aryan Eastward Migration**.
- **Sinoli Excavation & B.B. Lal's Hypothesis**:
  - **Site**: **Sinoli** (Baghpat, UP) → Discovery of **Copper Hoards** and **Chariots**.
  - **B.B. Lal's Thesis**: If **OCP** & **BRW** are proven to be **Vedic**:
    - **Vedic Age chronology shifts back**: **1200 BC → 2800 BC**.
    - **Indus Valley & Vedic cultures** → Proven **contemporary / identical**.

## Early Vedic Tribal Structure & Non-Aryan Contacts

### Rigvedic Tribal Groupings
- **Panchajanya** = **5 Original Wandering Aryan Tribes** (*Yadu, Turvasa, Puru, Anu, Druhyu*).

### Non-Aryan Classifications & Terminology
- **Avrata** = Those who do not follow Vedic laws (*Vrata*).
- **Akratu** = Those who do not perform rituals/sacrifices (*Karmakand*).
- **Dasa**:
  - **Original Status**: Originally an **Aryan tribe** (e.g., King **Devodasa** of **Bharata clan**; Avestan equivalent *Daha*).
  - **Evolution**: Suffered decline in fortune → became lowest tier of Aryans → Term later equated with **slaves** by Mauryan period.
- **Dasyu**:
  - **Definition**: Indigenous outsiders / hostile non-Aryans who fled into forests/hills (**Bandits / Outlaws**).
  - **Dasyu-hatya** = Slaying of Dasyus (celebrated in Rigveda).
  - **Trasadasyu** = Title awarded to kings (*Destroyer of Dasyus*; derived from *Tadana / Trasa* = beating/punishing).

## Geography & Rivers of Sapta Sindhu

### Core Region
- **Sapta Sindhu** = **Land of Seven Rivers** (Original homeland of Rigvedic Aryans).

### The Saraswati Debate
- **Saraswati** = **Most sacred / important river** in Rigveda.
- **Historiographical Interpretations**:
  1. **Mythical River**: Purely symbolic / metaphorical.
  2. **Helmand River (Harakhvati, Afghanistan)**: Proposed by **Hillebrandt** & **Romila Thapar**.
  3. **Ghaggar-Hakra River**: Seasonal river flowing **East to West** through Uttarakhand, Haryana, Rajasthan and Sindh → **Accepted by modern Indian archaeology & satellite imagery**.

### Rigvedic River Nomenclature (*Vishvamitra-Nadi Samvada*)
- **Sindhu** = Indus
- **Saraswati** = Ghaggar-Hakra / Sarasvati
- **Sutudri** = Sutlej
- **Vipasa** = Beas
- **Parushni** = Ravi
- **Asikni** = Chenab
- **Vitasta** = Jhelum
- **Ganga & Yamuna** = Mentioned only peripherally in late hymns.

## Sociopolitical Organization of Early Vedic Society

### Social Units Hierarchy
- **Kula** = Family / Joint Clan (Head = **Kulapa**).
- **Grama** = Group of Kulas wandering together as a band / fighting unit.
  - **Head**: **Gramani** / **Grama**.
  - **Nature**: Primary **fighting unit** during pastoral migrations (**R.S. Sharma** thesis).
  - **Sangrama** = Inter-grama cattle raids / conflicts (*Grama vs. Grama conflict*).
- **Vis / Vish** = Cluster of multiple Gramas living/moving in proximity.
  - **Head**: **Vispati**.
  - **Derived Terms**: *Pravesa* (entry), *Nivesa* (settlement/investment), *Upanivesa* (colonization).
- **Jana** = Largest tribal unit / collective people.
  - **Head**: **Rajan** (Tribal Chief).
  - **Rashtra** = Concept of collective people/nation in Rigveda (became territorial *Janapada* in Later Vedic Age).

### The Office of Rajan (Tribal Chief)
- **Selection**: **Elected** by tribal assembly/consensus (**NOT hereditary**).
- **Core Duty**: Protect cattle wealth of the Jana → Called **Gopa** (*Protector of Cows*).
- **Religious Function**: Conducted sacrifices and prayers on behalf of the whole Jana.

### Public Finance & Redistribution
- **Bali**:
  - **Nature**: **Voluntary, irregular donation/tribute** given by Jana members to Rajan (**NOT regular or compulsory tax**).
- **Bhaga**:
  - **Nature**: War booty or tribute obtained from raids.
  - **Redistribution**: Shared among all Jana members; **Rajan received a relatively larger share**.

## Tribal Assemblies & Administration

### Four Primary Assemblies
- **Sabha**:
  - **Composition**: Kin-based assembly of clan elders / **Kulapas** / elites.
  - **Women Participation**: **Attended** by women (*Sabhavati*).
  - **Continuity**: Only assembly that retained major political importance into Later Vedic times.
- **Samiti**:
  - **Composition**: General folk assembly of the entire **Jana**.
  - **Function**: Responsible for electing/deposing the **Rajan**.
  - **Women Participation**: **No mention** of female attendance.
- **Vidatha**:
  - **Composition**: Oldest tribal assembly.
  - **Function**: Dealt with **military policy**, **redistribution of war booty** and secular/civil affairs.
  - **Women Participation**: **Attended** by women.
- **Gana**:
  - **Composition**: Assembly for **religious and ceremonial** matters (Head = **Ganapati**).
  - **Women Participation**: **No mention** of female attendance.

### Military Structure & Functionaries
- **Standing Army**: **ABSENT** (due to lack of permanent regular taxation).
- **Militia System**: During war, Rajan mustered a **tribal militia** from volunteers.
- **Key Functionaries (Bureaucracy)**:
  - **Senani**: Chief military assistant; organized volunteers into structured fighting units.
  - **Purohita**: Chief priest; performed rituals/prayers for victory and welfare of Rajan & Jana.
  - **Gramani**: Leader of Grama / fighting contingent.

### Socio-Polity Evolution: Chiefdom → Hereditary Monarchy
- **Mechanism**:
  - Rajan sought to secure succession for his **son** over brothers/clan members.
  - Needed religious legitimization → Allied with **Brahmanas** (Purohits) via exclusive family sacrifices (*Rajasuya, Hiranyagarbha*).
  - **Outcome**: Established **Brahmana** (1st social rank) & **Kshatriya** (2nd social rank) alliance → Transformed tribal republic into **hereditary monarchy**.

## Military History: Dasharajna Yuddha (Battle of Ten Kings)

### Battle Specifications
- **Location**: Banks of River **Parushni** (**Ravi**).
- **Combatants**: **King Sudas** (of **Tritsu-Bharata clan**) vs. Confederacy of **10 Kings** (5 Aryan + 5 Non-Aryan tribes).
- **Outcome**: **King Sudas victorious**.
- **Historical Trajectory**: Tritsu-Bharatas later merged with Purus → Formed **Kuru clan** (protagonists of Mahabharata / Kurukshetra war in Later Vedic Age).

## Economic Life & Material Culture

### Pastoral Economy
- **Primary Occupation**: **Cattle Rearing** (*Gau*).
- **Subsidiary Occupation**: **Agriculture** (Shifting / pastoral supplementary farming).
- **Property Rights**: **Land WAS NOT private property** (communal tribal ownership).

### Cattle / Cow (*Gau*) Centrality & Vocabulary
- **Gopa** = King (*Protector of Cows*).
- **Gavyuti** = Unit of distance measurement (~12,000 feet).
- **Gaveshana / Anveshana** = Search for lost cows → Evolved into term for *search / research / conflict*.
- **Godhuli** = Dusk / Evening (time when cattle return, raising dust in slanted sunlight).
- **Gotra** = Cow-pen / common herd enclosure → Evolved into *exogamous clan lineage*.
- **Duhitri** = Daughter (*Literally: Milcher of Cows*).
- **Gavishti** = Cattle raid / war for cows.

### Secondary Animals & Metallurgy
- **Horse (*Ashva*)**:
  - **Status**: 2nd most important animal.
  - **Functions**: Pastoral hording, rapid movement, cavalry and **horse-driven chariots (*Ratha*)**.
- **Metallurgy**:
  - **Iron (*Krishna/Shyama Ayas*)**: **NOT MENTIONED** in Rigveda.
  - **Ayas**: Refers exclusively to **Copper / Bronze**.
  - **Stage**: Rigvedic material culture = **Chalcolithic Age**.

### Trade, Currency & Social Status of Merchants
- **Trade Volume**: Low, localized **barter system**. Cows served as primary unit of value.
- **Nishka**: Gold ingot / necklace ornament. Used as store of wealth and jewelry; **NOT a circulating currency**.
- **Pani**:
  - **Identity**: Subordinate / non-Aryan group absorbed into Vedic fold.
  - **Role**: Conducted inland trade between settlements.
  - **Social Status**: Trade viewed with low prestige in Vedic social hierarchy.

## Social Structure & Evolution of The Varna System

### Multi-Stage Varna Evolution (R.S. Sharma Thesis)
- **Phase 1 (Early)**: **2-Fold Division** → Based on *Varna* (Color/Identity): **Aryans vs. Dasa/Dasyu**.
- **Phase 2 (Mid)**: **3-Fold Division** → **Brahmana** (Priests), **Rajanya** (Warriors), **Vis/Vaishya** (Commoners).
- **Phase 3 (Late)**: **4-Fold Division** → Addition of **Shudra** (Servants/Labourers).

### Nature of Rigvedic Varna
- **NON-HEREDITARY**: Based strictly on **profession / occupational choice**.
- **Textual Evidence**: Rigvedic hymn (10th Mandala):
  > *"I am a poet, my father is a physician and my mother is a corn-grinder."*

### Textual Analysis of Purusha Sukta
- **Location**: **10th Mandala** of Rigveda (Widely recognized as a **later interpolation**).
- **Organic Metaphor**:
  - **Brahmana** = Created from **Mouth** (*Mukha*).
  - **Rajanya** = Created from **Arms** (*Bahu*). *(Note: Term used is **Rajanya**, NOT Kshatriya!)*
  - **Vaishya** = Created from **Thighs** (*Uru*).
  - **Shudra** = Created from **Feet** (*Pada*).
- **Absence of Term "Kshatriya"**: *Kshetra* (defined territory) did not exist in pastoral Rigvedic era; hence rulers were *Rajanya*, becoming *Kshatriya* only after territorial settlement (*Janapada* phase).

### Status of Women
- **High Egalitarian Status**:
  - Attended public assemblies (**Sabha**, **Vidatha**).
  - Full access to ritual sacrifices alongside husbands.
  - Practice of **Upanayana** (sacred thread initiation) and education.
  - **Absence of social evils**: No Sati, no child marriage, no purdah system.
