## Rig Vedic Society: Land & Social Hierarchy

- **Rig Vedic Society** = **Tribal & Pastoral**
- **Land** ≠ Private property → Not a source of hierarchy or social stratification
- **Core Unit of Social Relations & Hierarchy** = **Kula** (Family / Clan)
- **Intoxicants & Gambling**:
  - **Sura** = Intoxicating drink (present & consumed)
  - **Dice / Gambling** = Present & permitted, but generally criticized as a bad habit (*Aksha*)
  - **Literary Example**: Mahabharata → Yudhishthira betting Draupadi (viewed as moral criticism / embarrassment)
  - **Criteria of Embarrassment (Historical Methodology)**: Historical events containing embarrassing moments for heroes (e.g., Rama killing Vali from behind a tree, Yudhishthira gambling wife) → Higher probability of real historical occurrence vs. fabricated glorification

## Food Habits & Culinary Practices

- **Primary Food Items**: **Barley** (*Yava*), **Wheat**, **Vegetables**, **Milk & Milk Products**
- **Rice** = Added in Later Vedic Period (*Vrihi*)
- **Non-Vegetarian Dietary Habits**:
  - Consumed regularly / post-sacrificial rituals (**Bali**)
  - **Mythological Anchor**: Demon brothers **Atapi & Vatapi**
    - Vatapi → Transformed into a goat → Atapi served mutton to Vedic Rishis → Vatapi transformed back inside stomach to kill eater → **Agastya Rishi** consumed Vatapi, used yogic insight, digested Vatapi before transformation → Proves prevalence of non-veg consumption
- **Historiographical & Literary Debates on Meat**:
  - **D.N. Jha** (*Myth of the Holy Cow*) → Argument for beef consumption in ancient India
  - **Vedic Terminology for Cow**: **Aghanya** = Not to be killed / No violence (*Jaghanya* = Violent)
    - *UPPSC 2017 Prelims Anchor*: Cow designated as *Aghanya* in Vedic texts
  - **Buddha's Last Meal Debate**: *Sukara-maddava* translated by Max Müller as pork vs. linguistic translation as *Shakarkand* (sweet potato) mash (*Majja*) suitable for an 80-year-old toothless person

## Architectural Evolution & Settlement Structures

- **Rig Vedic Dwellings** = **Wattle and Daub**
  - **Wattle and Daub** = Wooden/bamboo structural framework plastered over with mud
  - **Archaeological Evidence**: Bamboo/wood decomposed over time, but bamboo impressions remain preserved on dried mud from **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)** settlements
- **Chronological Evolution of Building Materials**:
  - **Wattle & Daub** (Rig Vedic) → **Mud Walls** (Early Later Vedic) → **Mud Bricks** (Middle Later Vedic) → **Baked/Burnt Bricks** (Mahajanapada 2nd Urbanization)
- **Pur** = Fortified place / Elevated shelter built near settlements
  - **Function**: Protection/refuge during inter-tribal battles or natural calamities (storms/floods)
  - **Etymological Evolution**: **Pur** = Fort → Synonym for **City** (e.g., Hastinapur, Rajagriha)

## Position of Women in The Rig Vedic Period

### Structural Limitations (Non-Absolute Equality)
- **Patrilineal & Patriarchal Structure**: Birth of male child (*Kuta* / son) explicitly desired
- **Leadership Succession**: Lineage headship of **Kula** and **Jana** strictly male
- **Ritual & Funerary Restrictions**: No instance of female performing funeral rites; women could not be sole independent patrons (*Yajamana*) of major Vedic sacrifices

### Egalitarian & Empowered Status (Highest in Ancient History)
- **Ritual Participation**: Women participated in all major Vedic rituals alongside husbands
- **Social Evils Absent**: Absolute absence of **Parda** (veil) and **Sati**
- **Educational Access**:
  - Female students = **Brahmavadini** (pursued lifelong Vedic knowledge) vs. Male students = **Brahmachari**
  - Female teachers = **Upadhyaya**
  - **Upanayana Sanskar**: Girls performed sacred thread ceremony (*Yajnopavita*) and wore the sacred thread
  - **Female Vedic Scholars (Rishikas)**: Composed Rigvedic hymns → **Lopamudra**, **Sikata**, **Nivavari / Visvavara**, **Ghosha**, **Apala**
- **Deification of Independent Female Deities**:
  - **Goddesses**: **Saraswati**, **Usha** (Morning dawn), **Pratyusha** (Evening dusk), **Aditi**, **Lakshmi** (in Yajurveda), **Swaha** (Wife of Agni, invoked in sacrifices)
  - **Chhath Puja Origin Anchor**: Worship of Sun's wives **Usha** & **Pratyusha** at dawn and dusk
  - **Independent Identity**: Rigvedic goddesses possessed independent spiritual presence/power, not depicted merely as subordinate wives of male gods
- **Participation in Warfare**:
  - Women took part in tribal mass-mobilization battles
  - **Example**: **Vishpala** = Lost a leg/hand in battle, fitted with an iron prosthetic limb
  - **Sociological Context**: Tribal wars involved entire population (mass muster), not formal standing armies; female participation was normal, not an anomaly
- **Historiographical Debates: Reformism vs. Revivalism**:
  - **19th Century Reformists** (Raja Rammohan Roy, Vidyasagar, Keshub Chandra Sen, Nehru): Sought social progress by adopting Western enlightenment concepts
  - **19th Century Revivalists** (Dayananda Saraswati / Arya Samaj, Vivekananda): Sought social reform by restoring Vedic ancestral standards (female education, absence of caste/sati, Vedic science)
- **Political & Marital Rights**:
  - **Political Assemblies**: Women actively attended and participated in **Sabha** and **Vidatha**
  - **Marital Patterns**:
    - **Monogamy** = Most prevalent norm
    - **Polygamy** & **Polyandry** = Present (e.g., Draupadi choosing/consenting to polyandry)
    - **Marriage Age**: Post-puberty / Adult marriages standard; child marriage rare
    - **Swayamvara**: Right of girl to choose husband / evaluate candidates (e.g., Draupadi rejecting Karna based on eligibility, selecting Arjuna)
    - **Dowry (*Yautuka*) & Bride Price (*Kanya-shulka*)**: Both recognized based on socio-economic context
    - **Widow Remarriage**: Allowed and socially recognized
    - **Niyoga Practice**: Levirate/Sororate custom where widow/widower could marry deceased spouse's sibling → Cultural origin of informal teasing customs between brother-in-law & sister-in-law (*Devar-Bhabhi*, *Jija-Sali*)

## Vedic Textual Corpus & Rigveda Mechanics

- **Sole Text of Early Vedic Age** = **Rigveda Samhita**
- **Hierarchical Structural Division**:
  - **Rigveda Samhita** → **10 Mandalas (Chapters)** → **1,028 Suktas (Hymns)** → **Richas (Verses)** → **Padas (Phrases)**
  - **Mandalas 2 to 7** = **Family Books** (oldest core; composed by specific rishi families: Bhargava, Viswamitra, Vasishtha, Gautama, Atri, Bharadvaja)
  - **Compilation Legend**: Post-Mahabharata compilation under King **Janamejaya** (son of Pariksit, grandson of Abhimanyu)
- **Components of a Sukta**:
  1. **Devata**: Deity to whom the hymn is addressed
  2. **Chhanda (Meter)**: Metrical pattern (e.g., *Gayatri* = 3 lines × 8 syllables; *Trishtubh*, *Anushtubh*)
  3. **Rishi**: Specific seer/composer (e.g., Kuts Angiras)
- **Mantra** = Selected powerful Richas chanted together (e.g., **Gayatri Mantra** dedicated to Savitr/Surya in Gayatri meter)
- **Nature of Rigveda** = **Liturgic** (Worship methods & prayers for **Worldly Desires** / *Laukika Purushartha*: cattle, horses, wealth, male progeny, victory in war)
  - **Contrast with Bhakti Movement**:
    - **Rigvedic**: Materialistic requests, power, destruction of enemies, consumption of Soma
    - **Bhakti**: Spiritual liberation (*Moksha*), complete surrender, renunciation of material desires ("Tera Tujhko Arpan")
- **Oral Transmission & Philosophy**:
  - Preserved via oral tradition (*Shruti*) until written down around **7th Century AD**
  - **Mimamsa Philosophy View**: **Shruti** = Not composed by humans (**Apurusheya**), self-evident truth (**Svatah Pramana**)

## Ritual Mechanics (Karmakanda)

- **Principal Worship Mode** = **Karmakanda** (Sacrificial Rituals)
- **Architectural Reality**: **Zero** literary or archaeological evidence of **Temples** or **Idol Worship** in the Vedic Age
- **Core Sacrificial Matrix**:
  - **Yajamana (Patron)** → *Dakshina* → **Purohita / Brahmana (Priest)** → *Bali (Sacrifice)* → **Agni (Fire Intermediary)** → **Devas (Gods)**
  - **Yajamana**: Beneficiary who pays for ritual
  - **Purohita**: Priest executing ritual operations
  - **Dakshina**: Fee paid to priest for services rendered
  - **Dana**: Voluntary public charity (no direct service rendered)
  - **Bhiksha**: Alms given for individual basic survival
  - **Agni**: Fire acting as divine messenger/mediator carrying offerings to gods (**Swaha** = Wife of Agni)
- **Soma Drink**:
  - Divine herb drink offered to gods (distinct from alcoholic **Sura**)
  - Mentioned in **112 out of 1,028 Suktas** (Entire **9th Mandala** dedicated to Soma)
- **D.N. Jha Interpretation**: Sacrifices served to cull old, unproductive livestock

## Vedic Pantheon & Theological Spectrum

- **Deities** = **Personifications of Natural Forces & Calamities** (Thunder, Rain, Wind, Fire, Water)
- **Major Deities & Frequency**:
  - **Indra** (*Purandara* / God of Thunder & Rain): **250 Suktas** → Chief deity, war leader
  - **Agni** (Fire / Intermediary): **200 Suktas**
  - **Varuna** (Water / Cosmic Moral Order - *Rta*)
  - **Mitra** (God of Harmonious Relationships / Contracts)
  - **Vayu** (Wind)
  - **Vishnu** (*Upendra* / Younger brother of Indra): **4 Suktas**
  - **Rudra / Shiva**: **1 Sukta** (or 3)
  - **Female Deities**: Saraswati, Usha, Pratyusha, Aditi, Lakshmi
- **Divine Beings**:
  - **Apsaras**: Divine celestial dancers
  - **Gandharvas**: Divine musicians (Husbands of Apsaras; *Gandharvaveda* = Upaveda of Music)
- **Sacred Animals**: Cow and Ox (**Aghanya**)
- **Divine Status of Rishis**: Intermingled with gods (e.g., **Dadhichi Rishi**'s bones formed Indra's *Vajra*; **Durvasa**; **Gautama Rishi**'s curse on Indra)

### Theological Spectrum of Vedic Religion
- **Paganism**: Direct worship of nature and natural elements
- **Polytheism**: Worship of multiple distinct deities
- **Kathenotheism** (Max Müller concept): Opportunistic monotheism → Worshipping one god at a time as supreme in turn
- **Monotheism**: Belief in one supreme reality manifested through various gods
- **Monism**: Non-duality → God, universe and self are one (**Advaita** / *Aham Brahmasmi*)

## Vedic Cosmogony & Creation Hymns

### 1. Nasadiya Sukta (Rigveda 10.129 - Hymn of Creation)
- **Pre-Creation State**: Neither existence (*Sat*) nor non-existence (*Asat*), no light, no darkness, no void, no death, no immortality
- **Creation Process**: Single unknown entity (**Brahma**) manifested universe out of itself
- **Ultimate Agnosticism**: Cause unknown; poem ends with profound philosophical doubt: *"He who surveys it all from high heaven, He knows—or perhaps He knows not."*
- **Philosophical Scope**: Provided foundation for open-ended inquiry, including Samkhya and non-theistic/agnostic traditions

### 2. Hiranyagarbha Sukta (Rigveda 10.121)
- **Cosmic Origin**: Universe originated from a **Golden Womb / Cosmic Egg** (**Hiranyagarbha**)
- **Creation Event**: Explosion/manifestation of *Hiranyagarbha* generated all light, luminous cosmic bodies and life

## Later Vedic Period (1000 BC – 600 BC): Geographical & Textual Expansion

- **Textual Corpus**: 3 Later Samhitas (**Yajurveda**, **Samaveda**, **Atharvaveda**) + **Brahmanas**, **Aranyakas**, **Upanishads**
- **Geographical Shift**:
  - Expansion from **Sapta-Sindhu** (Punjab/North-West) to **Jambudvipa** (Indian Subcontinent)
  - **Three Regional Divisions of Jambudvipa**:
    1. **Aryavarta**: Northern Plains
    2. **Madhyadesa**: Middle Ganga Plain / Central India (Root of Nepal *Madhesi* term)
    3. **Dakshinapatha**: Southern Region
  - **Mountain Ranges**: **Himavant** (Himalayas) and **Mujavant** (Hindu Kush - source of Soma)
  - **Mount Meru**: Central cosmic mountain peak from which all major rivers descend

## Later Vedic Economy: The Iron Revolution & Agrarian Surplus

- **Socio-Economic Transition**: Semi-nomadic pastoralism → **Settled rural agrarian economy**
- **Iron Metallurgy**:
  - **Shyama Ayas** / **Krishna Ayas** = **Iron** (Beginning of Iron Age in North India)
  - **Early Usage**: Primarily military weapons (swords, axes) and hand tools (sickles, hoes)
  - **Iron Ploughshare**: Adopted in later phases of the period
- **Forest Clearance Technique**: **Slash-and-burn / Clearing by Fire**
  - **Literary Example**: Clearing of *Khandavaprastha* forest by fire to establish **Indraprastha** (Delhi)
- **Three Core Agricultural Innovations**:
  1. **Wet Paddy / Rice Transplantation** (*Vrihi*)
  2. **Manure Application** (Dumping organic animal waste in fields)
  3. **Iron Ploughshare**
- **Economic & Systemic Impact**:
  - **3 Innovations** → **Agrarian Surplus** → **Regular Taxation (Bali)** → **Territorial Kingdoms** → **2nd Urbanization**

## Later Vedic Polity & Socio-Political Stratification

### Territorial Identity & Social Stratification
- **Hierarchy of Political Units**:
  - **Kula (Family)** → **Grama (Village)** → **Vis (Clan)** → **Jana (Tribe)** → **Janapada (Territorial Realm)**
- **Rashtra** = Defined by land/territory (*Janapada*), no longer solely by tribal affiliation (*Jana*)
- **Property Rights**: Land recognized as private/family property → Unequal ownership → Social hierarchy
- **Storable Wealth**: Grain surplus (storable) > Cattle/milk (perishable) → Accelerated class stratification

### Regional Shift of Political Power
1. **Early Later Vedic Core**: **Upper Ganga Plain**
   - Major Kingdoms: **Kuru** (Hastinapur/Indraprastha) & **Panchala** (Ahichchhatra/Kampilya)
   - **Historical Anchor**: **Kuru** = First organized state in India (taxation & civil retinue); compilation of Vedic corpus under Kuru reign
2. **Middle Later Vedic Core**: **Middle Ganga Plain**
   - Rise of **Kashi** and **Kosala** (Ayodhya / Sravasti); branch of Kuru migrated east to establish **Vatsa** (Kaushambi at Ganga-Yamuna confluence)
3. **Late Later Vedic / 6th Century BC**: Shift to **Eastern Ganga Plain**
   - Rise of **Magadha** (First territorial Empire in India)

### Transformation: Tribal Chiefdoms to Hereditary Monarchies
- **Political Evolution**:
  - **Rigvedic Elected Rajan (Chief)** → (Iron & Surplus) → **Later Vedic Hereditary Monarchy (King)**
- **Taxation Evolution**: **Bali** transformed from voluntary/irregular tribal gift to **regular, compulsory taxation**
- **Bureaucracy & Assembly Decline**:
  - Regular tax revenue enabled Kings to maintain administrative officers and retinues
  - Tribal assemblies (**Sabha**, **Samiti**, **Vidatha**) lost decision-making power and declined
- **Sacred Legitimization by Priests**:
  - **Brahmanas** performed elaborate royal rituals (**Rajasuya**, **Ashvamedha**, **Vajapeya**)
  - Rituals elevated King to divine status and legitimized hereditary succession of the King's son
