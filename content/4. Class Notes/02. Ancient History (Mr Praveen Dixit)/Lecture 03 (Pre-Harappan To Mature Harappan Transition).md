## Pre-Harappan Phase
- **Pre-Harappan Regional Cultures** → **Amri-Nal** + **Damb Sadaat** + **Sothi-Siswal** + **Kot Diji**
- **Pre-Harappan Developments** → Foundation of Mature Harappan Phase:
  - **Fortified Settlements** → Boundary wall around settlement (safety against floods/animals, not military fort)
  - **Settlement Bipartition** → **Citadel** + **Lower Town** → Early administrative stratification
  - **Grid Planning** → Roads intersecting at **90°**
  - **Kiln-Baked Bricks** → Ratio **1:2:4** (majority houses retained **mud bricks**)
  - **Craft & Industry** → **Bead production** + **Copper tool production/use**
  - **Script & Seals** → Emergence of early **script** + **seal manufacturing**
  - **Religion & Agriculture** → **Mother Goddess worship** + **Agricultural expansion**
- **Causal Conclusion** → Pre-Harappan features = Mature Harappan features → **Urbanization = Indigenous + Gradual** (Rejects sudden foreign diffusion theory)

## Mature Harappan Phase (2600 BC – 1900 BC)
- **Phase Nomenclature** = **Integration Phase** / **Urbanization Phase**
- **Distinguishing Markers (Pre-Harappan vs. Mature Harappan)**:
  - **Pottery** → Regional potteries replaced by **Harappan Ware** (**Red Pottery with Black Designs**)
  - **Copper Tools** → Intensified production + universal usage
  - **Brick Technology** → **Kiln-baked bricks** standardized across **all sites**
  - **Craft Expansion** → Widespread **bead-making industry** in almost every site
  - **Literacy** → **Script/Writing** standardized across all sites
  - **Demographics** → Large urban centers with population of several thousands
  - **Pan-Harappan Uniformity** → Standardized **1:2:4 brick ratio** + **identical weight systems** + **uniform seal types** + **similar city layouts** (*Note*: Similar layout ≠ identical cities)

## Town Planning of Indus Valley Civilization

### Settlement Layout: Citadel vs. Lower Town
- **Citadel (Raised Platform)**:
  - **Structure** → Built on artificial mud-brick mounds/raised platforms
  - **Function** → Flood protection + administrative center + community gatherings + religious/ceremonial events
  - **Elitist Architecture = Absent** → **No royal palaces** / **No monarchic monuments** → King's palace theory **rejected**
- **Lower Town**:
  - **Structure** → Located at lower elevation; housed artisans, traders and common populace

### Grid System & Road Network
- **City Layout** → Main arterial road bisects city → Subsidiary streets intersect at **90°** → Divides city into **rectangular blocks**
- **Street Alignment** → **Houses NEVER open onto main road** → Open exclusively onto **subsidiary alleys/lanes** (Traffic safety + Dust prevention + Privacy)
- **Pavement** → Brick-paved main roads (**Kharanja** technology)

### House Architecture & Domestic Amenities
- **Courtyard Layout** → Rooms built around **central open courtyard** → All rooms open inward into courtyard
- **Privacy Design** → **Zero exterior windows or doors** on outer walls → Single main entrance opening to subsidiary alley
- **Building Materials** → **Kiln-baked bricks** (ratio **1:2:4**) + **Flat roofs** (used for storage/living; sloped roofs restricted to snow zones; thatched roofs indicate primitive housing)
- **Elevation** → Majority **single-story**; **double and triple-story houses** also present
- **Domestic Amenities**:
  - **Kitchen** → Dedicated hearth (**Chulha**) per house → Individual household cooking
  - **Sanitation** → Private toilets & bathrooms in majority houses
  - **Water Source** → Private drinking water **wells** in majority of houses

### Drainage, Sewage & Sanitation System
- **Drain Network** → Household toilets & kitchens → Subsidiary street drains → **Main covered drain along main road**
- **Drain Construction** → Drains fully covered using **bricks or stone slabs**
- **Maintenance Infrastructure** → Vertical inspection shafts (**Stumps / Manholes**) at regular intervals → Periodic manual cleaning
- **Solid Waste Management** → **Wooden screen/grate** fitted at terminal end of main sewage line → Traps solid waste (cleaned periodically) while liquid effluent passes
- **Effluent Disposal** → Discharged into **rivers/seas** OR directed into artificial **cesspools** (settling ponds for sludge drying/composting)
- **Toilet Typologies**:
  1. **Flush Toilets** → Connected directly to municipal covered drainage system
  2. **Pit Toilets** → Connected to **soak pits** via **terracotta ringwells**

### Fortification Walls
- **Construction** → Perimeter fortification walls around cities
- **Primary Function** → **Protection against seasonal floods + wild animals** (NOT built for military defense/warfare)

### Sociological Nature: Egalitarian & Utilitarian Utopia
- **Planning Triad** = **Egalitarian** + **Utilitarian** + **Symmetrical**
- **Urban Equity**:
  - Smallest edge-of-city houses connected to main streets & sewage network
  - **Absence of Ghettos / Slums** + **Absence of Oppressive Ruling Class Architecture**
  - All residences contained within protective fortification walls
- **Comparative Urbanism**:
  - **Mesopotamia / Egypt** → Focus on **monumental elitist structures** (**Ziggurats**, **Pyramids**, **Royal Palaces**) + lack of universal municipal sanitation
  - **Indus Valley** → Focus on **public utility** (**Granaries**, **Great Bath**, **Public Drainage**, **Universal Sanitation**) → Foreign commentary tag: *"Ancient Communist Utopia"*

### Demographics & Urban Scale
- **Population Density** → Mohenjo-Daro pop. ≈ **30,000–40,000** (~1,000–2,000 houses; area 5–6 hectares)
- **Spatial Scale** → Compact urban settlements (comparable to modern small towns/kashbas, not sprawling modern metropolises)
- **Demographic Statistics**:
  - **Life Expectancy** ≈ **29 years** (Driven down by high **infant mortality**)
  - **Family Size** → Large birth rates (8–12 children per family to offset high infant mortality from smallpox/infections)

## Pottery & Material Culture
- **Harappan Ware (Dominant Type)** → **Red Pottery with Black Painted Designs**
- **Perforated Ware** → Elongated pots with multiple perforations (debated utility: fruit washing, straining, incense/steam)
- **Elite / Glazed Ware** → Highly polished/glazed vessels made of **Steatite**

## Technology, Weights & Transport

### Metallurgy & Tools
- **Metal Horizon** → Mastery over **5 metals**: **Copper** + **Tin** + **Lead** + **Gold** + **Silver** (*Iron unknown*)
- **Bronze/Copper Technology** → Intensified tool production across all settlements

### Standardized Weights & Measures
- **Uniformity** → Standardized weight system across entire geographic domain
- **Dual System**:
  1. **Binary / Hexahedral System** → Lower denominations (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64... ratio based on 16)
  2. **Decimal System** → Higher denominations (100, 200, 500...)

### Transport Systems
- **Water Transport** → Primary mode of long-distance communication & trade (majority cities sited on river banks / sea coasts)
- **Land Transport** → Bullock carts equipped with **Solid Wooden Wheels** (**Spokeless wheels**)
- **Wheel Technology** → **Spoked wheels = ABSENT** in IVC (spoked wheels introduced later in Vedic age; solid wheels universal in IVC)

### Medical Knowledge & Early Surgery
- **Trepanation & Surgical Interventions** → Physical evidence of **drilling in human teeth and skulls** (found at sites like Kalibangan & Burzahom) → Indicates early cranial & dental surgical efforts

### Harappan Script & Writing System
- **Corpus & Status** → Over **400 distinct characters/symbols** discovered → **Undeciphered**
- **Longest Inscription** → **Dholavira Signboard** (**10 large characters**, discovered near city gate)
- **Text Length** → Typical inscriptions are short (**4 to 5 characters** long)
- **Direction of Writing** → Primarily **Right to Left** (Evidenced by character crowding on left margin); secondary patterns include **Left to Right** and **Boustrophedon** (alternating bi-directional snake-like lines)
- **Linguistic Nature Debate**:
  - Traditional view → Logographic/syllabic script representing an unknown language
  - Modern view (**Michael Witzel, 2004**) → Non-linguistic symbol system / non-script emblems (due to high symbol count + extremely short text length)

## Agriculture, Crops & Water Management

### Agrarian Base & Seasonality
- **Food Security** → **100% Indigenous production** (Proven by central city granaries & unique crop assemblage)
- **Primary Harvest Season** → **Rabi (Winter Season)**
- **Crop Inventory**:
  - **Core Rabi Crops** → **Barley** + **Wheat** + **Peas (Chickpea / Chana)** + **Lentils** + **Mustard** + **Sesame (Til)**
  - **Mature Phase Additions** → **Rice** (husks at Lothal & Rangpur) + **Millets**
  - **Primary Cash Crop** → **Cotton** (First in world to domesticate & weave cotton; exported textiles)
- **Agricultural Methods**:
  - **Multi-Cropping** → Practiced growing **2 crops simultaneously in same field**
  - **Tilling Technology** → Wooden ploughshares (**Ploughshare = Wooden**, with no iron tip; evidenced by terracotta plough models at **Banawali**, ploughed field furrows at **Kalibangan** and plough depictions on seals)
  - **Harvesting Tools** → Curved **Copper Sickles** (**Hasiya**)

### Regional Irrigation Models (4 Distinct Systems)
1. **Canal Irrigation** → Northern / Punjab & Haryana region (e.g., Harappa, Kalibangan)
2. **Tank / Pond Irrigation** → Southern / Sindh & Gujarat region
3. **Sabur Bends (Gabarbands / Check Dams)** → Western / Baluchistan hilly plateau (terraced stone bunds capturing hill runoff)
4. **Flood Irrigation** → Central Indus alluvial plain (storing seasonal river inundation)

### Rainwater Harvesting & Dholavira Water Management
- **Monsoon Adaptation** → Agricultural & urban life structured around **4-month monsoon**
- **Dholavira Hydraulic Model**:
  - Sited between two seasonal rivulets (**Mansar** & **Manhar**)
  - Constructed series of stone **Check Dams** + **feeder channels**
  - Diverted monsoon floodwaters into **16+ massive masonry reservoirs** surrounding Citadel & Lower Town
  - Control sluices raised/lowered to regulate floodwater intake and prevent urban inundation

## Trade & Economy (Domestic & External)

### Trade Network Dynamics
- **Domestic Trade** → Intra-regional exchange between urban centers, rural hinterlands and regional hunter-gatherer/chalcolithic cultures
- **External Trade** → Active trade with **Mesopotamia** (referred to as **Meluhha**), **Persian Gulf** (**Dilmun** / Bahrain) and **Oman** (**Magan**)
- **Trade Mechanism** → Indirect maritime & overland trade; barter system regulated by standardized weights & seals

### Import Inventory (Raw Materials & Sources)
- **Shells** ← **Kutch Region** (Gujarat)
- **Tin & Lapis Lazuli** ← **North Afghanistan** (**Shortughai**) & **Central Asia**
- **Carnelian (Red Stone)** & **Steatite (White Soft Stone)** ← **South Rajasthan** & **Gujarat**
- **Copper** ← **Ganeshwar Culture** (North Rajasthan), **Ahar Culture** (South Rajasthan), **Uttarakhand Mines**, & **Oman**
- **Gold** ← **Kolar Mines** (South Deccan / Karnataka)
- **Lead & Jasper** ← **Baluchistan**
- **Timber & Wool** ← **Burzahom** (Kashmir Neolithic)

### Export Inventory (Finished Goods & Destinations)
- **Cotton Textiles** → Exported to Mesopotamia & Middle East
- **Carnelian Beads** → Exported to Mesopotamia & Persian Gulf
- **Terracotta Pottery** → Regional export
- **Shell Objects** → Exported to Mesopotamia & Persian Gulf
- **Lapis Lazuli Beads** → Re-exported finished beads (Imported raw stone from Afghanistan → Crafted in IVC factories → Exported)
- **Ivory Objects** → Exported to Oman
- **Steatite Objects** → Exported to Oman & Mesopotamia
- **Failaka Hoard** → Cache of IVC seals & commodities discovered on **Failaka Island** (Persian Gulf) → Direct evidence of Gulf trade route

## Theories of State & Polity

### Weaponry & Military Evidence
- **Armament Nature** → **Absence of offensive war weaponry** + **Absence of army cantonments**
- **Weapon Morphology** → Spearheads & blades are **flat and lack a mid-rib** (Cannot pierce armor or dense bone; designed for hunting, domestic use and basic municipal law & order)

### Five Models of Harappan Governance
1. **Single Empire ruled by Priest-Kings** (**Mortimer Wheeler**):
   - Proposed single unified empire under teocratic priest-king based on "Priest-King" steatite statue at Mohenjo-Daro
   - *Status*: **Largely Rejected** (*"Killing the Priest-King"* thesis → Statue is not a king; society achieved complex governance without monarchic ruling class)
2. **Federal Model of Capital Districts** (**Stuart Piggott**):
   - Empire divided into province-like districts where capital cities (Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro) ruled surrounding village clusters
3. **Complex Conglomeration of States**:
   - Multi-tier polity combining city-states, chiefdoms, tribal autonomous zones and rural units
1. **City-State Confederation**:
   - Independent urban city-states purchasing agricultural supply from independent rural hinterlands
5. **Municipal Corporate / Merchant Council Governance**:
   - Administration managed by **Councils of Merchants & Artisans** (like merchant guilds/trade associations) governing municipal services, hygiene, standards and civic trade laws

## Religion & Ideology

### Historiographical Debates on IVC Religion
1. **Aryan Invasion & Shiva Assimilation Model** (**Mortimer Wheeler**):
   - IVC = Shiva-worshipping Dravidians → Invaded and destroyed by Indra-worshipping Vedic Aryans → Aryans later absorbed Shiva into Vedic pantheon as **Rudra** (*"Shiva was loser in short term, winner in long term"*)
2. **Indigenous Vedic Model** (**B.B. Lal**):
   - IVC religion = Early Vedic culture → Evidenced by **Fire Altars**, **Swastika symbols**, **Proto-Shiva**, **Linga worship**, **Peepal tree reverence** and **Soma ritual vessels**
1. **Dravidian Continuity & Migration Model** (**Iravatham Mahadevan**):
   - IVC = Dravidian language & culture (Linguistic pocket preserved in **Brahui language** of Baluchistan) → Peaceful migration of Aryans → Cultural synthesis pushing Dravidian populations southward

### Archaeological Features of Harappan Religion
- **Religious Architecture** → **Zero confirmed temples or dedicated shrines**
- **Fire Altars & Ritual Sacrifice**:
  - Found on Citadels at **Lothal** & **Kalibangan**
  - Enclosed clay fire pits containing ashes, charcoal, terracota cakes and **bovid animal bones** → Evidence of fire veneration & animal sacrifice
- **Mother Goddess Cult**:
  - Most prevalent cult across IVC sites
  - Terracotta female figurines featuring **bare upper torso**, **heavy neck jewelry** and **fan-shaped headgear**
  - **Plant-Womb Figurine** → Female figure with plant/tree emerging from womb → Symbol of **Earth Goddess / Fertility Cult**
- **Pashupati / Proto-Shiva Seal** (**John Marshall**):
  - Steatite seal depicting male deity seated in yogic posture (**Mulabandhasana** - heels pressed together under hips)
  - Wearing horned/trident (*Trishula*) headgear
  - Surrounded by **4 wild animals**: **Elephant**, **Tiger**, **Rhinoceros**, **Water Buffalo**, with **2 Deer/Ibex** below throne
- **Linga & Yoni Worship**:
  - Terracotta and polished stone cones (**Phallus/Linga**) and ring-stones (**Yoni**) → Fertility rites
- **Animal Worship**:
  - **Real Animals** → **Brahmany Humped Bull** (with prominent hump & dewlap; most sacred), Tiger, Rhinoceros, Elephant
  - **Mythical / Composite Animals** → **Unicorn** (one-horned mythic beast on seals), hybrid multi-headed creatures
- **Nature & Tree Worship**:
  - Elements: Water, Fire, Trees
  - **Peepal Tree (*Ficus religiosa*)** → Most sacred and revered tree (depicted extensively on seals & pottery)
- **Great Bath of Mohenjo-Daro**:
  - Bitumen-sealed masonry tank on Citadel with surrounding cloistered rooms
  - Interpreted as public ritual purification tank (**Ritual Ablution**)
- **Yogic Postures**:
  - Terracotta figurines depicted in various physical **Asanas** (including *Mulabandhasana*) → Precursor to Indian Yoga systems

### Burial & Cremation Practices
- **Dual Rites** → Both **Cremation** (burning) and **Burial** (inhumation) practiced
- **Cemetery Types**:
  - **Specialized Cemeteries** → Designated burial grounds outside city walls (e.g., **Cemetery R-37** & **Cemetery H** at Harappa)
  - **Unspecialized Burials** → Inhumation inside residential compounds, under house floors, or in backyards (practiced in select sites)
