### Central Ideology & Country Classifications of Kyoto Protocol

#### Central Ideology
- **Central Principle** = **Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR)**.
  - Global climate fight = Common responsibility.
  - Action & burden = Differentiated based on **respective national capacities**.

#### Classification of Parties under Kyoto Protocol
- **Annex I Countries**:
  - Industrialized / developed nations of today.
  - Emission reduction targets = **Legally binding**.
- **Non-Annex I Countries**:
  - Developing and underdeveloped nations.
  - Targets = **Not legally binding** (voluntary adoption permitted).
- **Annex II Countries**:
  - Subgroup under **Annex I**.
  - Includes highly advanced industrialized nations.
  - **Added Responsibilities**: Must provide **financial and technical aid** to developing nations for climate mitigation/adaptation.
  - Country Example: Japan → Belongs to both Annex I (industrialized → binding reduction) & Annex II (technically/financially strong → provides financial/tech assistance to developing states).

### Assigned Amount Units (AAUs) & Annex I Operational Dilemmas

#### Assigned Amount Units (AAUs)
- **Definition** = Maximum limit of carbon footprint ($CO_2$ equivalent emissions) permitted to an Annex I country over a commitment period.
- **Mathematical Example**:
  - Japan baseline emissions = **100 carbon footprint units**.
  - Kyoto mandate = **10% emission reduction**.
  - Japan's Assigned Amount Limit = **90 carbon footprint units** (= 90 AAUs).
  - Exceeding Assigned Amount Limit → Penalties applied.

#### Domestic Policy Options & Constraints for Annex I Countries
1. **Option 1: Reduce Production**:
   - Shutting down factories/industries → Industrial output ↓ → GDP decreases.
   - *Feasibility*: **Unacceptable** to any nation (no country sacrifices economic growth for climate targets).
2. **Option 2: Increase Expenditure in Renewable Energy, Green Tech & Sustainable Development**:
   - Investments in solar/wind, green technology, green buildings.
   - *Feasibility & Demographic Constraint (Case Study: Japan)*:
     - **Demographic Crisis**: Japan = Oldest population globally by demographic profile.
     - **2030 Metric**: **>50% of Japanese population >60 years old**.
     - Elderly population → Low resource demand + Low GDP contribution → Economic growth rate stagnant (~1%).
     - Declining domestic demand → Extremely high cost recovery risk for massive green tech investments.

### Kyoto Flexible Mechanisms (Kyoto Mechanisms)

#### Core Objective
- To assist **Annex I countries** in reducing carbon footprints and meeting Kyoto targets **without compromising economic/GDP growth**.
- Legal Approval: Approved at COP 7 (2001) in Marrakesh → Marrakesh Accords.

#### The 3 Flexible Mechanisms
1. **International Emission Trading (IET)**
2. **Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)**
3. **Joint Implementation (JI)**

### Detailed Mechanics: Carbon Leakage vs. Carbon Offsetting

#### Carbon Leakage (Banned Phenomenon)
- **Causal Chain**:
  - Annex I obligation transferred to private corporation (e.g., **Toyota** in Japan mandated to cut 10 carbon footprint).
  - Domestic green tech investment in Japan deemed financially unviable.
  - Toyota shuts down Japanese manufacturing plant → Relocates entire plant to a Non-Annex I country with weak environmental laws (e.g., **India**).
- **Corporate Advantages**:
  - Continued operation of inferior/polluting technology without government interference.
  - Cheap labor + low raw material costs + low running costs → Cheaper manufacturing cost → Export back to home market.
- **Environmental & Policy Flaw**:
  - Japan records reduced emissions on paper.
  - India sees equal/higher emission increase.
  - **Global GHG emissions remain unchanged** (problem shifted, not solved).
- **Definition**: **Carbon Leakage** = Relocation of high-emitting manufacturing from countries with strict environmental norms to countries with weak/lax norms.
- **Legal Status**: **Banned under Kyoto Protocol**.
- **Countermeasures / Prevention**:
  - Imposition of **Border Carbon Taxes / Import Tariffs** on goods manufactured in leakage-destination plants (e.g., Japanese border tax on Indian-made Toyota imports to equalize market costs and discourage carbon leakage).

#### Mechanism 1: International Emission Trading (IET) & Carbon Offsetting
- **Carbon Offsetting** = Offsetting domestic emission reduction obligations by investing in/purchasing emission reductions achieved in another territory.
- **Process**:
  - Annex I country/firm unable to reduce domestic emissions purchases **Carbon Credits** from international emission trading markets to compensate for excess domestic emissions.
- **Carbon Credit (Definition)**:
  - Definition = A tradable permit allowing the bearer/holder to emit 1 ton equivalent of CO₂ (1 tCO₂e = 1 carbon footprint).
  - Can be used internally to cover excess emissions OR traded/sold on open carbon markets to needy entities.
- **Major Emission Trading Systems (ETS)**:
  - **European Union ETS (EU ETS)**: Established **2005** → World's first carbon market.
  - **African Union ETS**
  - **California State ETS**
  - **Chinese National Carbon Market**: World's **largest carbon market** currently.
  - **Indian Carbon Market (ICM)**: Circular & legal amendments passed; scheduled operationalization **~2026**.
- **Economic Drawback of IET**: Pure financial expenditure for Annex I nation (capital leaves country to buy permits without creating domestic assets or ROI).

#### Mechanism 2: Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)
- **Definition** = An **Annex I country** (or its private firms) invests directly in **emission-reducing projects in a Non-Annex I (developing) country** to earn carbon credits.
- **Dual Benefit**:
  - Developing country receives green capital, infrastructure, and advanced technology.
  - Developed country earns **Carbon Credits** + financial return (interest/dividends).
- **Case Study: Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC)**:
  - **Funding**: Funded by Japan via **JICA (Japan International Cooperation Agency)** through soft loans.
  - **Emission Reduction Impact**:
    - **700g $CO_2$ saved** per passenger per single metro trip.
    - Daily rider volume = **30–40 lakh (3–4 million) passengers**.
    - Prevents massive vehicular gridlock & exhaust emissions in Delhi.
  - **CDM Outcome**: Emissions reduced in India → Converted to **Carbon Credits** → Handed over to Japan to satisfy Kyoto targets (plus loan interest paid to JICA).
- **Top Global CDM Beneficiaries**:
  - **#1 China** (highest CDM fund recipient).
  - **#2 India** (second highest recipient).
- **Other Indian CDM Example**: **UJALA Scheme** (replacing traditional inefficient lights with LED bulbs/streetlights).
- **Structural Limitation of CDM**:
  - Developing regions with high political/economic instability (e.g., African military coups, South American governance crises in Brazil/Argentina post-2019) risk capital expropriation.
  - Private Annex I capital shies away from unstable regions → Out of **100 Billion Dollar** total CDM corpus, only **~60 Billion Dollar** deployed (concentrated in China, India, SE Asia). Unstable regions rely instead on UN AID, US AID, World Bank, and NGOs.

#### Mechanism 3: Joint Implementation (JI)
- **Definition** = An **Annex I country** invests in emission-reducing projects in **another Annex I country** to earn carbon credits.
- **Rationale**: Serves as a secure alternative for Annex I capital when Non-Annex I developing destinations carry excessive political or financial risks.

### Chronology, Enforcement & Failure of Kyoto Protocol

#### Enforcement Timeline
- **1997**: Kyoto Protocol adopted at COP 3.
- **2001 (COP 7, Marrakesh)**: Marrakesh Accords approve Kyoto Flexible Mechanisms.
- **February 2005**: Kyoto Protocol **officially enforced** (required 7+ years of post-1997 negotiations).
  - *Enforcement Requirement*: Mandatory ratification by at least **75% of Annex I countries**.
- **Carbon Credit Price Surge**: Price rose from **1 USD** / credit in 2005 → **1,500 USD** / credit by 2009.

#### Primary Causes of Kyoto Protocol Failure
1. **Non-Participation of USA**:
   - **USA signed in 1997 but NEVER ratified/joined** the Kyoto Protocol.
   - *US Historical Share*: USA contributed **26% of all 20th-century global GHG emissions** (#1 historical emitter; Japan = #2 at ~10%).
   - Absence of the largest emitter undermined global political compliance.

#### Post-2012 Negotiations & Doha Amendment
- **COP 13 (2007, Bali) - Bali Action Plan**:
  - Initiated early negotiations for **post-2012 climate strategy** before 1st commitment period expired to avoid regulatory gaps.
- **COP 18 (2012, Doha) - Doha Amendment**:
  - Extended Kyoto Protocol into **Second Commitment Period**: **1st January 2013 – 31st December 2020** (8-year extension).
  - **Emission Target**: **18% reduction** below 1990 baseline levels (1st period target was 5%).
  - **Annex B**: Formally listed target commitments of countries accepting the 2nd Commitment Period.

#### Collapse of Second Commitment Period
- **Withdrawals**: **Japan, Canada, and Russia withdrew** from the 2nd Commitment Period (refused to accept binding targets while USA remained exempt; Japan's exit halted major CDM funding).
- **EU Crisis**: **European Union** remained committed, but was hit by the **Eurozone Debt Crisis (2012)** (PIGS nations: Portugal, Ireland, Italy, Greece, Spain bankrupted → widespread factory closures & high unemployment → natural emission reduction without active CDM/JI investments).
- **Final Closure**: Kyoto Protocol completely expired on **31st December 2020**.

### REDD & REDD+ (Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation)

#### Background & FAO Statistics
- **UN FAO World State of Forests Report (2005)**:
  - Published by **FAO every 2 years** (serves as global benchmark; inspired Forest Survey of India's *India State of Forest Report - ISFR*).
  - **Global Forest Loss Metric**: **13 million hectares** of forest land degraded or deforested annually (~half the geographical area of Uttar Pradesh; UP area = **27 million hectares**).
  - **Climate Contribution**: Deforestation & land degradation cause **~7% of total global atmospheric $CO_2$ increase**.
- **Top 3 Deforested Countries (2010–2020)**:
  1. **Brazil**: Deforestation driven by conversion to **pasture land** for animal husbandry/meat industry + crop land expansion.
  2. **Indonesia**: Deforestation driven by agriculture (**palm oil**, **coffee**, **rubber** plantations).
  3. **India**: Deforestation driven primarily by **population settlements & urbanization**.

#### Ecological Consequences of Forest Conversion (Forest vs. Monoculture Plantation)
- **Biomass Loss**: Agriculture/commercial plantations hold **only 10% of the biomass** of a natural tropical rainforest (**90% of stored carbon released** into atmosphere).
- **Structural Degradation**: Natural forest (multi-layered canopy, stratification, ground cover, zero visible soil) converted to open monoculture plantation (patches of exposed soil, zero animal/bird/arboreal biodiversity).
- *Commercial Examples*: Indonesia palm oil, Nilgiri tea plantations, Western Ghats coffee.

#### REDD vs. REDD+
- **REDD (Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation)**:
  - Initiated by **UN FAO (2005)**.
  - Focus: Promoting afforestation & standing forest protection.
  - *India's Stance*: **India NEVER joined REDD**. Criticized REDD for providing **zero monetary compensation** for opportunity costs incurred by developing nations preserving forests (e.g., Ecuador discovering oil in Amazon but barred from drilling without compensation).
- **REDD+ (REDD Plus)**:
  - Adopted at **COP 13 (2007, Bali)** as part of post-2012 framework.
  - **Core Mechanism**: Financial transfer from **Global North** (developed) to **Global South** (developing) via **GEF (Global Environment Facility)** ("Plus" = GEF integration).
  - Provides **direct monetary incentives** to developing states for conserving standing forests, sustainable forest management, and enhancing forest carbon stocks.
  - *India's Stance*: **India JOINED REDD+**.

### Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)

#### Nature, Status & Credibility
- **Status** = Most credible global authority, source of data, literature, and standard definitions (e.g., tipping points, carbon footprint) on climate change.
- **Establishment**: Founded in **1988** jointly by **UNEP** and **WMO (World Meteorological Organization)**.
- **Structure**: Independent UN body; membership automatically open to all UN and WMO member states.

#### Core Methodology
- **IPCC DOES NOT CONDUCT PRIMARY RESEARCH**.
- **Assessment Function**: Conducts systematic, long-term **review assessments** of peer-reviewed published scientific literature from international journals (e.g., *Nature*) and leading global research institutions (e.g., IISc, TERI, JNU).
- **Publication Frequency**: Publishes **Assessment Reports (AR)** every **~7 years**.

#### Historical Impact of IPCC Assessment Reports on Global Policy
1. **1st Assessment Report (FAR, 1990)** → Catalyzed creation of **UNFCCC** (1992 Earth Summit).
2. **2nd Assessment Report (SAR, 1995)** → Formed scientific foundation for **Kyoto Protocol** (1997).
3. **3rd Assessment Report (TAR, 2001)** → Established scientific frameworks for climate **Adaptation**.
4. **4th Assessment Report (AR4, 2007)** → Identified the **2°C global tipping point** (irreversible loss of marine ecosystems/corals).
5. **5th Assessment Report (AR5, 2013–14)** → Direct scientific foundation for **Paris Agreement** (2015).
6. **Special Report on 1.5°C (SR1.5, 2018)** → Outlined emission pathways to limit warming to 1.5°C.
7. **6th Assessment Report (AR6, 2021–2023)** → Triggered the **Global Stocktake (GST)** at COP 28 (Dubai, 2023).
