## Major Anthropogenic Sources of Methane (CH₄) Recap

- **Global CH₄ Breakdown**: **41%** → Agriculture (Rice cultivation) | **33%** → Dairy Sector.
- **Rice Cultivation Mitigation**:
  - Traditional Paddy → Standing water → Anaerobic wetland conditions → High CH₄ emissions.
  - **Direct Seeding of Rice (DSR) / Direct Sowing**: Eliminates standing water requirement → **Up to 80% decrease in CH₄ emissions**.

## Dairy Sector Mitigation (FAO Report – 4 Key Strategies)

### 1. Introducing New Efficient & Nutritious Fodder / Forage
- **Deficit in Indian Fodder Supply**:
  - **Green / Wet Fodder Deficit** → **~80%** (Rich in proteins & nutrients).
  - **Dry Fodder Deficit** → **~50%**.
  - Impact: Cattle consume garbage/rubbish → Impaired health → Low milk quality & yield.
- **High-Yield vs Indian Cow Milk Yield Comparison**:
  - **Holstein Cow** (Origin: **Netherlands / Holland**, also in **New Zealand**): Most productive globally → **6,000 L** milk per lactation cycle.
  - **Indian Indigenous Breeds**: **Gir** (North/West India), **Sahiwal** (North India), **Nellore** (Andhra Pradesh).
  - Yield: Max **1,500 L** per lactation cycle (**1/4th** of Holstein yield).
  - Cause: Nutrient-poor fodder, NOT inferior genetics.
- **Azolla Fern (Water Fern / Mosquito Fern)**:
  - Aquatic floating fern → High protein & nitrogen content (edible by humans & animals).
  - Function: Mix with existing fodder → Fodder quality ↑ → Milk yield ↑ → Fewer cattle required to satisfy milk demand → Livestock CH₄ emissions ↓.

### 2. Feed Management
- **Strategy**: Cultivate dual-purpose crop varieties satisfying human food + nutritious animal forage.
- **Mustard / Rapeseed Comparison**:
  - **Indian Mustard (Rapeseed)**: Contains **Thiosulfates** (chemical causing pungent smell) → Livestock refuse to eat leaves.
  - **Canadian Mustard (Canola)**: Lacks thiosulfates → Leaves utilized as animal forage.
  - **Pusa Canola Hybrid**: Developed by **ICAR / Pusa Institute** (Indian Agricultural Research Institute) → Hybrid of Canola + Indian Mustard → Low thiosulfates, suited for Indian climate → Seed oil for humans + Leaf fodder for livestock.
- **Other Dual-Purpose Crops**: Wheat (grain for humans, straw/grass for fodder), Maize/Corn, Rice.

### 3. Rumen Modification
- **Concept**: Gut microbial intervention analogous to human **probiotics** (e.g. *Lactobacillus* in curd).
- **Rumen Bacterial Substitution**:
  - Native Rumen Flora: **Methanogenic Bacteria** → Digest cellulose → Produce CH₄.
  - Intervention: Introduce **Methanotrophic Bacteria** (Protobacteria / Methanotrophs).
    - Etymology: *Trophos* = Nutrition.
    - Mechanism: Consume CH₄ as nutrient source → Oxidize CH₄ → CO₂.
  - Outcome: Livestock release CO₂ instead of CH₄ → Net radiative forcing impact ↓.

### 4. Selective Breeding
- Natural individual/breed variation in bovine enteric CH₄ production.
- Strategy: Identify & cross-breed naturally low-CH₄-emitting cows → Offspring inherit low-CH₄ traits.

## Other Methane (CH₄) Sources & Fire Dynamics

- **Anthropogenic**: Dairy, paddy, legacy landfill waste, coal mining, oil & gas production.
- **Natural / Fire Sources**: Forest fires, agricultural stubble burning.

### Forest Fire Dynamics & Methane Release Mechanics
- **Line Fire**:
  - Fire spread along a linear front.
  - Heated air rises → Creates **low-pressure zone** along the line.
  - Fresh air influx from **both sides** → Continuous O₂ supply → Complete combustion → Releases **CO₂** (tall, active flames).
- **Area Fire**:
  - Fire spread over a broad surface region.
  - Air rises over entire zone → Low pressure created.
  - Fresh air influx reaches **only the outer edges**.
  - **Internal Core**: Severe O₂ deficiency → Incomplete combustion → Emits **Carbon Monoxide (CO)** and **Methane (CH₄)** + dense smoke (primary cause of fire deaths = suffocation).
  - Example: Large-scale agricultural stubble burning acts as area fire → Releases CO & CH₄.
- **Zombie Fires (Ground / Peat Fires)**:
  - Occurrence: Cold / Arctic regions (**Russia, Canada**).
  - Mechanism: Ignition within **Peat** (partially decomposed organic matter under slow anaerobic degradation).
  - Features: Smolders underground without visible surface flames → Releases trapped CH₄, CO₂, and CO.
  - Significance: Termed **"Environmental Time Bombs"** → Unnoticed due to low population density & Arctic fog/smog mix → Frequency increases with global warming.

## Global Methane Initiatives & Policies

- **Global Methane Tracker (2024)**:
  - Institution: **International Energy Agency (IEA)** (UN framework context).
  - Objective: Track CH₄ emissions from fossil fuel producing companies globally.
- **Oil and Gas Decarbonization Charter (OGDC) (2024)**:
  - Signatories: **50 oil & gas companies** (representing **40% of global fossil fuel production**).
  - Objective: Reduce CH₄ emissions across upstream production processes. *(Indian oil companies are not signatories).*
- **Oil and Gas Methane Partnership 2.0 (OGMP 2.0) (2024)**:
  - Institution: **UNEP** (United Nations Environment Programme).
  - Objective: Standardized measurement, monitoring, and reporting framework for oil & gas industry CH₄ reductions.
- **International Methane Emissions Observatory (IMEO) (2021)**:
  - Launched 2021 (approved at G20 Summit) under **UNEP**.
  - Objective: Satellite & ground observation focusing on CH₄ emissions from **fossil fuels, agriculture, and waste**.
- **Global Methane Pledge (2021)**:
  - Launched by **UNEP** & EU/US at COP26 (Glasgow, 2021).
  - Target: **Reduce global CH₄ emissions by 30% below 2020 levels by 2030**.
  - **India's Stand**: **India has NOT joined**.
    - Reason: India's CH₄ emissions stem primarily from **agriculture (paddy) and dairy (bovines)**. Enforcing 30% cuts would restrict crop yields, jeopardize staple food security (rice), increase milk prices → risk adulterated milk & food inflation.
    - *Note*: IMEO still monitors India's emissions independently as a UN body.
- **Methane Alert and Response System (MARS) (2022)**:
  - Institution: **UNEP** under Global Methane Pledge (utilizes IMEO data).
  - Objective: High-resolution satellite detection of sudden major CH₄ plumes/leaks → Automated alerts sent to national governments (e.g. stubble burning events in Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan).
- **MethaneSAT (2024)**:
  - Developer: **Environmental Defense Fund (EDF)** (US NGO) + **Harvard University**.
  - Capability: Satellite orbiting Earth **15 times daily** to monitor oil & gas sector CH₄ emissions.
- **LOW-METHANE Initiative (Lowering Organic Waste Methane Initiative) (2023)**:
  - Launched at: **COP28 (Dubai, 2023)** under UN umbrella.
  - Objective: Reduce CH₄ emissions from **legacy waste dump sites, municipal solid waste, and organic waste**.

## Nitrous Oxide (N₂O)

- **Natural Sources**: Same as CO₂ & CH₄ → Biomass/organic matter breakdown, aerobic bacterial decomposition, volcanic eruptions, forest fires, **atmospheric lightning / thunder strikes** (nitrogen is intrinsic to organic matter).
- **Anthropogenic Sources**:
  - **Textile Industry**: Synthetic fiber production, specifically **Nylon**.
  - **Fertilizer Manufacturing Plants** (Nitrogenous fertilizers).
  - **Vehicular Emissions**, Mining operations, Wastewater treatment, Post-deforestation biomass burning.
- **Atmospheric Sink & Lifespan**: Naturally breaks down into atmospheric nitrogen (N₂). Long atmospheric residence time (**100–400 years**).

## Fluorinated Gases (F-Gases) – Man-Made GHGs

- **Categories**: **HFCs** (Hydrofluorocarbons), **PFCs** (Perfluorocarbons), **SF₆** (Sulfur Hexafluoride).
- **Key Characteristics**: **100% Synthetic / Man-made**, contain **Fluorine** atom(s).
- **Applications**:
  - **HFCs**: Refrigeration & Air Conditioning (HVAC), Aerosol propellant sprays, Organic solvents.
  - **PFCs**: Aerosol propellant sprays, Organic solvents, Semiconductor manufacturing.
  - **SF₆**: Circuit breakers (electrical switchgear / equipment), Semiconductor manufacturing (similar to NF₃).

### Colloid Science: Aerosols vs Hydrosols
- **Sol** = Colloidal solution / particles with diameter **1 nm to 1,000 nm** ($1\text{ nm} = 10^{-9}\text{ m} = \text{1 billionth of a meter}$).
- **Hydrosol Solution**: Colloidal particles suspended in **Water** → Examples: **Blood** (plasma, proteins, cell elements suspended in water), **Milk**.
- **Aerosol Solution**: Colloidal particles suspended in **Air** → Examples: Deodorants, perfumes, pesticide sprays, fire extinguishers, atmospheric air itself.
  - Spray Nozzle Mechanism: Liquid forced through nozzle → Atomized into $1\text{–}1000\text{ nm}$ droplets → Suspended in air as aerosol.
  - Role of F-Gases: HFCs/PFCs act as propellants and organic solvents in aerosol products.

## Global Greenhouse Gas Emission Breakdown (IPCC 2019/2020 Data)

- **Total Contribution by Gas**:
  - **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)**: **75%** of global GHG emissions.
    - *Fossil Fuel Industry*: **64%** of total global GHG emissions.
    - *Land Use / Agriculture / Deforestation / Land Degradation*: **11%** of total global GHG emissions.
  - **Methane (CH₄)**: **18%** of global GHG emissions.
  - **Nitrous Oxide (N₂O)**: **4%** of global GHG emissions.
  - **Fluorinated Gases (HFC, PFC, SF₆)**: **2%** of global GHG emissions.
- **Global Trend**: Aggregate GHG emissions increased from $<40\text{ Gt CO}_2\text{eq}$ to nearly **$60\text{ Gt CO}_2\text{eq}$**.

## Global Warming Potential (GWP)

- **Definition**: Relative metric comparing the heat-trapping capability of $1\text{ kg}$ of a specific GHG to $1\text{ kg}$ of **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)** over a specified time horizon (standard: **100 years**).
- **Baseline Benchmark**: **CO₂ = 1**.
- **Relative 100-Year GWP Values**:
  - **CO₂**: **1**
  - **CH₄**: **25 to 85** (Standard 100-yr value = **25**; short-term 20-yr value up to **85**).
  - **N₂O**: **350** ($1\text{ kg N}_2\text{O} = 350\text{ kg CO}_2$ heat equivalent).
  - **HFC**: **6,500**
  - **PFC**: **12,500**
  - **SF₆**: **21,000** ($1\text{ kg SF}_6 = 21,000\text{ kg CO}_2$ heat equivalent).
- **Atmospheric Lifespan Comparison**:
  - Natural GHGs (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O): ~100 years (N₂O up to 400 years).
  - Synthetic F-Gases: Up to **100× longer** (thousands of years; precise upper limits unknown).
- **Factors Determining GWP**:
  1. **Gas Concentration**: Higher atmospheric concentration → Higher net radiative impact.
  2. **Infrared Trapping Ability at Given Temperature**: Gases expand with heat → Trapping efficiency increases.
  3. **Prevailing Ambient Temperature**: Higher global baseline temp → Enhanced thermal absorption capability → Vicious climate feedback loop.

## Carbon Footprint & Calculation Methodology

- **Definition**: Total direct and indirect greenhouse gas emissions produced by a person, product, factory, industry, or nation, expressed in **Tons of CO₂ Equivalent (t CO₂e)**.
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\text{Carbon Footprint} = \sum \left(\text{Mass of } \text{GHG}_i \times \text{GWP}_i\right)$$
- **Correct Calculation Example**:
  - Factory output: $10\text{ t CO}_2$, $4\text{ t CH}_4$, $2\text{ t N}_2\text{O}$, $1\text{ t HFC}$, $1\text{ t SF}_6$.
  - *Incorrect method*: $10 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 18\text{ tons}$ (Ignores GWP differences).
  - *Correct Carbon Footprint Calculation*:
    - $\text{CO}_2$: $10\text{ t} \times 1 = 10\text{ t CO}_2\text{e}$
    - $\text{CH}_4$: $4\text{ t} \times 25 = 100\text{ t CO}_2\text{e}$
    - $\text{N}_2\text{O}$: $2\text{ t} \times 350 = 700\text{ t CO}_2\text{e}$
    - $\text{HFC}$: $1\text{ t} \times 6,500 = 6,500\text{ t CO}_2\text{e}$
    - $\text{SF}_6$: $1\text{ t} \times 21,000 = 21,000\text{ t CO}_2\text{e}$
    - **Total Carbon Footprint** = $10 + 100 + 700 + 6,500 + 21,000 = \mathbf{28,310\text{ t CO}_2\text{e}}$.

### Lifestyle & Product Supply Chain Carbon Anchors
- **Air Conditioning**: Electricity consumption → Coal thermal power ($70\%$ Indian grid) → High CO₂ footprint.
- **Tap Water**: Canal infrastructure (cement/steel) → Pumping from rivers (Yamuna/Ganga) → Water treatment → Booster pumps → High embodied carbon footprint.
- **Food (Bread)**: Urea fertilizer (manufactured via **Haber-Bosch process** using CH₄ natural gas) → Pesticide plants → Diesel tractors → Transport → Industrial baking → Non-biodegradable plastic packaging.
- **Imported vs Local Goods Case Study (Washington Apple vs Himalayan Queen Apple)**:
  - **Washington Apple (USA)**: Heavily subsidized US farming → Low market price BUT massive supply chain carbon footprint (transoceanic shipping, port transit, long-haul trucking).
  - **Himalayan Queen Apple (Himachal Pradesh)**: Cultivated in high Himalayas → Higher local price due to lower subsidies, BUT significantly lower transport carbon footprint to North India.
  - Environmental Imperative: **"Vocal for Local"** → Consuming locally produced goods minimizes transportation emissions.

## Fugitive Emissions

- **Definition**: Carbon/GHG emissions that occur across secondary supply chain stages (long-distance transport, pipeline leaks, processing transfers) that go unmeasured, unnoticed, or unaccounted for in standard product/service carbon footprint evaluations.
- **Example**: Diesel freight emissions transporting raw cotton from Maharashtra farms to Gujarat textile factories, and finished garments from Gujarat to Delhi retail markets.
- **Mitigation Mandate**: Enhance accounting precision to eliminate fugitive emissions → Transition to green steel, green cement, green transport, and localized production hubs.
