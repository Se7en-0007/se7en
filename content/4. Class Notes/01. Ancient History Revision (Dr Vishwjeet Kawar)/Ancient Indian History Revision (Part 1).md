## Basic Concepts: Age, Culture & Historic Periodization

### Concept of "Age"
- **Age** = Historical time period defined by the dominant material or technology driving human development.
- **Evolution Trajectory**: **Stone Age** (stone tools) → **Chalcolithic Age** (copper + stone) → **Bronze Age** (copper + tin alloy) → **Iron Age** (iron technology).

### Concept of "Culture"
- **Culture (General)** = Way of life of people in a specific area during a specific time period.
- **Culture (Archaeological Definition)** = Group of distinct objects found together within a specific region and time frame.
  - *Example*: **Harappan Culture** (2600–1900 BC, NW India) = Steatite seals + burned bricks + urban centers + underground drainage + standardized weight system.
- **Bases for Naming Cultures**:
  - **Material Used**: Bronze Age Culture
  - **Geography / Location**: Indus Valley Culture
  - **First Discovered Site**: Harappan Culture
  - **Pottery Style**: Northern Black Polished Ware (**NBPW**) Culture, Ochre Coloured Pottery (**OCP**) Culture

### Classification of Historical Periodization
- **Prehistory** (< 6th Century BC / 600 BC):
  - Definition = Period with **NO deciphered written records**.
  - Historical Reconstruction = Dependent purely on **archaeological / material remains**.
  - *Includes*: Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Chalcolithic, IVC, Vedic Period, Iron Age.
- **Protohistory** (3000 BC – 600 BC):
  - Definition = Written records exist but are **undeciphered** (e.g., **IVC** 2600–1900 BC) **OR** texts were composed orally during the period and written down much later after the 6th C. BC (e.g., **Vedic Period** 1500–600 BC).
- **Historic Period** (> 6th Century BC / 600 BC):
  - Definition = Period where written records are **available + written during that period + deciphered** (+ archaeological remains).

### Vedic Text Transmission
- **Vedas** = Composed by gods → Revealed to seers/sages (**Rishis**) → Transmitted generation to generation via **oral tradition** → Written down post-6th Century BC.

## Archaeology & Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)

### Excavation Methodologies
- **Vertical Excavation**:
  - Digging vertically down through stratigraphic layers.
  - Lowest layer = **Oldest / Earliest** | Uppermost layer = **Most recent**.
  - *Outcome*: Reveals **sequence and evolution of events over time**.
- **Horizontal Excavation**:
  - Excavating across a single horizontal layer/plane.
  - *Outcome*: Reveals **full spatial life and culture of people in one specific time period**.

### Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)
- **Primary Functions**:
  1. Archaeological Research.
  2. Protection of historical & cultural monuments.
- **Administrative Status**: Attached office under the **Ministry of Culture** (*NOT Ministry of Education*).
- **Historical Timeline**:
  - **1861**: Established under **Alexander Cunningham** (1st Director General).
  - **1866**: Abolished during the tenure of Governor-General **Lord Lawrence**.
  - **1871**: Revived under **Lord Mayo**; Alexander Cunningham re-appointed DG.
- **Key Director Generals (ASI)**:
  - **Alexander Cunningham** (1861–65, 1871–85): 1st DG, longest-serving DG overall, **"Father of Indian Archaeology"**. Emphasized written records for excavation.
  - **Sir John Marshall** (1902–1928): Emphasized horizontal excavation; officially announced discovery of **Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)** in **September 1924**.
  - **R.E.M. Wheeler** (1944–1948): Re-introduced vertical excavation and stratigraphy.
  - **Amalananda Ghosh** (1953–1968): Longest-serving DG in post-independence India. First to identify ceramic/pottery similarities between Early Harappan & Mature Harappan cultures → proved internal development of Harappan urbanism.

## Chronology & Technological Trajectory (Stone Age to 6th C. BC)

### Broad Chronological Timeline
- **Paleolithic Age**: 600,000 – 10,000 BC (Crude, large stone tools)
- **Mesolithic Age**: 10,000 – 4,000 BC (Microliths < 5 cm)
- **Neolithic Age**: 7,000 – 1,000 BC (Polished stone tools)
- **Chalcolithic Age**: 4,000 – 700 BC (Copper + Stone)
- **Indus Valley Civilization (Bronze Age)**: 2,600 – 1,900 BC (Bronze, 1st Urbanization)
- **Vedic Period**: 1,500 – 600 BC
  - **Early Vedic**: 1,500 – 1,000 BC
  - **Later Vedic**: 1,000 – 600 BC (Iron Age)

### Principles of Technological Trajectory & Co-existence
- **Non-Uniform Development**: Technological advancement is geographically uneven → Different ages **co-existed simultaneously** in different regions.
  - *Example (2600–1900 BC)*: NW India = **Bronze Age IVC** | Central & Western India = **Chalcolithic** | Other regions = **Neolithic**.

### Urbanization Milestones
- **First Urbanization** = **IVC** (2600–1900 BC, NW India) → Surplus food grain production → Cities/Towns → Disappeared post-1900 BC (reverted to rural).
- **Second Urbanization** = **6th Century BC** (600 BC, Gangetic Valley) → Surplus food grain production (iron plowshare) → Cities (**Mahajanapadas**) → Continued uninterrupted into modern times.
- *Demographic Fact*: Even during urban phases (IVC & modern era), **villages outnumbered towns**.

### Historical Diagnostic Rules & Cut-offs
- **Horses & Spoked Wheels**: Introduced **~1500 BC** with Vedic Aryans.
  - *Rule*: Before 1500 BC = **NO conclusive evidence of horse or spoked wheel** (IVC, Chalcolithic, Neolithic = NO).
- **Iron**: Introduced **~1000 BC** in Later Vedic period (*Krishna Ayas / Shyama Ayas*).
  - *Rule*: Before 1000 BC = **NO iron** (IVC, Chalcolithic, Early Vedic = NO).
- **Urban Centers**: Before 600 BC, present **ONLY during IVC** (2600–1900 BC).
  - *Rule*: Neolithic, Chalcolithic, Early Vedic, Later Vedic = **NO urban centers**.
- **Metal Coins**: Introduced **6th Century BC** (600 BC) as **Punch-Marked Coins** (silver/copper).
  - *Rule*: Before 6th C. BC = **Barter System** (NO metal money).

## Subsequent Political Chronology (600 BC – 7th C. CE)

### 600 BC – 200 BC (Mahajanapadas to Mauryas)
- **16 Mahajanapadas** (mentioned in Buddhist & Jaina texts).
- **Mid-6th C. BC Dominant 4**: **Vatsa**, **Avanti**, **Kosala**, **Magadha**.
- **Magadha Dynastic Sequence**: **Haryanka** → **Shishunaga** → **Nanda** → **Maurya** (**HASHNA MANAH**).
- **Nanda Dynasty**: Founded by **Mahapadma Nanda** (**"First Empire Builder in India"**) → Magadha became 1st Empire in ancient India.
- **Mauryan Empire** (321 – 185 BC): **1st Pan-India Empire** → Covered entire subcontinent except **deep South** (Cholas, Cheras, Pandyas) & **Northeast** (Brahmaputra Valley / Assam). Peak under **Ashoka** (d. ~232 BC).

### 200 BC – 300 CE (Post-Mauryan Period)
- **Northwest Political Vacuum** → Central Asian Invasions: **Indo-Greeks** → **Shakas** (5 branches; 4th branch = **Western Kshatrapas** in Gujarat/W. India 1st–4th C. CE) → **Kushans**.
- **Gangetic Valley / North India**: Last Mauryan king **Brihadratha** killed by **Pushyamitra Shunga** (185 BC) → **Shunga Dynasty** (~112 yrs) → **Kanva Dynasty** → Small regional families.
- **Deccan**: **Satavahanas** (200 BC – 300 CE).
- **Western India**: **Western Kshatrapas** (Shaka 4th branch).
- **Deep South**: **Cholas**, **Cheras**, **Pandyas** (Sangam Age, 3rd C. BC – 3rd C. CE).

### 300 CE – 600 CE (Gupta & Post-Gupta Period)
- **North India**: **Imperial Guptas** (mid-3rd C. – mid-6th C. CE).
- **Deccan**: **Vakatakas** (mid-3rd C. – early 6th C. CE; replaced Satavahanas).
- **Northeast (Assam/Brahmaputra Valley)**: **Kamarupa / Pragjyotisha Kingdom** under **Varman Dynasty** (mid-4th C. CE onwards; entry into historic period).
- **South India**: **Pallavas of Kanchi** (3rd/5th C. – 10th C. CE).

### Pre-Harsha Regional Dynasties (Mid-5th – Early 7th C. CE)
- **Maitrakas** of Vallabhi (Gujarat)
- **Maukharis** of Kannauj
- **Pushyabhutis / Vardhanas** of Thanesar (Haryana)
- **Later Guptas** of Magadha (distinct from Imperial Guptas)
- **Varmans** of Kamarupa (Assam)
- **Gaudas** of Bengal (Shashanka)
- **Harsha** (606 CE onwards) = Pushyabhuti ruler who unified North India.

## Stone Age: Paleolithic, Mesolithic & Neolithic

### Overview & Earliest Dates
- **India Stone Age Start**: **~600,000 years ago** (vs. Africa: ~2 million / 20 lakh years ago).
- **Bori Site (Maharashtra)**: Scientific date under research = **1.38 million years ago**.

### 1. Paleolithic Age (600,000 – 10,000 BC)
- **Geological Epoch**: **Pleistocene** (Ice Age).
- **Lifestyle**: Nomadic hunter-gatherers, living in rock-cut caves/shelters, wearing animal skins / tree bark (NO agriculture, NO cotton).
- **Sub-Divisions**:
  - **Lower Paleolithic**: Crude, largest core tools → **Handaxes, Cleavers, Choppers**.
  - **Middle Paleolithic**: Flake tools → **BPSC** (**Blades, Points, Scrapers**). Made via prepared core / **Levallois technique**.
  - **Upper Paleolithic**: **Parallel-sided blades** & **Burins** (screwdriver-like tools).
- **Key Paleolithic Sites**:
  - **Isampur** (Hunsgi Valley, Gulbarga dt., Karnataka) = **Largest Paleolithic site in India**, stone tool manufacturing center (Lower Paleolithic).
  - **Pallavaram** (near Madras, TN) = **1st Paleolithic tool discovered in India** (1863) by **Robert Bruce Foote** (**"Father of Indian Prehistory"**, GSI geologist).
  - **Attirampakkam** (Tamil Nadu).
  - **Bori** & **Chirki-Nevasa** (Maharashtra).
  - **Hiran Valley** (Gujarat).
  - **Didwana** (Rajasthan).
  - **Soan / Son River Valley** (Pakistan).
  - **Adamgarh Hills** & **Bhimbetka** (Madhya Pradesh).
  - **Belan Valley** (Uttar Pradesh).
  - **Kurnool**, **Nagarjunakonda**, **Renigunta** (Andhra Pradesh).
  - *Distribution Pattern*: Concentrated in **hilly slopes** (stone supply) & **river valleys** (water supply); absent/rare in alluvial plains.

### 2. Mesolithic Age (10,000 – 4,000 BC)
- **Epoch Transition**: Pleistocene → **Holocene** (~10,000 BC). Ice melted → Climate wet & warm → Dry & warm → Natural emergence of grain-bearing grasses & grass-eating animals.
- **Diagnostic Tools**: **Microliths** (small stone tools < 5 cm).
- **Lifestyle Shift**: Nomadic hunter-gatherers of Holocene + **Primitive cultivation** + **Animal domestication** (~5000 BC).
- **Earliest Animal Domestication Sites (~5000 BC)**: **Bagor** (Rajasthan - largest Mesolithic site) & **Adamgarh** (Madhya Pradesh).
- **Pottery Exceptions in Mesolithic**: **Langhnaj** (Gujarat, ~2500 BC context) & Belan Valley sites (**BMC**: **Baghai Khor**, **Morahana Pahar / Lekhahia**, **Chopani Mando**).
- **Key Mesolithic Sites**: **Bagor** (Rajasthan), **Langhnaj** (Gujarat), **Adamgarh** & **Bhimbetka** (MP), Gangetic Valley (**Sarai Nahar Rai**, **Damdama**, **Mahadaha** in UP), Belan Valley (**Chopani Mando**, **Baghai Khor**, **Morahana Pahar** in UP).

### 3. Neolithic Age (7,000 – 1,000 BC)
- **Diagnostic Tools**: **Polished stone tools**.
- **Lifestyle Shift**: **"First Farmers in India"** → Agriculture → Sedentary settled life → Mud-brick houses → Pottery production (storage/cooking) → Cotton weaving & clothing.
- **Coined Term**: **"Neolithic Revolution"** by **Gordon Childe** (Australian archaeologist).
- **Key Neolithic Sites & Findings**:
  - **Mehrgarh** (Balochistan, Pakistan): Earliest Neolithic site (~7000 BC). First farmers. Earliest evidence of **Wheat & Barley** cultivation (~6000 BC) and **Cotton** (~5000 BC). Mud-brick houses. **Goats buried with masters in graves**.
  - **Lahuradewa** (Sant Kabir Nagar dt., UP): Earliest evidence of **Rice cultivation** (overrides Mehrgarh/Koldihwa if listed in options).
  - **Burzahom** (near Srinagar, Kashmir): **Pit dwellings** (winter homes), absence of microliths, **Dogs buried with masters in graves**.
  - **Chirand** (Bihar): Antler **bone implements/tools**.
  - **Daojali Hading** (Brahmaputra Valley / Assam): **Jadeite stone tools** (imported from China).
  - **Koldihwa** & **Mahagara** (UP): Rice cultivation evidence.
  - **South Indian Sites**: **Utnur** (Telangana), **Muski**, **Hallur**, **Brahmagiri** (Karnataka), **Nagarjunakonda** (AP), **Paiyampalli** (Tamil Nadu).

## Chalcolithic Cultures & Sites (4,000 – 700 BC)

### Concept & General Features
- **Meaning**: Chalco (Copper) + Lithic (Stone). Culture based on **copper + stone**.
- **Material Dominance**: Stone objects predominated (copper was limited/new).
- **Geographic Distribution**: Semi-arid regions of Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Maharashtra; concentrated in hilly lands & river valleys.
- **Socio-Economic Limitations**:
  - **Settlements**: Rural background, subsistence agriculture (cultivated more cereals than Neolithic).
  - **Animal Domestication**: Animals slaughtered for meat; milk unused; **NO horses**.
  - **Metallurgy**: Copper + stone; **NO iron**; generally **NO bronze** (exceptions: low-grade bronze sheet at Ahar, bronze objects at Daimabad).
  - **Pottery**: Distinct painted ceramics, predominantly **Black and Red Ware (BRW)**.
  - **Housing**: Mud-brick houses (*exception*: burnt bricks at Gilund).
  - **Burial Practices**: Pots & copper objects buried with dead; NO iron. Orientation: **North-South** in Maharashtra/W. India; **East-West** in South India. High child mortality in W. Maharashtra (Jorwe culture) → low longevity.
  - **Social Inequality**: Rectangular chief/priest houses vs round huts for commoners.
  - **Religious Symbols**: Terracotta female figurines (**Mother Goddess**) & **Bull figurines**.
  - **Decline Factor**: Decreased rainfall / severe drought affecting agriculture.
  - **Relationship to Harappa**: Chronologically **post-Harappan** (most sites 1900–700 BC), but Technologically **pre-Harappan** (rural, backward).

### Regional Chalcolithic Cultures & Key Sites
1. **Ahar / Banas Culture (Rajasthan)**:
   - Sites: **Ahar**, **Balathal**, **Gilund** (Banas river valley, tributary of Chambal).
   - Features: High copper artifacts (near Khetri mines); low-grade bronze sheet at Ahar; **burnt bricks at Gilund** (exception).
2. **Kayatha & Malwa Cultures (Madhya Pradesh)**:
   - Kayatha Culture: **Kayatha**.
   - Malwa Culture: **Eran** (Bina river), **Malwa** & **Navdatoli** (Narmada river).
   - Navdatoli: Evidence of **almost all food grains** cultivated in Chalcolithic.
3. **Savalda & Jorwe Cultures (Maharashtra)**:
   - Savalda Culture: **Savalda** (Tapi river).
   - Jorwe Culture: Covered modern Maharashtra except Vidarbha & Konkan coast.
   - Sites: **Prakash** (Tapi river), **Nashik** (Godavari river), **Jorwe**, **Nevasa**, **Daimabad** (Pravara river, tributary of Godavari), **Inamgaon**, **Songaon**, **Chandoli** (Ghod/Bhima river, tributary of Krishna) (**ICS** = Inamgaon, Songaon, Chandoli).
   - **Daimabad**: Largest Jorwe site; bronze artifacts (man with chariot); almost reached urbanization.
   - **Inamgaon**: Large settlement, almost reached urbanization.
4. **Ganeshwar (Rajasthan)**:
   - Pre-Harappan Chalcolithic site (2800–2200 BC). Located near Khetri; **supplied copper to Harappans**; shares pottery similarities with **OCP** & Harappan culture.

## Indus Valley Civilization (Harappan Culture, 2,600 – 1,900 BC)

### General Characterization
- **Bronze Age Civilization** / **1st Urbanization in India**.
- **Geographic Extent**: >2,000 sites, >1.5 million sq km across India, Pakistan, Afghanistan.
- **Nomenclature Evolution**: Indus Valley Civilization → Indus Civilization → **Sindhu-Saraswati Civilization** (2/3rd sites in Saraswati basin).
- **Saraswati River**: Perennial Himalayan river draining into Rann of Kutch; dried up **~1900 BC**. Modern seasonal remnants = **Ghaggar** (India) & **Hakra** (Pakistan).

### Geographic Extremes
- **Northernmost**: **Shortughai** (Afghanistan) / **Manda** (Jammu, Chenab river).
- **Southernmost**: **Daimabad** (Maharashtra, Pravara river).
- **Easternmost**: **Alamgirpur** (UP, Hindon river).
- **Westernmost**: **Sutkagan-dor** (Pakistan, Dasht river).

### Site Distribution & Associated Rivers
- **Jammu**: **Manda** (Chenab river).
- **Punjab (India)**: **Ropar** (Sutlej river).
- **Punjab (Pakistan)**: **Harappa** (Ravi river - 1st discovered site), **Ganweriwala** (Hakra/Saraswati river).
- **Haryana**: **Rakhigarhi** (largest IVC site, 550 hectares), **Bhirrana** (oldest IVC site), **Banawali**, **Farmana** (all on Ghaggar/Saraswati river).
- **Rajasthan**: **Kalibangan** (Ghaggar/Saraswati river), **Karanpur**.
- **Sindh (Pakistan)**: **Mohenjo-daro** (Indus river - 2nd discovered site), **Chanhudaro** (Indus river).
- **Gujarat**: **Dholavira**, **Lothal**, **Surkotada**, **Desalpur**, **Nageshwar**, **Kuntasi**, **Prabhas Patan**, **Rojdi**, **Nagwada**, **Kirsara**, **Baksara**.
- **Uttar Pradesh**: **Alamgirpur**, **Hulas**.

### Multi-Phase Site Classification
- **Early + Mature + Late Harappan**: Harappa, Banawali, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi (**Happy Birthday Rahul** / **HBDR**).
- **Early + Mature Harappan**: Kalibangan (**KL**).
- **Mature + Late Harappan**: Lothal.

### Urban Planning & Architecture
- **Bipartite City Division**: Most cities divided into **Citadel** (Western, smaller, raised platform, public/ruling class) & **Lower Town** (Eastern, larger, residential).
- **Exceptions to Layout**:
  - **Chanhudaro**: City **WITHOUT citadel**.
  - **Dholavira**: Divided into **3 sections** (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town); stadium/open ground between Citadel & Middle Town; stone signboard with **10 large script signs**; water harvesting/reservoirs; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021).
- **Building Material**: Standardized **burnt bricks** in uniform ratio **1 : 2 : 4** (advancement over Egyptian dried bricks & Mesopotamian baked bricks).
- **Urban Infrastructure**: Grid street pattern (roads at right angles), underground drainage system, house courtyards & bathrooms.
  - **Kalibangan**: Wells in almost every individual house.
  - **Banawali**: Washbasins (toilet/wash setup).
- **Citadel Structures**:
  - **Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro)**: Rectangular tank, double flights of steps, floor made watertight with gypsum mortar, surrounding corridors & rooms, well in one room.
  - **Granaries**: Found at Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Kalibangan, Rakhigarhi. Mohenjo-daro granary = largest building in citadel. Harappa granary = 12 rooms arranged in 2 rows of 6 with central aisle.

### Agriculture, Economy & Trade
- **Agriculture**: **Surplus food grain production** (fertile soil, annual Indus floods, heavy vegetation). **Wooden plow used** (*NO iron plow*).
  - *Evidence*: **Kalibangan** = double-cropped/plowed field (2 sets of furrows at right angles, Early Harappan level); **Banawali & Cholistan** = terracotta plow models.
- **Irrigation**: **Shortughai** = canal traces; **Dholavira** = water reservoirs; **Balochistan/Afghanistan** = *gabarbands* & *nallas*.
- **Domestication**: Humped bull = most favored animal. **NO horses** (unconclusive/no evidence).
- **Trade**: Internal & External (Mesopotamia, Oman/Magan, Bahrain/Dilmun). Based on **Barter System** (*NO metal coins/money*).
- **Raw Material Provenance**:
  - **Copper**: Khetri (Rajasthan) & Oman (Magan) (nickel traces in copper).
  - **Lapis Lazuli**: Shortughai (Afghanistan) (blue stone).
  - **Carnelian**: Bharuch / Gujarat (red stone).
  - **Gold**: Karnataka & Afghanistan.
  - **Limestone**: Sukkur & Rohri Hills (Pakistan).
- **Mesopotamian Text Terms**: **Meluhha** = Harappan region | **Magan** = Oman | **Dilmun** = Bahrain.

### Script, Weights & Artifacts
- **Script**: Undeciphered, pictographic (375–400 signs), written Right to Left. **Boustrophedon method** (R to L then L to R on alternate lines) for longer inscriptions (longest = 26 signs). Dholavira signboard = 10 signs.
- **Weights**: Precise system made of **Chert stone**. Lower denominations = binary (multiples of 2); higher = decimal (multiples of 10).
- **Seals**: Made of **Steatite stone**, square/rectangular. Most common motif = **Unicorn**.
- **Seal Animals**:
  - *Represented*: Unicorn, Humped Bull, Buffalo, Elephant, Tiger, Rhino, Deer, Crocodile, Snake.
  - *NOT Represented*: **HOLIKA** (**Horse, Lion, Camel, Cow**).
- **Pashupati Seal (Mohenjo-daro)**: Male figure in yogic posture with 3 faces & 2 horns (Proto-Shiva). Surrounded by **Buffalo & Rhino** (left), **Elephant & Tiger** (right), **2 Deer/Antelopes** at feet.
- **Bronze Statues**: Dancing Girl (Mohenjo-daro), Man with Chariot (Daimabad).
- **Stone Statues**: Steatite Priest-King (Mohenjo-daro), Red Jasper Male Torso (Harappa).
- **Terracotta**: Mother Goddess, toy carts (used by common people, crude/unsophisticated).
- **Religion**: Idol worship (Pashupati, Mother Goddess), Fire Altars (**Lothal, Kalibangan, Banawali, Rakhigarhi**).
- **Decline**: Disappearance of urban features & uniformity post-1900 BC. Main causes: **Climate change & drying up of Saraswati River**. (*No Aryan invasion*).

## Vedic Period & Vedic Literature (1,500 – 600 BC)

### Aryans & Early Migration
- **Identity**: Pastoralists, speakers of Indo-European / Indo-Aryan languages.
- **Origin & Migration**: Russian steppes (between S. Russia & Central Asia) → Migrated in small waves (2000–1500 BC) to NW India.
- **Rigveda Composition**: ~1500 BC in **Sapta-Sindhu** region.
- **Parallel Iranian Text**: **Avesta** composed in Iran (~1400 BC) in Avestan language (shares linguistic & cultural similarities with Rigveda).
- **Primary Animal**: Horse (*Ashwa* mentioned 215 times in Rigveda).
- **Encountered Population Groups**:
  - **Dasa**: Early branch of Aryans (mentioned in Avesta), cattle keepers, soft treatment by Aryans.
  - **Dasyu**: Indigenous original inhabitants, non-cattle keepers, phallus worshippers, hostile treatment (*Dasyu-hatya* = killing of Dasyus).
- **Battle of Ten Kings (Dasharajna Yuddha)**: Fought between 10 tribes on the banks of **Parushni (Ravi)** river.

### Geography & Vedic Rivers
- **Sapta-Sindhu (7 Rivers)**: Indus + 5 tributaries + Saraswati.
- **River Names**:
  - Jhelum = **Vitasta**
  - Chenab = **Askini**
  - Ravi = **Parushni**
  - Beas = **Vipasha**
  - Sutlej = **Shutudri**
  - Saraswati = **Naditama / Devitama / Matetama**
- **Ganga & Yamuna**: Mentioned **only ONCE** in Rigveda.
- **Geographic Migration**: Early Vedic = NW India / Sapta-Sindhu → Later Vedic = Western UP (~1000 BC) → Eastern UP & Bihar (~600 BC).

### Comparison

| Feature | Early Vedic Period (1500–1000 BC) | Later Vedic Period (1000–600 BC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Occupation** | Pastoralism (cattle rearing). Agriculture = secondary. | Agriculture (expanded via clearing forests with iron). Pastoralism = secondary. |
| **Form of Wealth** | Cattle (Cow / *Gau* mentioned 176 times). Land was NOT property. | Land / Territory (*Janapada* became well-established property). |
| **Metals Used** | Copper / Bronze (*Ayas*). NO Iron. Spoke wheels introduced. | Iron (*Krishna Ayas / Shyama Ayas*) used for clearing forests. Wooden plowshare used. |
| **Agriculture Type** | Subsistence (wooden plow). | Subsistence (expanded land, introduction of Rice = *Vrihi*). |
| **Trade & Currency** | Barter system. NO metal money. Land/Territory insignificant. | Barter system. NO metal money. Land/Territory became vital. |
| **Political Unit** | *Jana* (Tribe/People - mentioned 275 times). *Janapada* mentioned ZERO times. | *Janapada* (Territory / Kingdom). Allegiance shifted from Jana to Janapada. |
| **Rajan (King) Status**| Chief of *Jana*. Post NOT hereditary. Power restricted by assemblies. | King of *Janapada*. Post became **HEREDITARY** & powerful. |
| **Tribal Assemblies** | *Sabha*, *Samiti*, *Vidata*, *Gana* were powerful. *Samiti* elected Rajan. | Assemblies weakened. *Sabha* & *Samiti* became advisory. *Vidata* disappeared. |
| **Taxation & Army** | Voluntary tribute (*Bali*). NO regular tax, NO standing army, NO formal justice system. | *Bali* became mandatory/compulsory. Revenue officers (*Bhagadugha* = collector, *Sangarahitri* = treasurer). NO standing army. |
| **Social Structure** | Flexible, based on occupation. 2 main classes initially (Brahmana, Kshatriya). | Rigid, based on **BIRTH**. 4 Varnas (*Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra*). |
| **Varna Roles & Tax** | No Shudras/Vaishyas initially. Spoils of war divided amongst 3 groups (*Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vis*). | *Vaishya* = ONLY tax/tribute payers. *Shudra* = served 3 varnas, disabled from Veda, rituals, assemblies, Upanayana. |
| **Upanayana & Dvijas**| Open to male members. | Restricted to male members of *Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya* (*Dvija* / twice-born). Excluded *Shudras* & women. |
| **Condition of Women**| Better. No child marriage. Veda study allowed. Chose husband. Assembly participation (*Sabha*). Remarriage allowed. | Deteriorated. Child marriage common. Unwelcome birth. Excluded from Vedas, assemblies, rituals, Upanayana. (*NO Sati in either period*). |
| **Religious Focus** | Prayers dominant. Natural forces personified (Indra, Agni, Varuna, Soma). | Rituals & Sacrifices dominant (animal slaughter). New deities (Prajapati, Vishnu, Rudra, Pushan = god of Shudras). |
| **Idol Worship & Temples** | NO temples, NO idol worship. | NO temples. Primitive symbolic worship began at end. |

### Vedic Literature Breakdown

#### Classification: Shruti vs. Smriti
- **Shruti ("That which is heard")**: Divine origin (*Apaurusheya* - not man-made), revealed to seers, transmitted orally. Includes all **4 Vedas** (*Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka, Upanishad*).
- **Smriti ("That which is remembered")**: Human origin. Includes Epics (**Ramayana, Mahabharata** - final compilation in Gupta period), **Puranas** (18 major puranas compiled in Gupta & post-Gupta periods), **Dharmashastras**.
- **Chronological Sequence**: Vedas (Vedic period) → Epics (finalized in Gupta period) → Puranas (Gupta & post-Gupta).

#### Four Vedas
1. **Rigveda (Rigveda Samhita)**:
   - Composed in Early Vedic period (Sapta-Sindhu).
   - **1028 hymns (suktas)** divided into **10 Mandalas (Books)**.
   - **Mandalas 2 to 7** = Oldest, "Family Books" (composed in Early Vedic period).
   - **Mandalas 1, 8, 9, 10** = Composed later in Later Vedic period.
   - **Mandala 9** = Dedicated entirely to **Soma** (god of plants/intoxicating drink).
   - **Mandala 10** contains:
     - **Nasadya Sukta**: Origin of the universe.
     - **Purusha Sukta**: Origin of 4 Varnas (*Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra*) from Cosmic Purusha. (*Shudra mentioned in Rigveda 10th Mandala, but as a Later Vedic addition*).
   - **Primary Gods**: Indra (1st, 250 hymns), Agni (2nd, 200 hymns), Varuna (3rd, ocean/water), Soma (4th, plants).
   - **Goddesses**: **USA** (**Usha** = dawn/wealth, **Sinivali** = children, **Aditi** = freedom/health).
1. **Yajurveda**: "Ritual Veda". Rules, procedures and formulas for priests during sacrifices. (Later Vedic text).
2. **Samaveda**: Shortest Veda. Hymns set to musical tunes for chanting. **Origin of Indian music**. (Later Vedic text).
3. **Atharvaveda**: Latest Veda. Charms, magic spells and prayers to avert disease and evil spirits. **Origin of Ayurveda**. (Later Vedic text).

#### Upanishads ("Vedanta")
- Philosophical texts composed at the end of the Vedic period in Eastern UP / Bihar (Panchala, Videha).
- Criticized ritualism & animal sacrifices; emphasized **Gyana** (Knowledge of *Atman* / soul & *Brahman* / universal soul) for attaining **Moksha** / salvation.
- **Key Philosophical Parables**:
  - **Katha Upanishad**: Story of **Yama & Nachiketa**.
  - **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad**: Story of **Gargi & Yajnavalkya**.
  - **Chandogya Upanishad**: Story of **Uddalaka Aruni & Shvetaketu**.
