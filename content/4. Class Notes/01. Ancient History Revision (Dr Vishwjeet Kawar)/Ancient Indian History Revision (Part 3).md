## Post-Mauryan Period: Invasions, Native Dynasties & Trade (200 BC – 300 CE)

### Foreign Invasions In Northwest India

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Se7en-0007/images/refs/heads/main/Foreign%20Invasions.png)

#### 1. Indo-Greeks (Bactrian Greeks)
- **Political Impact**: First foreign rulers to establish kingdoms in NW India (*Capitals: Sakala / Sialkot & Takshashila*). Famous ruler: **Menander I (Milinda)**.
- **Religious Conversion**: Menander converted to Buddhism under **Nagasena** → Recorded in Pali text **Milindapanho** (*"Questions of Milinda"*).
- **Numismatic Breakthroughs**:
  - **First to issue inscribed coins** in India (*with names and portraits of kings*).
  - **First to issue Gold coins** in India (*issued rarely/limited initially*).
  - **Agathocles Coins** (discovered at Ai Khanoum, Afghanistan): Contains the **first known visual depiction of Hindu deities**: **Balarama** (obverse) and **Vasudeva Krishna** (reverse).

#### 2. Shakas (Scythians)
- **Expansion**: Replaced Indo-Greeks. Divided into **5 branches** across India & Afghanistan.
- **4th Branch (Western Kshatrapas)**: Ruled Western India (Gujarat/Malwa) from 1st C. CE to 4th C. CE (*defeated by Chandragupta II*).
- **Rudradaman I** (130–150 CE):
  - Famous Western Kshatrapa ruler. Great patron of Sanskrit.
  - Issued the **Junagadh / Girnar Rock Inscription** (dated 72 Shaka Era = **150 CE**): **First long inscription in pure classical Sanskrit** in India.
  - *Sudarshana Lake Record*: Inscription records that **Sudarshana Lake** was originally constructed under **Chandragupta Maurya** by provincial governor **Pushyagupta**, repaired under Ashoka (Tushaspha) and repaired without forced labor (*Vishti*) by **Rudradaman I**.
- **Era Origin**: **Vikram Samvat / Era** started in **57–58 BC** when King of Ujjain (**Vikramaditya**) defeated Shakas (*57–58 years ahead of Gregorian calendar*).

#### 3. Kushanas (Yuezhi / Tocharian Central Asian Tribe)
- **Prominent King**: **Kanishka I** (c. 78 CE onwards).
- **Shaka Era Origin**: Kanishka started the **Shaka Era** in **78 CE** upon his accession (*78 years behind Gregorian calendar; adopted as India's National Calendar on March 22, 1957*).
- **Numismatic Achievements**:
  - Issued the **largest number of gold coins** for the first time in pre-Gupta India.
  - Gold coins possessed the **highest degree of metallic purity** in ancient India.
  - **First image of Lord Buddha** (*labeled BODDO in Greek script*) appeared on Kushan coins under Kanishka.
- **Rabatak Inscription** (Afghanistan): Gives genealogy of Kushan kings (*Kujula Kadphises → Vima Taktu → Vima Kadphises → Kanishka*).
- **Cultural & Religious Contributions**:
  - Patronized **Mahayana Buddhism**. Convened **4th Buddhist Council** in Kashmir.
  - Court Scholars: **Ashvaghosha** (playwright/biographer), **Charaka** (physician), **Vasumitra** (philosopher), **Nagarjuna** (philosopher).

### Native Dynasties & Regional Kingdoms

#### 1. Shunga Dynasty (185 BC – 73 BC, Gangetic Valley)
- **Origin**: Founded by **Pushyamitra Shunga** (*commander-in-chief of Mauryan army*) after killing last Mauryan king **Brihadratha** in 185 BC.
- **Brahmanical Revival**: Performed **Ashvamedha** & **Rajasuya** sacrifices (*revived Vedic ritualism*).
- **Literary Works**:
  - **Malavikagnimitram**: Sanskrit play by **Kalidasa** depicting love story of Shunga prince **Agnimitra** & Malavika.
  - **Patanjali**: Court priest/grammarian of Pushyamitra Shunga. Authored **Mahabhasha** (*commentary on Panini's Ashtadhyayi*) & **Yogasutra**. Mentioned Shunga Ashvamedha Yajna & *Yavan* (Indo-Greek) invasions.
- **Art & Architecture**: Renovated and added stone railings/toranas to **Bharhut Stupa** and **Sanchi Stupa**.
- **Besnagar / Vidisha Pillar Inscription** (MP):
  - Erected by **Heliodorus** (Greek ambassador sent by Indo-Greek king Antialcidas to Shunga court of King Bhagabhadra).
  - Inscribed in **Prakrit language** & Brahmi script. Dedicated to **Vasudeva Krishna** (*Garuda pillar*). Heliodorus calls himself a *Bhagavata* (Vishnu devotee) and states 3 steps to heaven: **Control, Attention, Generosity** (*Mnemonic: CAG*).

#### 2. Chedi / Mahameghavahana Dynasty (Kalinga / Coastal Odisha)
- **Key Ruler**: **King Kharavela** (3rd ruler of dynasty, 1st C. BC). Devout follower of **Jainism**.
- **Hatigumpha Inscription** (Udayagiri Caves near Bhubaneswar):
  - Undated Prakrit inscription giving a year-by-year account of Kharavela's first 13 years of reign.
  - Records his military campaigns, repair of Kalinga capital, excavation of caves for Jaina monks at **Udayagiri-Khandagiri**.

#### 3. Tribal Republics (Gana-Sanghas of Post-Mauryan Era)
- **Yaudheyas**: Ruled Punjab & Haryana. Issued copper coins dedicated to Karttikeya.
- **Malavas**: Ruled Rajasthan.

#### 4. Satavahana Dynasty (200 BC – 300 CE, Deccan & Andhra)
- **Geographic Extent**: Modern Maharashtra andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, parts of Gujarat/MP.
- **Capital**: **Pratishthana / Paithan** (Maharashtra); later shifted to **Dhanyakataka / Amaravati** (Andhra Pradesh).
- **Rulers & Inscriptions**:
  - **Simuka**: Founder of dynasty.
  - **Satakarni I**: 3rd ruler. Recorded in **Naneghat Inscription** (issued by his widow **Queen Naganika**). Performed Ashvamedha & Rajasuya sacrifices. Gives the **earliest inscriptional evidence of land grants** (*Agrahara*) with tax exemptions to Brahmanas.
  - **King Hala** (17th ruler): Authored **Gathasaptasati** (*Sattasai*) in **Maharashtri Prakrit** (*700 erotic/romantic poems*).
  - **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (c. 106–130 CE): Greatest Satavahana king. Recorded in **Nashik Prashasti Inscription** (issued by his mother **Gautami Balashri**). Defeated Western Kshatrapa ruler **Nahapana**. Called *Ekabrahmana* (peerless Brahmana) and *Khatiya-dapa-manamada* (destroyer of Kshatriya pride).
  - **Vashishthiputra Pulumavi**: Expanded empire into Andhra region. Repaired **Amaravati Stupa**. Contemporary of **Rudradaman I** (who defeated him twice but spared him due to marriage alliance).
  - **Yajnashri Satakarni**: Issued coins featuring **ship motifs / navigation symbols**, indicating flourishing overseas maritime trade.
- **Socio-Cultural Features**:
  - **Metronymics**: Kings took names from mothers (*Gautamiputra, Vashishthiputra*), but society was strictly **patrilineal and patriarchal**.
  - **Religion**: Followed Brahmanism (worshiped Vasudeva, Indra, Surya), but patronized Buddhism (*excavated rock-cut Chaityas & Viharas at Karle, Bhaja, Kanheri, Bedsa; donated land to Buddhist Sangha*).
  - **Coinage**: Issued coins of **Lead**, **Potin** (alloy), Copper and Bronze (*NO Gold coins*).

### Six Orthodox Schools of Indian Philosophy (Shaddarshana)

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Se7en-0007/images/refs/heads/main/6%20Philosophies.png)

#### Medieval Vedanta Sub-Schools & Reformers
1. **Advaita Vedanta** (Non-Dualism): Authored by **Adi Shankaracharya** (9th C. CE). *Atman* and *Brahman* are identical. World is *Maya* (illusion). Path to Moksha = **Jnana** (Knowledge).
2. **Vishishtadvaita** (Qualified Non-Dualism): Authored by **Ramanujacharya** (12th C. CE). *Atman* is part of *Brahman* but distinct. Path to Moksha = **Bhakti** (Devotion).
3. **Dvaita** (Dualism): Authored by **Madhvacharya** (13th C. CE). *Atman* and *Brahman* are completely separate entities. Path to Moksha = **Bhakti & Meditation**.

## The Gupta Empire: Classical Age (mid-3rd C. – mid-6th C. CE)

### Dynastic Trajectory & Imperial Rulers

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Se7en-0007/images/refs/heads/main/Guptas.png)

#### 1. Sri Gupta & Ghatotkacha (c. 275 – 319 CE)
- Founders of dynasty in UP/Bihar. Feudatories of Kushanas. Assumed modest title *Maharaja*.

#### 2. Chandragupta I (319 – 335 CE)
- First powerful independent Gupta emperor. Assumed title **Maharajadhiraja**.
- Started the **Gupta Era** in **319–320 CE**.
- Married Lichchhavi princess **Kumaradevi** → issued Gold coins featuring joint portraits (*King-and-Queen type*).

#### 3. Samudragupta (335 – 380 CE)
- Called **"Napoleon of India"** by V.A. Smith due to unbroken military conquests. Called himself **Lichchhaviduhitra** (*grandson of Lichchhavis*).
- **Prayaga Prashasti / Allahabad Pillar Inscription**:
  - Composed by court poet **Harishena** in classical Sanskrit (**Champu Kavyastyle** - prose + verse).
  - Categorizes Samudragupta's 4-fold expansion policies:
    1. **Aryavarta (North India)**: Defeated 9 Naga kings → **Direct Annexation**.
    2. **Dakshinapatha (South India)**: Defeated 12 Southern kings (*including Vishnugopa of Kanchi*) → **Grahanamokshanugraha Policy** (*conquered, liberated and restored kingdoms upon payment of tribute*).
    3. **Atavika / Forest Kingdoms & Border States** (*Kamarupa, Nepala, Samatata, Republics*): Reduced to tributary vassals. Destroyed tribal republics (*Yaudheyas, Malavas*).
    4. **Outlying Foreign Rulers** (*Shakas, Kushanas, Ceylon*): Submitted, sent envoys, offered daughters in marriage. King Meghavarna of Ceylon sent envoy to seek permission to build monastery at Bodh Gaya.
  - Harishena compares Samudragupta to gods **Kubera, Varuna, Indra, Yama** (*Mnemonic: KVIY*).
- **Numismatics**: Issued 6 types of gold coins (*Archer, Battle-axe, Tiger-slayer, Kacha, Ashvamedha, Veena-player*).

#### 4. Chandragupta II "Vikramaditya" (380 – 415 CE)
- Defeated last Western Kshatrapa Shaka ruler **Rudrasimha III** → annexed Gujarat/Malwa → assumed title **Vikramaditya** & **Shakari** (*slayer of Shakas*).
- **Mehrauli Iron Pillar Inscription** (Delhi): Commemorates King Chandra's victories across 7 tributaries of Indus; celebrated for rust-resistant metallurgy. Dedicated to Lord Vishnu.
- **Matrimonial Alliances**: Married daughter **Prabhavatigupta** to Vakataka king **Rudrasena II** → exercised indirect control over Deccan during her regency.
- **Nine Gems (Navaratnas) at Ujjain Court**:
  1. **Kalidasa** (Dramatist/Poet).
  2. **Amarasimha** (Lexicographer → *Amarakosha*).
  3. **Varahamihira** (Astronomer/Mathematician).
  4. **Dhanvantari** (Physician).
  5. **Vetalabhatta** (Magician).
  6. **Ghatakarpara** (Sculptor/Architect).
  7. **Kshapanaka** (Astrologer).
  8. **Shanku** (Architect).
  9. **Vararuchi** (Grammarian).
- **Faxian / Fa-Hien Visit**: Chinese Buddhist pilgrim visited India (400–411 CE). Recorded peaceful society, low crime, mild punishments, land of Brahmanism, neglect of Kapilavastu/Kushinagar.

#### 5. Kumaragupta I (415 – 455 CE)
- Founded the **Nalanda Mahavihara (Nalanda University)** in Bihar (*center of Mahayana learning*).
- Assumed title *Mahendraditya*. Performed Ashvamedha Yajna.

#### 6. Skandagupta (455 – 467 CE)
- Last major Gupta emperor. Repelled the first major invasion of **Hunas** (Central Asian nomads).
- Records Huna victory in **Bhitari Pillar Inscription** (Ghazipur, UP) and **Junagadh Inscription** (Gujarat).

### Administration, Economy & Social Life

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Se7en-0007/images/refs/heads/main/Gupta%20Adm.png)

- **Decentralized Administration**: Feudatory system (*Samanta system*) predominated. Kings granted land to religious beneficiaries (**Agrahara grants**) with judicial & administrative immunity.
- **Revenue**: Land tax (*Bhaga*) raised to **1/4th to 1/6th** of produce. Mandatory forced labor (**Vishti**) imposed on villagers for royal troops.
- **Numismatics**:
  - **Dinara**: Gold coins issued in **largest numbers** in ancient India (*though metallic purity was lower than Kushan gold coins*).
  - **Rupaka**: Silver coins issued post-Shaka conquest.
- **Social Structure & Status**:
  - **Brahmanas**: Prestige reached peak due to land grants (*Agrahara*).
  - **Shudras**: Status **IMPROVED**; allowed to listen to Epics (Ramayana, Mahabharata) & Puranas, worship Krishna/Shiva and perform domestic rituals. Functioned as agriculturalists.
  - **Untouchables (Chandalas)**: Increased numbers; lived outside settlements; struck wood blocks when entering cities to warn upper castes (recorded by Faxian).
  - **Condition of Women**: Increased patriarchal subordination, early marriage. **First epigraphic evidence of Sati** appears in **510 CE Eran Stone Inscription** (Bhanugupta's reign - *Goparaja's wife performed Sati*).

### Golden Age Achievements: Science, Literature & Art

#### 1. Mathematics & Astronomy
- **Aryabhata I** (Gupta Period, 5th C. CE):
  - Texts: **Aryabhatiya** & **Surya Siddhanta**.
  - Discoveries: Earth rotates on its axis causing day & night; scientific explanation for solar & lunar eclipses; calculated value of Pi ($\pi pprox 3.1416$); calculated earth's circumference; length of solar year = 365.25 days.
- **Varahamihira** (Gupta Period, 6th C. CE):
  - Texts: **Panchasiddhantika** (5 astronomical systems) & **Brihat Samhita** (encyclopedia of architecture, flora, fauna, weather).
- **Brahmagupta** (Post-Gupta, 7th C. CE):
  - Texts: **Brahmasphutasiddhanta** & **Khandakhadyaka**.
  - Discoveries: Formulated rules for operating with **zero**, positive & negative numbers; earliest systematic work on **Algebra**; solved quadratic equations.
- **Mahaviracharya** (9th C. CE, Rashtrakuta Court):
  - Text: **Ganitasarasamgraha**. **First Indian mathematician to separate Mathematics from Astronomy**.
- **Bhaskaracharya II** (12th C. CE):
  - Text: **Siddhantashiromani** (4 parts: **Lilavati** (arithmetic), **Bijaganita** (algebra), **Grahaganita** (planetary math), **Goladhyaya** (spheres)).

#### 2. Medicine
- **Vagbhata** (Post-Gupta): Authored **Ashtangasamgraha** & **Ashtangahridaya**.
- **Ancient Indian Medical Trio (Brihattrayi)**: **Charaka** (Charaka Samhita), **Sushruta** (Sushruta Samhita - surgery/rhinoplasty), **Vagbhata**.

#### 3. Literature
- **Kalidasa**:
  - Plays: **Abhijnanashakuntalam**, **Malavikagnimitram**, **Vikramorvashiyam**.
  - Epics/Poems: **Raghuvamsha**, **Kumarasambhava**, **Meghaduta**, **Ritusamhara**.
- **Epics & Puranas**: Final compilation of **Ramayana**, **Mahabharata** (*including Bhagavad Gita*) and major **18 Puranas** took place during the Gupta Period.

#### 4. Art & Architecture
- **Beginnings of Free-Standing Hindu Temples**: **Dashavatara Temple** at Deogarh (UP - *earliest Panchayatana temple with a Shikhara*), Bhitargaon Brick Temple (UP), Nachna Kuthara (MP).
- **Rock-Cut Caves**:
  - **Ajanta Caves** (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, MH): 30 Buddhist caves (6 Satavahana phase + 24 Gupta/Vakataka phase). Famous for Fresco paintings (*Fresco-secco technique: Dying Princess, Bodhisattva Padmapani*).
  - **Bagh Caves** (Dhar, MP): 9 Buddhist rock-cut caves on Baghani river.
  - **Udayagiri Caves** (Vidisha, MP): 20 Hindu/Jaina caves; features famous **Varaha Avatar** (Boar incarnation) relief of Vishnu.

## Post-Gupta Period & Harshavardhana (mid-6th C. – 7th C. CE)

### Political Disintegration & Regional Dynasties
- **Maitrakas of Vallabhi** (Gujarat): Center of Hinayana Buddhist learning.
- **Maukharis of Kannauj** (UP): Held central Gangetic Valley.
- **Pushyabhutis / Vardhanas of Thanesar** (Haryana): Founded by Pushyabhuti.
- **Later Guptas of Magadha**: Distinct from Imperial Guptas.
- **Varman Dynasty of Kamarupa** (Assam): Ruled Brahmaputra Valley (*King Bhaskaravarman*).
- **Gaudas of Bengal**: Ruled by King **Shashanka** (*anti-Buddhist; cut Bodhi tree*).

### Harshavardhana (606 – 647 CE)
- **Ascension**: Unified Thanesar & Kannauj; made **Kannauj** his capital. Era started in **606 CE** (**Harsha Era**).
- **Southern Expedition Defeat**: Harsha attempted to expand south into Deccan → **Defeated on the banks of Narmada River** by Western Chalukya king **Pulakeshin II** (c. 618–619 CE). Recorded in **Aihole Inscription** (authored by court poet **Ravikirti**).
- **Literary Works**: Authored 3 Sanskrit plays: **Nagananda**, **Priyadarsika**, **Ratnavali**.
- **Court Poet**: **Banabhatta** authored **Harshacharita** (biography) & **Kadambari**.
- **Xuanzang / Hiuen Tsang Visit**: Visited India during Harsha's reign (630–645 CE). Attended **Kannauj Assembly** and **Prayag Assembly** (Panchavarsha Parishad). Studied at **Nalanda University** under **Silabhadra**.

## Chronology of Early South Indian Kingdoms (6th – 14th C. CE)

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Se7en-0007/images/refs/heads/main/Deep%20South.png)

### 1. Deccan Dynastic Sequence

#### A. Chalukyas of Badami / Vatapi (mid-6th C. – mid-8th C. CE)
- **Capital**: Badami / Vatapi (Bagalkot dt., Karnataka). Founder: **Pulakeshin I**.
- **Pulakeshin II** (610–642 CE): Greatest king. Defeated Harsha on Narmada River; defeated Pallava king Mahendravarman I. Established **Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi** branch under brother Vishnuvardhana.
- **Defeat & Death**: Defeated and killed by Pallava king **Narasimhavarman I Mahamalla** (642 CE) → Narasimhavarman assumed title **Vatapikonda** (*conqueror of Vatapi*).
- **Record**: **Aihole Prashasti Inscription** (written by **Ravikirti** in Sanskrit).

#### B. Rashtrakutas of Manyakheta (mid-8th C. – mid-10th C. CE)
- **Capital**: Manyakheta / Malkhed (Gulbarga dt., Karnataka).
- **Founder**: **Dantidurga** (753 CE) after defeating last Badami Chalukya king **Kirtivarman II**. Performed *Hiranyagarbha* ritual at Ujjain.
- **Krishna I**: Constructed rock-cut monolithic **Kailashnath Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora**.
- **Amoghavarsha I** (814–878 CE): Devout Jaina. Authored **Kavirajamarga** (*earliest Kannada work on poetics*) & **Prashnottara Ratnamalika**.
- **Sub-dynasty Replaced**: Overthrown in 973 CE by **Tailapa II**, founding Western Chalukyas of Kalyani.

#### C. Western Chalukyas of Kalyani (10th C. – 12th C. CE)
- **Capital**: Kalyani (Bidar dt., Karnataka). Founded by **Tailapa II** (973 CE) after defeating Rashtrakuta Karka II.
- **Vikramaditya VI**: Court scholar **Vijnaneshwar** authored **Mitakshara**.

---

### 2. Deep South Dynastic Sequence (Tamil Country)

#### A. Pallavas of Kanchi (4th C. – early 10th C. CE)
- **Capital**: Kanchipuram (Tamil Nadu). Reached zenith under **Simhavishnu** (6th C.).
- **Mahendravarman I** (600–630 CE): Authored satirical play **Mattavilasa Prahasana** (*Farce of Drunken Sport*). Converted from Jainism to Shaivism by saint **Appar**.
- **Narasimhavarman I "Mahamalla"** (630–668 CE): Defeated Pulakeshin II → took title **Vatapikonda**. Built monolithic **Ratha Temples (Pancha Rathas)** and **Shore Temple** at **Mamallapuram / Mahabalipuram**.
- **End**: Last Pallava king **Aparajita** defeated in 897 CE by Chola king **Aditya I**.

#### B. Imperial Cholas of Tanjore (mid-9th C. – 13th C. CE)
- **Capital**: Tanjore / Thanjavur; later **Gangaikondacholapuram**.
- **Founder**: **Vijayalaya** (850 CE, feudatory of Pallavas).
- **Aditya I**: Defeated Aparajita Pallava → annexed Kanchi.
- **Raja Raja Chola I** (985–1014 CE): Built **Brihadisvara Temple** at Thanjavur (1010 CE). Conquered Northern Sri Lanka & Maldives.
- **Rajendra Chola I** (1014–1044 CE):
  - Conquered entire Sri Lanka. Conducted naval expedition against **Srivijaya Empire** (SE Asia).
  - Marched to River Ganga → assumed title **Gangaikondachola** (*conqueror of Ganga*) → built new capital **Gangaikondacholapuram**.
- **Local Self-Government**: Famous for autonomous village administration (**Sabha / Mahasabha**) recorded in **Uttiramerur Inscriptions** of Parantaka I (*detailed committee system: Samvatsara, Eri, Thotta variyams*).
- **End**: Last Chola king **Rajendra III** defeated in 1279 CE by Pandya king **Maravarman Kulasekara I**.

#### C. Later Pandyas of Madurai (13th C. – 14th C. CE)
- **Capital**: Madurai. Overthrew Cholas; continued until Delhi Sultanate invasions (*Malik Kafur, 1311 CE*).

---

### 3. Late Medieval Regional Successors (12th C. – 14th C. CE)
- **Yadavas of Devagiri**: Ruled Maharashtra (*Capital: Devagiri / Daulatabad*).
- **Hoysalas of Dvarasamudra**: Ruled Karnataka (*Capital: Halebidu*).
- **Kakatiyas of Warangal**: Ruled Andhra/Telangana (*Capital: Warangal*). Built Ramappa Temple.
