* **Colonial Transformation** = Indian economy restructured to serve British imperial interests.
* **Economic Share Collapse**:
  * **Early 18th Century** ⇒ India accounted for **~23%** of world economy.
  * **1947 (Independence)** ⇒ India’s share plummeted to **~3%**.
* **Earlier Invaders vs. British** ⇒ Earlier invaders settled in India without making structural changes or draining wealth as tribute.

## Deindustrialisation - Ruin Of Artisans And Handicraftsmen

### One-Way Free Trade
* **Charter Act of 1813** ⇒ Permitted **one-way free trade** for British merchants.
* **Impact of Imports** ⇒ Cheap, machine-made British textiles flooded the Indian market.
* **Export Barriers** ⇒ British tariffs of **nearly 80%** imposed on Indian textiles.
* **1820 Transition** ⇒ European markets virtually closed to Indian textiles; India transformed from **net exporter to net importer**.
* **Steel Rail Network** ⇒ Accelerated the penetration of European goods into remote village markets.

### No Steps towards Modern Industrialisation
* **Livelihood Loss** ⇒ Destructive deindustrialisation occurred without corresponding modern industrial growth (unlike European models).
* **Loss of Patrons** ⇒ Indian princes and nobility lost power, shifting tastes to Western values and abandoning local artisans.

### Ruralisation
* **Artisan Displacement** ⇒ Faced with low wages and forced low-price sales (Bengal EIC rules), artisans abandoned crafts.
* **Pressure on Land** ⇒ Displaced artisans moved to villages and became agriculturists, causing an **overburdened agricultural sector** and village economic collapse.

## Impoverishment Of Peasantry

* **Maximisation of Rents** ⇒ Chief interest of British land revenue policy.
* **Permanent Settlement ( Bengal )** ⇒ Enforced transferability of land, stripping tenants of traditional rights.
* **Summary Evictions & Illegal Dues** ⇒ Executed by zamindars alongside forced labor (**'begar'**) to maximize profit without reinvesting.
* **Peasant Triple Burden** ⇒ Subjugation under the **Government** (revenue demand) + **Zamindar** (rent) + **Moneylender** (exorbitant interest).
* **Moneylender Supremacy** ⇒ Acted as grain-merchants, bought crops cheaply, and manipulated colonial judiciary and laws.
* **Outcome** ⇒ Peasants became landless, heavily indebted, and perished during famines; true across Zamindari, Ryotwari, and Mahalwari systems.

---

## Emergence Of Intermediaries, Absentee Landlordism, Ruin Of Old Zamindars

* **1815 Land Transition** ⇒ **Half of Bengal's total land** passed to new urban-based merchants and moneylenders.
* **Absentee Landlordism** ⇒ Urban buyers had no benevolent or traditional ties to tenants, focusing solely on rent extraction.
* **Subinfeudation** ⇒ Proliferation of rent-collecting intermediaries increased the financial burden on cultivators.
* **Alliance with Imperialism** ⇒ New zamindars' survival depended on the perpetuation of British rule → consistently opposed the national movement.

## Stagnation And Deterioration Of Agriculture

* **Low Productivity Factors**:
  * Cultivators lacked both means and incentive to invest.
  * Zamindars lacked rural roots and interest in development.
  * Government spent negligibly on agricultural, technical, or mass education.
  * **Fragmentation of land** due to subinfeudation blocked modern technological applications.

## Famine And Poverty

* **Nature of Famines** ⇒ Not mere foodgrain scarcity, but a direct consequence of colonial-engineered poverty.
* **Death Toll (1850–1900)** ⇒ **About 2.8 crore people** died in recurring famines.

## Commercialisation Of Indian Agriculture

* **Definition** ⇒ Agriculture shifted from "way of life" to business enterprise; crops grown for national/international sale rather than local consumption.
* **Commercial Crops** ⇒ Cotton, jute, groundnuts, oilseeds, sugarcane, tobacco (more remunerative than foodgrains).
* **Plantation Sector** ⇒ Tea, coffee, rubber, indigo; heavily dominated by European capital, producing for global markets.
* **Stimulants to Commercialisation**:
  * Spread of money economy.
  * Competition and contract replacing custom and tradition.
  * Emergence of a unified national market and internal trade.
  * Rail-road communications and entry of British finance capital.
* **Impact on Peasantry**:
  * **Forced Process** ⇒ Peasants lacked investable surplus and lived at bare subsistence levels.
  * **Market Vulnerability** ⇒ Cultivators exposed to international price shocks.
  * **1860s Cotton Boom & 1866 Slump** ⇒ Slump brought massive indebtedness, land alienation, and triggered the **Deccan Riots of the 1870s**.

## Destruction Of Industry And Late Development Of Modern Industry

* **Deindustrialisation Cases**:
  * **Textiles** ⇒ British paid for Indian textiles using Bengal revenues instead of pounds, impoverishing local weavers.
  * **Ship-building Industry** ⇒ Westerly (Surat, Malabar) and Easterly (Bengal, Masulipatnam) yards crushed:
    * **1813 British Law** ⇒ Prohibited ships below **350 tonnes** from Indo-British trade routes.
    * **1814 British Law** ⇒ Indian-built ships denied "British-registered vessel" status, blocking trade with America and Europe.
  * **Steel Industry** ⇒ Growth blocked; **Tatas** forced to produce high-standard steel for British railroads, locking them out of the high-volume, lower-grade domestic steel market.
* **Indian Intermediaries** ⇒ Indian traders, bankers, and moneylenders functioned as junior partners to British capital:
  * Moneylenders funded revenue collections.
  * Traders distributed imported goods and collected raw materials.
  * Situation retarded development of a healthy, independent industrial bourgeoisie.
* **Late Industrial Growth (Second Half of 19th Century)**:
  * **1853** ⇒ First cotton textile mill in Bombay by **Cowasjee Nanabhoy**.
  * **1855** ⇒ First jute mill in Rishra, Bengal.
  * **Foreign Ownership** ⇒ Managing agencies dominated; foreign capital rushed in due to high profits, cheap labor, raw materials, ready markets, and administrative support.
  * **Indian-Owned Industries** ⇒ Cotton/jute (19th c.), sugar/cement (20th c.).
  * **Handicaps** ⇒ Underdeveloped credit, no tariff protection, unequal foreign competition, and soundly backed British rivals.
  * **Lopsided Pattern** ⇒ Neglected core/heavy industry and power generation; favored specific regions (created regional disparities, slowing nation-building).
  * **Manpower Deficit** ⇒ Lack of technical education caused shortages of technical personnel.

## Nationalist Critique Of Colonial Economy

* **Early Intellectuals' Shift** ⇒ Shifted from 1850s belief that British rule would modernize India to disillusionment by the 1860s.
* **Foremost Economists**:
  * **Dadabhai Naoroji** (The Grand Old Man of India) ⇒ Wrote *Poverty and UnBritish Rule in India*, formulated the **theory of economic drain**.
  * **Mahadev Govind Ranade**, **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**, **G. Subramania Iyer**, **Prithwishchandra Ray**, and **Romesh Chandra Dutt** (*The Economic History of India*).
* **Core Critique** ⇒ Essence of colonialism lay in transforming India into raw-material supplier, finished-goods importer, and a safe harbor for British capital.
* **National Goal** ⇒ Demanded complete severance of economic subservience and development of an independent economy through local industrialization.

### Economic Drain
* **Definition** ⇒ Portion of India's national product transferred to Britain for political reasons, without any material/economic return.
* **Key Components of Drain**:
  * Salaries and pensions of civil and military officials.
  * Interests on public loans taken by the Indian Government from abroad.
  * Profits on foreign investments.
  * Civil and military stores purchased in Britain.
  * Payments for British shipping, banking, and insurance (stunting local service sectors).
* **Economic Consequences**:
  * Retarded domestic capital formation; accelerated British industrial development.
  * Squeezed wealth re-entered India as finance capital, compounding the drain.

### British Policies Making India Poor
* **National Issue** ⇒ Poverty identified as man-made, explainable, and removable.
* **Capital Nationalism** ⇒ True industrialization must rest on **Indian, not foreign capital**.
* **Foreign Capital Dangers** ⇒ Suppresses local enterprise, drains profits, causes political subjugation, and creates powerful pro-imperial lobbies.

### Growth of Trade and Railways to Help Britain
* **Unfavourable Trade** ⇒ Relegated India to exporting raw materials and importing manufactures.
* **Railways Critique** ⇒ Not coordinated with industrial needs; sparked a commercial, not industrial revolution.
* **Subsidy Formulation (G.V. Joshi)** ⇒ "Expenditure on railways should be seen as an Indian subsidy to British industries".

### One-Way Free Trade and Tariff Policy
* **Ruination of Handicrafts** ⇒ Unfairly exposed traditional industries to premature competition.
* **Regressive Taxation** ⇒ Heavily taxed the poor (salt tax, land revenue) while sparing bureaucrats and British capitalists.
* **Nationalist Demands** ⇒ Reduction of land revenue, abolition of salt tax, imposition of income tax, and excise duties on luxuries.

### Effect of Economic Drain
* **Drain Scale (Contemporary Estimates)** ⇒ Exceeded total land revenue, or equaled half of total government revenue, or comprised **one-third of total savings** (**~8% of national product**).
* **Political Clarity** ⇒ Concept easily understood by peasants, serving as a powerful anti-colonial rallying point.

### Economic Issue A Stimulant To National Unrest
* **Undermining Hegemony** ⇒ Exposed the myth of the "benevolent moral foundation" of British rule.
* **1875–1905 (Moderate Phase)** ⇒ Served as the primary stimulant for national consciousness.
* **Political Demand Evolution** ⇒ Progressed from purse control/political sharing to **self-rule (Swadeshi/Swaraj)** in the first decade of the 20th century.

## Stages Of Colonialism In India

* **Historical Evolution** ⇒ Changing British positions in the global economy altered colonial patterns (formulated by Marxist historians like **Rajni Palme Dutt**).

### First Stage
* **Name & Period** ⇒ **Period of Merchant Capital (Mercantilism)** / Monopoly Trade and Direct Appropriation (1757–1813).
* **Primary Objectives**:
  * Monopolise trade with India against European and Indian rivals.
  * Direct appropriation of state revenues through control of state power.
* **Characteristics**:
  * **No basic changes** introduced in administration, judiciary, transport, communication, education, culture, social structures, or methods of production.
  * Top-level revenue collection and military organizations reformed solely for efficiency.
  * Large-scale drain of wealth (**2–3% of Britain’s national income**) financed the British Industrial Revolution.
  * India remained a net exporter of textiles; weavers were economically coerced and ruined.

### Second Stage
* **Name & Period** ⇒ **Colonialism of Free Trade** (1813–1860s); initiated by the Charter Act of 1813.
* **Ideological Shift** ⇒ British industrial capitalists wanted India to serve as a market for manufactured goods (textiles) and an exporter of raw materials (cotton, foodgrains).
* **Dominant Features**:
  * **Free Trade Integration** ⇒ Indian import tariffs abolished or reduced to nominal rates.
  * **Foreign Capital Entry** ⇒ Free entry allowed for British capitalists in plantations, mining, shipping, and trade with active state support.
  * **Agrarian Overhaul** ⇒ Permanent Settlement and Ryotwari introduced to capitalize agrarian structures.
  * **Comprehensive Administration** ⇒ Penetrated remote villages to deliver imports and extract agricultural raw materials.
  * **Legal Overhaul** ⇒ Criminal, contract, and procedural laws modernized to promote capitalist commercial relations.
  * **Modern Education** ⇒ Introduced to provide cheap administrative clerks, foster modernization, and build loyalty to the Crown.
  * **Burden Increase** ⇒ Peasant taxes rose steeply to fund expensive administrative and military networks.
  * **Trade Absorption** ⇒ India absorbed **10–12% of British exports** and ~20% of its textile exports.
  * **Wartime Deployment** ⇒ Indian Army deployed for colonial expansion in Asia and Africa.

### Third Stage
* **Name & Period** ⇒ **Era of Foreign Investments and International Competition for Colonies** (1860s onwards).
* **Global Context**:
  * British industrial supremacy challenged by Europe, USA, and Japan.
  * Intensified industrialization via electricity and petroleum.
  * Unified global markets through transport revolutions.
* **Dominant Features**:
  * **Reactionary Administration** ⇒ Consolidation of control under viceroyalties of **Lytton, Dufferin, Lansdowne, and Curzon**.
  * **Export of Capital** ⇒ Heavy British capital invested in Indian railways, government loans, trade, plantations, coal, jute, and banking.
  * **Trusteeship Ideology** ⇒ Self-government training ideas abandoned; replaced by permanent **"trusteeship"** over the "permanently immature" Indian populace.
  * **Racist Justification** ⇒ Geography, race, climate, and religion cited to declare Indians unfit for democracy—framing colonial exploitation as the **"White Man’s burden"**.
