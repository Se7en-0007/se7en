## Under Company Rule
* **EIC Policy (First 60 years)** ⇒ Complete neglect of education (EIC was a purely trading and profit-making concern).
* **Individual Exceptions**:
  * **Warren Hastings (1781)** ⇒ Established **Calcutta Madrasah** for study of Muslim law and related subjects.
  * **Jonathan Duncan (1791)** ⇒ Established **Sanskrit College** at Benaras for study of Hindu law and philosophy.
  * **Wellesley (1800)** ⇒ Set up **Fort William College** for training of civil servants in Indian languages and customs (closed in 1802).
* **Primary Objective** ⇒ Ensure regular supply of qualified Indians to help in administration of law in EIC courts; utility in correspondence with Indian states.
* **Pressure for Western Education**:
  * **Enlightened Indians** ⇒ Modern, secular, Western education seen as the only remedy for social, economic, and political ills.
  * **Christian Missionaries** ⇒ Expected modern education to destroy faith in native religions and promote conversion to Christianity.
  * **Serampore Missionaries** ⇒ Prominent, enthusiastic advocates for the spread of education.

## A Humble Beginning by Charter Act of 1813
* **Charter Act of 1813** ⇒ First legislative recognition of principle to encourage learned Indians and promote modern sciences.
  * **Financial Mandate** ⇒ EIC directed to sanction **Rs 1 Lakh annually** (withheld until 1823 due to controversy over direction of expenditure).
* **Calcutta College (1817)** ⇒ Set up by educated Bengalis (including **Raja Rammohan Roy**) to impart Western humanities and sciences.
* **Government Action** ⇒ Established three Sanskrit colleges at Calcutta, Delhi, and Agra.

---

## Orientalist-Anglicist Controversy
* **The Conflict (General Committee on Public Instruction)**:
  * **Anglicists** ⇒ Government education funds should be spent exclusively on modern Western studies.
  * **Orientalists** ⇒ Traditional Indian learning should remain the primary focus, while Western sciences and literature are taught to prepare students for employment.
* **Medium of Instruction Dispute (Among Anglicists)**:
  * One faction supported **English** as the medium.
  * Another faction supported **Indian languages (vernaculars)** as the medium.

---

## Lord Macaulay’s Minute (1835)
* **The Resolution** ⇒ Lord Macaulay's famous minute settled the Anglicist-Orientalist dispute in favor of the **English-medium Anglicists**.
* **Core Provision** ⇒ Limited government resources to be devoted exclusively to teaching Western sciences and literature through the medium of **English language** alone.
* **Macaulay’s Premise** ⇒ Declared "Indian learning was inferior to European learning" (which held true for contemporary physical and social sciences).
* **Impact of Policy**:
  * English made the official medium of instruction in schools and colleges.
  * Opening of a few English-medium schools/colleges instead of a wide network of elementary schools, leading to **neglect of mass education**.
* **Downward Filtration Theory**:
  * **Concept** ⇒ Educate a small section of upper and middle classes to create a class "**Indian in blood and colour but English in tastes, in opinions, in morals and in intellect**".
  * **Function** ⇒ Educate elite to act as interpreters between the British government and masses, and enrich vernaculars to filter Western knowledge down.
  * **Nationalist Outcome** ⇒ Ideas filtered down to masses, but not in the format rulers desired; instead, they spread via political parties, press, pamphlets, and public platforms. Modern education stimulated nationalists' social analysis of colonialism.

## Efforts of Thomson
* **James Thomson (Lt. Governor NW Provinces, 1843–53)** ⇒ Developed a comprehensive scheme of village education through the medium of vernacular languages.
* **Curriculum** ⇒ Taught practical subjects like mensuration and agricultural sciences.
* **Objective** ⇒ Train personnel for the newly set up Revenue and Public Works Departments.

## Wood’s Despatch (1854)
* **Charles Wood Despatch** ⇒ Considered the **"Magna Carta of English Education in India"** (the first comprehensive plan for the spread of education).
* **Key Provisions**:
  * **Mass Education** ⇒ Asked the Government of India to assume direct responsibility for mass education, on paper repudiating the "downward filtration theory".
  * **Systematic Hierarchy**:
    * Bottom: Vernacular primary schools in villages.
    * Middle: Anglo-Vernacular High Schools and affiliated colleges at district levels.
    * Top: Affiliating universities in presidency towns (**Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras**).
  * **Medium of Instruction** ⇒ **English** for higher university studies; **vernaculars** at the school level.
  * **Social & Vocational Focus** ⇒ Emphasized female education, vocational training, and teachers' training.
  * **Secularism** ⇒ Education imparted in government institutions to be secular.
  * **Grants-in-Aid** ⇒ System of grants-in-aid introduced to encourage private Indian enterprise.
* **Developments & Outcomes**:
  * **1857** ⇒ Universities set up at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras.
  * Departments of Education set up in all provinces.
  * **Bethune School (Calcutta, 1849)** ⇒ Founded by **J.E.D. Bethune** (President of Council of Education), launching the modern female education movement. Girls' schools brought under grants-in-aid and inspection.
  * **Agriculture Institute** started at Pusa (Bihar).
  * **Engineering Institute** started at Roorkee.
  * Ideas dominated for five decades, leading to rapid Westernization of education run by European headmasters/principals.

## Hunter Education Commission (1882–83)
* **Background** ⇒ Transfer of education to provinces in 1870 led to primary and secondary education suffering due to provincial resource shortages.
* **Commission Mandate** ⇒ Appointed under **W.W. Hunter** (1882) to review progress since the Despatch of 1854, confining recommendations primarily to primary and secondary education.
* **Key Recommendations**:
  * **Primary Education** ⇒ Urged state's special care for primary education; must be imparted through **vernacular languages**.
  * **Decentralisation** ⇒ Recommended transfer of control of primary education to newly set up **district and municipal boards**.
  * **Secondary Education Divisions**:
    * **Literary** ⇒ Academic stream leading to university entrance.
    * **Vocational** ⇒ Practical stream for commercial and professional careers.
  * **Female Education** ⇒ Urged expansion of inadequate facilities, especially outside presidency towns.
* **Impact** ⇒ Rapid growth of secondary and collegiate education with Indian private participation; establishment of **Punjab University (1882)** and **Allahabad University (1887)**.

## Indian Universities Act, 1904
* **Context** ⇒ Turn of 20th century political unrest. Official view: private management deteriorated education quality, turning institutions into "factories producing political revolutionaries". Nationalists accused government of ignoring illiteracy.
* **Raleigh Commission (1902)** ⇒ Appointed to review conditions and prospects of universities (barred from primary/secondary review).
* **Key Provisions of 1904 Act**:
  * **Study & Research** ⇒ Universities directed to prioritize active study and research.
  * **Fellows** ⇒ Number and tenure of university fellows reduced; most fellows to be nominated by the government.
  * **Government Veto** ⇒ Government acquired veto power over university senate regulations (could amend or pass its own regulations).
  * **Affiliation** ⇒ Imposed stricter conditions for affiliation of private colleges.
  * **Funding** ⇒ **Rs 5 Lakh per annum for 5 years** sanctioned for improvement of higher education.
* **Critique** ⇒ Curzon justified state control in the name of quality and efficiency; actually aimed to restrict education and discipline educated classes toward loyalty. Gokhale labeled it a **"retrograde measure"**.

---

## Government Resolution on Education Policy (1913)
* **Background** ⇒ Progressive state of **Baroda** introduced compulsory primary education throughout its territories in 1906.
* **Gokhale's Resolution (1910)** ⇒ Imperial Legislative Council motion for compulsory primary education (withdrawn upon govt. considerations).
* **Gokhale's Bill (March 1911)** ⇒ Free and compulsory primary education bill rejected by a select committee.
* **Provisions of 1913 Resolution**:
  * Government refused the responsibility of compulsory primary education.
  * Accepted policy of **removal of illiteracy**; urged provincial governments to provide free elementary education to poorer and backward classes.
  * Encouraged private efforts; improved quality of secondary schools.
  * Decided to establish a university in each province to encourage teaching activities.
  * **Women's Education** ⇒ Curriculum of practical utility for girls; reduced exam emphasis; increased female teachers and inspectors.

---

## Sadler University Commission (1917–19)
* **The Commission** ⇒ Calcutta University Commission appointed under **Michael Sadler** to study Calcutta University; recommendations applied widely.
* **Observations & Recommendations**:
  * **Precondition** ⇒ Improvement of secondary education is a necessary precondition for improving university standards.
  * **12-Year School Course** ⇒ Students to enter university after intermediate stage (rather than matric) for a **3-year degree course**.
    * Purpose: Prepare students for university level, relieve universities of below-standard students, and provide collegiate vocational training.
  * **Administrative Boards** ⇒ Separate board of secondary and intermediate education to control secondary school systems.
  * **Autonomy** ⇒ Centralized, unitary, residential-teaching autonomous universities preferred over scattered, affiliated colleges; less rigidity in framing regulations.
  * **Expansion** ⇒ Focus on female education, applied scientific/technological training, and teachers' training.
* **Impact**:
  * Seven new universities set up between 1916 and 1921: **Mysore, Patna, Benaras, Aligarh, Dacca, Lucknow, and Osmani** (later **Delhi, Agra, Annamalai**).
  * Expansion of teaching and residential universities, replacing purely examining and degree-conferring colleges.
  * Creation of **Academic Council** for curriculum development, exams, and research.
  * **Inter University Board (1925)** established to coordinate across Indian universities.
  * Focus on student welfare with dedicated boards.

## Education Under Dyarchy
* **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms** ⇒ Education shifted to **provincial ministries** (transferred subjects).
* **Impact**:
  * Direct central government interest and liberal funding (since 1902) stopped.
  * Financial difficulties prevented massive expansion, but education grew due to philanthropic and private Indian initiatives.

---

## Hartog Committee (1929)
* **The Commission** ⇒ Appointed by the Simon Commission as an auxiliary committee under **Phillip Hartog** to address deteriorating education standards caused by rapid expansion.
* **Key Recommendations**:
  * **Primary Education**:
    * Identified severe shortcomings, wastage, and stagnation.
    * **Wastage** ⇒ Withdrawal of children before completing literacy (due to poverty, caste, religion, seasonal diseases).
    * **Stagnation** ⇒ Detention of children in the same class for more than one year (due to single-teacher schools, low teacher training).
    * **Solutions** ⇒ Government must hold direct control and inspection of primary schools; standardise primary education to **four years**; align curriculum with local conditions; increase school inspection; use primary schools as community centers.
  * **Secondary School Education**:
    * Noted highly examination-oriented nature with high matriculation failure rates.
    * Recommended **diversified curriculum** with commercial and industrial subjects to match student aptitude.
  * **Higher Education**:
    * Criticized low standards, university overcrowding, and poor libraries.
    * Recommended establishing affiliated colleges along with unitary residential universities; introducing honours courses; and admitting students strictly on ability/aptitude.
  * **Women's Education** ⇒ Equal importance to education of boys and girls; more primary schools for girls; home science, music, hygiene in secondary schools; training of women teachers.

## Sargent Plan of Education (1944)
* **The Plan** ⇒ Prepared by **Sir John Sargent** (Educational Advisor to the Government of India) for post-WWII development.
* **Objective** ⇒ Universal literacy in India within **40 years**.
* **Key Recommendations**:
  * **Pre-Primary** ⇒ Free education for ages 3 to 6 to provide social experience; female teachers.
  * **Primary/Basic** ⇒ Universal, compulsory, free education for **6 to 14 years age group** divided into Junior Basic (6-11) and Senior Basic (11-14). Principle: **"learning through activity"** and basic crafts.
  * **High School** ⇒ 6-year course for selected students (above-average ability) from age 11. Two streams: **Academic** (Arts and pure sciences) and **Technical/Vocational** (applied sciences, industrial, commercial). Mother tongue as medium; English compulsory second language.
  * **University** ⇒ 3-year degree course after higher secondary; intermediate course abolished; stricter admissions based on capability; set up an all-India organization on the lines of the **University Grants Committee** of England.
  * **Others** ⇒ Liquidate adult illiteracy in 20 years; medical check-ups in schools; special education for physical/mental handicapped; setting up employment bureaus.
* **Critique** ⇒ Indian educationists rejected the 40-year target as too long, implementation costs as exorbitant, and the English model as unsuited for local conditions.

## Wardha Scheme of Basic Education (1937)
* **Origin** ⇒ All India Education Conference (October 1937) at Wardha; **Zakir Hussain Committee** formulated detailed national scheme (**Nai Talim**) based on Gandhi's articles in the weekly *Harijan*.
* **Philosophy** ⇒ Western education created a gulf between the elite few and masses. Principle: **"learning through activity"**.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * Free and compulsory education for all from ages **7 to 14**.
  * Medium of Instruction ⇒ **Mother tongue** (Hindi taught in areas where it was not the mother tongue).
  * **Basic Handicraft** ⇒ Syllabus built around a basic craft (spinning, weaving, carpentry, agriculture, pottery, leatherwork, home science for girls) to teach social and scientific implications.
  * Hindi, mathematics, general science, social studies, painting, music, and physical education integrated into curriculum.
  * **No religious and moral education** included.
  * Schools to establish active contact with surrounding communities through service.
* **Fate** ⇒ Blocked by the outbreak of World War II and the subsequent resignation of Congress ministries in October 1939.

## Development Of Vernacular Education
* **Chronological Milestones**:
  * **1835, 1836, 1838** ⇒ **William Adam’s reports** exposed severe defects in Bengal and Bihar vernacular education.
  * **1843–53** ⇒ **James Thomason’s experiments** in NW Provinces set up model schools (*tehsildari*) and teacher training schools.
  * **1853** ⇒ **Lord Dalhousie’s minute** strongly advocated for vernacular mass education.
  * **1854** ⇒ **Wood’s Despatch** provided for improving standards, government supervision, and normal training schools.
  * **1854–71** ⇒ Vernacular schools increased more than five-fold.
  * **1882** ⇒ **Hunter Commission** urged special state efforts for vernacular-medium mass education.
  * **1904** ⇒ Education policy emphasized vernacular education and increased grants.
  * **1929** ⇒ **Hartog Committee** highlighted gloomy, stagnated primary education picture.
  * **1937** ⇒ Received strong encouragement under provincial **Congress ministries**.

---

## Development Of Technical Education
* **Engineering Milestones**:
  * **1847** ⇒ **Engineering College at Roorkee** set up (initially open only to Europeans and Eurasians).
  * **1856** ⇒ **Calcutta College of Engineering** set up.
  * **1858** ⇒ Poona Overseers' School raised to **Poona College of Engineering** (affiliated to Bombay University).
  * **Guindy College of Engineering** affiliated to Madras University.
* **Medical Milestones** ⇒ Started with establishment of **Calcutta Medical College (1835)**.
* **Curzon's Role** ⇒ Broadened professional courses (medicine, agriculture, veterinary, engineering); established **Pusa Agriculture College** (acted as parent institution).

## Evaluation Of British Policy On Education
1. **Underlying Motivations**:
   * Guided by colonial administrative and economic concerns, not philanthropy.
   * Agitations by missionaries and Indian reformers forced state action.
   * English-medium promoted to ensure a cheap supply of educated Indians for subordinate posts in administration and British businesses.
   * Expected educated Indians to expand the market for British manufactured goods.
   * Expected Western education to reconcile Indians to British rule by glorifying conquerors.
2. **Decline of Traditional System** ⇒ Traditional Indian learning decayed from lack of support, accelerated by the **1844** directive declaring English mandatory for public employment.
3. **Mass Neglect & Illiteracy** ⇒ Deepened linguistic and cultural gulf between educated elite and masses. Illiteracy was widespread (**84% in 1911**, **92% in 1921**).
4. **Class Monopoly** ⇒ High costs turned modern education into a monopoly of the upper and richer classes in urban areas.
5. **Neglect of Women's Education** ⇒ Avoided to prevent backlash from orthodox Indian sections; had no immediate administrative utility for colonial rule.
6. **Neglect of Science and Technology** ⇒ Severely neglected; by 1857, only 3 medical colleges (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras) and 1 engineering college (Roorkee, exclusive to Europeans) existed.
