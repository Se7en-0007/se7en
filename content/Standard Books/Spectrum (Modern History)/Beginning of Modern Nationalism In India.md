## Factors In The Growth Of Modern Nationalism
*   **Nationalism Growth** = Mix of Indian response to British stimulus (new institutions, opportunities, resources) + reaction to colonial policies.
*   **Core Growth Factors**:
    *   **French Revolution** → Worldwide upsurge of concepts of **nationalism** & **self-determination**.
    *   **Indian Renaissance**.
    *   **British Modernisation** (infrastructure, administration) → Unintended offshoot of nationalist awakening.
    *   **Reactionary Policies** of British imperialists.

---

## Understanding of Contradictions in Indian and Colonial Interests
*   **Colonial Rule** = Major cause of India's economic backwardness.
*   **Conflict of Interests**: Interests of all Indian classes (**peasants**, **artisans**, **workers**, **intellectuals**, **educated**, **capitalists**) $\leftrightarrow$ Interests of British colonial rulers.
*   **Nationalist Movement** = Consequence of these inherent contradictions.

---

## Political, Administrative and Economic Unification of The Country
*   **Direct vs. Indirect Rule**: Provinces under **direct** British rule; Princely States under **indirect** rule → **Political unity** imposed by British power.
*   **Administrative Unification**: Unified **professional civil service** + **unified judiciary** + codified **civil & criminal laws** → Cultural unity upgraded to political unity.
*   **Transport & Communication Development** (Railways, roads, electricity, telegraph) ← Driven by British administrative convenience, military defense, and commercial economic penetration.
*   **Unification → Nationalist Impact**:
    *   **Economic Linkage**: Regional economic fates linked (e.g., crop failure in one region → affected prices & supply in another).
    *   **Physical Connectivity**: Allowed leaders & people from different regions to meet, exchange ideas, and mobilize public opinion.

---

## Western Thought and Education
*   **Modern Education** → Opportunities to assimilate **Western liberal and radical ideas** (conceived by rulers for administrative self-interest but boomeranged).
*   **Philosophical Influence**: European thinkers (**Milton**, **Shelley**, **John Stuart Mill**, **Rousseau**, **Paine**, **Spencer**, **Voltaire**) → Infused **rational**, **secular**, **democratic**, and **nationalist** ideas.
*   **English Language**: Served as a common medium of communication across diverse linguistic regions.
*   **Middle-class Intelligentsia** (lawyers, doctors, journalists, teachers) → Studied in England → Compared free British institutions with denial of basic rights in India → Formed the **nucleus of political unrest** & provided leadership.

---

## Role of Press and Literature
*   **Press Growth**: Unprecedented rise of Indian-owned English & vernacular newspapers in 2nd half of 19th century (despite colonial restrictions).
*   **1877 Scale**: **169 vernacular newspapers**; combined circulation $\approx$ **1,00,000**.
*   **Dual Function**: Criticized official policies + urged people to unite → Spread ideas of **self-government**, **democracy**, **civil rights**, and **industrialisation**.
*   **Exchange Medium**: Allowed leaders from different regions to share political programs.

---

## Rediscovery of India’s Past
*   **Historical Researches**:
    *   *European Scholars*: **Max Mueller**, **Monier Williams**, **Roth**, **Sassoon**.
    *   *Indian Scholars*: **R.G. Bhandarkar**, **R.L. Mitra**, **Swami Vivekananda**.
*   **Outcome**: Revealed a past of advanced political, economic, social institutions, flourishing foreign trade, and rich cultural heritage.
*   **Indo-Aryan Common Origin Theory**: Proved Indians belonged to the same ethnic group as Europeans → Psychological boost to self-respect.
*   **Myth Demolition**: Demolished colonial myths that India was characterized by a "long history of servility to foreign rulers."

---

## Progressive Character of Socio-religious Reform Movements
*   **Social Reform** → Eradication of social evils dividing society → Brought different social sections together → **Fostered nationalism**.

---

## Rise of Middle-Class Intelligentsia
*   **Origin**: Created by British administrative and economic innovations in urban towns.
*   **Percival Spear Definition**: A well-integrated, dynamic, all-India minority class sharing a common foreground of **knowledge, ideas, and values**, with a unified sense of purpose and hope.
*   **Role**: Formed the vanguard of the national movement; provided leadership to the **Indian National Congress** in all its growth stages.

## Impact of Contemporary Movements in The World
*   **Global Influences**:
    *   Rise of independent nations from the ruins of **Spanish and Portuguese empires** in South America.
    *   National liberation movements of **Greece** and **Italy**.
    *   National struggle of **Ireland** (deeply influenced the nationalist ranks).

---

## Reactionary Policies and Racial Arrogance of Rulers
*   **Racial Arrogance**: Systematic perpetuation of white superiority myths via deliberate policies of discrimination & segregation.
*   **Lytton’s Reactionary Measures**:
    *   **ICS Exam Age Limit (1876)**: Maximum age reduced from **21 to 19 years** (to discourage Indians).
    *   **Delhi Durbar (1877)**: Grand imperial celebration while the country suffered from a severe famine.
    *   **Vernacular Press Act (1878)**: Gagged native language newspapers.
    *   **Arms Act (1878)**: Criminalized possession of arms by Indians without license.
*   **Ilbert Bill Controversy (Ripon's Government)**:
    *   *Objective*: Abolish "judicial disqualification based on race distinctions"; give Indian covenanted civil service members equal powers to try Europeans.
    *   *Reaction*: Stiff, organized opposition by the European community → Forced Ripon to modify/defeat the bill's original purpose.
    *   *Double Lesson for Nationalists*:
        1.  Justice and fair play could not be expected where European interests clashed with Indian ones.
        2.  Organized, sustained agitation was the only effective way to secure rights.

## Political Associations Before The Indian National Congress
*   **Early 19th Century Associations**:
    *   Dominated by **wealthy and aristocratic elements**.
    *   Localized/regional character.
    *   *Primary Demands* (via long petitions to British Parliament): **Administrative reforms**, **associating Indians with administration**, and **spreading education**.
*   **Second Half of 19th Century Associations**:
    *   Dominated by the **educated middle class** (lawyers, journalists, doctors, teachers).
    *   Wider perspective and broader, more proactive political agenda.

---

## Political Associations in Bengal
*   **1836: Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha**:
    *   Founded by associates of **Raja Rammohan Roy**.
*   **Zamindari Association (Landholders' Society)**:
    *   Founded to safeguard the interests of **landlords**.
    *   *Significance*: Marked the beginning of organized political activity and the use of **constitutional agitation** methods for redressal of grievances.
*   **1843: Bengal British India Society**:
    *   *Objective*: Collect & disseminate information on the actual condition of people in British India; secure rights of all classes via peaceful/lawful means.
*   **1851: British Indian Association**:
    *   *Merger*: Landholders' Society + Bengal British India Society.
    *   *Petition to British Parliament Demands*:
        *   Establishment of a separate popular legislature.
        *   Separation of executive from judicial functions.
        *   Reduction of higher officers' salaries.
        *   Abolition of salt, abkari, and stamp duties.
    *   *Outcome*: Partially accepted in **Charter Act of 1853** (added 6 legislative members to the Governor General's Council).
*   **1866: East India Association**:
    *   Founded by **Dadabhai Naoroji** in London.
    *   *Objective*: Discuss the Indian question; influence public men in England to promote Indian welfare.
    *   *Growth*: Later opened branches in major Indian cities.
*   **1875: Indian League**:
    *   Started by **Sisir Kumar Ghosh**.
    *   *Objective*: Stimulate nationalism; encourage political education among the masses.
*   **1876: Indian Association of Calcutta (Indian National Association)**:
    *   Superseded the Indian League.
    *   *Founders*: Younger Bengal nationalists led by **Surendranath Banerjea** and **Ananda Mohan Bose** (discontented with conservative, pro-landlord British Indian Association policies).
    *   *Objectives*: Promote political, intellectual, and material advancement; create strong public opinion; unify people under a common political program.
    *   *Key Agitations & Work*:
        *   Protested against the **ICS age limit reduction in 1877** (led the famous *Indian Civil Service Agitation*).
        *   Demanded simultaneous civil service exams in England & India; Indianisation of higher administration.
        *   Campaigned against the **Arms Act (1878)** and **Vernacular Press Act (1878)**.
    *   *Features*: Kept membership fees low to attract poorer sections; opened branches inside/outside Bengal.
    *   *Forerunner status*: Sponsored the first **All-India National Conference** in Calcutta (**December 28–30, 1883**), attended by over 100 delegates. Merged with the **Indian National Congress in 1886**.

## Political Associations in Bombay
*   **1867: Poona Sarvajanik Sabha**:
    *   Founded by **Mahadev Govind Ranade** and others.
    *   *Function*: Serve as a bridge between the government and the people.
*   **1885: Bombay Presidency Association**:
    *   Started by **Badruddin Tyabji**, **Pherozeshah Mehta**, and **K.T. Telang**.

## Political Associations in Madras

## Pre-Congress Campaigns
*   Early political associations organized major campaigns before the Indian National Congress appeared:
    1.  **For imposition of import duty on cotton** (1875).
    2.  **For Indianisation of government service** (1878–79).
    3.  **Against Lytton’s Afghan adventure**.
    4.  **Against the Arms Act (1878)**.
    5.  **Against the Vernacular Press Act (1878)**.
    6.  **For the right to join the volunteer corps**.
    7.  **Against plantation labour** & the **Inland Emigration Act**.
    8.  **In support of the Ilbert Bill**.
    9.  **For an All-India Fund for Political Agitation**.
    10. **Campaign in Britain** to vote for the pro-India party.
    11. **Against reduction in maximum age for ICS** (led by the Indian Association as the *Indian Civil Service Agitation*).
