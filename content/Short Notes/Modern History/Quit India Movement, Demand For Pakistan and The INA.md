## Quit India Movement
*   **July 14, 1942 (Wardha CWC meeting)** → Accepted idea of mass struggle; Gandhi drafted resolution calling for British withdrawal + non-violent non-cooperation against Japanese invasion.

### Why Start A Struggle Now
*   **Cripps Mission Failure (1942)** → Exposed British refusal to grant genuine constitutional advance → Indian silence = accepting British unilateral right to decide India's fate.
*   **Wartime Economic Hardships** → Popular discontent due to soaring prices, acute shortage of rice/salt → Aggravated by boat commandeering in Bengal/Orissa and British **scorched earth policy** in Assam, Bengal, Orissa.
*   **Imminent British Collapse** → News of crushing British reverses in South-East Asia → Public panic → Citizens withdrew deposits from banks and post offices; heightened willingness to express anger.
*   **Shattered White Prestige** → Defeat of European power by an Asian power (Japan) broke the myth of white invincibility → British racial bias during South-East Asia evacuation (separate evacuation routes: **White Road** for Europeans vs. **Black Road** for Indians) exposed.
*   **Mass Psychological Conditioning** → Congress leadership wanted to prepare the Indian masses to resist possible Japanese invasion.

### The ‘Quit India’ Resolution
*   **July 1942 (Wardha CWC)** → Authorized Gandhi to take charge of the non-violent mass movement; drafted 'Quit India' Resolution (Proposed by **Jawaharlal Nehru**, Seconded by **Sardar Patel**).
*   **August 8, 1942 (Gowalia Tank, Bombay)** → AICC meeting ratified the resolution.
*   **AICC Core Objectives**:
    *   Demand immediate, unconditional end to British rule in India.
    *   Commitment of free India to defend itself against all forms of Fascism and Imperialism.
    *   Form a provisional representative government after British withdrawal.
    *   Sanction a nationwide civil disobedience movement.
    *   **Gandhi** named leader of the struggle.

### Gandhi’s General Instructions to Different Sections
*(Spelt out at Gowalia Tank but not officially issued as formal guidelines due to sudden arrests)*
*   **Government servants** → Do not resign, but openly declare allegiance to the Congress.
*   **Soldiers** → Do not leave the army, but refuse to fire on compatriots.
*   **Students** → If confident in your resolve, leave schools and colleges to join the struggle.
*   **Peasants** → If zamindars are anti-government, pay mutually agreed rent; if zamindars are pro-government, withhold rent entirely.
*   **Princes** → Support the masses; accept the sovereignty of your own people.
*   **Princely states’ people** → Support the ruler only if he is anti-government; declare yourselves part of the Indian nation.
*   **The "Do or Die" Mantra** → *“We shall either free India or die in the attempt; we shall not live to see the perpetuation of our slavery.”*

### Spread of The Movement
*   **August 9, 1942 (Early Hours)** → Pre-emptive strike: all top Congress leaders arrested → CWC, AICC, and PCCs declared unlawful under **Criminal Law Amendment Act of 1908**; public assemblies banned under **Rule 56 of Defence of India Rules**.
*   **Younger Leadership Emerges** → Absence of veterans → Militant youth took the initiative; relatively unknown **Aruna Asaf Ali** presided over the session on August 9 and hoisted the tricolour at Gowalia Tank.

#### Public on Rampage
*   **Sabotage of Authority Symbols** → Public forcibly hoisted flags on government buildings, blew up bridges, cut telegraph lines, and dismantled railway tracks.
*   **Storm Centres** → Most intense in **Eastern United Provinces (UP)** and **Bihar**.
*   **Student Mobilization** → Strikes, demonstrations, writing/distributing illegal news sheets (*patrikas*), acting as couriers.
*   **Industrial Strikes** → Mass shutdowns in Ahmedabad, Bombay, Jamshedpur, Ahmednagar, and Poona.

#### Underground Activity
*   **Objective** → Maintain popular morale, distribute arms/ammunition, and sustain a line of command.
*   **Participants** → Socialists, Forward Bloc, Gandhi ashramites, revolutionary nationalists.
*   **Key Organizers** → Rammanohar Lohia, Jayaprakash Narayan, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta, Biju Patnaik, Chhotubhai Puranik, Achyut Patwardhan, Sucheta Kripalani, R.P. Goenka.
*   **Secret Communications** → **Usha Mehta** established an underground radio transmitter in Bombay.

#### Parallel Governments
*   **Ballia (August 1942 - 1 week)** → Established under **Chittu Pandey**; released arrested Congress leaders and assumed local administration.
*   **Tamluk (Midnapore, Dec 1942–Sept 1944)** → ***Jatiya Sarkar***; organized cyclone relief work, issued grants to schools, distributed paddy to the poor, and formed armed volunteer units named ***Vidyut Vahinis***.
*   **Satara (Mid-1943 to 1945)** → ***Prati Sarkar*** under **Y.B. Chavan**, **Nana Patil**, etc. → Organized Nyayadan Mandals (people's courts), village libraries, prohibition campaigns, and "Gandhi marriages".
*   **Collaborative Support Network** → Businessmen (funded + sheltered activists), students (couriers), villagers (information blackout to authorities), pilots/train drivers (transported bombs/materials), and low-level government/police officials (leaked intelligence).

### Extent of Mass Participation
*   **Youth** → School and college students led protests, processed, and ran underground networks.
*   **Women** → Active leadership and field resistance (**Aruna Asaf Ali**, **Sucheta Kripalani**, **Usha Mehta**).
*   **Workers** → Staged strikes, braved brutal police firing and repression.
*   **Peasants** → Formed the backbone of the struggle; focused actions on state symbols (complete absence of anti-landlord rent-riots; even some zamindars participated).
*   **Government Officials** → Erosion of state loyalty; lower cadres of administration and police passed key warnings to satyagrahis.
*   **Muslims** → Sheltered underground leaders; zero communal clashes during the movement.
*   **Opposition / Boycott**:
    *   **Communists** → Boycotted the movement; Germany's invasion of USSR (1941) caused them to back the British war effort → Rebranded "Imperialist War" as a **"People's War"**.
    *   **Muslim League** → Opposed the movement; feared British withdrawal would leave Muslims at the mercy of Hindu majoritarian rule.
    *   **Hindu Mahasabha** → Boycotted the movement.
    *   **Princely States** → Low-key, passive response.

### Government Repression
*   No formal martial law, but brutal state violence: agitations tear-gassed, lathi-charged, and fired upon.
*   **Casualties** → Estimated **10,000** killed; severe collective fines levied on rebellious villages; widespread mass floggings; total press censorship.

### Estimate
*   **Spontaneity** → Unprecedented public initiative due to sudden loss of leadership; deep nationalist penetration.
*   **Social Base** → Backed by students, peasants, and workers; traditional elite/bureaucracy remained loyal.
*   **Significance** → Demonstrated that India could no longer be governed without the consent of Indians; placed **complete independence** on the immediate, non-negotiable agenda.

### Gandhi Fasts
*   **February 1943** → Gandhi started a **21-day fast** in prison → Launched to protest against the brutal violence of the colonial state (refused government demands to condemn public violence).
*   **Impact**:
    *   Rekindled public morale; sparked worldwide strikes, demonstrations, and *hartals*.
    *   **Three members** of the Viceroy’s Executive Council resigned in protest.
    *   Exposed British high-handedness; Gandhi survived, defeating official expectations of his demise.
*   **March 23, 1943** → **Pakistan Day** observed by the Muslim League.

### Famine Of 1943
*   **Worst-Affected Areas** → Southwest Bengal (Tamluk, Contai, Diamond Harbour), Dacca, Faridpur, Tippera, Noakhali.
*   **Toll** → **1.5 to 3 million** died from starvation, malnutrition, and epidemics (malaria, cholera, smallpox).
*   **Nature** → Highly **man-made** crisis.
*   **Core Causes**:
    1.  Massive diversion of foodstuffs to feed Allied armies.
    2.  Halt of rice imports from Burma and South-East Asia due to Japanese occupation.
    3.  Wartime administrative mismanagement, delayed rationing (limited only to major cities), and deliberate hoarding/profiteering.

## Rajagopalachari Formula

### The Formula
*   Prepared by **C. Rajagopalachari (CR)** in **1944** to resolve the Congress-League constitutional deadlock → Represented tacit acceptance of the demand for Pakistan; supported by Gandhi.
*   **Core Terms**:
    *   Muslim League to endorse the Congress demand for immediate independence.
    *   League to cooperate with Congress in forming a provisional interim government at the center.
    *   Post-war, the **entire population** (not just Muslims) of Muslim-majority areas in the North-West and North-East to decide on secession via a plebiscite.
    *   In case of partition, joint agreements to be made for safeguarding critical common matters (Defense, Commerce, Communications, etc.).
    *   Terms to be implemented **only if** Great Britain transfers full powers to India.

### Objections
*   **Jinnah’s Stand** → Rejected the plan; demanded Congress accept the **two-nation theory** first; insisted only the Muslim population of those areas vote in the plebiscite; strongly opposed a common center.
*   **Hindu Leaders** → Led by **Vir Savarkar**, strongly condemned the CR Formula.

## Desai-Liaqat Pact
*   Drafted in **1945** by **Bhulabhai Desai** (Congress assembly leader) and **Liaqat Ali Khan** (League deputy assembly leader) for an interim central government.
*   **Core Proposals**:
    *   Congress and Muslim League to nominate an **equal number of persons** in the Central Executive Council.
    *   **20%** of seats reserved for minor communities.
*   **Outcome** → No formal agreement was reached, but the **Congress-League parity** principle set a major precedent.

## Wavell Plan
*   **June 1945** → Shimla Conference convened by Viceroy Lord Wavell; wartime coalition allowed negotiations with released Congress leaders.

### Why The Government Was Keen On A Solution Now
1.  **UK General Elections** scheduled for mid-1945 → Churchill's Conservative party wanted to demonstrate sincere intent to resolve the deadlock.
2.  **Allied Pressure** → Massive diplomatic pressure from US/China to secure complete Indian cooperation against remaining Japanese forces.
3.  **Divert Nationalist Energy** → Deflect public anger into peaceful constitutional discussions.

### The Plan
*   Proposed reconstructing the Governor-General’s Executive Council as an interim measure under the 1935 Act:
    *   All members of the Executive Council to be Indians (except Governor-General and Commander-in-Chief).
    *   **Caste Hindus and Muslims to have equal representation** (Parity).
    *   Reconstructed council to function as an interim government (not responsible to the Central Assembly).
    *   Governor-General's veto power to be restricted (exercised only on advice of ministers).
    *   Parties to submit a joint list (or separate lists) for council nominations.
    *   Open path for constitution-making after the war was won.

### Muslim League’s Stand
*   Demanded that **all Muslim nominees** to the council must be selected exclusively by the League → Feared other minorities (Sikhs, Depressed Classes, Christians) would vote with Congress, reducing League to a 1/3rd minority.
*   Opposed Wavell's plan to nominate **Khizr Hyat Khan** (Punjab Unionist representative).
*   Claimed a communal veto: decisions opposed by Muslims to require a **two-thirds majority** for approval.

### Congress Stand
*   Strongly opposed being labeled a "purely caste Hindu party"; asserted its right to nominate representatives from all communities (including nationalist Muslims).

### Wavell’s Mistake
*   Wavell announced the breakdown of talks, giving Jinnah/League a virtual **veto** over all future constitutional developments → Artificially boosted League's status, boosted Jinnah's position, and exposed Churchill's real Conservative attitude.

## The Indian National Army And Subhas Bose
*   **Subhas Chandra Bose** → Resigned from ICS (4th rank) in 1921 to join national struggle; political mentor was **Chittaranjan Das**; Mayor of Calcutta in 1923; jailed multiple times.
*   **Ideological Break** → Opposed Gandhi's non-violent methods; resolved to utilize the international situation to wage armed struggle.
*   **Anti-Compromise Conference (Ramgarh, March 1940)** → Jointly organized by Forward Bloc and Kisan Sabha → Called for a worldwide struggle starting April 6 (National Week) to block the export of Indian resources/men for the imperialist war.
*   **Escape from India** → Under house arrest after protesting Holwell Monument (Calcutta) → Escaped in January 1941 as **Ziauddin** (helped by Bhagat Ram) → Traveled via Peshawar and Kabul to Moscow, and then to Berlin.
*   **German Operations**:
    *   Met Hitler under alias **Orlando Mazzotta**.
    *   Formed the ***Mukti Sena*** (Freedom Army) using captured Indian POWs in Germany and Italy (HQ in Dresden).
    *   Earned the title **"Netaji"**; coined the slogan **"Jai Hind"** at the Free India Centre.
    *   Began regular propaganda broadcasts from **Berlin Radio** (Jan 1942).
    *   Traveled via German and Japanese submarines to reach Japan in early 1943.

### Origin and First Phase of The Indian National Army
*   **Conception** → Conceived by **Mohan Singh** (Dec 1941), an Indian army officer who did not retreat with British in Malaya and turned to the Japanese for assistance.
*   **Initial Enlistment** → Japanese handed over Indian POWs to Mohan Singh; Singapore's fall (1942) provided a massive recruit base → By late 1942, **40,000 men** were ready.
*   **First Division** → Formed in **September 1942** (16,300 men) → Intended to act only on AICC invitation; served as a check against Japanese misconduct in Southeast Asia.
*   **First Phase Collapse** → Serious differences over INA's role → Japanese wanted a token force of 2,000, while Mohan Singh wanted a full-scale combat army → Mohan Singh arrested by the Japanese.
*   **Rashbehari Bose's Crucial Spadework**:
    *   Fled to Japan in 1915 after failed revolutionary attempts; became naturalized citizen; founded the Indian Club of Tokyo.
    *   Formed the **Indian Independence League (Tokyo, 1942)** to mobilize East Asian Indians.
    *   **Bangkok Conference (June 1942)** → Formally resolved to place INA under the Indian Independence League, chaired by Rashbehari Bose.
    *   Traveled to Singapore and happily transferred control of the League and INA to Subhas Bose in **July 1943** (Rashbehari died on Jan 21, 1945).
*   **Second Phase of INA (Netaji Supreme Command)**:
    *   **August 25, 1943** → Netaji officially took over as Supreme Commander of the INA.
    *   **Provisional Government for Free India (Singapore, Oct 21, 1943)** → Formed by Netaji (Key portfolios: H.C. Chatterjee - Finance, M.A. Aiyar - Broadcasting, Lakshmi Swaminathan - Women).
    *   **Slogans & Regiments** → *"Give me blood, I will give you freedom!"* (given in Malaya); ***Rani Jhansi Regiment*** formed for women; HQ shifted to Rangoon (Jan 1944) with war cry *"Chalo Delhi!"*.
    *   **Andaman and Nicobar Transfer** → Handed over by Japanese in November 1943; renamed **Shahid Dweep** and **Swaraj Dweep**.
    *   **"Father of the Nation"** → Coined by Netaji during a July 6, 1944 broadcast on Azad Hind Radio, seeking Gandhi's blessings for the final war.
*   **Indo-Burma Front Campaign**:
    *   One battalion under **Shah Nawaz Khan** accompanied the Japanese Army to Imphal.
    *   **Japanese Discrimination** → Demoralized the INA by denying them equal rations, arms, and using them for menial labor.
    *   **March 18, 1944** → INA crossed the Burma border onto Indian soil.
    *   **April 14, 1944** → **Colonel Malik** (Bahadur Group) hoisted the INA tricolour at **Moirang, Manipur** (military administration run for 3 months).
*   **Allied Counter-Attack & Surrender**:
    *   Allied victory forced a massive Japanese retreat → INA brigades withdrew starting **July 18, 1944**.
    *   **August 15, 1945** → Japan surrendered in WWII → Formal surrender of the INA.
    *   **August 18, 1945** → Netaji reportedly died in a plane crash at Taipei, Taiwan.
    *   **Legacy** → Return and court-martial of INA POWs triggered unprecedented public solidarity and revolutionary defense campaigns across India.
