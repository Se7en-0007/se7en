## Constitutional Development Between 1773 And 1858
* **1765–1772 (Dual System of Government)** ⇒ EIC had authority without responsibility; Indian representatives had responsibility without authority.
  * **Consequences** ⇒ Rampant corruption, private trade, peasant oppression, excessive revenue collection, Company's bankruptcy, and servants' immense wealth.
  * **Deputy Diwans** ⇒ **Mohammad Reza Khan** (Bengal) and **Raja Shitab Rai** (Bihar).
  * **1767 Intervention** ⇒ British Government demanded a **10% share** of the EIC's plunder (**£4 million** annually).
  * **Outcome** ⇒ British Government decided to regulate and control the Company.

### The Regulating Act of 1773
* **Objective** ⇒ Introduce centralized administration and parliamentary supervision over the EIC.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **British Cabinet Control** ⇒ EIC Directors required to submit all correspondence on civil, military, and revenue affairs.
  * **Executive Structure** ⇒ Governor-General of Bengal (**Warren Hastings**) + **4 council members** representing civil/military government; decision by majority vote.
  * **Judiciary** ⇒ **Supreme Court of Judicature** established at Calcutta with original and appellate jurisdictions.
    * *Weakness* ⇒ Clashed with the Executive Council over vague jurisdictions.
  * **Provincial Control** ⇒ Governor-General given vague, problematic supervisory powers over Bombay and Madras.

### Amendments (1781)
* **SC Jurisdiction Defined** ⇒ Limited within Calcutta; required to administer personal laws of defendants.
* **Immunity** ⇒ Government servants immune from SC jurisdiction for actions done in their official capacity.
* **Social Usages** ⇒ Traditional social and religious customs of the subjects to be respected.

### Pitt’s India Act of 1784
* **EIC Status** ⇒ Company territories designated as **"British possessions"**; EIC became a subordinate department of the State.
* **Dual System of Control**:
  * **Board of Control** ⇒ Set up in England (Chancellor of Exchequer, Secretary of State, + 4 Privy Councillors appointed by the Crown) to control civil, military, and revenue affairs.
  * **Executive Changes** ⇒ Governor-General’s Council reduced to **3 members** (including the Commander-in-Chief).
  * **Provincial Subordination** ⇒ Bombay and Madras Presidencies made explicitly subordinate to the Governor-General.
  * **Aggressive Foreign Policy** ⇒ General prohibition placed on aggressive wars and treaties (frequently breached).

### The Act of 1786
* **Cornwallis' Demands Conceded** ⇒ Combined the offices of **Governor-General** and **Commander-in-Chief**.
* **Veto Power** ⇒ Governor-General empowered to override the Executive Council's decisions by taking personal responsibility (later extended to all GGs).

### The Charter Act of 1793
* **Monopoly Renewal** ⇒ EIC's commercial privileges renewed for another **20 years**.
* **Financial Tribute** ⇒ EIC to pay **£5 lakh** annually to the British Government from Indian revenues after meeting essential expenses.
* **Appointments** ⇒ Royal approval mandated for appointing Governor-General, Governors, and Commander-in-Chief.
* **Official Discipline** ⇒ Senior officials barred from leaving India without permission (treated as resignation).
* **Country Trade** ⇒ EIC empowered to grant trade licences to individuals/employees ("privilege" or "country trade") ⇒ paved way for opium shipments to China.
* **Separation of Powers** ⇒ Revenue administration separated from judiciary ⇒ abolition of **Maal Adalats** (revenue courts).
* **Payment Source** ⇒ Home Government members paid out of Indian revenues (continued until 1919).

### The Charter Act of 1813
* **Laissez-Faire & Napoleon's Continental System** ⇒ Continental blockade closed European ports to British trade ⇒ British business interests demanded the end of EIC monopoly.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **Monopoly Ended** ⇒ EIC's trade monopoly in India abolished, **except** for trade in **tea** and trade with **China**.
  * **Territorial Sovereignty** ⇒ Possession of territories and revenues retained for 20 years, explicitly reserving Crown sovereignty.
  * **Dividend** ⇒ Shareholders guaranteed a **10.5% dividend** from Indian revenues.
  * **Education Grant** ⇒ **Rs 1 lakh** set aside annually for the promotion of literature, learning, and science (first state responsibility for education).
  * **Parliamentary Supervision** ⇒ Regulations of Madras, Bombay, and Calcutta Councils to be laid before Parliament.
  * **Accounts Separation** ⇒ Separate accounts kept for commercial transactions and territorial revenues.
  * **Missionaries** ⇒ Christian missionaries permitted to enter India and preach.

### The Charter Act of 1833
* **Context** ⇒ Influence of Whig liberal ideals and industrial interests in Britain.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **Monopoly Abolished** ⇒ Company's monopoly over tea and China trade completely ended.
  * **Colonization Allowed** ⇒ Restrictions on European immigration and property acquisition lifted ⇒ opened door to European colonization.
  * **Extreme Centralization**:
    * Governor-General of Bengal designated as **Governor-General of India**.
    * Bengal, Madras, Bombay, and all other territories placed under his complete civil, military, revenue, and expenditure control.
    * Governments of Bombay and Madras **deprived of legislative powers** (left with proposing draft bills to the GG).
  * **Law Member** ⇒ A **fourth (Law) member** (Macaulay) added to the GG's Council for professional advice on legislation.
  * **Codification** ⇒ Law Commission set up under Macaulay to codify and consolidate Indian laws.
  * **Section 87** ⇒ No Indian citizen to be denied employment under the EIC based on religion, color, birth, descent, etc. (remained on paper but became a sheet-anchor for political agitation).
  * **Slavery** ⇒ EIC directed to ameliorate slave conditions and ultimately abolish slavery (**abolished in 1843**).

### The Charter Act of 1853
* **Commercial Exit** ⇒ EIC to hold territories in trust for the Crown until Parliament provided otherwise (no fixed period).
* **Court of Directors** ⇒ Strength reduced from 24 to **18** (6 nominated by the Crown).
* **Services Thrown Open** ⇒ EIC’s patronage ended; civil services thrown open to open competitive examinations.
* **Executive & Legislative Separation**:
  * Law member became a full member of the Executive Council.
  * **6 additional members** added to the council for legislative purposes.
  * **Local Representation** ⇒ 4 of the 6 legislative members represented local governments of Madras, Bombay, Bengal, and NWFP.
  * **Indian Legislative Council** established; bills required GG’s assent (GG held veto power).

### The Act for Better Government of India 1858
* **Trigger** ⇒ Revolt of 1857 exposed EIC administrative failures and lack of accountability.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **Direct Crown Rule** ⇒ India to be governed directly by and in the name of the Crown.
  * **Secretary of State (SOS)** ⇒ Created to wield power (replacing the Board of Control and Court of Directors). SOS was a British Cabinet member answerable to Parliament.
  * **India Council** ⇒ **15-member advisory body** to assist the SOS.
  * **Dual System Ends** ⇒ Dual control system introduced by Pitt’s Act of 1784 abolished.
  * **Viceroy** ⇒ Governor-General given the title of Viceroy; became subordinate to the SOS based in London.

## Developments After 1858 Till Independence

### Indian Councils Act 1861
* **Significance** ⇒ Introduced the principle of non-official representation in legislative bodies; laid the foundation of legislative devolution and cabinet government.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **Portfolio System** ⇒ Introduced by **Lord Canning**; each department was headed by an official spokesperson responsible to the government.
  * **Legislative Devolution** ⇒ Legislative powers restored to Bombay and Madras; provision for establishing legislative councils in other provinces.
  * **Representative Element** ⇒ Viceroy's Executive Council expanded by adding **6 to 12 additional members** for legislative purposes (at least half had to be non-officials, chosen from elite sections like princes and landlords).
* **Weaknesses**:
  * **No Real Power** ⇒ Councils purely advisory; could not discuss important, financial, or executive actions without previous approval.
  * **No Budget Control** ⇒ Members could not vote on or discuss the budget.
  * **Veto & Ordinance** ⇒ Viceroy possessed veto power and could issue emergency ordinances (6 months' validity); SOS could disallow any act.

### Indian Councils Act 1892
* **Trigger** ⇒ Demands of the newly founded **Indian National Congress (1885)** to reform and expand legislative councils.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **Council Enlargement** ⇒ Number of non-official members increased in both Imperial and Provincial Councils.
  * **Indirect Election** ⇒ Principle of representation accepted. Universities, district boards, municipalities, zamindars, and chambers of commerce empowered to recommend non-official members. (Term 'election' was avoided).
  * **Powers Enhanced** ⇒ Members entitled to express views on financial statements (budget discussion) and put questions to the executive on matters of public interest (required **6 days' notice**).

### Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)
* **Objective** ⇒ Bring representative and popular elements into governance while retaining real power with the executive.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **Council Expansion** ⇒ Strength of Imperial Legislative Council increased (from 16 to 60).
  * **First Indian Member** ⇒ Satyendra Prasad Sinha joined the Viceroy’s Executive Council as the **Law Member**.
  * **Provincial Executive Councils** enlarged; legislative powers of central and provincial councils increased.
* **Weaknesses & Separatism**:
  * **Separate Electorates for Muslims** ⇒ Institutionalized communal division; Muslims accorded representation in excess of population, and Muslim voters given lower income qualifications than Hindus.
  * **Indirect & Remote** ⇒ System of election was highly indirect, leaving representation of the general public remote and unreal.
  * **No Real Power** ⇒ Real power remained with the government; councils limited to criticism only.

### Government of India Act 1919 (Montague-Chelmsford Reforms)
* **August 1917 Declaration** ⇒ Declared the gradual introduction of responsible government in India as an integral part of the British Empire (British Parliament to determine the time/manner of steps).
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **Bicameralism at Centre** ⇒ Indian Legislative Council replaced by:
    1. **Council of State** (Upper House)
    2. **Legislative Assembly** (Lower House)
  * **Direct Elections** ⇒ Introduced for both houses, though franchise was highly restricted (based on property, tax, or education).
  * **Communal Representation Extended** ⇒ Separate electorates granted to **Sikhs, Christians, and Anglo-Indians**, besides Muslims.
  * **Dyarchy in Provinces** ⇒ Introduced in the provincial executive (Transferred vs. Reserved subjects).
    * *Transferred Subjects* ⇒ Administered by Governor with popular ministers (Education, Local Government).
    * *Reserved Subjects* ⇒ Administered by Governor and Executive Council (Finance, Police, Land Revenue).
  * **Budget Separation** ⇒ Central and provincial budgets separated first time; provinces authorized to make their own budgets.
  * **High Commissioner** ⇒ Appointed in London (6-year term) to look after Indian trade in Europe; took over some functions of the SOS.
  * **SOS Payment Shift** ⇒ SOS salary paid by British Exchequer (undoing the 1793 injustice).
* **Weaknesses**:
  * **Unitary & Centralized** ⇒ Overall structure remained highly centralized; Dyarchy in the provinces failed.
  * **Executive Supremacy** ⇒ Central Legislature was a non-sovereign body with no power to replace the government. Viceroy retained extensive veto, certification, and ordinance-making powers.

### Simon Commission (1927–1930)
* **Context** ⇒ Appointed in November 1927 (2 years ahead of the 10-year schedule) as the Indian Statutory Commission.
* **Recommendations (1930)**:
  * Abolish Dyarchy.
  * Extend responsible government in provinces.
  * Establish a federation of British India and Princely States.
  * Continue communal electorates.
* **Aftermath** ⇒ Three Round Table Conferences ⇒ **White Paper on Constitutional Reforms (1933)** ⇒ Lord Linlithgow's Joint Committee Report (1934) ⇒ Government of India Act of 1935.

### Government of India Act 1935
* **Anatomy** ⇒ 321 articles and 10 schedules.
* **Core Provisions**:
  * **All-India Federation** ⇒ Envisaged merging Governors’ Provinces, Chief Commissioners’ Provinces, and Princely States (Princes to sign an **Instrument of Accession**).
    * *Outcome* ⇒ Federation never materialized due to opposition from Indian parties.
  * **Dyarchy in Federal Executive** ⇒ Dyarchy (rejected by Simon Commission) introduced at the federal level.
  * **Bicameral Federal Legislature** ⇒ Council of States (permanent Upper House) and Federal Legislative Assembly; provision for joint sittings in deadlocks.
  * **Three Subject Lists**:
    1. **Federal List**
    2. **Provincial List**
    3. **Concurrent List**
    * *Residuary Powers* ⇒ Vested in the discretion of the Governor-General.
  * **Veto Power** ⇒ GG could veto federal bills; King-in-Council could disallow acts assented to by the GG.
  * **Provincial Autonomy**:
    * Dyarchy in the provinces abolished; distinction between Reserved and Transferred subjects removed.
    * Provinces derived power and authority directly from the Crown.
    * Independent financial powers granted; provinces could borrow money on their own security.
  * **Provincial Bicameralism** ⇒ Introduced in **6 provinces** (Madras, Bombay, Bengal, United Provinces, Bihar, Assam); other 5 provinces remained unicameral.
  * **Communal Franchise Extended** ⇒ Communal electorates and weightage extended to **depressed classes, women, and labor**.
  * **Franchise** ⇒ Extended to about **10%** of the total population.
  * **Federal Court** ⇒ Established in **1937** with original and appellate powers (Privy Council in London remained supreme).
  * **Abolition** ⇒ India Council of the Secretary of State abolished.
* **Critique & Operation**:
  * Central Government continued to run under the 1919 Act with minor amendments until August 15, 1947.
  * Provided a rigid constitution with no internal growth mechanism (right of amendment reserved for British Parliament).
  * Promoted separatist tendencies via communal electorates.
  * Congress rejected the Act and demanded a **Constituent Assembly** elected on adult franchise.

## Evolution Of Civil Services In India

### Cornwallis’ Role
* **Contribution** ⇒ First to organize and organize the Civil Services; raised salaries, strictly enforced rules against private trade, banned presents/bribes, and enforced promotions based on seniority.

### Wellesley’s Role
* **1800 (Fort William College)** ⇒ Set up by Wellesley for training of new civil service recruits.
* **1806 (Haileybury College)** ⇒ Set up in England (East India College) after Court of Directors disapproved Fort William College; imparted 2 years' training.

### Charter Act of 1853
* **Monopoly Ended** ⇒ Company's patronage dissolved; covenanted civil services thrown open to an open competitive examination.
* **Exclusion of Indians**:
  * Systematically barred from high posts from the beginning.
  * Cornwallis' belief: **“Every native of Hindustan is corrupt.”**
  * **Charter Act of 1793** had reserved all posts worth **£500 per annum** for covenanted servants.
  * *Reasons for Exclusion* ⇒ Belief that only English could serve British interests; belief that Indians were incapable/untrustworthy; intense European competition for lucrative jobs.

### Indian Civil Service Act of 1861
* **Covenanted Posts Reserved** ⇒ Certain key administrative offices reserved for covenanted civil servants.
* **Exam Hurdles** ⇒ Examinations held only in London in English; syllabus focused on classical Greek and Latin learning.
* **Age Limit Reduction (Lytton's Assault)**:
  * Maximum age: **23** (1859) → **22** (1860) → **21** (1866) → **19** (1878 under Lytton).
* **Pioneer** ⇒ **Satyendra Nath Tagore** became the first Indian to qualify for the ICS in **1863**.

### Statutory Civil Service
* **1878–79 (Lytton's Scheme)** ⇒ **1/6th** of covenanted posts to be filled by Indians of "high families" nominated by local governments (subject to approval of SOS and Viceroy).
* **Fate** ⇒ Failed and was abolished.

### Congress Demand and Aitchison Committee
* **INC Demands (1885)** ⇒ Lowering of ICS age limit and holding exams simultaneously in India and Britain.
* **Aitchison Committee (1886 under Dufferin)**:
  * Recommended dropping terms 'covenanted' and 'uncovenanted'.
  * Classified services into:
    1. **Imperial Indian Civil Service** (exam in England)
    2. **Provincial Civil Service** (exam in India)
    3. **Subordinate Civil Service** (exam in India)
  * Recommended raising the maximum age limit to **23**.
* **1893 Resolution** ⇒ House of Commons passed a resolution supporting simultaneous exams, but it was never implemented (opposed by SOS Kimberley).

### Montford Reforms 1919
* **Recommendations**:
  * Simultaneously hold exams in India and England.
  * **1/3rd** of recruitments to be made in India itself, raising it by **1.5% annually**.

### Lee Commission (1924)
* **Recommendations**:
  * SOS should continue to recruit for ICS, Irrigation branch of Service of Engineers, and Indian Forest Service.
  * Recruitments for transferred fields (Education, Medical) to be made by provincial governments.
  * ICS direct recruitment parity of **50:50** between Europeans and Indians to be reached in **15 years**.
  * A **Public Service Commission** to be immediately established.

### Government of India Act, 1935
* **PSC Established** ⇒ Provided for establishing a Federal Public Service Commission and Provincial PSCs.

### Evaluation of Civil Services under British Rule
* **Colonial Agents** ⇒ Despite slow Indianization post-1918 under nationalist pressure, senior and key positions remained dominated by Europeans.
* **No Real Power** ⇒ Indian bureaucrats did not transfer political power; they functioned as agents to serve the imperialist interests of their British masters.

## Evolution Of Police System In Modern India
* **Pre-Colonial Context** ⇒ Autocratic regimes lacked formal police; Mughal faujdars (law & order) and amils (revenue collectors/rebel suppression). Kotwal maintained order in cities. Under EIC Dual Rule (1765–72), zamindars maintained thanedars, but often neglected duties and colluded with dacoits.
* **Timeline of Developments**:
  * **1770** ⇒ Institutions of faujdars and amils abolished.
  * **1774 (Warren Hastings)** ⇒ Restored faujdars; asked zamindars to assist in suppressing dacoits and violence.
  * **1775** ⇒ Faujdar thanas established in major towns of large districts, assisted by smaller police stations.
  * **1791 (Cornwallis)** ⇒ Regular police organized. Districts divided into **thanas** (circles) under an Indian **Daroga**, with a **Superintendent of Police (SP)** heading the district. Zamindars relieved of police duties.
  * **1808 (Mayo)** ⇒ Appointed an SP for each division, assisted by spies (**goyendas**), who committed severe abuses on locals.
  * **1814** ⇒ Court of Directors abolished the offices of darogas and subordinates in all Company possessions except Bengal.
  * **Bentinck (1828–35)** ⇒ Abolished the office of SP. Collector/Magistrate headed the police; Divisional Commissioner acted as SP. (Led to a badly organized police force and heavy burden on collectors). Separation occurred first in Presidency towns.
  * **Indian Police Act, 1861** (following 1860 Police Commission):
    * Civil constabulary system; maintained village watchman setup but connected directly to the constabulary.
    * Ranks uniformly introduced: **Inspector-General** (province head), **Deputy Inspector-General** (range head), and **SP** (district head).
  * **1902–03 (Curzon / Frazer Commission)**:
    * Recommended no promotion of junior officials to high ranks; senior ranks to be directly recruited.
    * Established training schools for constables and officers.
    * Created the **Department of Criminal Intelligence (DCI)** at the Centre and **Criminal Investigation Departments (CIDs)** in provinces. (In 1929, CID was divided into Special Branch, CID, and Crime Branch).
    * *Role* ⇒ Police functioned as a repressive colonial tool, suppressing the national movement and losing mass empathy.

## Military Under The British
* **Pre-1857 Structure** ⇒ Two forces:
  1. **Queen’s Army** (serving Crown troops on duty in India)
  2. **Company’s Troops** (mixture of European regiments and locally recruited Native regiments with British officers).
* **Post-1857 Reorganisation (Dufferin Warning 1888)** ⇒ Aimed to prevent recurrence of revolt and defend empire borders from rivals (Russia, France, Germany).
* **Key Features**:
  * **European Dominance** ⇒ White army ratio increased to **1/3rd (33%)** of the total force (vs. 14% before 1857).
  * **Proportions Carefully Fixed** ⇒ **1:2** in the Bengal Army; **2:5** in Madras and Bombay armies.
  * **Monopoly over Core Departments** ⇒ European monopoly maintained over artillery, tanks, and armed corps.
  * **Equipment Discrimination** ⇒ Indians given inferior quality rifles until 1900.
  * **Officer Exclusion** ⇒ No Indians allowed in officer ranks; highest rank an Indian could reach till 1914 was **Subedar**. Commissioned ranks opened to Indians only from 1918. (1926 Sandhurst Committee visualised only a 50% Indianized officer cadre for 1952).
  * **Balance and Counterpoise (Divide and Rule)** ⇒ 1879 Commission stressed **"counterpoise of natives against natives"**.
  * **Martial vs. Non-Martial Race Theory** ⇒ Formulated under Lord Roberts (Commander-in-Chief 1887–92):
    * *Martial Races* ⇒ Sikhs, Gurkhas, and Pathans (helped suppress the 1857 revolt; recruited heavily).
    * *Non-Martial Races* ⇒ Awadh, Bihar, Central and South Indian soldiers (had participated in the 1857 revolt; declared unfit).
  * **Caste & Communal Segregation** ⇒ Regiments made up of mixed socio-ethnic companies to balance each other.
  * **Sec of State Charles Wood** ⇒ "I wish to have a different and rival spirit in different regiments, so that Sikh might fire into Hindu, Gorkha into either..."
  * **Isolation** ⇒ Soldiers isolated from the population; newspapers, journals, and nationalist publications banned.

## Development Of Judiciary In British India
* **Pre-Colonial Context** ⇒ Autocratic system lacked gradation or regular organization of courts. Hindus used caste elders, village panchayats, or zamindars; Muslims used the **Qazi** in towns and capitals. Rajas and Badshahs arbitrary fountainheads.
* **EIC Common Law Origin** ⇒ **Mayor's Courts** established in Madras, Bombay, and Calcutta in **1726**.

### Reforms under Warren Hastings (1772–85)
* **District Diwani Adalats** ⇒ Civil disputes under the collector; Hindu law for Hindus, Muslim law for Muslims. Appeal lay to the **Sadar Diwani Adalat** (President + 2 council members).
* **District Fauzdari Adalats** ⇒ Criminal disputes under an Indian officer assisted by qazis/muftis, supervised by the collector. Administered Muslim law. Capital punishment and property acquisition appeals went to **Sadar Nizamat Adalat** at Murshidabad (headed by deputy nizam, chief qazi, chief mufti).
* **Supreme Court (1773 Regulating Act)** ⇒ Established at Calcutta for British subjects; original and appellate jurisdictions. Clashed with Council.

### Reforms under Cornwallis (1786–93) - Separation of Powers
* **Criminal System Reformed** ⇒ District Fauzdari Courts abolished; **Circuit Courts** established at Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad, and Patna under European judges (courts of appeal).
* **Sadar Nizamat Adalat** shifted to Calcutta under GG and Council.
* **Zila Court** ⇒ District Diwani Adalat designated as Zila/City Court under a **District Judge**. Collector relieved of magisterial functions.
* **Gradation of Civil Courts**:
  1. **Munsiff’s Court** (Indian officers)
  2. **Registrar’s Court** (European judge)
  3. **District Court** (District Judge)
  4. **Four Circuit Courts** (Provincial courts of appeal)
  5. **Sadar Diwani Adalat** (Calcutta)
  6. **King-in-Council** (appeals of £5,000 and above).
* **Cornwallis Code (1793)**:
  * Separation of revenue and justice administration.
  * European subjects brought under court jurisdiction.
  * Government officials made answerable to civil courts for official acts.
  * Established the **principle of sovereignty of law**.

### Reforms under William Bentinck (1828–33)
* **Circuit Courts Abolished** ⇒ Functions transferred to collectors under the supervision of the Commissioner of Revenue and Circuit.
* **Allahabad Courts** ⇒ Sadar Diwani and Sadar Nizamat Adalats set up at Allahabad for Upper Provinces.
* **Language Shift** ⇒ Suitors allowed to use Persian or vernacular languages in lower courts; English replaced Persian in the Supreme Court.
* **Codification (1833 Law Commission)** ⇒ Set up under **Macaulay**; drafted the Civil Procedure Code (1859), Indian Penal Code (1860), and Criminal Procedure Code (1861).

### Later Developments
* **1860** ⇒ Europeans lost special privileges except in criminal cases (could only be tried by European judges).
* **1865** ⇒ Supreme Court and Sadar Adalats merged into **High Courts** at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras.
* **1935** ⇒ Government of India Act provided for a **Federal Court** (established in 1937) to settle inter-government disputes and hear limited appeals from High Courts (Privy Council supreme).

### Evaluation
* **Positive Aspects** ⇒ Rule of law established, codified laws replaced arbitrary personal laws, government servants made answerable to civil courts, European subjects brought under jurisdiction.
* **Negative Aspects** ⇒ System became highly complicated and expensive, rich could manipulate courts, ample scope for chicanery/false evidence, delayed justice, overburdened courts, European judges unfamiliar with local traditions.

## Major Changes In Administrative Structure After 1857

### Genesis of Administrative Changes: New Stage of Colonialism
* **Lessons from 1857** ⇒ British sought to narrow the ruler-subject gap to reduce mass alienation. Associating elite natives in administration gave the rulers an idea of local customs to prevent future revolts.
* **Economic Drive** ⇒ Post-1857 intensification of Industrial Revolution in Europe, USA, and Japan led to cut-throat colonial competition. British lost global industrial supremacy.
* **Capital Investments** ⇒ Huge British investments in Indian railways, loans to GoI, tea plantations, coal-mining, jute, shipping, and banking.
* **Objective** ⇒ Consolidate position to secure British economic interests against political dangers, extension of imperial control, reflected in reactionary policies of **Lytton, Dufferin, Lansdowne, Elgin, and Curzon**.

### Administration: Central, Provincial, Local

#### Central Government
* **1858 Act** ⇒ Governance transferred to British Crown. Power wielded by **Secretary of State (SOS)** (Cabinet member) and **15-member advisory council** in London. Power concentrated in London, reducing Viceroy to a subordinate role.
* **Viceroy Executive Council** ⇒ Official advisors and department heads.
* **Indian Councils Act 1861**:
  * Added **5th member** (jurist) to Viceroy's council.
  * For legislation, Viceroy added **6 to 12 members** (at least half non-officials, chosen from loyal elites).
  * *Council Weaknesses* ⇒ Purely advisory; could not discuss important, executive, or financial/budget matters. Bills needed Viceroy's approval; SOS could disallow acts. Viceroy could issue emergency ordinances (6 months).

#### Provincial Government
* **1861 Act** ⇒ Restored legislative powers to Madras and Bombay.
* **Presidencies vs. Provinces** ⇒ Madras, Bombay, and Calcutta Presidencies enjoyed more rights, ruled by Governor + Executive Council of 3. Other provinces ruled by Lieutenant Governors or Chief Commissioners appointed by the GG.
* **Financial Devolution**:
  * **1870 (Mayo's First Step)** ⇒ Granted fixed sums out of central revenues to provinces to administer police, jails, education, medical services, and roads. (Beginning of bifurcating finances).
  * **1877 (Lytton)** ⇒ Transferred land revenue, excise, general admin, law, and justice departments to provinces. Provinces to receive fixed shares of stamp duty, excise, and income tax.
  * **1882** ⇒ Divided revenues into 3 heads: General (Central), Provincial (Provincial), and Divided (Shared). Central government retained supreme control.

#### Local Bodies
* **Rationale for Local Bodies** ⇒ Overcentralization financial stress; need to transplant European civic amenities; check rising nationalist demands; check political awakening of Indians; utilize local taxes to avoid central treasury burden.
* **Evolutionary Stages**:
  * **1864–1868** ⇒ Nominated bodies, headed by District Magistrates (DM); used as additional tax collection instruments.
  * **Mayo’s Resolution of 1870** ⇒ Permitted local taxation to balance budgets in transferred provincial departments (medical, education, roads). Emphasized "local interest, supervision and care".
  * **Ripon’s Resolution of 1882 (Father of Local Self-Government)**:
    * Advocated local bodies for political and popular education.
    * Urban and rural boards charged with definite duties and revenues.
    * Non-officials to be in the majority and act as Chairpersons.
    * Official interference kept to a minimum (checking acts, not dictating policies).
    * Official sanction retained for raising loans, property sales, or major works.
    * *Weaknesses* ⇒ Elected members remained in minority in district boards; DM continued as district board head; limited franchise; government held suspension powers. Bureaucracy was hostile, and **Curzon** later increased official control.
  * **Royal Commission on Decentralisation (1908)**:
    * *Village Panchayats* ⇒ Urged giving panchayats judicial, school, and village works powers with adequate income.
    * *Sub-district Boards* ⇒ Recommended establishing taluka/tehsil sub-district boards with separate revenues.
    * *Taxation* ⇒ Recommended removing tax restrictions and stopping regular provincial grants-in-aid (except for large projects).
    * *Municipalities* ⇒ To take responsibility for primary education.
  * **GoI Resolution of 1915** ⇒ Recommended changes left on paper.
  * **Resolution of May 1918** ⇒ Suggested local bodies be representative with real authority.
  * **Under Dyarchy (1919 Act)** ⇒ Local self-government became a **transferred** subject under popular Indian ministers.
    * *Failure* ⇒ Finance remained a **reserved** subject, starving Indian ministers of funds. Simon Commission noted deterioration of local finances and lack of panchayat progress (except in UP, Bengal, Madras).
  * **GoI Act 1935 and After** ⇒ Provincial autonomy provided impetus; popular ministries made funds available. Scrapped central-provincial tax demarcation.
    * *New Restrictions* ⇒ Placed limits on local terminal taxes, professions, and municipal properties; provincial governments ignored Decentralisation Commission's liberal taxation policies.
