*   **Tropical cyclones** = rapidly rotating, violent storms originating over tropical oceans in late summers.
*   **Key Characteristics**:
    *   **Closed low-pressure centre** with steep pressure gradients driving wind speeds.
        *   *Category 1 barometric pressure*: > 980 millibars.
        *   *Category 5 barometric pressure*: < 920 millibars.
    *   **Closed low-level atmospheric circulation** (cyclonic circulation) driven by rapid upward movement of hot, moist air + **Coriolis force**.
    *   **Strong winds (squalls)** = sudden, violent gusts.
    *   **Spiral arrangement of thunderstorms** producing **torrential rainfall**.

---

##### **Conditions Necessary for The Formation of A Tropical Cyclone**

*   **Core Requirements**:
    1.  **Large sea surface** with temperature **> 27°C**.
    2.  **Coriolis force** significant enough to create a cyclonic vortex.
    3.  **Pre-existing weak low-pressure area** or low-level cyclonic circulation.
    4.  **Low vertical wind shear**.
    5.  **Upper-level divergence**.

###### **Good Source of Latent Heat**
*   Ocean waters **> 27°C** extending **60–70 m deep** ⇒ supply continuous moisture & **latent heat of condensation** (fuel for storm).
*   Thick warm layer ⇒ prevents deep convection currents from churning and mixing cooler deep waters with warm surface waters.

###### **Why Do Tropical Cyclones Form Mostly on The Western Margins of The Oceans (Eastern Margins of The Continents) ?**
*   **Easterly trade winds** drag surface ocean waters westward ⇒ accumulates thick layer of warm water (**> 27°C**) on western margins (warm ocean currents).

###### **Why Are Tropical Cyclones Very Rare on The Eastern Margins Of The Oceans (Western Margins of The Continents) ?**
*   **Cold ocean currents** flow on eastern margins ⇒ lower surface temperatures below threshold needed for breeding cyclonic storms.
*   *Exceptional Case*: Strong **El Niño** years ⇒ weak Walker Cell ⇒ accumulation of warm waters in Eastern Pacific ⇒ strong hurricanes form there.
*   *South Atlantic & SE Pacific*: Low sea-surface temperatures due to cold currents + Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (**ITCZ**) seldom occurs ⇒ cyclones do not originate.

###### **Why Do Tropical Cyclones Weaken On Landfall ?**
*   Landfall ⇒ cut off from ocean moisture supply ⇒ deprivation of **latent heat of condensation** ⇒ storm dissipates.
*   **Latent Heat Cycle**: High insolation & warm climate ⇒ rising humid air parcel ⇒ ambient pressure ↓ with altitude ⇒ adiabatic lapse rate (cooling) ⇒ condensation of moisture ⇒ **latent heat of condensation released** ⇒ air parcel heated further, becomes less dense ⇒ further uplifted ⇒ draws in fresh surrounding moisture-laden air ⇒ cycle repeats.

###### **Coriolis Force**
*   Zero at the equator ⇒ increases with latitude.
*   **Significant enough** to create a storm (cyclonic vortex) at **≥ 5° latitude**.

###### **Why Do Cyclones Occur Mostly In Late Summers In The Northern Hemisphere ?**
1.  **High specific heat of water** + mixing delay ⇒ ocean waters attain maximum temperature in **August** (continents peak in June–July).
2.  **Doldrums (ITCZ)** over oceans are farthest from the equator ⇒ Coriolis force is maximized, enhancing whirling motion.

###### **Low-level Disturbances**
*   **Low-level disturbance** = low-pressure trough moving east-to-west as **easterly wave disturbances** within the **ITCZ** (thermal origin).
*   Serve as **seedling circulations** (birthplace) for tropical cyclones.
*   *Disturbance* = persistent group of thunderstorms with heavy rain & strong wind gusts.

###### **Why Fewer Cyclones Form Over The Arabian Sea As Compared To The Bay Of Bengal ?**
*   *Global Context*: ~80 cyclones form annually (majority in Western Pacific & Western Atlantic). North Indian Ocean has an annual average of ~5 cyclones (5–6% of global total).
*   **Bay of Bengal (BoB) vs. Arabian Sea ratio** = **4:1**.

*   **More Low-Level Disturbances In Bay Of Bengal**:
    *   BoB receives in-situ developments + **remnants of typhoons** from Northwest Pacific moving across South China Sea. NW Pacific has highest global typhoon frequency (~35%).
    *   Arabian Sea cyclones form in-situ or as remnants crossing the south peninsula; most BoB cyclones weaken over land, keeping migration to Arabian Sea low.

*   **Monsoons**:
    *   Monsoon winds cause upwelling and keep Arabian Sea cooler.

*   **The Surface Temperature Of The Bay Of Bengal Is Higher**:
    *   BoB surface temp is **22°C–31°C**. Arabian Sea is **1°C–2°C cooler** due to monsoon winds.

*   **Monsoon Winds Drive Away Moisture**:
    *   Strong winds drive evaporated moisture over Arabian Sea away towards India, preventing localized concentration.

*   **Vapour Pressure of Water And Rate of Evaporation**:
    *   *Vapour pressure* = counter-pressure exerted by water molecules on air. High vapour pressure = high rate of evaporation.
    *   Modified by:
        1.  *Air pressure*: High wind speeds ↓ air pressure (Bernoulli's principle) ⇒ ↑ evaporation.
        2.  *Temperature*: High water temp ⇒ water molecules gain kinetic energy ⇒ vapour pressure ↑ ⇒ ↑ evaporation.
        3.  *Salinity*: High salinity reduces kinetic energy of water molecules ⇒ ↓ evaporation.

*   **The Arabian Sea Surface Has Higher Salinity**:
    *   BoB receives huge freshwater discharge (Ganga, Brahmaputra, Irrawaddy, Godavari) ⇒ salinity in Northern BoB drops as low as **31 ppt**.
    *   Arabian Sea evaporation > precipitation + runoff ⇒ much higher surface salinity.

*   **Higher Stratification In The Bay Of Bengal**:
    *   Low density of freshwater forms a ~1 m uniform surface layer on BoB ⇒ **inhibits vertical mixing** of water.
    *   High salinity of Arabian Sea surface makes water denser ⇒ **increases vertical mixing** with cooler deep waters ⇒ keeps surface cooler.

###### **Temperature Contrast Between Air Masses**
*   Convergence of differing air masses along the **ITCZ** when farthest from equator ⇒ creates thermal/density instability ⇒ low-level disturbances intensify into a **depression**.

###### **Wind Shear**
*   **Vertical Wind Shear** = difference between wind speeds at different altitudes.
*   **Low shear** is critical; **high wind shear** prevents development of towering cumulonimbus clouds and cyclonic vortex.

###### **Why Is Convective Cyclogenesis (Tropical Cyclogenesis) Confined To Tropics ?**
*   **65%** of cyclonic activity occurs between **10° and 20° latitude** (equatorward of subtropical jet stream) where vertical wind shear is weak.
*   Temperate regions have westerlies ⇒ high wind shear ⇒ inhibits convective cyclogenesis.

###### **Why There Are Very Few Tropical Cyclones During The Southwest Monsoon Season ?**
*   **Large Vertical Wind Shear**: Strong westerlies in lower troposphere (< 3 km) + strong easterlies in upper troposphere (> 9 km) ⇒ inhibits convective cloud growth.
*   **Less Time For Development**: Cyclogenetic zone shifts to narrow North BoB near monsoon trough. Depressions make landfall over Odisha/West Bengal coast in **1–2 days**, preventing intensification into mature cyclones.

###### **Upper Air Disturbance**
*   Upper tropospheric cyclones (prevailing in summer, above 6000 m) or remnants of temperate cyclones (**upper tropospheric westerly troughs** / TUTT) move into tropics.
*   **Eastern side of troughs** has divergence ⇒ forces surface air ascent ⇒ intensifies thunderstorms.
*   **Westerly Troughs in Forecasting**:
    1.  *Fast-moving troughs* ⇒ create high vertical wind shear over disturbances ⇒ inhibits strengthening.
    2.  *Slow-moving troughs* ⇒ drive tropical cyclones eastward or northeastward.

##### **Convective Cyclogenesis (Development of Tropical Cyclones)**

*   **Cyclogenesis** = development or strengthening of atmospheric cyclonic circulation.
*   **Three major types**: Convective (tropical), Frontal (extratropical), and Mesocyclones (tornadoes/waterspouts).

###### **Stage 1: Tropical Disturbance**
*   Multiple thunderstorms over warm tropical seas merge under high insolation ⇒ form intense, localized thermal convection currents ⇒ low-pressure system.

###### **Stage 2: Tropical Depression (Maximum Sustained Wind Speed < 63 Kmph)**
*   System acquires a whirling motion due to **Coriolis force**.
*   *Sustained winds*: **< 63 kmph** (IMD uses a **3-minute average** for maximum sustained surface wind).

###### **Stage 3: Tropical Storm (63 Kmph < Maximum Sustained Wind Speed < 119 Kmph)**
*   Organized cyclonic shape emerges; **no eye** is present.
*   *Sustained winds*: **63–119 kmph**.

###### **Stage 4: Tropical Cyclone (Maximum Sustained Wind Speed > 119 Kmph)**
*   Wind speeds reach **> 119 kmph** ⇒ develops a central **eye** of relative calm and lowest surface pressure.

###### **Vertical Structure of A Tropical Cyclone**
1.  **Lowest layer (Inflow layer)**: Extends **up to 3 km**; draws in warm moist air; drives the storm.
2.  **Middle layer**: Extends **from 3 km to 7 km**; main cyclonic storm activity (rapid ascent).
3.  **Outflow layer**: Lies **above 7 km** (maximum outflow at **12 km and above**); movement of air is **anticyclonic**.

###### **Convective Cyclogenesis Mechanism – Early Stage**
*   Uplifted warm air cools adiabatically with altitude ⇒ moisture condenses ⇒ **latent heat released** ⇒ air gets warmer & lighter ⇒ further uplifted ⇒ sucks in fresh air rapidly ⇒ surrounding air undergoes deflection via **Coriolis force** ⇒ creates spiralling cyclonic vortex.

###### **Eye Formation**
*   **Centripetal acceleration** (inward pull of low pressure) vs. **centrifugal/tangential force** (outward push of high-speed curving winds) ⇒ balances to force a region of calmness at the centre.
*   *Relation*: Greater wind speed ⇒ greater centrifugal force ⇒ larger eye region.
*   Warm, moisture-deprived air becomes cold and dense aloft ⇒ **descends to surface** through the cylindrical eye and outer edges.

###### **Characteristics of The Eye**
*   Roughly circular zone of relative calm, light winds, and fair weather.
*   **Lowest barometric pressure** at surface occurs in the eye.
*   Little or no precipitation; blue skies/stars often visible.
*   Sinking air undergo **adiabatic compressional warming** ⇒ eye is **up to 10°C warmer** than surroundings at **12 km altitude**, but only **0°–2°C warmer** at surface.
*   Diameter: ranges from **8 km to > 200 km** (average **30–60 km**).

###### **Characteristics of Eyewall**
*   Roughly circular ring of deep convection surrounding the eye.
*   **Most violent region** of the cyclone ⇒ experiences **maximum sustained winds** + heaviest rainfall.

###### **Convective Cyclogenesis Mechanism – Mature Stage**
*   Spiralling horizontal winds organize into multiple distinct convective rain bands separated by calm regions. Cloud size decreases from centre to periphery.

###### **Central Dense Overcast (CDO)**
*   Dense, symmetric cirrus cloud shield (mostly hexagonal ice crystals) over eye and eyewall.
*   Symmetric, cold cloud tops obscure the eye on satellite until storm intensifies to very severe cyclonic levels.

###### **Rain Bands (Spiral Bands)**
*   Narrow, spiralling bands of convection aligned with horizontal winds.
*   *Direct circulation*: Warm, moist air converges at surface ⇒ ascends through bands ⇒ diverges aloft ⇒ descends on both sides.
*   *Composition*: **Cumulonimbus** at the core (highest rainfall) ⇒ **Nimbostratus** (prolonged rain) & **Cumulus** (least rain) at outer margins.

##### **Breeding Grounds for Tropical Cyclones**

*   Confined to warm tropical ocean currents:
    *   **Western Pacific** (*Typhoons*): Philippines, Eastern China, Japan (**highest global frequency**).
    *   **Western Atlantic & Eastern Pacific** (*Hurricanes*): SE Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico, East Pacific.
    *   **North Indian Ocean** (*Cyclones*): Bay of Bengal, Arabian Sea.
    *   **South Indian Ocean**: SE Africa, Madagascar, Mauritius.
    *   **South-West Pacific**: NW Australia (*Willy-willies*).

###### **Why does the northern hemisphere experience twice the number of tropical cyclones compared to the southern hemisphere ?**
1.  **Favourable Position Of The Thermal Equator**: Thermal equator lies mostly north of the equator due to pressure belts ⇒ warm ocean currents flow slight-to-the-north of the equator.
2.  **Water's High Specific Heat**: Southern hemisphere has vast oceanic surface ⇒ high specific heat absorbs energy slowly ⇒ Southern hemisphere oceans are cooler.
3.  **High Wind Shear In The Southern Hemisphere**: Stronger temperature gradients + less land friction ⇒ stronger wind speeds aloft (high vertical wind shear) ⇒ inhibits cyclone growth.
4.  **More Freshwater Flow Into The Northern Hemisphere**: Major rivers (Ganga, Mississippi, Amazon, Pearl, Mekong, Yellow, Yangtze) empty into NH basins ⇒ ↓ surface salinity ⇒ ↑ stratification ⇒ inhibits vertical mixing ⇒ surface waters remain warmer.

###### **Medicanes**
*   **Medicanes** = extratropical cyclones forming over Mediterranean Sea in fall/winter (1–2 times a year).
*   **Characteristics**: Symmetric structure, warm core, colder upper core than tropical cyclones, visible eye, winds at least tropical-storm strength; smaller diameter & lower speeds.

###### **Regional Names For Tropical Cyclones**
*   **Indian Ocean**: Cyclones
*   **Atlantic**: Hurricanes
*   **Western Pacific**: Typhoons
*   **Western Australia**: Willy-willies

##### **Path of Tropical Cyclones**

*   Path is steered by Coriolis force, trade winds, westerlies, and upper westerly troughs.
*   **Parabolic Path**: Parabolic axis runs parallel to isobars.
*   **In the Northern Hemisphere**:
    *   *Westward movement* (0°–20° latitude): Driven by **easterly trade winds**; speed **15–20 kmph**.
    *   *Northward turn* (~20° latitude): Coriolis force deflections to the right (east); speed slows to **< 10 kmph**.
    *   *North-eastward turn* (~25° latitude): Deflected further right by Coriolis force.
    *   *Eastward movement* (~30° latitude): Driven by **westerlies**; speeds accelerate to **≥ 25 kmph**.
    *   *Dissipation* (> 30° latitude): Subsides due to cold waters & high wind shear of temperate westerlies.
*   **Coastline Stalling**: Greatly expanded subtropical high-pressure + northward westerly migration ⇒ weakens steering winds ⇒ cyclone stalls near coast, dropping catastrophic rainfall.
*   **Fujiwhara Effect** = simultaneous formation of two storms close together ⇒ revolve around their common centre; smaller/less intense storm moves faster; may merge or repel.

###### **Which Sector Of The Cyclone Experiences The Strongest Winds ?**
*   **Right side of the storm** (relative to forward motion direction) over oceans.
*   *Cause*: Forward translation speed adds to rotational/swirling wind speed on the right side (e.g. 145 kmph stationary storm moving at 16 kmph ⇒ 161 kmph on right side, 129 kmph on left side).

##### **Damage Associated with Tropical Cyclones**

| Intensity Class | Damage Expected | Actions / Measures |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Depression / DD** | Minor damage to unsecured structures | Fishermen warned not to venture into open seas |
| **Cyclonic Storm** | Damage to thatched huts, broken tree branches, minor power/comm line damage | Total suspension of fishing operations |
| **Severe Cyclone** | Extensive damage to thatched huts, flooding of coastal escape routes | Coastal hutment dwellers moved to safer places |
| **VSCS** | Extensive damage to kutcha houses, flying debris hazard, minor rail/road traffic disruption | Mobilise coastal area evacuations |
| **ESCS** | Large-scale power/comm line disruption, extensive rail/road flooding | Coastal evacuations, suspend/divert traffic |
| **Super Cyclone** | Extensive structural collapse of buildings, total power/comm failure, bridge damage, wide-scale sea water inundation | Large-scale coastal evacuations, total rail/road suspension |

###### **Floods**
*   Precipitation of **50 cm/day** is common. Rain range: trace to **250 cm**.
*   *Rainfall intensity zone*: **85 cm/day** (within 50 km radius from centre); **35 cm/day** (between 50–100 km).

###### **Wind**
*   VSCS & Super Cyclones uproot major structures and disrupt communications.
*   *Past 100-year peak wind (India)*: **260 kmph** during **Oct 1999 Paradeep Super Cyclone**.

###### **Storm Surge**
*   **Storm Surge** = abnormal rise of coastal sea level as cyclone makes landfall, driven by high-velocity wind convergence dragging water column.
*   *Amplitude*: Catastrophic heights favored by stronger cyclones + **shallow coastal bathymetry** (low gradient).
*   *Impact*: Severe seawater inundation, loss of life, soil salinity rendering land agricultural unfit for **2–3 seasons**.

###### **What Is Storm Tide ?**
*   **Storm Tide** = combination of storm surge + astronomical high tide.
*   Landfall coinciding with astronomical high tide maximizes surge heights.

###### **Vulnerability Profiles**
*   *1970 Bhola Cyclone*: West Bengal & Bangladesh (Extremely Severe, deadliest global cyclone, **500,000+ deaths**).
*   *1999 Odisha Cyclone*: Paradeep (Super Cyclone, strongest recorded in NIO, **260 kmph**, **30,000+ deaths**).
*   *2008 Nargis Cyclone*: Myanmar Irrawaddy delta (Extremely Severe, costliest regional cyclone, **100,000+ deaths**).
*   *High Fatalities Factor*: Low-gradient delta regions face maximized storm surge risk.

###### **States Vulnerable to Cyclones**
*   **Frequency of SCS**: **Andhra Pradesh (AP) > Tamil Nadu (TN) > West Bengal (WB) > Odisha > Gujarat**.
*   **Frequency of CS**: **Odisha > AP > WB > TN > Gujarat**.
*   **West Coast**: **Gujarat** is the most vulnerable state.
*   **Peak Storm Seasons**: **May** and **October–November**.
    *   *SCS monthly frequency*: **November > May > October > December**.
    *   *CS monthly frequency*: **October > November > June**.
    *   *Depression monthly frequency*: **August > September > July**.
*   Andhra Pradesh and Odisha hold the highest risk of catastrophic landfall.

##### **Positive Effects of Tropical Cyclones**

###### **Heat Balance**
*   Winds & vertical convection regulate ocean surface temperatures, preserving global planetary heat balance.
*   Maintains survival range for marine primary producers and coral ecosystems.

###### **Tropical Cyclones Bring Rainfall To The Rain Shadow And Other Parched Regions**
*   Triggers precipitation over rain-shadow regions of Western Ghats, semi-arid Southern India (Telangana, Rayalaseema, Hyderabad-Karnataka, Vidarbha), and arid regions like Oman.

###### **Break Up Red Tide**
*   *Red Tide* = toxic algal blooms depleting ocean oxygen.
*   Cyclonic winds & waves churn the ocean, breaking up algal/bacterial patches and ending red tides early.

###### **Replenish Barrier Islands**
*   Moves sand, nutrients, and ocean-bottom sediment toward barrier islands, shifting them closer to mainland.

###### **Speed Dispersal To Faraway Locations**
*   Winds blow spores and seeds up to **1000 miles inland**, replenishing ecosystems post-fire/urbanisation.

##### **Changing Cyclone Patterns in The Indian Ocean**

###### **All Because of Global Warming**
*   South Indian Ocean baseline temperatures rose from **26.5°C** to **30°C–32°C**.
*   Threshold temperatures (**24°C–26°C**) are expanding further from the equator, widening cyclogenetic zones and intensification.
*   Exacerbated by global climate feedback loops: **El Niño, Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), Southern Annular Mode (SAM), and Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO)**.

###### **Cyclone Patterns in The Arabian Sea**
*   Historically, **~50%** of Arabian Sea storms dissipated quickly due to cold sea surface temperatures (Somali/Findlater current upwelling).
*   *Historical Frequency*: One Extremely Severe storm every 4–5 years.
*   *Current Frequency*: Highly compressed intervals (e.g. 5 extremely severe cyclones within 15 years, 1998–2013).
*   *Recent extreme examples*: **Nilofar** (2014, Cat 4), **Chapal** & **Megh** (2015, occurring within 1 week of each other).

###### **Role of El Nino Modoki**
*   *El Niño Modoki* (pseudo-El Niño) ⇒ suppresses cyclogenesis in Bay of Bengal but creates heavy atmospheric convergence over Arabian Sea ⇒ triggers high cyclone frequency in Arabian Sea.

##### **Twin Cyclones**

*   **Twin Cyclones** = pair of tropical cyclones forming on opposite sides of the Equator at nearly the same time.
*   *Example (May 2022)*: **Asani** (NH, counter-clockwise) and **Karim** (SH, clockwise).
*   Fueled by the **MJO** coupled with **Rossby waves** promoting high atmospheric convection.

###### **Fujiwhara Effect**
*   Two storms spinning in the same direction close together revolve around their common centre. Can result in merging or separate pathways.

###### **Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO)**
*   **MJO** = eastward-moving convective trough (series of thunderstorms) in the tropical band (**30°N to 30°S**) recurring every **30–60 days**.
*   *vs. ENSO*: ENSO is stationary in the Pacific (lasts seasons); MJO is a moving disturbance (multiple events per season, week-to-week variations).
*   Comprises a dipole: **enhanced convective phase** (high rainfall) and **suppressed convective phase**.

###### **Madden-Julian Oscillation’s Impact on Weather**
*   Associated with deep convection, heavy precipitation, and surface westerly wind bursts.
*   Modulates monsoon timing/strength and alters global cyclone frequency.
*   Can trigger jet stream shifts leading to cold outbreaks/flooding in North America.

###### **How Does MJO Help The Formation of Twin Cyclones?**
*   Enhanced MJO phase drives intense thunderstorm activity which provides the raw convective energy/circulation. Earth's rotation provides the spin, but MJO wind patterns intensify it.

###### **How ?**
*   Backside of MJO draws air inward at low levels against easterly trade winds ⇒ generates **low-level westerly wind bursts** along the Equator.
*   Westerly wind bursts combined with trade wind easterlies further away on either side ⇒ creates a low-level environment of extreme shear & enhanced rotation on both sides of the Equator.

##### **Warning of Tropical Cyclones**

*   **Detection Parameters**:
    1.  Fall in atmospheric pressure.
    2.  Increase in wind velocity.
    3.  Track and direction of storm.
*   **Monitoring Tools**: Aircraft weather radar + high-resolution satellite radiometers mapping cloud cover.
*   **Warning Windows**: Possible to detect from genesis, offering **48-hour advanced warnings**. 12-hour advanced track projections have lower precision.

###### **4-Stage IMD Warning System For Tropical Cyclones**
*   Introduced in **1999** for disaster managers.

1.  **Pre-Cyclone Watch**:
    *   Issued when depression forms over BoB, regardless of distance.
    *   Advanced window: At least **72 hours** before adverse weather.
    *   Frequency: At least **once a day**.
2.  **Cyclone Alert (Colour code Yellow)**:
    *   Advanced window: At least **48 hours** before.
    *   Storm position: Located **> 500 km** from coast.
    *   Frequency: Every **3 hours**.
3.  **Cyclone Warning (Colour code Orange)**:
    *   Advanced window: At least **24 hours** before.
    *   Storm position: Located **< 500 km** from coast.
    *   Specifies expected landfall place and time.
4.  **Post-landfall outlook (Colour code Red)**:
    *   Advanced window: **12 hours** before landfall.
    *   Storm position: Located **< 200 km** from coast. High precision.
