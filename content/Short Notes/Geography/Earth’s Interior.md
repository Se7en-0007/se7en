## The Internal Structure of The Earth
*   **Concentric Layers**: **Crust** (silicate solid) → **Mantle** (viscous molten) → **Outer Core** (viscous liquid) → **Inner Core** (dense solid).
*   **Mechanical Division**: **Lithosphere** → **Asthenosphere** (upper viscous mantle) → **Mesospheric Mantle** (below asthenosphere) → **Outer Core** → **Inner Core**.
*   **Chemical Division**: **Crust** → **Upper Mantle** → **Lower Mantle** → **Outer Core** → **Inner Core**.

### The Crust
*   **Mass & Volume**: **0.5–1.0%** of Earth's volume, **< 1%** of Earth's mass.
*   **Density**: Increases with depth; average crustal density $\approx$ **2.7 g/cm³** (Earth's average density = **5.51 g/cm³**).
*   **Thickness**:
    *   **Oceanic Crust**: **5–30 km** (dense, thin).
    *   **Continental Crust**: **50–70 km** (up to **70–100 km** in Himalayas; lighter, thick).
*   **Temperature**: Increases by **30°C/km** in upper crust → reaches **200°C–400°C** at mantle boundary.
*   **Composition**:
    *   **Upper Crust**: Outer sedimentary cover underlain by **acidic, crystalline, igneous & metamorphic rocks** (felsic).
    *   **Lower Crust**: **Basaltic & ultrabasic rocks** (mafic).
    *   **Felsic Rocks**: Rich in feldspar + quartz → enriched with **Si, O, Al, Na, K** (lighter elements).
    *   **Mafic Rocks**: Rich in **Mg + Fe** (heavier elements).
    *   **Sial vs. Sima** *(obsolete classification)*: Sial (**Silica + Aluminium**) = Continents (lighter) vs. Sima (**Silica + Magnesium**) = Oceans (heavier).

### Most Abundant Elements of The Earth's Crust
*   **Composition by Weight (%)**:
    1.  **Oxygen (O)**: **46.6%**
    2.  **Silicon (Si)**: **27.7%**
    3.  **Aluminium (Al)**: **8.1%**
    4.  **Iron (Fe)**: **5.0%**
    5.  **Calcium (Ca)**: **3.6%**
    6.  **Sodium (Na)**: **2.8%**
    7.  **Potassium (K)**: **2.6%**
    8.  **Magnesium (Mg)**: **1.5%**
*   **Mnemonic**: **OS AIC** (**O**xygen, **S**ilicon, **A**luminium, **I**ron, **C**alcium).

### Most Abundant Elements of The Earth
*   **Whole Earth Composition by Weight (%)**:
    1.  **Iron (Fe)**: **34.6%**
    2.  **Oxygen (O)**: **29.5%**
    3.  **Silicon (Si)**: **15.2%**
    4.  **Magnesium (Mg)**: **12.7%**
    5.  **Others**: Nickel (Ni), Sulphur (S), Titanium (Ti), etc.
*   **Mnemonic**: **iOS** (**I**ron, **O**xygen, **S**ilicon).

### The Mohorovicic (Moho) Discontinuity
*   **Boundary**: Separates **Crust** from **Asthenosphere** (upper mantle).
*   **Seismic Velocity**: Abrupt discontinuity/increase at boundary.
*   **Depth**: Average **8 km** (beneath oceans), **30 km** (beneath continents).
*   **Cause**: Mineralogical change from **feldspar-bearing rocks** (above) to **feldspar-free rocks** (below).

### Lithosphere
*   **Definition**: Rigid outer shell, thickness **10–200 km**; includes **Crust + Uppermost Solid Mantle**.
*   **Tectonics**: Broken into **tectonic (lithospheric) plates**; lateral movements → folding & faulting.
*   **Heat Engines**: Driven by **primordial heat** + **radioactive decay** (Uranium, Thorium, Potassium).

### The Mantle
*   **Mass & Volume**: **83%** of Earth's volume, **67%** of Earth's mass.
*   **Boundary**: Extends from Moho discontinuity to **2,900 km** (Gutenberg discontinuity).
*   **Structure**:
    *   **Upper Mantle**: Density **2.9–3.3 g/cm³**.
    *   **Lower Mantle**: Extends below asthenosphere; **Solid state**; density **3.3–5.7 g/cm³**.
*   **Composition**: Silicate rocks enriched in Fe & Mg relative to crust; **45% O, 23% Mg, 21% Si**.
*   **Convection**: Temperature gradient (**200°C** at top → **4,000°C** at core-mantle boundary) → **Convective material circulation** (ductile silicate behavior due to high heat) → powers **tectonic plate motions**.
*   **Seismicity**: High pressure inhibits seismicity, but deep earthquakes occur down to **670 km** in **subduction zones**.

### Asthenosphere
*   **Concept**: Highly viscous, mechanically weak, ductile upper mantle layer (**80–200 km** depth).
*   **Functions**:
    *   Aids **plate tectonics** & **isostatic adjustments** (buoyancy balance of topography).
    *   **Main source of magma** for volcanic eruptions.

### The Core
*   **Mass & Volume**: **16%** of Earth's volume, **33%** of Earth's mass.

### The Outer Core
*   **Depth**: **2,900 km to 5,100 km**.
*   **State**: **Liquid (fluid)** ← ambient pressure is insufficient to solidify it despite high temperatures.
*   **Composition**: **Iron + Nickel (Nife)** + trace light elements; density **9.9–12.2 g/cm³**.
*   **Temperature**: **4,400°C** (outer regions) to **6,000°C** (inner boundary).
*   **Heat Sources**: Gravitational compression + latent heat of crystallization (inner core growth) + radioactive decay (K, U, Th).
*   **Geodynamo**: Convection currents + **Coriolis effect** → generates **Earth's magnetic field**.

### The Inner Core
*   **Depth**: **5,100 km to Earth's centre** (6,378 km).
*   **State**: **Solid** ← crushing gravitational pressure overrides extreme temperatures to prevent melting.
*   **Composition**: Primarily **Iron (80%) + Nickel**; density **12.6–13.0 g/cm³**.
*   **Dynamics**: Rotates slightly faster than Earth's surface; too hot to sustain permanent magnetism.
*   **Temperature**: **6,000°C** (comparable to Sun's surface).
*   **Pressure Rule**: Ambient pressure $\uparrow$ → melting point of solids $\uparrow$ *(Exception: Ice, where pressure $\uparrow$ → melting point $\downarrow$)*.

### Seismic Discontinuities
*   **Definition**: Internal boundary zones where seismic waves abruptly change velocity/direction due to physical/chemical phase changes.
    1.  **Mohorovicic (Moho)**: Separates **Crust** from **Mantle**.
    2.  **Asthenosphere**: Low-velocity, highly ductile, viscous zone in the upper mantle.
    3.  **Gutenberg**: Separates **Mantle** from **Outer Core** (at 2,900 km).
    4.  **Lehmann**: Separates **Outer Core** (liquid) from **Inner Core** (solid) (at 5,100 km).

## Understanding The Earth’s Interior
*   **Significance**: Deciphering internal forces (heat, seismic, magnetic) is key to explaining surface-shaping processes.
*   **Endogenous Processes**: Powered by internal heat (e.g., folding, faulting, volcanism, earthquakes) → creates major features (mountains, plateaus, lakes).
*   **Exogenous Processes**: Powered by external atmospheric/solar forces (e.g., weathering, erosion).

### Importance of Understanding The Earth’s Interior
Provides scientific explanation for:
*   Internal & surface structural composition.
*   Frictional, seismic, and thermal forces driving geophysical phenomena.
*   Geodynamo mechanism and mineral distribution patterns.

### Geophysical Phenomena Like Volcanism, Earthquakes
*   **Earthquakes**: Tectonic plate displacements powered by **convection currents in the mantle**.
*   **Volcanism**: Magma venting through crustal fissures opened by tectonic movements.

### Earth’s Magnetic Field
*   **Source**: Convection currents in the **liquid outer core**.
*   **Ecological Role**: Shields Earth’s atmosphere from destructive **solar winds** → preserves atmosphere → **enables life**.

### Internal Structure of Various Solar System Objects
*   **Comparative Planetology**: Solar system objects formed from the **same nebular cloud** → share similar structural differentiation processes.

### Evolution & Present Composition of The Atmosphere
*   **Atmospheric Origins**: Gases ($O_2$, greenhouse gases like $CO_2$, ozone precursors) and pressure required for life were unlocked from the interior via **volcanic outgassing**.

### Mineral Exploration
*   **Genesis**: Deep mantle crystallization at **150–800 km** depths (e.g., diamonds).
*   **Transportation**: Brought to accessible crustal levels exclusively via **volcanic activity**.

## Direct Sources of Information
*   **Deep Mining**: Extremely shallow; deepest gold mines in South Africa (**Mponeng** & **TauTona**) reach only **3.9 km**.
*   **Deep Drilling**: Deepest is **Kola Superdeep Borehole** (Soviet Union, 1970s, Kola Peninsula) reaching only **12 km**.
*   **Volcanic Eruptions**: Direct ejection of magma from asthenospheric depths → provides physical samples of upper mantle.

## Indirect Sources of Information

### Seismic Waves
*   **Principle**: **Most critical indirect source**. Wave velocity is directly proportional to medium density and elasticity ($Velocity \propto Elasticity, Density$). Waves undergo **reflection** (bouncing) or **refraction** (bending) at density/compositional boundaries.

### Meteorites
*   **Correlation**: Co-genetic origin with Earth from the same nebula.
*   **Evidence**: Meteoroids burn away outer shells during atmospheric entry, exposing **dense iron-nickel cores** → confirms Earth's metallic core model.

### Gravitation
*   **Gravity Anomaly**: Variations in gravitational pull relative to latitude and local crustal mass distribution → maps **mass anomalies in Earth’s crust**.

### Magnetic field
*   **Indication**: Shifts in magnetic field lines and the geodynamo effect map core convective dynamics.

### Sources of Earth’s Internal Heat

#### Radioactive Decay
*   **Mechanism**: Disintegration of radioactive isotopes (primarily in crust and mantle).
*   **Fission Theory**: Potential self-sustained nuclear fission of concentrated Uranium at the core-mantle boundary.
*   **Contribution**: Generates **>50%** of Earth's total internal heat.
*   *Note*: **No nuclear fusion** occurs inside Earth (insufficient pressure and mass).

#### Primordial Heat
*   **Sourcing**: Remnant kinetic energy from planetesimal impacts during accretion + internal friction (heavy Fe sinking, light Si rising) + latent heat of crystallization of solidifying core.

#### Tidal Friction
*   **Process**: Gravitational pulls of Moon & Sun cause solid-body and oceanic flexing → frictional energy dissipation → converts to heat.
*   **Consequence**: Dissipation of rotational energy → Earth's rotation slows by **0.002 seconds per century**.

## Seismic Waves (Earthquake Waves)
*   **Definition**: Vibrational energy waves generated by earthquakes, volcanic activities, mass movements, or anthropogenic explosions.
*   **Main Classification**: **Body Waves** vs. **Surface Waves**.

### Body Waves
*   **Genesis**: Released at the **focus (hypocentre)** → travels in all directions through the Earth's 3D interior.
*   **Subcategories**: **P-waves** (Primary) and **S-waves** (Secondary).

### Primary Waves (P-Waves)
*   **Acoustic Analogy**: **Longitudinal, compressional, or pressure waves**; wave propagation is parallel to particle displacement (similar to sound waves).
*   **Physics**: Induces alternating **squeezing (compression)** and **stretching (rarefaction)** → creates transient density variations.
*   **Characteristics**: **High frequency**, **least destructive**, vertical trembling (up-down motion).
*   **Propagation**: Penetrates **all physical phases (Solids > Liquids > Gases)**. Velocity increases with medium elasticity/shear strength.
*   **Velocity Range**: **5 to 8 km/s** in crust; up to **13.5 km/s** in lower mantle; drops to **11 km/s** in inner core.

### Why Do P-Waves Travel Faster Than S-Waves ?
*   **P-waves** are compressional → force is applied parallel to wave travel → efficient energy transfer → **~1.7 times faster** than S-waves.
*   **S-waves** are shear/transverse → force is applied perpendicular to wave travel → slower energy transmission.

### P-Waves As An Earthquake Warning
*   **Lead Time**: Early detection of fast, non-destructive P-waves on crustal seismographs provides a **60–90 second warning window** before slower, highly destructive S-waves and surface waves arrive.

### Secondary Waves (S-Waves)
*   **Ripple Analogy**: **Transverse, shear, or distortional waves**; particle vibration is perpendicular to wave propagation (similar to water ripples/light waves).
*   **Physics**: Distorts the medium by creating alternating **crests and troughs**.
*   **Characteristics**: **High frequency**, moderately destructive, horizontal trembling (side-to-side motion).
*   **Propagation Constraint**: **Cannot pass through fluids (liquids & gases)** ← fluids have zero shear strength. Speed is proportional to the shear strength of solid rock layers.

### Surface waves (L-Waves)
*   **Genesis**: Secondary waves generated when body waves interact with boundary lithospheric surface rocks; travel **strictly along the surface**.
*   **Characteristics**: **Longest wavelength (long-period)**, low-frequency transverse waves; slow attenuation (loses energy slower with distance compared to 3D body waves).
*   **Destructiveness**: Possess the **largest amplitude** → **most destructive** of all seismic waves.

#### Love Waves
*   **Motion**: Fastest surface wave; vibrates ground strictly in a **horizontal side-to-side** plane.

#### Rayleigh Waves
*   **Motion**: Rolls along the ground like ocean waves → displaces ground in an **elliptical up-and-down + side-to-side motion** parallel to wave travel.
*   **Impact**: Responsible for the **majority of earthquake shaking and structural damage**.

### How Do Seismic Waves Help In Understanding The Earth’s Interior ?
*   **Analysis Methods**: Tracking arrival times, ray path curvature (refraction), and wave absences (**shadow zones**).
*   **Velocity Changes**: Sharp changes in velocity at depth reveal shifts in **composition and density**.
*   **Directional/Phase Changes**: Abrupt refraction or wave termination reveals structural **phase shifts** (solid $\leftrightarrow$ liquid).

---

### Emergence of Shadow Zone of P-Waves & S-Waves

#### S Wave Shadow Zone
*   **Characteristics**: Completely absent in seismographs located **beyond 103°** from the epicentre.
*   **Discovery**: Direct proof of a **liquid outer core** which absorbs/attenuates shear S-waves.

#### P Wave Shadow Zone
*   **Characteristics**: Forms an angular band of omission between **103° and 142°** from the epicentre.
*   **Discovery**: Caused by strong **downward refraction** as P-waves slow down at the solid-to-liquid boundary (mantle-outer core transition).

#### No Shadow Zone
*   **Characteristics**: Region **within 103°** of the epicentre; records **both P and S-waves** → proves the crust and mantle are solid.

#### Shadow Zone For Both P Wave and S Wave
*   **Characteristics**: Seismographs **beyond 142°** record only P-waves (not S-waves). 
*   **Significance**: The complete window of **103° to 142°** acts as a combined shadow zone where neither direct P nor direct S-waves arrive.

---

### Why Do Sound Waves Travel Faster In A Denser Medium Whereas Light Travels Slower ?
*   **Sound (Mechanical Compressional Wave)**: Greater density → stronger lattice/material elasticity → faster transmission of compressional/rarefaction cycles → **velocity increases with density**.
*   **Light (Transverse Electromagnetic Wave)**: Greater density → increased subatomic scattering and effective path length → higher refractive index → **velocity decreases with density**.
