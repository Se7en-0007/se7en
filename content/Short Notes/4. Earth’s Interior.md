### 4.1. The Internal Structure of The Earth
* **Concentric Layers**: Distinct physical/chemical properties:
  * **Crust**: Silicate solid
  * **Mantle**: Viscous molten rock (ductile)
  * **Outer Core**: Viscous liquid
  * **Inner Core**: Dense solid
* **Layer Classification**:
  * **Mechanical**: Lithosphere → Asthenosphere (upper viscous mantle) → Mesospheric Mantle (lower mantle) → Outer Core → Inner Core
  * **Chemical**: Crust → Upper Mantle → Lower Mantle → Outer Core → Inner Core

### The Crust
* **Volume & Mass**: **0.5–1.0%** of Earth's volume, **< 1%** of mass.
* **Density**: Increases with depth; average crust density **2.7 g/cm³** (Earth average: **5.51 g/cm³**).
* **Thickness**: 
  * **Oceanic Crust**: **5–30 km** (thin, dense basaltic/mafic rocks).
  * **Continental Crust**: **50–70 km** (thick, lighter felsic rocks); up to **70–100 km** in Himalayas.
* **Temperature**: Increases with depth; **200°C to 400°C** at mantle boundary. Rate: **+30°C per km** in upper crust.
* **Composition**: 
  * Sedimentary cover → Crystalline igneous/metamorphic rocks (acidic/felsic) → Basaltic/ultrabasic lower layer.
  * **Continental Crust**: Lighter (felsic) sodium potassium aluminium silicate rocks (e.g., **granite**); formerly called **Sial** (Silica + Aluminium; obsolete).
  * **Oceanic Crust**: Dense (mafic) iron magnesium silicate igneous rocks (e.g., **basalt**); formerly called **Sima** (Silica + Magnesium; obsolete).
  * **Felsic**: Rich in feldspar/quartz, lighter elements (Si, O, Al, Na, K).
  * **Mafic**: Enriched in heavier elements (Mg, Fe).

### Most Abundant Elements of the Earth's Crust
* **Mnemonic (OS AIC)**:
  1. **Oxygen (O)**: **46.6%**
  2. **Silicon (Si)**: **27.7%**
  3. **Aluminium (Al)**: **8.1%**
  4. **Iron (Fe)**: **5.0%**
  5. **Calcium (Ca)**: **3.6%**
  6. **Sodium (Na)**: **2.8%**
  7. **Potassium (K)**: **2.6%**
  8. **Magnesium (Mg)**: **1.5%**

### Most Abundant Elements of the Earth
* **Mnemonic (iOS)** (Whole Earth):
  1. **Iron (Fe)**: **34.6%**
  2. **Oxygen (O)**: **29.5%**
  3. **Silicon (Si)**: **15.2%**
  4. **Magnesium (Mg)**: **12.7%**
  * Remaining: **Nickel (Ni)**, **Sulphur (S)**, **Titanium (Ti)**, etc.

### The Mohorovicic (Moho) Discontinuity
* **Boundary**: **Crust** ⇋ **Asthenosphere** (upper mantle).
* **Characteristics**: Marked by sharp increase in **seismic velocity**.
* **Depth**: Avg **8 km** beneath oceans, **30 km** beneath continents.
* **Cause**: Rock composition transition: **feldspar-bearing** rocks (crust) → **feldspar-free** rocks (mantle).

### Lithosphere
* **Definition**: Rigid outer layer, **10–200 km** thickness; includes **crust** + **uppermost solid mantle**.
* **Tectonic Plates**: Lithosphere is fractured into plates; movements cause **folding** & **faulting**.
* **Heat Engine**: Plate tectonics driven by **primordial heat** + **radioactive decay** (U, Th, K) in crust and mantle.

### The Mantle
* **Mass & Volume**: **83%** of Earth’s volume, **67%** of mass.
* **Extents**: From **Moho discontinuity** to **2,900 km** depth.
* **State & Density**:
  * **Upper Mantle**: Density **2.9–3.3 g/cm³**.
  * **Lower Mantle**: Extends below asthenosphere; **solid state**; density **3.3–5.7 g/cm³**.
* **Composition**: **45% Oxygen**, **23% Magnesium**, **21% Silicon**; rich in iron/magnesium relative to crust.
* **Temperature**: **200°C** (upper boundary) to **4,000°C** (core-mantle boundary).
* **Convective Circulation**: Temperature differential drives solid-state convection (mantle silicates are **ductile** at high temps) → powers **plate tectonics** at surface.
* **Deep Seismicity**: High pressure should stop earthquakes, but earthquakes are recorded down to **670 km** in subduction zones.

### Asthenosphere
* **Definition**: Upper portion of mantle (below lithosphere), extending **80–200 km** depth (*astheno* = weak).
* **Properties**: Highly viscous, mechanically weak, **ductile**; density > crust.
* **Functions**:
  * Facilitates lithospheric plate movements and **isostatic adjustments** (buoyancy balance of crustal blocks).
  * Main reservoir of **magma** erupted via volcanic vents.

### The Core
* **Mass & Volume**: **16%** of Earth’s volume, **33%** of mass.

### The Outer Core
* **Extents**: **2,900 km** to **5,100 km** depth.
* **State**: **Liquid (fluid)**; ambient pressure is insufficient to solidify iron at its extreme temperature.
* **Composition**: Iron mixed with Nickel (**Ni-Fe**) + trace light elements.
* **Properties**: Density **9.9–12.2 g/cm³**; temperature **4,400°C** (outer) to **6,000°C** (near inner core).
* **Heat Sources**: Gravitational compression, latent heat of crystallization (released as inner core grows), and radioactive decay of K, U, Th.
* **Geodynamo**: Convection currents + **Coriolis effect** (Earth's rotation) → generates **Earth's magnetic field**.

### The Inner Core
* **Extents**: **5,100 km** to Earth's center (**6,378 km**).
* **State**: **Solid**; immense gravitational pressure overrides temperature to prevent melting.
* **Composition**: Primarily **Iron (80%)** and **Nickel** (**Ni-Fe**).
* **Properties**: Density **12.6–13.0 g/cm³**; temperature **~6,000°C** (as hot as Sun's surface, 1,000°C hotter than old estimates); rotates slightly faster than Earth's surface; too hot to retain permanent magnetic field.
* **Melting Point Rule**: Ambient Pressure ↑ → Melting Point ↑ (Exception: **Ice** where Pressure ↑ → Melting Point ↓).

### Seismic Discontinuities
* **Definition**: Structural boundary zones marked by sudden changes in seismic wave velocity/direction due to abrupt density, chemical, or phase shifts.
  1. **Mohorovicic Discontinuity (Moho)**: Crust ⇋ Mantle transition.
  2. **Asthenosphere boundary**: Lithosphere ⇋ Viscous upper mantle transition.
  3. **Gutenberg Discontinuity**: Mantle ⇋ Outer Core boundary.
  4. **Lehmann Discontinuity**: Outer Core (liquid) ⇋ Inner Core (solid) boundary.

### 4.2. Understanding the Earth’s Interior
* **Objective**: Understanding interior structure & forces (heat, seismic, magnetic) is essential to explain surface geological processes and earth evolution.
* **Process Divisions**:
  * **Endogenous Processes** (*Endo* = in): Forces originating from deep within Earth (e.g., folding, faulting → mountains, plateaus, lakes, volcanism, earthquakes).
  * **Exogenous Processes** (*Exo* = out): Forces acting on or above surface (e.g., weathering, erosion).

### Importance of Understanding the Earth’s Interior
* **Key Insights**:
  * Explains tectonic movements and catastrophic geophysical phenomena (**volcanism, earthquakes**).
  * Explains origin and behavior of **Earth's magnetic field**.
  * Aids in mapping the **internal structure of other solar system bodies**.
  * Traces the **evolution & composition of the atmosphere** (volcanic outgassing released respiration/greenhouse gases, ozone, and established surface pressure).
  * Drives **mineral exploration** (heavy minerals like **diamonds** form in mantle at **150–800 km** depth, brought to surface by volcanic conduits).

### Geophysical Phenomena Like Volcanism, Earthquakes
* **Tectonic-Current Link**: Mantle convection currents transfer internal heat → drives lithospheric plate movement → generates stress release (**earthquakes**) and magma ascent (**volcanism**).

### Earth’s Magnetic Field
* **Convective Source**: Fluid motion in liquid outer core → **convection currents** → generates magnetic field.
* **Atmospheric Protection**: Magnetic field deflects highly energetic **solar winds**, preserving the atmosphere and shielding surface life.

### Internal Structure of Various Solar System Objects
* **Common Origin**: Entire solar system formed from a singular **nebular cloud** → planetary interiors share similar evolutionary/gravitational differentiation mechanisms as Earth.

### Evolution & Present Composition of The Atmosphere
* **Volcanic Outgassing**: Core/mantle heat drives volcanism → releases **O₂**, **CO₂**, greenhouse gases, water vapor, and **ozone** precursor elements → builds necessary atmospheric pressure and temperature regulation.

### Mineral Exploration
* **Mantle Sourced**: High-pressure minerals (e.g., **diamonds**) form at depths of **150–800 km** within the mantle → transported rapidly to the crust via violent **volcanic eruptions**.

### 4.3. Direct Sources of Information
* **Deep Mining**: Deepest mines are gold mines in **South Africa**—**Mponeng** (deepest, **3.9 km**) and **TauTona** (second deepest). Extremely limited coverage.
* **Deep Drilling**: Deepest borehole is the **Kola Superdeep Borehole** (Soviet Union, 1970s) on the Kola Peninsula (White Sea ⇋ Barents Sea) → reached only **12 km**.
* **Volcanic Eruptions**: Direct ejection of deep magmatic materials onto the surface for analysis.

### 4.4. Indirect Sources of Information
* **Gravitation & Diameter**: Calculations of mass and size allow estimation of internal pressures.
* **Geysers, Hot Springs, Volcanism**: Surface heat manifestations indicating a thermal interior.

### Seismic Waves
* **Primary Tool**: Seismic wave analysis is the **most crucial indirect source** for mapping deep earth.
* **Wave Physics**: Wave velocity varies with medium **elasticity (shear strength)** and **density**:
  * **Density & Elasticity ↑ → Velocity ↑**.
  * Wave path bends (**refracts**) or bounces (**reflects**) at boundaries of contrasting density.

### Meteorites
* **Common Parentage**: Meteorites and Earth accreted from the same initial **nebular cloud**.
* **Core Analogy**: Atmospheric friction burns outer meteoric layers, exposing their dense, heavy metal cores → confirms composition of Earth's metallic core.

### Gravitation
* **Mass Distribution**: Earth’s uneven mass distribution causes variations in local gravitational force.
* **Gravity Anomaly**: Difference between observed gravity and expected theoretical value → maps mass anomalies in Earth's crust.

### Magnetic field
* **Geodynamo Analysis**: External magnetic field changes and shifts provide clues to the convective state of the liquid iron outer core.

### Sources of Earth’s Internal Heat

### Radioactive Decay
* **Fission Energy**: Natural nuclear decay of **Uranium**, **Thorium**, and **Potassium** isotopes inside the **crust** and **mantle** provides **> 50%** of total internal heat.
* **Georeactor Theory**: Possible concentration of Uranium at mantle base to sustain localized nuclear fission.
* **No Fusion**: Extreme pressure/temp for **nuclear fusion** is impossible inside Earth; Earth is not massive enough.

### Primordial Heat
* **Formational Kinetic Energy**: Kinetic energy of impacting comets/meteorites converted to heat during Earth's accretion.
* **Frictional Sinking**: Heat generated by dense materials (iron) sinking and lighter materials (silicon) rising.
* **Crystallization**: **Latent heat** released as the outer core cools and solidifies into the inner core.

### Tidal Friction
* **Gravitational Flexing**: Moon & Sun's gravitational pull creates a slight, daily structural bulge in Earth's solid body and oceans.
* **Heat Generation**: Flexing friction converts rotational energy into heat, causing a minor rise in internal temperature.
* **Rotational Slowdown**: Frictional drag slows Earth's rotation rate by **0.002 seconds per century**.

### 4.5. Seismic Waves (Earthquake Waves)
* **Definition**: Energy waves traveling through Earth's layers, triggered by earthquakes, volcanism, magmatic movements, landslides, or artificial explosions.
* **Primary Division**: **Body Waves** (internal) and **Surface Waves** (crustal).

### Body Waves
* **Definition**: Waves originating at the earthquake's focus (**hypocentre**), propagating outward in all directions through the Earth's interior.
* **Types**: **P-waves** (primary) and **S-waves** (secondary).

### Primary Waves (P-Waves)
* **Characteristics**: Fastest seismic waves; recorded **first** on a seismograph.
* **Wave Type**: **Longitudinal / compressional / pressure** waves (vibration direction is **parallel** to wave propagation).
* **Physical Effect**: Squeezing (**compression**) and stretching (**rarefaction**) of materials → creates localized density and pressure changes.
* **Destructiveness**: High frequency, but **least destructive** body wave. Trembling motion on surface is **vertical (up-down)**.
* **Propagation Mediums**: Travels through **all states of matter** (Solids, Liquids, Gases).
* **Velocity Relationship**: Depends on shear strength (elasticity); **Solids > Liquids > Gases**.
  * Speed range: **5 to 8 km/s** in crust, up to **13.5 km/s** in lower mantle, dropping to **11 km/s** in inner core.
  * Travel **~1.7x faster** than S-waves (compression waves transmit kinetic energy more easily).

### Why Do P-Waves Travel Faster Than S-Waves?
* **Energy Transmission**: P-waves are compressional, pushing particles directly in the direction of travel → fast energy transfer. S-waves are shear (transverse), displacing particles **perpendicular** to propagation → slower transmission through solid elastic bonds.

### P-Waves As An Earthquake Warning
* **Early Warning Systems**: Fast-traveling, non-destructive P-waves reach stations before slower, highly destructive S-waves and surface waves.
* **Warning Window**: Time gap of **60 to 90 seconds** (depending on focal depth/distance) allows automated shutdown of critical systems before destructive waves hit.

### Secondary Waves (S-Waves)
* **Characteristics**: Arrive second at seismographs; also called **transverse / shear / distortional** waves.
* **Vibration Mode**: Particle vibration is **perpendicular** to wave propagation (similar to water ripples or light).
* **Physical Effect**: Generates crests and troughs, causing severe shear distortion of the medium.
* **Destructiveness**: High frequency; **moderately destructive** (causes **side-to-side/horizontal** surface shaking).
* **Propagation Constraint**: **Cannot pass through fluids (liquids/gases)**. Fluids lack shear strength and cannot support shear stresses. Travels only through solid structures at speeds proportional to solid shear strength.

### Surface waves (L-Waves)
* **Origin**: Body waves reaching the surface and interacting with surface rocks → generates long-period (**L-waves**).
* **Characteristics**: Slowest seismic waves, recorded **last** on a seismograph.
* **Wave Mode**: Low-frequency, long-wavelength transverse (shear) waves. Propagate exclusively along Earth’s surface, dying out rapidly at depth.
* **Energy Retention**: Retain energy over longer distances than body waves (propagate in a 2D plane rather than 3D sphere).
* **Destructiveness**: Extremely high amplitude (displacement) → **most destructive** of all seismic waves.

### Love Waves
* **Vibration**: Fastest surface waves. Moves the ground in a horizontal, **side-to-side** motion (perpendicular to wave direction).

### Rayleigh Waves
* **Vibration**: Roll along the ground (similar to lake/ocean waves). Moves particles in an elliptical path—**up-and-down** and **side-to-side** along the wave direction.
* **Impact**: Responsible for the vast majority of surface shaking, structural collapse, and earthquake **damage**.

### How Do Seismic Waves Help In Understanding The Earth’s Interior?
* **Data Sources**: Analysis of arrival times, velocity jumps, reflection/refraction, and the existence of distinct **shadow zones**.
* **Velocity Discontinuities**: Sudden shifts in velocity map boundaries of contrasting **composition and density**.
* **Phase Discontinuities**: Interruption or extreme bending of wave propagation reveals change in **phase (solid vs. liquid)**.

### Emergence of Shadow Zone of P-Waves & S-Waves

### S Wave Shadow Zone
* **Mechanism**: S-waves cannot travel through liquids (shear waves are attenuated/absorbed).
* **Zone Extents**: **Beyond 103°** from the epicentre, no direct S-waves are recorded.
* **Significance**: This massive shadow zone (covering almost 40% of the globe) **proved the existence of a liquid outer core**.

### P Wave Shadow Zone
* **Mechanism**: P-waves can travel through liquid, but refract (bend) sharply due to the sudden density drop from the semisolid mantle to the liquid outer core.
* **Zone Extents**: A narrow band around Earth **between 103° and 142°** from the epicentre receives no direct P-waves.

### No Shadow Zone
* **Zone Extents**: **Within 103°** of the epicentre, both P and S-waves are recorded clearly.
* **Significance**: Confirms that the Earth's crust and mantle are **solid** (able to transmit shear S-waves).

### Shadow Zone For Both P Wave and S Wave
* **Zone Extents**: The ring **between 103° and 142°** away from the epicentre receives neither direct P-waves nor S-waves.
* **Significance**: Represents a combined shadow zone. Seismographs located **beyond 142°** record only refracted P-waves (proving a solid inner core density increase that speeds them up, but no S-waves, which are blocked entirely by the outer core).

### Why Do Sound Waves Travel Faster In A Denser Medium Whereas Light Travels Slower?
* **Sound (Mechanical Wave)**: Travels by mechanical compression/rarefaction. Higher density of a substance is generally associated with much higher **elasticity**. High elasticity allows the medium to quickly spring back during compression/rarefaction, boosting sound velocity.
  * *Exception*: Mercury is denser than iron, but less elastic. Sound travels faster in iron.
* **Light (Electromagnetic Wave)**: Transverse electromagnetic wave. Travel is hindered by matter. Higher physical density increases the effective path length of light (due to interactions with atomic particles), which results in a **higher refractive index** and **lower light velocity**.
