## Indian Polity

### PIL
* **Why in News** ⇒ During Sabarimala review petitions hearing, **Supreme Court (SC)** raised concerns over PIL misuse & questioned petitioner's *locus standi*.
* **SC Observations** ⇒ Criticised conversion of PIL into:
  * "Private interest litigation"
  * "Publicity interest litigation"
  * "Paisa interest litigation"
  * "Political interest litigation"
  * **Filing Basis** ⇒ Mere newspaper articles should not trigger PIL filings.
* **Sabarimala Case** ⇒ **Indian Young Lawyers Association** challenged the prohibition of women's entry; **2018 (5-Judge Bench)** upheld women's right to enter, stating **untouchability (Article 17)** does not apply.
* **Way Forward (PIL Filtration)**:
  * Check if petitioner is **bonafide**
  * Impose **costs** on frivolous/motivated petitions
  * Mandatory **affidavits** disclosing petitioner's interest & **prior PIL history**
  * Distinguish genuine public interest from private/political gains.
* **Meaning and Nature (Prelims Context)**:
  * **PIL** = Judicial innovation, **not defined** in Constitution or any statute.
  * **Jurisdiction** ⇒ Filed under **Article 32** (SC) and **Article 226** (High Courts).
  * **Core Articles Linked** ⇒ Articles **14, 19, 21** & Directive Principles of State Policy (**DPSPs**).
  * **Nature** ⇒ **Non-adversarial**; focuses on public interest/injury over private disputes.
* **Origin & Evolution**:
  * **USA** ⇒ 1960s; **India** ⇒ Late 1970s–early 1980s.
  * **Pioneering Judges** ⇒ **Justice P.N. Bhagwati** & **Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer**.
* **Key Evolution Cases**:
  * ***Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)*** ⇒ 1st PIL case (focused on undertrial prisoners).
  * ***S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (1981)*** ⇒ Relaxed *locus standi* rule.
* **Procedure and Features**:
  * **Flexibility** ⇒ Simple pleadings, **epistolary jurisdiction** (letters/postcards treated as petitions), relaxed court fees.
  * **Objective** ⇒ Redress public injury & enforce public duty.
* **Important PIL Precedents**:
  * ***Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. UoI (1984)*** (Bonded labour)
  * ***Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation*** (Pavement dwellers' rights)
  * ***M.C. Mehta v. UoI*** (Environmental protection)
  * ***Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)*** (Sexual harassment guidelines)
  * ***NALSA v. UoI*** (Transgender rights)
  * ***DC Wadhwa v. State of Bihar (1986)*** (Re-promulgation of ordinances)

### Bulldozer Justice
* **Why in News** ⇒ A 5-year-old gifted a bulldozer replica to Uttar Pradesh CM, renewing the debate on "bulldozer justice".
* **Bulldozer Justice** = Normalisation of **extrajudicial state actions** bypassing due process.
  * **Historical Precedent** ⇒ **Turkman Gate demolitions (1976)**.
  * **Trend** ⇒ Instant retributive justice favored over slow judicial processes.
* **Judicial Delays Factor**:
  * **Case Pendency** ⇒ **5.5 Crore** total pending cases (**SC has ~90,000**).
  * **Judge-to-Population Ratio** ⇒ **15 judges per million** population (**India Justice Report 2025**).
  * **Law Commission Recommendation (1987)** ⇒ **50 judges per million** population.
* **Concerns Highlighted**:
  * State acting as **investigator + judge + executioner** simultaneously.
  * Undermining **Rule of Law**, legal safeguards, and institutional credibility.
  * Unanswered question: Why were unauthorized structures allowed to exist in the first place?
* **Way Forward** ⇒ Enhance **judicial capacity**, fill vacancies, upgrade infrastructure, stream investigative processes, and establish **fast-track courts**.

### Governor & Hung Assembly
* **Why in News** ⇒ Following **2026 Tamil Nadu Assembly elections**, Governor delayed inviting the **TVK leader** to form the government.
* **Hung Assembly** = Scenario where no single political party wins an absolute majority.
  * **Constitutional Gap** ⇒ Constitution empowers the Governor under **Article 164(1)** but **does not prescribe a detailed procedure** for government formation.
  * **Sources of Guidance** ⇒ Conventions, Commission recommendations, and SC judgments.
  * **Primary Duty** ⇒ Governor must explore all options (coalitions, independent MLAs) to form a **stable government**; **Article 356 (President's Rule)** is a last resort.
* **Order of Preference (Sarkaria Commission 1988 & Punchhi Commission 2010)**:
  1. **Pre-poll alliance** of parties.
  2. **Single largest party** claiming support.
  3. **Post-poll coalition** of parties.
  4. **Post-poll alliance** with outside support.
* **Floor Test Guidelines**:
  * **Majority Test** ⇒ Must occur on the floor of the Legislative Assembly, not in the Governor's office (**Committee of Governors**).
  * ***S.R. Bommai v. UoI (1994)*** ⇒ Government must enjoy the confidence of the House; floor test to be conducted within **24–48 hours**, live-streamed, **not by secret ballot**.
  * **Article 174(2)(b)** ⇒ Governor's power to dissolve the Legislative Assembly.
  * ***Rameshwar Prasad (2006)*** ⇒ Confirmed floor test is the sole method to determine majority.
* **Way Forward** ⇒ Constitutional requirement = Majority of members present and voting. **Justice Kurian Joseph Committee** recommended a **new Schedule** to codify Governor’s discretionary powers.

## Parliament

### Legal Fiction
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC in ***Registrar Cane Cooperative Societies v. Gurdeep Singh Narval (2026)*** reiterated that a legal fiction (deeming provision) must be confined *strictly* to its specified purpose.
* **Legal Fiction Definition** = A legal device treating something as true for a specific legal purpose, even if factually untrue.
  * ***Bengal Immunity Co. Ltd. v. State of Bihar (1955)*** ⇒ Legal fiction is created for a definite purpose and cannot extend beyond its legitimate field.
  * ***J.K. Cotton Spinning and Weaving Mills Ltd. v. UoI (1987)*** ⇒ Deeming fiction must be confined to its stated purpose.
* **Legal Fiction under Tenth Schedule**:
  * **Tenth Schedule** ⇒ Added by **52nd Amendment Act, 1985** (Anti-Defection Law) to prevent opportunistic party hopping.
  * **Grounds for Defection** ⇒ Voluntarily giving up membership of a party, or voting against party whip/directions.
  * **Paragraph 3 Deletion** ⇒ Split provision (1/3rd members) was deleted by the **91st Amendment Act, 2003** (restored primacy of political party over legislature party).
* **Paragraph 4 (Merger Exception)**:
  * Defection does not apply if **original political party** merges with another, and at least **two-thirds (2/3rd)** of the members of the **legislature party** agree.
  * **The Controversy** ⇒ Can a merger occur *solely* by 2/3rd of the legislature party switching sides without the original political party merging?
  * **Paragraph 4(2) Deeming Fiction** ⇒ Deems that a merger has occurred if 2/3rd of legislators consent.
* **Concerns**:
  * Tenth Schedule is increasingly ineffective due to pending litigation.
  * Defects bypass democracy by creating 2/3rd majorities to secure constitutional amendments.
* **Way Forward**:
  * ***K.M. Singh Case (2020)*** ⇒ SC recommended an **independent tribunal** headed by retired judges to handle defection cases.
  * **Law Commission (1999)** ⇒ Recommended deleting Paragraph 4 (merger exception) entirely.

## Citizenship

### Citizenship and Passport
* **Why in News** ⇒ Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) clarified that an Indian passport is primarily a **travel document** and **not conclusive proof** of citizenship.
* **Core Issue** ⇒ India has **no single, definitive** citizenship document.
* **Citizenship under Indian Law**:
  * **Articles 5 to 11** (Part II of Constitution).
  * **Citizenship Act, 1955** ⇒ Acquired via **Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, or Incorporation of Territory**.
* **Status of Indian Passport**:
  * Does not create citizenship or conclusively settle a citizenship dispute.
  * **Section 20 (Passports Act)** ⇒ Allows passport issuance to a non-citizen in the public interest.
  * **Examples** ⇒ Special travel documents issued to Tibetan refugees and Sri Lankan Tamils.
* **Judicial Position**:
  * ***State of AP v. Abdul Khader (1962)*** ⇒ Passport is evidence of nationality, but courts must examine birth, domicile, and migration.
  * ***Sarbananda Sonowal v. UoI (2005)*** ⇒ Burden of proving citizenship lies on the person claiming it.
* **Universal Citizenship Document Absence**:
  * Most Indians acquire citizenship **by birth** and receive **no separate citizenship certificate**.
  * Certificates under **Sections 5 & 6** of Citizenship Act are issued only to those who register or naturalize.
  * Citizenship is established through a **combination of documents** (birth certificates, school records, land deeds).
* **NRC & Aadhaar**:
  * **Citizenship Rules, 2003** ⇒ Directs the creation of the **National Register of Indian Citizens (NRIC)**.
  * **Aadhaar** ⇒ Strictly a proof of **identity/residency**, **not citizenship**.
  * **Assam NRC Experience** ⇒ **19 Lakh people excluded** from final list due to spelling differences, missing documents, and difficulties in proving family links.
* **Way Forward** ⇒ Strengthen civil registration & universal birth registration; improve accessibility of historical/archival family records.

## Fundamental Rights

### Right of Way
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC in ***Maniyar Iliyaz @ Shaik Riyaz v. P. Ayyappan*** declared the right to walk on safe, demarcated footpaths is a **Fundamental Right under Article 21**.
* **Key SC Observations**:
  * Demarcated footpaths ensure civilisational advancement, city beauty, and equitable access.
  * **Article 39(b) Connection** ⇒ Access to common spaces must not become a monopoly of the motorized class.
  * **Walking = Multi-faceted Freedom** ⇒ Linked to freedom of speech/expression, peaceful protest, association, and physical/mental well-being (meditation, discovery, resistance).
* **Issues and Need for Statutory Framework**:
  * **No national law** dedicated to pedestrian rights; laws are highly fragmented.
  * **Motor Vehicles Act, 1988** ⇒ Accords greater importance to wheeled vehicles than pedestrians.
  * Diffused municipal responsibility leads to heavily encroached footpaths.
  * **SC Recommendation** ⇒ Enact a dedicated statutory law and establish a **full-time regulator**.
* **Way Forward & Caution** ⇒ Right of way must become public culture. However, the judgment cautioned that clearing footpaths **must not be used to criminalize the survival of the urban poor** or gentrify public spaces by removing informal commercial activity.

### 142 - Complete Justice
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC in ***In Re: Phalodi Accident v. NHAI (2025)*** declared the **Right to safe travel on National Highways (NHs)** is part of the Right to Life under **Article 21**.
* **The NH Dilemma** ⇒ NHs constitute only **2% of Indian roads** but account for **30% of total road fatalities**.
  * **State Goal** ⇒ Reduce road accidents by **50% by 2030**.
* **Article 142 (Complete Justice)**:
  * Empowers the SC to pass any decree/order necessary for doing **complete justice** where existing law is silent or insufficient.
  * ***Delhi Judicial Service Association v. State of Gujarat (1991)*** ⇒ Article 142 power is of a different level and quality.
  * ***Hitesh Bhatnagar v. Deepa Bhatnagar (2011)*** ⇒ Urged extraordinary care and caution in using 142.
  * ***Canara Bank v. Debasis Das (2003)*** ⇒ Focuses on delivering substantive justice (legal or natural).
  * **Article 226 vs Article 142** ⇒ ***Anil Kumar Jain v. Maya Jain (2009)*** clarified that High Courts under Article 226 do **not** possess the same complete justice power as SC under Article 142.
* **Concerns** ⇒ Judicial activism bordering on **judicial overreach**; violates the **Separation of Powers**.

### Preventive Detention
* **Why in News** ⇒ Allahabad HC in ***Chander Pal Singh*** issued critical directives against the misuse of preventive detention.
* **Allahabad HC Observations**:
  * Termed deprivation of personal liberties in UP "highly irresponsible".
  * Police and magistrates are using preventive powers to jail individuals over minor property/neighborhood disputes and citing vague "communal tensions".
* **Key Directives**:
  * Magistrates must **justify** detention decisions.
  * Compensation for unlawful detention can be **recovered directly from the salary** of the concerned magistrate/police officer.
  * Promotes appellate scrutiny and constitutional challenges.
* **Static Context (Article 22)**:
  * ***Dhanya M. v. State of Kerala*** ⇒ Individual liberty cannot be curtailed lightly.
  * **Article 22(3) to (7)** ⇒ Governs detention without trial.
  * **Article 22(4) Limit** ⇒ Max detention is **3 months** unless extended by an **Advisory Board**.
  * ***S.K. Nazneen Case*** ⇒ Must distinguish between threat to **"Public Order"** (grave threat to society) vs. **"Law and Order"** (routine crime).

### Paternity
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC in ***CP v. AP (2026)*** clarified principles for DNA testing in paternity disputes.
* **Legal Position on DNA Testing**:
  * **Indian Evidence Act, 1872** & **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023** place heavy burden of proof on the party challenging legitimacy.
  * ***Goutam Kundu Case*** ⇒ DNA tests cannot be ordered routinely; requires a strong *prima facie* case.
  * ***Shri Banarsi Dass*** ⇒ Courts must prioritize child legitimacy over forensic curiosity.
  * ***Dipanwita Roy*** ⇒ Refusal to undergo ordered DNA test can lead to court drawing an **adverse inference**.
  * ***Aparna Ajinkya Firodia*** ⇒ Emphasized necessity, proportionality, and avoiding social stigma.
* ***CP v. AP (2026)* Ruling** ⇒ Upheld trial court's DNA test direction, asserting:
  * Paternity was directly in issue.
  * No other evidence was capable of resolving the dispute.
  * **DNA testing is a last resort**, applied strictly under necessity and proportionality.

## Judiciary

### Data and Justice
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC launched **"One Case, One Data (OCOD)"** & **"Su-Sahayak"** digital initiatives.
* **One Case, One Data (OCOD)** = Unified judicial data platform creating a single digital trail for disputes.
  * **Function** ⇒ Links court records and litigant actions; reduces manual verification; standardises data to procedural bottlenecks.
* **Su-Sahayak** = AI-powered chatbot on the SC website.
  * **Function** ⇒ Assists in navigating case status, cause lists, orders, and e-services (text-based).
  * **Companion AI Tools**:
    * **SUVAS** ⇒ Translates judgments.
    * **SUPACE** ⇒ Processes facts and legal precedents.
* **Benefits** ⇒ Better access to justice, interoperability, standardized data-driven administration.
* **Concerns** ⇒ High cost of digital scanner setups/cloud backups for independent practitioners; creation of unregulated "digital middlemen"; risk of private data leaks; AI model bias.

### Ordinance
* **Why in News** ⇒ Presidential Ordinance increased the SC sanctioned judge strength from **34 to 38** to counter backlogs.
* **Legislative Route**:
  * **Section 2 of SC (Number of Judges) Act, 1956** was last amended in 2019 (increasing strength to 34).
  * **Article 124(1)** ⇒ Parliament is the sole authority to increase SC judge strength.
  * **Article 123** ⇒ President can promulgate an **Ordinance** when Parliament is not in session. Carries same force as an Act of Parliament.
  * **Timeline** ⇒ Must be approved by Parliament within **6 weeks** of reassembly; President can withdraw it anytime.
* **Ordinance & Judicial Independence**:
  * Judiciary Independence = **Basic Structure** of Constitution.
  * ***D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar*** ⇒ Re-promulgating ordinances without legislative approval is a **fraud on the Constitution**.
  * ***Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar*** ⇒ SC ruled against using ordinance-making power as a parallel legislative channel.

### Draft SC Rules on AI
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC AI Committee released the draft **"Regulations for Use of AI in Courts, 2026"**.
* **Key Provisions**:
  * **Assistive Role Only** ⇒ Transcription, translation (SUVAS), case scheduling, and cause-list management. AI must remain subservient to human judgment.
  * **DPDP Act Compliance** ⇒ AI data processing must respect the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act**.
  * **Absolute Prohibitions** ⇒ AI **cannot** be used for determining judicial outcomes, AI-assisted sentencing, witness profiling, risk scoring, evaluating bail eligibility, or surveillance of judicial officers.
* **Institutional Framework** ⇒ Proposed setting up a full-time **"apex body"** at the SC to supervise and set standards for AI adoption.

## Policies, Bills, Acts and Laws

### Bail Norm
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC in ***Syed Iftikhar Andrabi v. NIA*** reiterated that **bail should remain the rule, even under UAPA**, where prolonged trial delays violate **Article 21**.
* **Section 43D(5) of UAPA** ⇒ Imposes strict twin conditions for bail (court must find allegations *prima facie* true).
* **Precedent Shifts**:
  * ***NIA v. Zahoor Ahmad Shah Watali (2019)*** ⇒ Court needs to look at broad probabilities of allegations, not elaborate evidence evaluations (highly restrictive for bail).
  * ***UoI v. K.A. Najeeb (2021)*** ⇒ Strict UAPA bail conditions **cannot override Article 21** (personal liberty & speedy trial); prolonged custody without trial justifies bail.
* ***Andrabi* Case (2026)**:
  * Granted bail to Andrabi (5+ years in pre-trial custody).
  * **UAPA Conviction Rate** ⇒ Extremely low (**2% to 6%**).
  * Disapproved the reasoning in ***Gulfisha Fatima (2026)***, which denied bail to Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam.
  * **Outcome** ⇒ Referred the question on Section 43D(5) interpretation to a **larger Bench**.

#### Parole vs. Furlough vs. Anticipatory Bail
* **Parole**:
  * **Nature** ⇒ Temporary release of a convict for a **specific emergency/reason** (family death, marriage).
  * **Right** ⇒ Not an absolute right; granted at state executive discretion.
  * **Sentence Impact** ⇒ Parole time **does not count** toward the sentence.
  * **Jurisdiction** ⇒ State Subject (governed by varying state-specific prison rules).
* **Furlough**:
  * **Nature** ⇒ Periodic release granted to long-term prisoners to maintain social/family ties.
  * **Right** ⇒ Treated as a **matter of right** for reform.
  * **Sentence Impact** ⇒ Counts as remission of sentence (**does count** toward completion).
* **Anticipatory Bail**:
  * **Nature** ⇒ Pre-arrest protection for a person apprehending arrest for a non-bailable offense.
  * **Provision** ⇒ **Section 482 of BNSS** (formerly S.438 CrPC); applied through High Court or Session Court.
  * ***Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia (1980)*** ⇒ SC linked anticipatory bail directly to **Article 21** liberty protections.

### Sedition
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC in ***Kamran v. Madhya Pradesh (2026)*** allowed lower courts to proceed with pending **Section 124A (Sedition)** trials/appeals **if the accused raises no objection**, despite its constitutionality being under challenge.
* **IPC vs. BNS**:
  * **Section 124A (IPC)** ⇒ Replaced by **Section 152 (BNS, 2023)** (subverting sovereign/unity of India).
  * **BNS Penalty** ⇒ Minimum punishment increased to **7 years** (up from 3 years).
* **Context**:
  * ***S.G. Vombatkere v. UoI (2022)*** ⇒ SC put Section 124A trials in abeyance, ordering no fresh FIRs.
  * ***Kamran (2026)* Clause** ⇒ Forces a "Hobson’s Choice" on the accused (choose between delayed justice in abeyance vs. active trial under a potentially unconstitutional law).
* **Constitutional Position**:
  * ***Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962)*** ⇒ Sedition applies **only** if there is incitement to violence or public disorder; criticism of government is protected.
  * ***I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu*** ⇒ Laws must march in step with the times.

### Euthanasia of Dogs
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC refused to modify its 2025 order directing removal of stray dogs from high-footfall public institutions.
* **Core Rulings**:
  * Strays cannot be re-released into institutional areas after vaccination/sterilisation.
  * **ABC Rules 2023** + **Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960** ⇒ Do **not** grant stray dogs an absolute right to occupy public spaces.
  * **Article 21** ⇒ Includes right of citizens to access public spaces without fear of physical harm.
* **Directives to States**:
  * Set up at least one fully functional **Animal Birth Control Centre** per district.
  * Ensure anti-rabies vaccines/immunoglobulins are available in government hospitals.
  * Permitted **euthanasia** of rabid, incurably ill, or demonstrably dangerous dogs, assessed by veterinary experts.

### DNA Records Collection
* **Why in News** ⇒ State police forces began collecting DNA records of suspects arrested for heinous crimes (punishable by 7+ years); over 1 lakh profiles stored by NCRB.
* **Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022 (CrPI)**:
  * Empowers police/prisons to collect physical and biological samples (DNA, prints, iris scans) of convicts and arrested persons.
  * **Retention Period** ⇒ **75 years**.
  * **Database Integration** ⇒ NCRB operates a national-level searchable database with **video analytics** to match crime scene CCTV footage with the database.
* **NAFIS (National Automated Fingerprint Identification System)** ⇒ Launched in 2022 by NCRB; houses **1.27 Crore fingerprint records** to solve interstate cases.

## Constitutional, Non-Constitutional and other Bodies

### Finance Commission
* **Why in News** ⇒ **16th Finance Commission (FC)** retained vertical devolution at **41%** and revised horizontal tax devolution criteria.
* **States' Concerns**:
  * **Cesses & Surcharges** ⇒ Not shared with states; account for **15%+** of gross tax revenues. States demanded capping them at 8%–10%.
  * **CSS (Centrally Sponsored Schemes)** ⇒ Restricts fiscal autonomy.
  * States demanded vertical devolution increase from 41% to 50%.
* **16th FC Recommendations**:
  * Retained **41% vertical devolution**.
  * Cesses and surcharges **excluded** from divisible pool.
  * Abolished revenue-deficit, state-specific, and sector-specific grants.
  * Directed states to discontinue **off-budget borrowings** and bring all liabilities onto their budgets; keep fiscal deficits **below 3%**.
* **Horizontal Devolution Criteria & Weights**:
  * **Income Distance** ⇒ **42.5%**
  * **Population** ⇒ **17.5%**
  * **Area** ⇒ **10%**
  * **Forest Cover** ⇒ **10%**
  * **Demographic Performance** ⇒ **10%**
  * **State Contribution to national GDP** ⇒ **10%** (replaced the older tax effort metric).
* **Impact & Disparities**:
  * Combined share of populous northern states (UP, MP, Bihar, WB) rose from 42.5% (6th FC) to 51% (15th FC), while southern states (AP, TN, Kerala, Karnataka) declined from 24.8% to 15.8%.
  * Under 16th FC, southern states' share slightly recovered to **17%**.
  * **Highest gainers** ⇒ Karnataka, Kerala, Gujarat.
  * **Largest decline** ⇒ MP, Arunachal Pradesh, UP.
  * **Top Recipients** ⇒ UP (17.62%), Bihar (9.95%), MP (7.35%).

## Delimitation

### EAC Delimitation Formula
* **Why in News** ⇒ Economic Advisory Council to the PM (**EAC-PM**) recommended a multi-factor formula for Lok Sabha constituency splits.
* **Historical Context** ⇒ Seat distribution per state was frozen based on the **1971 Census** through a 1976 Constitutional Amendment.
* **EAC-PM Formula**:
  * Maintain current state-wise seat proportions to protect demographic performers.
  * **Suggested Splitting** ⇒ Out of 543 seats, split **170 constituencies** (59 two-way splits, 111 three-way splits).
  * **Lok Sabha Size** ⇒ Increases the house to **824 seats**.
  * **Proportional Balance** ⇒ Southern states would hold **23.6%** of seats (vs 23.7% now); 6 most populous northern states would hold **45.2%** (vs 45.6% now).
  * **Delimitation Basis** ⇒ Turnbull-maximizing model analyzing voter turnout, urban share, SC/ST share, and linguistic diversity.

## Elections

### EC Appointments
* **Why in News** ⇒ SC questioned the lack of an independent appointment mechanism for Election Commissioners, calling it "tyranny of the elected".
* **Constitutional Base** ⇒ **Article 324(2)**: President appoints CEC and ECs, subject to any law made by Parliament.
* ***Anoop Baranwal Case (2023)* Interim Selection Committee**:
  * PM + Leader of Opposition (LoP) + **Chief Justice of India (CJI)**.
* **CEC and ECs (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023**:
  * Replaced the CJI with a **Union Cabinet Minister** nominated by the PM.
  * **Current Selection Committee** ⇒ PM (Chair) + Cabinet Minister + LoP (gives the executive a 2:1 majority).
  * **Search Committee** ⇒ Led by the Law Minister to prepare shortlist.
* **Challenged Grounds** ⇒ Replacing the CJI with a Cabinet Minister gives the executive dominant control, neutralizing the independence of the ECI.

### Right to Vote
* **Why in News** ⇒ Debate on voting rights brought into focus by US "Save America Act".
* **India vs. USA Comparison**:

| Parameter | India | USA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Constitutional Basis** | **Article 326** (Universal Adult Suffrage). | No explicit single federal constitutional guarantee; evolved via **15th, 19th, 24th, 26th Amendments**. |
| **Legal Status** | A **Constitutory/Constitutional Right** (*PUCL 2003*), **not** a Fundamental Right. | Evolved through protection against discrimination (race, sex, poll tax). |
| **Administration** | Highly centralized via **ECI (Article 324)**. | Decentralized; managed by state and local bodies with varying rules. |
| **Registration** | State-facilitated registration drives (BLOs). | Individual responsibility to actively register. |
| **Turnout Focus** | State actively facilitates voting to maximize participation. | Heavy individual burden to meet registration requirements. |

* **Voter Choice Precedents**:
  * ***ADR v. UoI (2002)*** ⇒ Voter's right to know candidate profiles/records.
  * ***PUCL v. UoI (2013)*** ⇒ Led to the introduction of **NOTA (None of the Above)** and guaranteed voter secrecy.

## Census

### Digital Census
* **Why in News** ⇒ The **2027 Census** house-listing phase has progressed, but population pre-testing is delayed due to the **inclusion of caste**.
* **Significance of Census 2027**:
  * First post-Independence Census to conduct a **comprehensive caste enumeration** (last caste census was in 1931).
  * First delimitation-linked Census after the expiry of the constitutional freeze.
  * First **Digital Census** utilising mobile electronic devices/smartphones and offering self-enumeration.
* **Methodology**:
  * Counted at usual place of residence (present for at least 1 night during enumeration).
  * **Household** = Group sharing a common kitchen (includes domestic help and paying guests).
* **Challenges**:
  * **Caste database complexity** ⇒ SECC 2011 produced 46 lakh distinct caste entries and 8 crore errors, making it unusable.
  * Excludes **1.58 Crore NRIs** living abroad.
  * Potential for deliberate misreporting, digital illiteracy of enumerators, and hardening of caste identities.
