# Health

## Policies

### Abortions
* **Supreme Court Directive** ⇒ Asked Union government to amend **Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) Act** to remove time limits for minor rape victims.
* **Precedent Case** ⇒ 15-year-old rape survivor allowed termination in **30th week** of gestation.
* **SC Observations**:
  * Minor’s right to continue pregnancy → **Right to Reproductive Autonomy** (Article 21).
  * State cannot compel any woman (especially minor) to carry illegitimate pregnancy to term.
* **Safety vs. Legal Friction**:
  * **AIIMS Opposition** ⇒ Opposed 30-week procedure citing danger to the teenage mother's health.
  * **Existing MTP Law** ⇒ Legal limit for termination = **24 weeks** of gestation.
  * **Cognitive Barrier** ⇒ Minor/parents with lay knowledge cannot make a studied assessment of life-threatening clinical risks.

### Preventive Healthcare
* **NCD Burden** ⇒ Non-Communicable Diseases = leading cause of death in India.
  * Diseases: Heart attacks, strokes, cancer, diabetes, and chronic infections.
  * Scale: **270 million** Indians living with chronic NCDs; vast majority unaware of condition.
* **Critical Prevention Window** ⇒ **30–40 years** age group.
  * Early metabolic & cardiovascular risks begin to take hold.
  * Reversal possible during this window → opportunity lost once symptoms manifest.
* **Way Forward** ⇒ Shift health from a standard policy intervention to an active, daily philosophy of lifestyle correction.

### JANANI Platform
* **Name** = Journey of Antenatal, Natal and Neonatal Integrated Care.
* **Nodal Agency** = Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (**MoHFW**).
* **Core Concept** = Upgraded digital version of the existing **Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) portal**.
* **Key Functions**:
  * Creates a **longitudinal digital health record** tracking women across reproductive age.
  * Captures service delivery events across the full continuum of maternal-neonatal care.
* **Key Features**:
  * QR-enabled digital **Mother and Child Health (MCH) Cards**.
  * Automated real-time alerts for high-risk pregnancies.
  * Real-time dashboards & automated due-list generation for frontline healthcare workers.
* **Interoperability** ⇒ Integrated with **U-WIN** (vaccination) and **POSHAN** (nutrition) for seamless data exchange.

### SEHAT Mission
* **Name** = Science Excellence for Health through Agricultural Transformation.
* **Collaborating Ministries** = Union Health Ministry + Union Agriculture Ministry.
* **Lead Institutions** = Indian Council of Agricultural Research (**ICAR**) + Indian Council of Medical Research (**ICMR**).
* **Core Framework** = "Healthy Food, Healthy Farms and a Healthy India" (linking farms → food plates → public health).
* **Key Focus Areas**:
  * Bio-fortified crops & nutrient-rich traditional crops (millets).
  * Integrated Farming Systems to tackle nutritional deficiencies & NCDs.
  * Transition from reactive treatment-oriented healthcare to preventive, One Health-led framework.

### Childhood Diabetes Care
* **National Framework** = MoHFW guidelines for universal childhood diabetes care.
* **Core Objectives**:
  * Universal diabetes screening of all children from birth to **18 years**.
  * Immediate blood glucose testing for suspected cases → timely referral to district facilities.
  * Free-of-cost care package at public hospitals (screening, diagnostics, lifelong insulin therapy, monitoring devices).
* **Pathology**:
  * Pancreas fails to produce sufficient insulin (Type 1) OR body develops insulin resistance (Type 2).
  * Hyperglycaemia (prolonged high blood sugar) → permanent damage to blood vessels and nerves.
* **"4Ts" Awareness Framework** ⇒ Early warning signs for Type 1 diabetes:
  * **Toilet** (frequent urination)
  * **Thirsty** (excessive thirst)
  * **Tired** (lethargy)
  * **Thinner** (rapid weight loss)
* **Empowerment** ⇒ Structured caregiver training on home insulin administration, digital glucose monitoring, and emergency daily disease management.

### Medical Inflation
* **Inflation Status** ⇒ Economic Survey showed health inflation slowed to **3%** in Dec 2025 (down from 6% in 2023).
* **Socio-Economic Bottleneck**:
  * Most Indians lack health insurance → heavy dependence on Out-of-Pocket Expenditure (**OOPE**).
  * **Avg OOPE per hospitalization** = **₹34,064** (Rural: ₹31,484; Urban: ₹38,688).
  * **OOPE by Hospital Type**:
    * Public government hospitals: **₹6,631**.
    * Charitable/NGO hospitals: **₹39,530**.
    * Private corporate hospitals: **₹50,508**.
  * **The "Missing Middle"** = Citizens not poor enough for public welfare (AB-PMJAY) but too poor to afford private commercial insurance.
* **Fiscal Gap** ⇒ India's public health expenditure is **< 2% of GDP** (Budget 2026 allocation = **0.26% of GDP**).
* **Key Solutions**:
  * **SC Guidelines** ⇒ Advised State governments to regulate pricing in private clinics.
  * **National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM) 2022** → needs urgent expansion to cap essential drug costs.
  * **Clinical Establishments Act, 2010** ⇒ Mandates private/public facilities to charge rates within a range determined by Centre & States.

### CCPA vs 100% Claim
* **Regulatory Dispute** ⇒ Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) action against deceptive "100% pure" claims on packaged food.
* **Core Principle** ⇒ Technical compliance with food-safety parameters does not shield a business from action under deceptive advertising laws.
* **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)**:
  * Statutory body under **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**; Department of Consumer Affairs.
  * Mandate: Investigate suo motu, recall unsafe goods, order modification of misleading ads, and penalize manufacturers, endorsers, or advertisers.
* **Misleading Advertisement (Section 2(28))**:
  * Falsely describes a product or service.
  * Gives a false guarantee of quality, substance, quantity, or purity.
  * Deliberately conceals important ingredient/allergen details.
* **Unfair Trade Practice (Section 2(47))** ⇒ Making false/misleading representations regarding product standards, composition, or style.
* **The "100%" Purity Illusion**:
  * Growing use on food labels; not explicitly defined under **FSSAI** advertising regulations.
  * Creates false impression of absolute superiority.
* **Front Label vs. Fine Print Rule** ⇒ Prominent claims on the front label shape the consumer's first impression; fine print disclaimers cannot contradict or dilute the main claim.
* **Market Paradigm Shift** ⇒ **Caveat Emptor** (buyer beware) → **Caveat Venditor** (seller beware).

### PCPNDT Act and India’s Missing Girls
* **Context** ⇒ SC dismissed medical appeals under the Act, emphasizing zero-tolerance for procedural non-compliance to curb female foeticide.
* **Demographic Crisis (Child Sex Ratio Decline)**:
  * 1981: **962** → 1991: **945** → 2001: **927** → 2011: **919**.
  * Sex ratio at birth has recently improved marginally to around **929** females per 1000 males.
  * Driven by: Deep-rooted patriarchy, son preference, and misuse of ultrasound diagnostic technologies.
* **PCPNDT Act, 1994 (Amendments: 2002, 2013)** - Core Provisions:
  * Complete prohibition of sex selection before & after conception.
  * Prenatal diagnostics permitted strictly for medical genetic abnormalities.
  * Mandatory registration of all ultrasound machines & genetic clinics.
  * Mandatory preservation of detailed medical records, specifically **'F' Form**.
  * Penalties for violation: Up to **5 years** imprisonment and cancellation of medical registration.
* **Supreme Court Jurisprudence** ⇒ *Voluntary Health Association of Punjab v. UoI* linked female foeticide directly to patriarchal attitudes.
* **Complementary Schemes** ⇒ Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana.

### Oxytocin and Maternal Health
* **Crisis** ⇒ Suspected fake/substandard oxytocin linked to maternal deaths during delivery in Rajasthan; WHO requested traceability details.
* **Clinical Significance of Oxytocin**:
  * Natural hormone produced by the **pituitary gland**.
  * Synthetically manufactured as an injection given during labor/delivery.
  * **Primary Function** = Stimulates uterine contractions to prevent fatal **Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH)**.
  * Ineffective/substandard medicine lacks active pharmaceutical ingredients → immediate life-threatening maternal bleeding.
* **Countermeasures** ⇒ Government mandates barcodes/QR codes on primary/secondary packaging of drugs covered under **Schedule H2** to ensure supply-chain traceability.
* **CDSCO (Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation)**:
  * India's National Regulatory Authority for drugs & cosmetics.
  * Functions under **Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS)**, MoHFW.
  * Mandate: Approve clinical trials/new drugs, lay down quality standards, coordinate State Drug Control Organisations.

### Junk Food Advertising
* **The Threat of UPFs (Ultra-Processed Foods)**:
  * UPFs contain high quantities of industrial additives (emulsifiers, flavors, sweeteners) and are highly processed.
  * High in Fat, Sugar, and Sodium (**HFSS**).
  * Direct causal link: UPF consumption → poor diet quality, obesity, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, Type 2 diabetes (*The Lancet*, Nov 2025).
  * Overconsumption is driven by industrial design and chemical formulations that mimic substance addiction.
* **Misleading Advertising Practices**:
  * Indian food industry spends **₹170 crore/month** on over **2 lakh junk food ads**.
  * Targets children and parents using celebrity/influencer endorsements to project unhealthy pre-packaged cereals or biscuits as "healthy choices" (selective disclosures).
* **Policy Gap & Legal Landscape**:
  * **National Multisectoral Action Plan (NMAP) (2017-2022)** originally envisaged bans on HFSS advertising → implementation remains weak.
  * **Supreme Court (2026 PIL)** ⇒ Observed that **Front-of-Pack Labeling (FOPL)** is a vital component of the Right to Health (Article 21).
  * **Economic Survey 2025-26** ⇒ Highlighted public concerns regarding deteriorating national diets.

# Diseases and Lifestyle

### Hantavirus
* **Trigger** ⇒ 2026 outbreak of hantavirus on the expedition cruise ship **MV Hondius** (sailing from Ushuaia, Argentina) causing 3 deaths.
* **Pathogen Biology & Transmission**:
  * Zoonotic virus carried primarily by **rodents** (rats, mice).
  * Humans infected via contact with infected rodent urine, saliva, or droppings (especially during cleaning of unventilated spaces).
  * Low human-to-human transmission except for the **Andes virus strain** (which spreads via close physical contact).
* **Clinical Manifestations**:
  * **HPS** (Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome) → severe lung infection, fluid buildup, respiratory failure.
  * **HFRS** (Haemorrhagic Fever with Renal Syndrome) → targets kidneys and blood vessels.
* **Treatment** ⇒ No specific antiviral cure or approved vaccine exists; management depends entirely on supportive care (oxygen, mechanical ventilation, fluid regulation).

### Physical Activity
* **Global Status**:
  * Levels of physical activity have remained stagnant over 2 decades despite policy adoptions.
  * Associated mortality: **> 5 million deaths** annually linked to physical inactivity.
  * **1 in 3 adults** and **8 in 10 adolescents** fail to meet WHO exercise targets.
* **WHO Guidelines** = **150 minutes** of moderate-intensity activity weekly for adults; **60 minutes** daily for children.
* **Socio-Economic Divide**:
  * **High-Income Countries** → active through leisure/free-time sports.
  * **Low- & Middle-Income Countries (LMICs)** → active through necessity (manual labor, active transport/walking).
  * **Opportunity Gap** ⇒ 40% disparity in active leisure access between wealthy men in rich nations and disadvantaged women in poor nations.
* **Climate Change Interplay**:
  * Active transit (cycling, walking) cuts carbon emissions.
  * Extremes (heatwaves, flooding, air pollution) directly disrupt physical activity.
* **Policy Evaluation (2004–2025)**:
  * Assessment of 661 national physical activity policies across 200 countries showed high adoption but failed execution.
  * Only **38.7%** of policies integrated 3 or more government sectors.
  * **26.5%** of national policies lacked measurable targets.
* **Systems-Level Way Forward** ⇒ Shift from framing exercise as an individual choice to a system issue:
  * Invest in trans-sectoral urban design (parks, public transit, sidewalks, protected bicycle lanes).
  * Address car-centric infrastructure.

### PHEIC Ebola
* **WHO Declaration** ⇒ Ebola outbreak caused by **Bundibugyo ebolavirus (BDBV)** in the DRC and Uganda declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC).
* **PHEIC Concept**:
  * Highest global health alert under the **International Health Regulations (IHR)**.
  * Triggered when a health event is sudden, unexpected, serious, and risks international spread.
  * Declared immediately by the WHO Director General without waiting for the Emergency Committee due to severe BDBV unknowns.
* **The BDBV Crisis**:
  * BDBV is a rare strain with **no licensed vaccine** or tested therapy (existing licensed vaccines *Ervebo* and *Zabdeno-Mvabea* target *only* the Zaire strain).
  * Concentrated in active conflict and high-displacement border regions → leads to undetected cases and high sample positivity rates.
* **Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) Biology**:
  * Zoonotic filovirus; spills over from wild animals (fruit bats, non-human primates) to humans.
  * Human-to-human transmission via direct contact with infected blood, secretions, bodily fluids, or contaminated surfaces.
  * Average fatality rate = **50%** (varies between 25% and 90% depending on access to healthcare).
* **Vaccine Challenges in NTDs (Neglected Tropical Diseases)**:
  * Ebolaviruses consist of 4 distinct disease-causing species (Zaire, Sudan, Bundibugyo, Taï Forest) → require species-specific vaccines.
  * Market Failure: High biosafety level development (BSL-4) costs hundreds of millions of dollars; vaccine production lacks commercial incentive due to poor, marginalized target populations.
* **Global NTD Commitments**:
  * **London Declaration (2012)** → $785 million committed to combat 10 NTDs.
  * **Kigali Declaration (2022)** → $1.5 billion pledged.

### Eliminate TB
* **PreVenTB Trial (Published in BMJ)**:
  * Large-scale trial conducted by the **Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)** across 18 sites, involving **12,700+ household contacts** of TB patients.
  * First global trial to include **Extrapulmonary TB (EPTB)** as an efficacy endpoint.
* **Vaccines Tested & Key Efficacy Results**:
  1. **VPM1002** (single-dose vaccine based on a modified, genetically engineered BCG platform):
     * **50.4%** efficacy against Extrapulmonary TB (EPTB).
     * **64.6%** efficacy against all TB in children/adolescents aged 6–14 years.
     * **21.4%** overall efficacy across all age groups.
  2. **Immuvac**:
     * **>60%** efficacy against EPTB in children aged 6–10 years.
     * **>60%** efficacy in preventing active disease progression among those who developed latent infections.
* **Clinical Insights**:
  * Active TB occurs as **Pulmonary TB (PTB)** (infectious/transmissible) or **Extrapulmonary TB (EPTB)** (debilitating, hard to diagnose).
  * Reduced vaccine efficacy in individuals with **low Body Mass Index (BMI)** highlights the necessity of co-implementing nutritional support.

### Shigellosis
* **Trigger** ⇒ Sustained Shigellosis outbreak in Kerala (March–June 2026); 132 confirmed cases, 3 deaths.
* **Pathogen Profile**:
  * **Shigella** is a gram-negative, highly contagious, rod-shaped bacterium.
  * Causes **Shigellosis** → acute diarrheal illness, severe stomach cramps, fever, and dysentery (bloody stools).
  * Historical Anchor: Isolated by Japanese physician **Kiyoshi Shiga in 1897** during a severe epidemic.
* **Transmission & Epidemiology**:
  * Extremely low infectious dose → **10–100 bacteria** can cause full-scale infection.
  * Faeco-oral transmission (contaminated hands, water, or food).
  * Reservoirs: Humans and primates. Can persist in sewage-polluted water bodies, contaminating aquatic life (sewage-harvested fish) and farm irrigation.
  * Demographic risk: Highest in children **under 5**; rises steadily again in populations **above 40 years**.
* **Antibiotic Resistance** ⇒ High nationwide prevalence of **Multi-Drug Resistant (MDR) Shigella**.
  * Doctors must use local **antibiograms** (drug sensitivity charts) to guide precise, non-blind antibiotic use.

### Brain-Eating Amoeba
* **Trigger** ⇒ Surge of amoebic meningoencephalitis in Kerala in early 2026 (133 positive cases, 33 deaths).
* **Clinical Shift** ⇒ Shift from Primary Amoebic Meningoencephalitis (PAM) to slower-progressing **Granulomatous Amoebic Encephalitis (GAE)**.
* **Naegleria fowleri (The PAM Pathogen)**:
  * Free-living, single-celled amoeba found in warm freshwater bodies (lakes, rivers, inadequately chlorinated pools).
  * Infection route: Contaminated water enters through the nose, travels along the olfactory nerve to the brain, and destroys brain tissues.
  * Causes **PAM** → rapid destruction of brain matter with a **97% fatality rate**.
* **GAE Pathogen** ⇒ Mainly caused by **Acanthamoeba**; slower clinical progression, comparatively better survival outcomes.
* **Climate Linkage**:
  * *N. fowleri* thrives in warm freshwater up to **46°C**.
  * Rising global temperatures, prolonged heatwaves, and carbon emissions increase water temperatures, expanding favorable habitats.

### Nipah
* **Context** ⇒ Monsoon re-emergence of Nipah virus in Kerala. Shows high ecological vulnerability but robust local healthcare containment protocols.
* **Pathogen Classification** ⇒ WHO **Priority Pathogen** due to extreme lethality, outbreak potential, and pandemic risk.
* **Transmission Dynamics**:
  * Primary natural hosts = **Fruit bats**.
  * Zoonotic spillover to humans via consumption of bat-contaminated raw fruits or contact with water sources polluted by bats.
  * Secondary transmission = Highly infectious person-to-person spread via close respiratory/fluid contact.
* **Epidemiology Timeline**:
  * Historic outbreaks: **2001 (Siliguri, West Bengal)**, 2007.
  * Kerala outbreaks: **2018** (17 deaths, 18 confirmed, index patient infected 15 others), followed by sporadic cases/outbreaks in 2019, 2021, 2023, 2024, and 2025.
* **Ecosystem Connection** ⇒ Human encroachment and anthropogenic destruction of traditional forest zones on bat-habitat fringes drive zoonotic spillover.
* **Preparedness Paradigm** ⇒ Requires a unified **One Health approach** (monitoring human, animal, and environmental vectors together) rather than isolated reactive healthcare:
  * Early clinical screening system adept at detecting acute encephalitis clusters.

### Sickle Cell Anaemia
* **Eradication Drive** ⇒ President declared India would eradicate the disease well before the national target of **2047**.
* **National Mission, 2023** progress:
  * **7 crore** citizens (aged 0–40 years) screened ahead of schedule.
* **Socio-Genetic Vulnerability** ⇒ Genetic studies indicate sickle cell prevalence among **tribal communities** is many times higher than the general population.

# Drugs & R&D

### Syrups - Schedule K
* **Regulatory Action** ⇒ MoHFW deleted the term “syrup” from **Schedule K** of the Drugs Rules, 1945.
* **Consequence** ⇒ Over-the-counter (OTC) cough syrups are banned; they must now be sold strictly on a doctor's prescription through licensed pharmacies.
* **The Global Trigger** ⇒ Contamination of export-grade Indian cough syrups with toxic **ethylene glycol (EG)** and **diethylene glycol (DEG)**, causing the deaths of **>300 children** globally (Gambia, Uzbekistan, etc.) since 2022.
* **Clinical Health Hazards of OTC Syrups**:
  * Many are unscientific "cocktails" of bronchodilators, antihistamines, and decongestants.
  * Side effects: Tremors, cardiac palpitations, heavy sedation, paradoxical agitation in infants.
  * Pediatric caution: American Academy of Pediatrics states cough suppressants are ineffective under 6 years and dangerous as they mask underlying critical infections (pneumonia, asthma).
* **Structural Manufacturing & Policy Challenges**:
  * Prescription requirements curb consumer abuse but **cannot prevent manufactured contamination**.
  * Contamination is caused by quality-control failures, lack of raw-material batch testing, and weak CDSCO oversight.
  * Implementation friction: Small-scale manufacturers oppose high-end chromatographic batch testing due to financial costs.
  * Understaffing: India has around **36 State drug controllers** who are chronically understaffed → weak inspection and enforcement capacity, especially in rural areas.

# Education

### Health Education
* **Capacity Expansion (2025–26)**:
  * Approved: 43 new medical colleges, 11,682 MBBS seats, and 8,967 PG seats.
  * Decadal growth: Medical colleges rose from **596 (2021–22) to >818 (2025–26)**; MBBS seats reached **1.29 lakh**; PG seats reached **85,000**.
* **The Quality & Faculty Crisis**:
  * Expansion without quality safeguards → critical shortage of teaching faculty.
  * **11 of 18 AIIMS** institutions operate with a **40% vacancy** in teaching/research faculty.
  * Private colleges function on high capitation fees with zero public service obligations.
* **The CHC Specialist Deficit**:
  * Specialist shortage has persisted since 2014 despite PG seat expansion.
  * Community Health Centres (**CHCs**) face a **79.9% specialist vacancy rate** (shortfall of **~17,500** specialists).
  * Structural impact: A CHC serves 1.6–2 lakh people and requires 5 specialists (physician, surgeon, obstetrician, pediatrician, anesthetist). Due to vacancies, only **882 out of 5,491 CHCs** are fully operational nationwide (averaging only 1 functional CHC per district).
* **Socio-Geographical Bottlenecks** ⇒ Newly qualified specialists refuse rural, tribal, or difficult postings due to lacking hospital equipment, poor staff housing, and inadequate schooling for children.
* **Systemic Medical Reforms**:
  * Transition from conventional checklist compliance to outcome-based evaluations.
  * Update Competency-Based Medical Education (**CBME**) and integrate a national faculty pool of **Professors of Practice**.
  * Integrate AI early into the undergraduate syllabus to augment clinical judgment.
* **Incentive Solutions**:
  * **Chhattisgarh Rural Medical Corps Scheme Model** → classify rural CHCs into normal, difficult, and most difficult categories; provide priority PG admissions, financial incentives, and quality family quarters.
  * **"All or None" Posting** → post all 5 vital specialists to a CHC simultaneously so they can operate as a functional clinical unit (OTs, labor rooms, and ICU beds require collective presence).

### NTA’s ‘Zero Error’ Failure
* **NEET-UG 2026 Compromise** ⇒ Mass leakage of question papers compromised the exam, forcing the NTA to order a nationwide re-test for **22.79 lakh** medical aspirants.
* **Institutional Vulnerabilities**:
  * NTA functioned without a full-time chief for over a year following the 2024 NEET controversy (67 top scorers received full marks).
  * Abhishek Singh assumed charge in March 2026, announcing a "Zero Error, Zero Tolerance" policy.
  * Investigation findings: A compromised "guess paper" containing **120 out of 410 actual questions** was circulated on Telegram channels nearly a month before the exam.
* **Radhakrishnan Committee Recommendations** (Constituted post-2024 controversies):
  * Identified the traditional **Pen-and-Paper Testing (PPT)** model as a major security risk prone to leaks.
  * Recommended transitioning completely to **Computer-Based Testing (CBT)** or **Computer-assisted Secure PPT** (where encrypted question papers are transmitted digitally and printed locally inside the exam hall).
* **NTA CBT Bottleneck** ⇒ In 2026, NTA had only 552 CBT centers, limiting daily CBT testing capacity to just **1.5 lakh candidates/day** (making CBT for 22 lakh candidates impossible in a single day without major expansion).

### On-Screen Marking (OSM)
* **The Reform** ⇒ CBSE Class 12 Board examinations fully transitioned to On-Screen Marking (OSM) in 2026 (~1 crore answer scripts scanned and digitally evaluated).
* **Definition** = Student handwritten answer sheets are scanned, anonymized, uploaded, and evaluated by teachers on computer screens.
* **Poor Implementation & Saftey Failures**:
  * CBSE fully implemented OSM across all high-volume subjects in 2026, ignoring its governing body's recommendation to run controlled pilot projects first.
  * Teachers were notified of the digital transition barely **10 days** before the first Class 12 board exam.
  * Issues: Evaluators lacked software training, leading to high screen fatigue and digital evaluation errors.
* **Post-Result Crisis**:
  * CBSE pass percentage dropped from **88.39% to 85.29%**; number of high scorers (>90%) fell sharply.
  * Errors reported: Mismatched answer books, blurred scans, unscanned supplementary sheets, unmarked answers, and crashed verification portals.
  * Under pressure, over **1.25 lakh students** applied to verify nearly **4 lakh answer scripts** within hours of result declaration.
  * CBSE had to involve IT experts from **IIT Madras** and **IIT Kanpur** for immediate troubleshooting.
* **Tendering Scandals** ⇒ Allegations of lowered financial eligibility criteria for scanning vendors, removing software security standards, and bypassing statutory **CERT-In audits**.

### NITI Aayog Report
* **Report Name** = *School education system in India - temporal analysis and policy roadmap for quality Enhancement*.
* **The "Pyramid" Schooling Problem**:
  * Universal primary school access is achieved, but the secondary retention pipeline is highly dysfunctional.
  * Scale: **14.71 lakh schools** housing **24.69 crore students**.
  * Structural drop: India has **7.3 lakh primary schools**, but the number plunges to just **1.64 lakh higher secondary schools**.
  * Dropout rate: **4 out of every 10 children** drop out before completing higher secondary education (Grades 11–12).
* **The Zero-Enrolment Waste** ⇒ **7,993 schools** recorded zero student enrolment but continued receiving funds and teacher allocations due to un-updated administrative databases.
* **The Policy Prescription**:
  * Transition to **"Cylindrical" Schooling** → create unified, composite schools hosting Grades 1–12 under a single roof to plug the leaky pipeline.
  * Introduce AI and computational thinking starting from Grade 3.

### Three-language Study
* **CBSE Mandate** ⇒ From July 1, study of **three languages** is compulsory for Class 9.
* **The Formula**:
  * R1, R2, R3 are mandatory; at least **two must be native Indian languages**.
  * A foreign language (French, German) can only be taken as the 3rd language if the other two are native Indian, or as a 4th optional subject.
* **Stress Reduction Clauses**:
  * No external Board examination for the third language (R3) at Class 10.
  * R3 is evaluated through entirely school-based internal assessments.
  * No student will be barred from Class X Board exams due to R3 performance.
* **Teacher Shortages** → Schools advised to use inter-school resource sharing, virtual/hybrid language classrooms, and engage retired postgraduates.

### Three Language Formula
* **Supreme Court Case** ⇒ SC declined to stay the CBSE's May 15 circular mandating 3 languages but agreed to review concerns of student hardship.
* **Petitioners' Key Legal Arguments**:
  * Language is a matter of personal choice; compulsory imposition by executive order violates personal autonomy.
  * NEP 2020 explicitly guarantees flexibility and states that no language shall be imposed on any State or student.
  * CBSE, as an executive agency, lacks constitutional authority to impose language mandates without parliamentary legislation.

# Vulnerable Sections

## PWDs

### Persons with Disabilities
* **Policy Proposal** ⇒ Civil society calls for a statutory **Minimum Universal Disability Pension Floor Rate (MUDPFR)** to guarantee uniform social security.
* **Demographic Underestimation**:
  * 2011 Census recorded **2.68 crore** PwDs.
  * Current conservative estimates place the population of PwDs at **4.5–6 crore**.
* **Dysfunctional State Pension Systems**:
  * **Indira Gandhi National Disability Pension Scheme** covers a tiny fraction of the eligible population.
  * Pension rates are arbitrarily determined by state budgets and range from a dismal **₹300 to ₹500/month** in most states (with a few outlier states giving ₹1,000–₹3,000).
  * **Fiscal Neglect** ⇒ India spends only **0.02% of GDP** on disability welfare (including pensions).
  * **Global Spending Comparison (Disability welfare % of GDP)**:
    * OECD nations: **2.2%**
    * Brazil: **0.45%–0.50%**
    * Australia: **0.35%–0.40%**
    * South Africa: **0.12%–0.15%**
* **MUDPFR Cost-Benefit Economics**:
  * ₹8,000/month for 40 lakh severe beneficiaries → **₹38,400 crore/annum** (**0.08% of GDP**).
  * ₹10,000/month for 65 lakh beneficiaries → **₹78,000 crore/annum**.
  * Even a rate of ₹15,000/month would keep national expenditure well under **0.2% of GDP**.
  * High returns: Welfare pensions stimulate local economies with a **fiscal multiplier of 1.4–1.6**; socio-economic returns exceed the pension costs by **48%** (2025 Pro Bono Economics Report).
* **Constitutional & Global Anchors**:
  * Operationalizes **Article 41** (Right to public assistance) and **Section 24** of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act, 2016.
  * Aligns with Article 28 of the UN Convention on the Rights of PwDs and SDG 1.3.
* **Proposed National Disability Pension Authority** ⇒ Centralized, independent agency (on the lines of SASSA in South Africa or NDIA in Australia) to run a national disability registry, ensure pension portability across states, and monitor performance.

# Governance

## E-Governance and Portals

### Central Prabhari Officer (CPO) Portal
* **Objective** ⇒ Launched by **NITI Aayog** to strengthen real-time, data-driven governance.
* **Application** ⇒ Tracks and coordinates fields observations under the **Aspirational Districts Programme (ADP)** and **Aspirational Blocks Programme (ABP)**.
* **Function** ⇒ Allows ground-level Prabhari Officers to submit real-time observations, bridging the gap between field realities and central policy interventions.

### NeSDA 2025
* **Name** = National e-Governance Service Delivery Assessment 2025.
* **Agency** = Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (**DARPG**).
* **Cycle** = Biennial assessment framework.
* **Core Function** = Measures the depth, accessibility, and maturity of online e-governance service delivery from the citizen's perspective.
* **Standard** = Based on the **Online Service Index (OSI)** of the UN e-Government Survey, customized for Indian federalism.
* **NeSDA 2025 Framework**:

  * Mandate: Evaluates **59 mandatory services** for every State/UT, and **43 services** for Central Ministries.
  * Parameters: Accessibility, Content Availability, Ease of Use, Information Security & Privacy, E-Participation, and use of Emerging Technologies.

## IT and Social Media

### Tackling takedowns
* **Censorship Disputes** ⇒ Criticism of the Union government's aggressive use of **IT Act, 2000** and **IT Rules, 2021** to censor lawful online speech.
* **The IT Rules Weaponization**:
  * Amendments enable government agencies to pressure platforms (Meta, X) to take down content within tight **3-hour timelines**.
  * Failure to comply results in safe harbor revocation, personal criminal liability for platform employees, and direct prosecution.
* **Sections 69A and 79(3)(b) IT Act** ⇒ Heavily utilized to block accounts of media outlets, opposition figures, and independent political commentators.
* **Institutional Concerns**:
  * Use of the **Sahyog Portal** allows police officials to issue takedown requests under Section 79(3)(b), bypassing judicial oversight.
  * Undermines the landmark ***Shreya Singhal vs. Union of India*** Supreme Court ruling which mandated that platform "actual knowledge" of illegal content must come via court orders or formal government notifications, not police requests.

### India’s Data Standards Issue
* **The Problem** ⇒ Fragmentation and lack of shared statistical standards across Central Ministries, making data integration laborious, error-prone, and inconsistent.
* **Anatomy of Data Incoherence**:
  * NITI Aayog (June 2025) noted that welfare databases fail to use shared standards for common indicators.
  * Welfare mismatch: Listing the same beneficiary multiple times leads to severe fiscal leakages, inflating public welfare spending by **4%–7% annually**.
  * Health gap: Childhood tuberculosis cases are recorded separately in multiple unlinked state systems, counting the same patient multiple times.
* **Fiscal Savings through Data standardisation (Deduplication)**:
  * **PM-KISAN**: Deleted 17.1 million ineligible names → saved **₹90 billion** in FY24.
  * **LPG Connections**: Removed 35 million bogus entries → saved **₹210 billion** over 2 years.
  * **Ration Cards**: Eliminated 16 million fake cards → saved **₹100 billion** annually.
* **Economic Value** ⇒ OECD estimates improving public-sector data sharing adds up to **1.5% to 2.5% of GDP**.
* **The Policy Solution**:
  * **National Data Governance Framework Policy (NDGFP)** → proposes creating the **India Data Management Office (IDMO)** under MeitY.
  * IDMO powers: Authority to set binding standards across all Ministries, audit database compliance, and resolve definition disputes.
  * Scale up **data.gov.in** as a centralized, schema-consistent repository.

### Service Digitisation
* **The Promise** ⇒ Digitization removes middleman rent-seeking, reduces bureaucratic corruption, and lowers transaction costs (e.g., Estonia digitizing divorce in Dec 2024).
* **The Reality in India**:
  * Highly dysfunctional services at State levels (such as online property registration and marriage records).
  * Data security is taken for granted; **CERT-In** has failed to secure public databases.
  * DigiLocker integration failures force users to type identical details repeatedly on online forms.
  * Defunct platforms: **e-Sanad** portal (designed for digital document verification before Apostille stamping) remains sputtering, forcing citizens back into the hands of touts.

### Telegram - Section 69A of IT Act
* **Trigger** ⇒ Government ordered a temporary ban on Telegram to prevent paper leaks during the NEET-UG retest.
* **Section 69A of IT Act, 2000**:
  * Empowers the Central Government to block public access to any online information, app, or website.
  * Ground: Necessary in the interest of India's sovereignty, defense, security of the state, friendly foreign relations, or public order.
  * Must adhere to the procedural rules established in **2009 Blocking Rules**.
* **Judicial Safeguards**:
  * ***Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India*** ⇒ SC ruled that freedom of speech and expression (**Article 19(1)(a)**) and freedom to practice any trade/profession (**Article 19(1)(g)**) over the internet are constitutionally protected.
  * **Proportionality Test** ⇒ Platform blocking must be necessary, use the least restrictive means, and be subject to judicial review.
* **Socio-Economic Impact of Bans**:
  * Telegram has **150 million Indian users**.
  * A blanket ban destroys the livelihood of digital creators, educators, and entrepreneurs who run business groups over the app, violating Article 19(1)(g).
* **Section 79 Intermediary Safe Harbour**:
  * Platforms are immune from liability for third-party user content, provided they exercise due diligence and act swiftly to take down illegal content upon receiving "actual knowledge".

# Schemes

### Jan Suraksha Schemes

#### Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY)
* **Objective** ⇒ Provides life insurance cover for death due to any cause.
* **Eligibility** ⇒ Individual bank/post office account holders aged **18–50 years** (must consent to auto-debit).
* **Coverage Period** ⇒ One-year renewable cover running from **1 June to 31 May**.
* **Annual Premium** ⇒ **₹436**, deducted via auto-debit. Pro-rated premium structure by enrollment quarter:
  * June–August: ₹436
  * September–November: ₹342
  * December–February: ₹228
  * March–May: ₹114
* **Benefit** ⇒ **₹2 lakh** payable to nominee on subscriber's death.
* **Lien Period** ⇒ **30 days** from date of enrollment.

#### Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY)
* **Objective** ⇒ Accidental death and disability insurance cover.
* **Eligibility** ⇒ Bank/post office account holders aged **18–70 years**.
* **Annual Premium** ⇒ **₹20/annum** (auto-debit).
* **Benefits**:
  * **₹2 lakh** for accidental death.
  * **₹2 lakh** for total/irrecoverable loss of both eyes or both hands/feet.
  * **₹1 lakh** for loss of one eye or use of one hand/foot.

#### Atal Pension Yojana (APY)
* **Objective** ⇒ Universal social security pension for workers in the unorganized sector.
* **Eligibility** ⇒ Bank account holders aged **18–40 years** who are **NOT income-tax payers**.
* **Benefits** ⇒ Guaranteed minimum monthly pension of **₹1,000, ₹2,000, ₹3,000, ₹4,000, or ₹5,000** upon attaining **60 years**, depending on age of entry and contribution scale.
* **Family Security**:
  * Monthly pension is payable to the subscriber, and subsequently to the surviving spouse.
  * Post the death of both, the total accumulated pension corpus is returned to the nominee.
  * If the subscriber dies prematurely (before 60), the spouse can continue contributions for the remaining vesting period.

### PM-AJAY AND AJAY MOBILE APP
* **Name** = Pradhan Mantri Anusuchit Jaati Abhyuday Yojana.
* **May 2026 Portal/App Launch** ⇒ Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment launched digitized platforms to streamline fund flows and track welfare outcomes for Scheduled Caste (SC) communities.
* **Portal Features**:
  * Unified command database and progress dashboard tracking **47,000 SC-majority villages** and over **4 million** beneficiaries.
  * Digital Village Development Plans (VDPs) using **50 socio-economic indicators** across 10 domains (replacing paper planning).
  * Milestone-linked fund tracking for Grants-in-Aid and hostel construction.
* **AJAY Mobile App**:
  * Field-level offline survey capabilities for grassroots workers.
  * Real-time geo-tagging and site photos for infrastructure verification.

# Police Reforms

### Prisons in India Overcrowded
* **Data Source** = **Prison Statistics 2024** report by the National Crime Records Bureau (**NCRB**).
* **The Overcrowding Paradox**:
  * National occupancy rate declined to a decade-low of **112.7%** in 2024, but structural overcrowding remains severe.
  * Indian prison capacity = 4.53 lakh inmates; actual inmate population = **> 5.11 lakh** (operating across 1,333 jails).
  * Capacity increased by 24% (2015–2024) via renovations and building 120 new prisons, but failed to keep pace with incarceration.
* **Overcrowding Hotspots**:
  * **Delhi** ⇒ Highest prison occupancy rate in India at **194%** in 2024.
  * **Jammu & Kashmir** ⇒ Occupancy surged from 78% (2015) → **148%** (2024).
* **The Undertrial Crisis**:
  * Undertrials accounted for **73%** of India's total prison population in 2024.
  * Share of actual convicts has declined from 32% (2016) to **26.6%** (2024).
  * Extreme states: **Delhi** and **Bihar** hold over **87%** of their prison inmates as undertrials.
* **Critical Staff Shortages**:
  * Parliamentary Standing Committee expressed concern: nearly half of sanctioned prison posts remain vacant in 8 States/UTs.
  * **$\ge$ 60% of sanctioned prison staff posts** are vacant in Delhi and Jammu & Kashmir, compromising security and administration.
* **Committee Recommendations** ⇒ Construct new prisons/separate barracks, transfer inmates between prisons to balance occupancy, and guarantee free legal aid to eligible undertrials.
