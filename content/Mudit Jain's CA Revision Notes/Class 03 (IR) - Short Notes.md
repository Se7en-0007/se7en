### Pakistan
#### Indus Waters Treaty
* **Abeyance (1960 IWT)** → India suspended treaty operations temporarily due to Pakistan's sustained cross-border terrorism.
* **Indus Waters Treaty (IWT) 1960 Anatomy**:
  * *Signatories* → India, Pakistan, and **World Bank**.
  * *Eastern Rivers* → **Ravi, Beas, Sutlej** → Allocated to **India** (unrestricted use).
  * *Western Rivers* → **Indus, Jhelum, Chenab** → Allocated to **Pakistan** (India allowed limited non-consumptive usage: run-of-the-river power, domestic, agriculture).
  * *Article III(1)* → Obligates India to let Western Rivers flow to Pakistan.
* **3-Step Dispute Resolution Mechanism**:
  1. **Permanent Indus Commission (PIC)** (bilateral tech-discussions).
  2. **Neutral Expert** (for specified technical differences).
  3. **Court of Arbitration (CoA)** (for legal disputes).
* **Core Friction Points**:
  * *Objectionable Dams* → Pak objects to India's **Kishanganga** (tributary of Jhelum) and **Ratle** (Chenab) hydropower projects.
  * *Biodiversity* → Habitat degradation of **Indus river dolphins** in Ravi.
  * *Climate Change* → Glacier melt, shifting rainfall patterns, and data-sharing constraints.
* **International Water Laws (India, China, Pakistan are NOT parties)**:
  * *Helsinki Rules 1966* → Equitable use of international drainage basins.
  * *Helsinki Convention 1992* → Prevention and control of transboundary water pollution.
  * *UN Watercourses Convention 1997* → Non-navigational uses of international watercourses.

### Bangladesh
#### Dhaka’s New Padma Barrage
* **Padma Barrage** → Bangladesh approved barrage on **Padma River** (Ganga in Bangladesh) 180 km downstream of India's **Farakka Barrage** to resolve seasonal water scarcity.
* **Farakka Barrage Context** → Diverts Ganga water to Bhagirathi-Hoogly to flush Kolkata Port.
* **1996 Ganges Water Treaty**:
  * *Issues* → 2019 review showed repeated occurrences of low flow at Farakka in drier years; treaty failed to account for climate-led flow unpredictability.
  * *Expiration* → Set to expire in **December 2026**.
  * *Other Agreements* → Critical agreements like the **Teesta River Treaty** remain unsigned.
* **Downstream Impacts of Farakka**:
  * *Padma Siltation* → Reduced flow leads to silt sinking to riverbed → overflows and causes quick flooding during monsoons.
  * *Groundwater* → Lowered recharge levels + increased salinity.
  * *Sundarbans Mangroves* → World's largest contiguous mangrove forest; needs brackish water but receives saline water → less fish → hits fisherfolk livelihoods.
  * *Sea-level Rise* → Threatens to submerge **17%** of Bangladesh's territory.
* **Environmental/Geopolitical Alternatives**:
  * *Hydrology* → Avoid massive barrages; deploy a series of smaller check-dams.
  * *China Factor* → China expanding as a dominant water manager in South Asia, reducing India's regional leverage.

## West Asia

### Iran Conflict
#### Persian Gulf Crisis
* **Trigger** → War between U.S., Israel, and Iran + expiration of U.S. sanctions waivers for India's **Chabahar Port** investments.
* **Conflict Mechanics**:
  * *Strikes* → Israel hit Iran's **Natanz** (nuclear) & **Kharg Island** (oil); Iran retaliated on Israel's **Dimona** and energy facilities in Saudi, Qatar, UAE, Kuwait.
  * *Chokepoint Blockade* → Iran blockaded **Strait of Hormuz** (handles **21 million barrels/day** — 1/5th global oil, 80% Asian LNG flow).
  * *Ground War* → Israel invaded Lebanon to push Hezbollah north of the **Litani River**.
  * *Mediation* → Pakistan-mediated talks in Islamabad (approaching China for concessions) failed.
* **International Legal Frameworks**:
  * *UNCLOS* → Outlines "transit passage" through international straits (U.S. has not signed UNCLOS, Iran signed but not ratified).
  * *UN Charter* → Misuse of **Article 51 (Self-Defense)** and **Article 2(4)**.
* **Focal Crises**:
  * *Ecological* → Persian Gulf supports 5000-6000 **dugongs** and **Hawksbill turtles**.
  * *Desalination Toxicity* → 200+ desalination plants dumping hot, highly saline brine, destroying coral reefs and mangroves.
* **India Multi-Sectoral Impact**:
  * *Energy* → India imports **90%** of its crude oil.
  * *Economy* → RBI: Every 10% crude price rise drops GDP growth by **15 bps** and rises inflation by **30 bps**.
  * *Trade* → 80% of Basmati rice exports stranded; Chabahar $620 million investment threatened; IMEC ports (**Haifa & Jebel Ali**) hit by strikes.
  * *Soft Power* → Pakistan's successful mediation role enhanced its soft power relative to India.
* **Way Forward**:
  * Develop alternative entry points for **IMEC** to bypass Hormuz: **Salalah, Duqm, Muscat** (Oman) + temporary transit through Egypt.

#### Seafarers’ Safety
* **Casualties** → 3 Indian seafarers killed aboard *Settebello* during U.S. Navy missile strikes.
* **Strategic Context** → India has **3.5 lakh seafarers** (1 in 6 global mariners).
* **Sanctioned Vessels** → Face unilateral U.S./EU/UK port, banking, and insurance bans.
* **India Stance** → Formulated **Operation Sankalp** (naval escort against Houthi attacks); proposed inter-ministerial framework (MEA, Defence, Shipping, Unions) + informed consent/advisories for war-risk zones.

## Europe

### EU
#### Keeping India’s carbon money at home
* **CBAM Definition** → Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism = import carbon fee based on embedded emissions. Definitive phase began **Jan 1, 2026**.
* **FTA Friction** → India-EU FTA (concluded 2026) offers **zero exemptions** from CBAM for Indian exports.
* **Impacted Sectors** → Carbon-heavy exports: **steel, aluminium, cement, fertilizers, electricity, hydrogen**.
  * *Subsidy Inconsistency* → EU producers get decarbonisation subsidies under EU ETS; violates GATT Article III (National Treatment).
* **Technical Exit Route**:
  * *Article 9 of CBAM* → Allows deduction of carbon price already paid in the country of origin.
  * *India CCTS* → Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (notified 2023) sets up domestic carbon pricing.
  * *Proposed Countermeasure* → **IBAM (India Border Adjustment Mechanism)** = India collects carbon fee at export point, keeping revenues home in a **green transition fund**, instead of losing it to EU borders.

#### Return of the Anaimangalam Copper Plates
* **Leiden Copper Plates** → Returning to India after 2 centuries at Leiden University.
  * *Significance* → Recorded **Raja Raja Chola I’s (985–1014 CE)** land gift at Anaimangalam village to a Buddhist Vihara (**Chulamanivarma Vihara**).
  * *Vihara Origin* → Built by **Sri Mara Vijayotunga Varman** of Java (Srivijaya) in honor of his father **Sri Chudamani Varman**.
  * *Implementation* → Commanded by Raja Raja Chola I; executed by **Rajendra Chola I (1014–1044 CE)**.
* **Plates Composition** → Set of **21 large plates** (stringed with royal Chola ring/seal) and 3 small plates.
  * *Large Plates* → 5 Sanskrit plates, 16 Tamil plates, compiled by Rajendra Chola I.
  * *Small Plates* → Compiled under **Kulottunga Chola I (1070–1120 CE)**; granted extra land/paddy after Javanese emissaries appealed for enforcement.
  * *Chola Royal Insignia* → Tiger (Cholas), Two Fish (Pandyas), Bow (Cheras) → signifying Chola hegemony over Pandyas and Cheras.
* *Parallel Request* → Efforts to retrieve **Velvikkudi copper plates** (Pandya ruler Parantaka Nedunchadaiyan, 765-815 CE) from British Museum.

## Organisations and Groupings

### OPEC
* **UAE Exit** → UAE terminated its nearly 6-decade OPEC membership (joined 1967).
  * *Capacity Conflict* → UAE holds 113B barrels reserves (6th largest); invested **$150 billion** (2023-27) to expand production capacity to **5 mbpd**.
  * *The Quota Bottleneck* → Under Saudi-led OPEC quotas, UAE output was restricted to 3.2–3.5 mbpd, wasting **1.5 mbpd spare capacity**.
  * *Diversification* → UAE needs oil revenues immediately to finance **AI, data centres**, and transition projects.
  * *Geopolitical Rift* → Wider differences with Saudi Arabia over Yemen, Sudan, Libya, and quotas; UAE views OPEC as a tool of Saudi hegemony; UAE maintains closer ties to Israel.
* **Impact on OPEC+** → Reduces OPEC's global market share below **36.7%** (2025), eroding its pricing power against US shale, Norway, and Brazil.
* **Significance for India** → India imports 90% of its crude oil; increased UAE capacity promises lower global oil prices, supply flexibility, and JV downstream investments at **Fujairah Port**.
