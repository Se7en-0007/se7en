- The key issue in the economic discussions around the **BRICS summit in New Delhi** is the **gap between India’s international economic rhetoric and its national industrial capabilities**.
- India’s **manufacturing base**, rather than its diplomacy, is the **binding constraint** on the economic agenda of the summit.
- The same weakness appears in two relationships: **India cannot export manufactured goods to Russia at a comparable scale**, while it remains **dependent on Chinese inputs** for domestic manufacturing and manufactured exports.

## Modi And Putin

- **Prime Minister Narendra Modi** met **Russian President Vladimir Putin** ahead of the BRICS summit, with **trade, energy and defence** expected to be central to their conversation.
- In recent bilateral engagements, India and Russia have tried to bring greater balance to their **weak economic partnership**.
- They are associated with the first **India-Russia international industrial trade fair**, **INNOPROM. India**, held in Delhi from **September 9 to 11** as a platform for trade and industrial cooperation.

## What Does The Trade Profile With Russia Reveal About What India Can Sell?

- India’s exports to Russia remain **below $5 billion**, against imports of **$63.8 billion** in the year to **March 2025**.
- The resulting trade deficit is **nearly $59 billion**.
- **Russian oil and other natural resources** dominate India’s imports from Russia.
- Russia is a substantial market for manufactured goods, so the constraint is not the market but **India’s supply-side capability**.

## What Does China’s Export Record To Russia Show About The Size Of The Gap?

- China exports to Russia in categories including **cars, machinery, electronics and industrial equipment**.
- These are precisely the categories that **India cannot supply at comparable scale**.
- The contrast highlights the limitations of India’s manufacturing base in translating diplomatic ties into large-scale manufactured exports.

## Why Does The Goal Of Economic Security Collide With What Indian Industry Needs?

- **Chinese inputs** are embedded in Indian production across **electronics, machinery, chemicals, auto components and pharmaceutical inputs**.
- These inputs also feed India’s own exports of manufactured goods.
- A limited manufacturing base therefore has two consequences: an inability to export significantly to Russia and continuing import dependence on China.
- Bridging the gap requires **sustained economic reform, simpler regulation, greater competitiveness, less corruption, deeper domestic supply chains and a stronger manufacturing ecosystem**.

## Challenges To Widening India’s Manufacturing Base

### Firms Stay Small, And Stay Small For Long

- India’s manufacturing size distribution is dominated by **tiny units**, leaving few firms capable of fulfilling a large export order.
- **Most registered manufacturing units in India employ fewer than ten workers**.
- The proposed fix is to make support conditional on growth in **employment and turnover**, instead of enabling firms to remain below a small-unit threshold.

### Duties On Inputs Tax The Exporter

- Tariffs on **intermediate goods** raise the cost of components that a finished-goods exporter must purchase.
- Duties on electronic components have been cut in successive Budgets because they increased assembly costs.
- The proposed fix is a **single low duty band** on intermediate and capital goods, while reserving protection for finished goods.

### Logistics Cost Eats The Margin

- Dependence on road freight and long **dwell time at ports** raise the delivered price of Indian goods.
- The **National Logistics Policy of 2022** aimed to bring logistics costs as a share of output closer to competitor levels.
- The proposed fix is to link port and freight-corridor funding to published **turnaround and transit-time targets**.

### Assembly Has Grown Faster Than Component Making

- Incentives have drawn final assembly into India without creating an adequate domestic base for parts, keeping import content high.
- **Mobile phone exports have risen sharply**, but **display panels and battery cells** continue to be largely imported.
- The proposed fix is to condition incentive payouts on a rising schedule of **domestic value addition**, rather than output value alone.
