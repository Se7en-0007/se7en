- **BRICS** is an **intergovernmental organisation** named after its founding members - **Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa**.
- India is hosting the **two-day BRICS summit on September 12-13**, attended by **Chinese President Xi Jinping**, **Russian President Vladimir Putin**, **Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian** and others.
- The **Iran war** and the upcoming **US mid-term elections in November** may have occupied US President Donald Trump, but he had earlier targeted BRICS with **tariff threats** over its alleged **“un-American” policies**.
- The West’s relations with key BRICS members have been deteriorating:
    
    - **Iran and Russia** have been removed from **SWIFT**, the secure global messaging network used by financial institutions to transmit information and payment instructions safely, and are engaged in military conflict.
    - The US has been in a bitter **trade war with China** for years and is pressuring countries to limit dependence on **Chinese products**.
    - India faced the **weaponisation of the Western financial system** when the US imposed **steep tariffs last year** amid disagreements over trade-deal negotiations.
    - India’s sovereign decision to buy **Russian oil** during the Ukraine war also made it a target.
- These are presented as prominent impacts of **over-reliance on the Western economic order**.

## Strict Patent Monopolies

- The most severe impact of Western dominance over international **intellectual property** was felt during the **Covid-19 pandemic**, when developed nations denied a waiver for vaccines.
- In **October 2020**, **India and South Africa** sought a waiver for all WTO members from certain provisions of the **Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights Agreement** in relation to the **“prevention, containment or treatment” of Covid-19**.
- At the peak of the pandemic, developed countries turned down the demand for the waiver, in what was described as a shock to the developing world.
- The unsuccessful attempt by **India, South Africa and sixty other developing countries** to secure the **Covid TRIPS Waiver** highlighted the danger of a patent regime that gives **primacy to patents over patients**.
- The waiver sought to prevent barriers to:
    
    - **Timely access to affordable medical products**, including vaccines and medicines.
    - Scaling up **research, development, manufacturing and supply** of essential medical products.
- **TRIPS** is an international agreement administered by the **World Trade Organisation** that establishes minimum standards for protecting intellectual property; it came into effect in **January 1995**.
- The patent regime expands both the **scope and duration of patentability**, increasing patent holders’ **monopolistic control of technology** and imposing dynamic costs, especially on developing countries that are generally **technology consumers**.

## Ever-Expanding Sanctions Regime

- **Steep US tariffs** exposed the risks of overdependence on the US market for sanctions.
- In his working paper, **“Asphyxiation by Sanctions: Harm, Fear and Smog”**, former RBI Governor **Urjit R Patel** called the US the **“hegemonic sanctioner”**.
- Patel argued that the emerging international financial architecture around **BRICS** and the **Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank** should be seen as a **“risk mitigant”** and a rational response to the **ever-expanding sanctions regime**.
- Of **1,325 global sanctions since 1949**, the US imposed **486**.
- The US currently administers **over 30 sanctions programmes**, making it responsible for **three times as many sanctions as any other country or international body**.
- US-led sanctions have risen in recent decades, partly due to the **collapse of the Soviet Union**.
- The US has **“pioneered secondary sanctions on an industrial scale”**, often with allies such as the **G7** and the **European Union**, forming a **“posse”**.
- These **extraterritorial sanctions** aim to impede economic and commercial activity by third countries that would not otherwise violate a primary sanctioner’s rules.

### Chabahar Port

- India’s plan to develop **Chabahar Port** began in **2003**, but US sanctions targeting Iran caused a **“hard break”** in its progress.
- The project was revived between **2015 and 2017**, after the US eased sanctions under the **2015 Iran nuclear deal**.
- Soon after India signed a long-term **10-year agreement** with Iran in **2024** to operate and develop Chabahar Port, the US State Department warned that entities doing business with Iran could face potential sanctions risks.

## Double Standards In Western Economic Policies

- The US has explicitly imposed tariffs to cut imports from developing countries while introducing subsidy schemes under laws such as the **“One Big Beautiful Bill Act”**.
- The **European Union** has implemented environment-linked trade regimes that act as barriers to India’s export growth.
- BRICS nations have **“condemned and rejected”** the EU’s **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism** and similar restrictive trade measures, stating that they undermine their transition to a cleaner economy.
- The Global North’s industrial policies are **endogenously financed** and implemented with little regard for international trade rules, enabled by its structural power and reinforced by strong control over key technologies through:
    
    - **Patents**
    - **Trade secrets**
    - **Trademarks**
    - **Related mechanisms**
- Western economies deploy **massive industrial subsidies, tariffs and tech controls** to support their strategic sectors, while bypassing multilateral WTO rules when convenient.
- Northern **“green tariffs”** impose trade penalties on goods from developing countries that lack the capital to immediately shift to low-carbon production, thereby externalising the cost of climate adaptation onto the **Global South**.
- Indian trade experts argue that trade-climate linkage through regulations such as **CBAM** is less about environmental protection and more about advancing the commercial interests of the developed world.
- A **2021 UNCTAD study** estimated that the EU’s carbon tax would reduce global carbon emissions by only **0.1%**, while substantially impeding exports from developing countries.
