- On **September 11, 2001**, **19 terrorists associated with al-Qaeda** hijacked **four civilian airliners**.
- **Two aircraft struck the twin towers of New York City’s World Trade Center**, one struck the **Pentagon**, and the fourth crashed in **Pennsylvania**.
- The **World Trade Center was completely destroyed** and **2,977 people were killed**.
- The attacks demonstrated a new face of **asymmetric warfare**, in which **non-state actors** operating from distant sanctuaries could inflict **strategic shock** on a global superpower.

## Operation Enduring Freedom

- The US launched **Operation Enduring Freedom** on **October 7**, a month after the attacks.
- Its objectives were to:
    
    - **Dismantle al-Qaeda**
    - Remove the **Taliban regime** that had provided haven to the terrorist outfit
    - Capture or kill top leadership and **high value targets**, including **Osama bin Laden**, through overwhelming force
- Local Afghan forces such as the **Northern Alliance** were part of the US-led international coalition.
- The Taliban regime was quickly overthrown, followed by the **strategic expansion of counter terrorism operations**.

## Global War On Terror To ‘Forever War’

- The **Global War on Terror (GWOT)** became the organising framework of US national security.
- It entailed:
    
    - **Unprecedented global intelligence networks**
    - **Special forces** - small teams central to counterterrorism
    - Intelligence-led **precision strikes by armed drones**
    - Scrutiny of **terrorist funding**
- Initial objectives were specific, but the mission gradually expanded into **counter-insurgency, regime change, and nation-building**.
- This fundamentally changed the complexion of war into a **“Forever War”**.

## Tactical And Technological Superiority

- US forces possessed overwhelming **tactical and technological superiority**.
- **Satellites, signal intelligence, cyber capabilities, and networked command systems** compressed the **“sensor to shooter” cycle** from days to hours or even minutes.
- The **al-Qaeda core leadership** was dismantled, and **Osama bin Laden** was eliminated in **Abbottabad, Pakistan, in 2011**.
- **ISIS**, after establishing the **“Caliphate”** - a self-proclaimed Islamic state spanning parts of Iraq and Syria - was also annihilated.
- However, **tactical edge could not compensate for strategic outreach**: **“winning battles does not necessarily mean winning the war.”**

## Iraq And Afghanistan

- The **Iraq invasion in 2003** opened another theatre, diverted resources, and created vulnerabilities later exploited by extremist outfits.
- Major combat operations under **Operation Enduring Freedom** concluded on **December 28, 2014**, though wider involvement of US-led coalition forces continued.
- After two decades, US forces withdrew from Afghanistan after enormous expenditure and extensive human cost.
- The departure of **Major General Christopher Donahue**, Commander of the **82nd Airborne Division**, with the last US personnel in a **C-130 aircraft from Kabul on August 30, 2021**, marked the literal end of US military presence in Afghanistan.

## The Most Enduring Lesson

- External military power can destroy **terrorist infrastructure** but cannot substitute for **legitimate politics, effective governance, and indigenous institutions**.
- Military interventions can create **strategic space** and an environment for a political process, but cannot ensure **political legitimacy**.
- The principal error in GWOT was not the use of force - which was inevitable to destroy the al-Qaeda network - but losing sight of **specific aims and missions**.
- Since terrorism is a tool of **political violence**, it is crucial to address the **political and socio-economic issues** in which the extremist narrative thrives.

## Terrorism Transformed

- Two decades ago, terrorist organisations were comparatively **hierarchical**, with recognisable leadership, known sanctuaries and training camps, financing networks, and centrally planned operations.
- Currently, terrorist outfits are **networked, decentralised, digitised, and individualised lone actors**.
- **Al-Qaeda and ISIS** have not disappeared completely but have dispersed into parts of **Africa, West Asia, and South Asia**.
- New havens are **weak states**, **civil war-prone regions**, and **ungoverned spaces**.
- Training is imparted through **smartphones** rather than camps.
- Recruitment and radicalisation now occur through **encrypted communications and social media**, without physical contact or formal organisation.
- **Lone wolves** and small autonomous cells can undertake violent acts with minimal warning.

## Technology And Terrorism

- **Commercial drones** are employed for reconnaissance and delivery of explosives.
- **Artificial intelligence** is used for propaganda, psychological warfare, and cyber warfare.
- **Cryptocurrencies** and dubious financial channels make tracking difficult.
- Terrorism is becoming increasingly **faceless, anonymous, and high-tech**.
- The distinction between **domestic and foreign terrorism** is becoming blurred.

## The War On Terror

- During the war on terror, US forces decimated al-Qaeda leadership and severely degraded its core, but **did not end terrorism**.
- **Jihadist ideology, affiliates, and radicalised elements** survived.
- When American forces withdrew, the **Taliban were back in Kabul**.
- Iraq showed that **regime change is easier than building a stable political order**.
- **Tactical dominance did not translate into strategic outcomes**, creating a paradox that resists simplistic analogy.

## Great Power Competition

- The US paid a heavy price for involvement in **West Asia and Afghanistan**.
- While embroiled in GWOT, **China rose rapidly** and **Russia reasserted itself**, bringing **great power competition** back to centre stage.
- Ironically, the US is once again stuck in **West Asia**.
- Ignoring past lessons can turn into future **blind spots**.

## Technology As The New Battlespace

- Inexpensive **autonomous systems** give non-state actors and proxy outfits significant capability to conduct operations with **strategic effect**.
- There is now considerable overlap between **conventional war and terrorism**, leading to **hybrid warfare**.
- Only **prevention and pre-emptive measures** can eliminate perpetual **“never ending wars.”**
- Key measures include:
    
    - **Coordinated real-time intelligence**
    - **Border management**
    - **Financial disruption**
    - **Precise counterterrorism actions**
    - **Good governance**
    - **Counter radicalisation**
    - Addressing the conditions that extremist organisations exploit
- **9/11 changed the very face of terrorism**, while the wars that followed exposed the **limits of military power**.
