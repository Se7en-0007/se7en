- Ahead of the **BRICS summit in New Delhi on September 12-13**, Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** gifted Russian President **Vladimir Putin** a **Russian translation** of the traditional Tamil text **Thirukkural** during their bilateral meeting on **September 11**.
- Authored by the poet-philosopher **Thiruvalluvar**, also known as **“Valluvar”**, the **Thirukkural** is a seminal work of **ancient Tamil literature and philosophy**.

## What Is Thirukkural?

- **Thirukkural** translates as **“sacred verses”** or **“sacred couplets”** in English.
- It consists of **1,330 stanzas or couplets**, each of **seven words**, called **kurals**.
- These are arranged into **133 chapters**, with **10 couplets** in each chapter.
- Regarded as an influential work on **ethics**, it is divided into **three books**:
    
    - **The Book of Virtue (Dharma)** - deals with morality (**aram**) and the values of an individual; it has **38 chapters**.
    - **The Book of Polity** - contains teachings on materialism (**porul**) and socio-economic values; it has **70 chapters**.
    - **The Book of Love** - details teachings of love (**inbam**); it has **25 chapters**.

## Thirukkural Translation Series

- The **Thirukkural** was first published in English in **1840** and has since been translated into **more than 60 different languages**.
- The edition gifted to Putin was translated into Russian by **Naira Mkrtchyan**.
- It was published this year by the **Central Institute of Classical Tamil** as part of its **Thirukkural translation series**.

## Themes Of Thirukkural

- The **Thirukkural** emphasises **moral conduct** and addresses the realities of human life.
- Thiruvalluvar discouraged divisions based on **religion, caste, creed, and race**.
- He asserted that **morality and kindness** are indispensable for a meaningful human life.
- Though composed more than **two millennia ago**, between **300 BCE and 500 BCE**, it retains profound relevance in modern times.
- Thiruvalluvar warned that life devoid of morality leads to **misery**, as individuals become dominated by **jealousy, greed, wrath, and harshness**.
- To elevate human life and social well-being, he outlines virtues such as **spirituality, charity, and love**.

## Kadavul Vazhthu

- The concept of God is set out in the first chapter, **Kadavul Vazhthu** or **The Praise of God**.
- For Thiruvalluvar, God is **“pure intelligence”** and **omniscient**, embodying **wisdom, compassion, morality, and love**.

## Inbam

- The topic of love, **inbam**, is classified into two forms:
    
    - **Kalaviyal** - refers to **pre-marital love**, subsequently tied to marriage **without any ceremony**.
    - **Karpiyal** - refers to **traditional marital love**.
- Thiruvalluvar employs **poetic imagery** and different **dramatic and lyrical situations** to explore the countless characteristics of love.
- His verses present love not merely as **passion**, but as a source of **tranquil joy**.

## Education

- Some scholars draw parallels between Thiruvalluvar’s reflections on education and those of the ancient Greek philosopher **Plato**.
- For the Tamil philosopher, **virtue, wisdom, and education are inseparable**: one who has received proper education is a **virtuous man**.

## Reception

- **E S Ariel**, who translated the text into French in the nineteenth century, described the **Thirukkural** as **“a book without a name by an author without a name”**.
- **George Uglow Pope (1820-1908)**, an Anglican Christian missionary and Tamil scholar, was among the first scholars to study Thiruvalluvar systematically.
- He honoured Thiruvalluvar with the title **“The Bard of Universal Man”**.
