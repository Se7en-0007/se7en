- **The United States-led global war on terror inadvertently emboldened Iran**, elevating it into a **regional power capable of confronting the U.S.** in the ongoing conflict.

## Strategic Containment

- Before **2001**, Iran faced strategic containment by two hostile regimes: **the Taliban in Afghanistan** and **Saddam Hussein’s Iraq**.
- U.S. invasions toppled both governments, thereby eliminating **Iran’s most dangerous neighbours**.
- The new governments in Afghanistan and Iraq proved **friendlier to Tehran**.
- In Iraq, the collapse of the **Sunni regime** produced a **Shia-led government with deep ties to Iran**, converting an enemy into a **strategic partner**.
- Analysts held that Iran had surpassed the U.S. as the **most influential power in Iraq**, substantially improving its strategic position.

## Sectarianism Was The Fuel

- The invasions of Afghanistan and Iraq created regional chaos that fuelled sectarian conflict centred on the **Shia-Sunni divide**.
- This environment enabled terrorist groups such as **al-Qaeda in Iraq**, which later evolved into the **Islamic State of Iraq and Syria - ISIS**.
- Regional powers took opposing sides: **Iran bolstered Shia militias in Iraq**, while **Saudi Arabia supported Sunni extremists**.
- This **sub-conventional proxy warfare** entrenched Iran as a **regional power broker**, contrary to the U.S. objective of isolating Iran.
- As an ideologically **anti-Shia** organisation, ISIS posed an unprecedented threat to Iran, compelling it to become a central actor in the **anti-ISIS coalition** through military advisers and regional allies.
- Iran consolidated its influence by acting as a key counter-terrorism player, exploiting power vacuums and using the crisis to strengthen its diplomatic position.
- A shared enemy created a narrow opportunity for dialogue with the U.S., but no synchronisation occurred because **President George W. Bush** had described Iran as part of the **“Axis of Evil”** in his **2002 State of the Union address**, alongside Iraq and North Korea, citing their pursuit of **weapons of mass destruction** and support for terrorism.

## The Role Of The ‘Axis Of Resistance’

- The **“Axis of Resistance”** was a political, military and social network linking **Iran, Iraq, Syria, Lebanon and Palestine**.
- It was conceived as a **“ring of fire” around Israel**, functioning as a form of **forward defence** for Iran.
- Iran gained buffer zones in **Iraq, Lebanon, Syria and Yemen** through key Axis members: **Hezbollah, Hamas, the Houthis and Shia militias in Iraq**.
- The Axis aided the emergence of the **“Shia Crescent”**, an arc of Iranian influence extending through **Iraq, Syria and Lebanon**.
- Iran gained international legitimacy for its military activities in Iraq and Syria.
- **Hezbollah** became battle-hardened and politically entrenched, while the **Houthis** evolved from a local insurgent movement into a political insurgent group.
- Iran concentrated on the **Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps - IRGC**, which controls strategic assets and specialises in **sub-conventional warfare**, including control of proxies; its conventional military received far less attention.
- The U.S. withdrawal from Iraq and the **Arab Spring in 2011** created vacuums that Iran was uniquely positioned to fill.
- Iran invested heavily in the Axis to secure an **asymmetric advantage** over the U.S. and Israel, since matching their conventional military strength was difficult for Tehran.

## The “Forever Wars” In West Asia

- The **“Forever Wars”** drained American resources and attention, allowing Iran to expand its influence unimpeded while reinforcing its hardline posture.
- The rise of ISIS paradoxically benefited Iran, which became among the first and most important military supporters of the Iraqi government against the group.
- Iran deployed its elite **Quds Force** to coordinate Baghdad’s defence, cementing its influence over Iraq’s security apparatus.
- In Syria, defending the **Assad regime** protected Iran’s only major state ally and consolidated its supply routes to Hezbollah in Lebanon.

## The Transformation Of Iran

- The global war on terror transformed Iran from a **contained state** into a **dominant regional power**.
- By removing hostile regimes, creating power vacuums and unintentionally strengthening Iran’s alliance network, U.S. actions generated outcomes **diametrically opposed** to their stated objectives.
- The war enabled Tehran to project power in **Afghanistan, Lebanon, Syria and Yemen**.
- Iran’s role as a **pivotal broker** was thereby cemented, with lasting consequences for West Asian geopolitics.
- Iran increasingly invested in the Axis while rallying domestic support around **nationalism rather than religion**.
- Iran’s present resilience against the **U.S.-Israeli offensive in West Asia** can be viewed as a manifestation of the strategic advantages arising from the global war on terror.
