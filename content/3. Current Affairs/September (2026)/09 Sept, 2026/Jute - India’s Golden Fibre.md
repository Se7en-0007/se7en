## Sustaining Livelihoods Through Innovative Policy Interventions

- **India leads global raw jute production**, supporting millions of livelihoods and advancing sustainable industrial growth.
- In **FY 2025-26**, India produced **76 lakh bales of raw jute** and **12.80 lakh MT of jute goods**.
- India exported **22,700 MT of raw jute worth ₹225.11 crore** and **1,71,300 MT of jute goods worth ₹3,155.27 crore** in FY 2025-26.
- Government interventions have strengthened **farmer incomes**, modernised the value chain, and promoted **product diversification**.
- Jute’s **biodegradable nature** and expanding applications make it a key alternative to synthetic materials.

## India’s Golden Fibre Industry

- **Jute and mesta** are collectively classified as **raw jute** in the trade and industrial context due to their comparable end uses.
- **Mesta** is a bast fibre crop cultivated as a cost-effective alternative to jute, particularly in drier agro-climatic regions.
- Jute is called the **“Golden Fibre”** because of its characteristic **golden colour** and **silky lustre**.
- **India is the world’s largest producer of raw jute.**
- Production of jute and mesta increased from **88.02 lakh bales in 2024-25** to **94.03 lakh bales in 2025-26**, according to the **Third Advance Estimate of Production of Food Grains (2025-26)**.
- India is the leading global producer of jute goods, accounting for about **75% of estimated world production** as of **July 2026**.
- The industry has a heavy industrial base in eastern India, especially **West Bengal**.
- It provides direct employment to about **3.70 lakh workers** in organised mills and diversified jute units.
- Nearly **40 lakh farm families** depend on jute cultivation for livelihoods.
- As of **December 2025**, India had **120 composite jute mills**, of which **89** were in **West Bengal**.
- India is fully **Atmanirbhar** in jute goods, with domestic output adequately meeting national demand.
- Domestic consumption of jute goods was **12.05 lakh MT** in FY 2025-26, constituting **94%** of total production of **12.80 lakh MT**.

## Tracing The History Of Jute Production In India

- The history of jute in India dates back to the **third century BC**.
- It became a significant commercial commodity during the **British Colonial period** because of its **strategic military applications**.
- The **East India Company** established numerous jute mills in Bengal, making the region a centre for jute cultivation and processing.
- Following independence and Partition in **1947**, nearly **80%** of jute-growing areas became part of **East Pakistan - now Bangladesh**.
- Most jute mills remained in India, creating an acute shortage of raw jute for processing.
- The government launched the **“Grow More Jute” campaign** in **September 1949**, encouraging farmers to shift from paddy cultivation to jute.
- Raw jute production more than doubled from **6.7 lakh tonnes in 1951-52** to **14.56 lakh tonnes in 1961-62**.
- The **Jute Packaging Act, 1987** mandated the use of jute sacks for packaging essential commodities, ensuring a stable market for Indian jute farmers.
- Jute subsequently emerged as a **strategic green fibre**, contributing to rural livelihoods and environmentally sustainable industrial development.
- **West Bengal** has the largest share of raw jute cultivation, followed by **Bihar, Assam, Odisha, and Jharkhand**.
- Jute is generally sown during **March-April** and harvested within **100-110 days**.
- It requires hot and humid conditions and rainfall of **700-1,500 mm** during the crop-growing period.
- Cultivation is concentrated in eastern and northeastern India, is predominantly **rainfed**, and is primarily undertaken by **small and marginal farmers**.

## Why Is Jute An Important Natural Fibre?

- Jute is a **biodegradable** and **recyclable** natural fibre with rapid growth, cost-effectiveness, and wide availability.
- It is **strong, durable, breathable, and versatile**.
- Its applications include **packaging, construction, agriculture, and industrial textiles**.
- Its functional attributes include **thermal and acoustic insulation**, high **moisture absorption**, and **low static generation**.
- Jute blends well with both **natural and synthetic fibres**, enabling value-added and textile applications.

## Can Jute Be Eaten As Food?

- **Jute leaves** have long been consumed as a vegetable in **Africa, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia**.
- In **Japan**, jute leaves are recognised and consumed as a **health food**.
- Jute leaves are rich in **vitamins, carotenoids, calcium, potassium, and dietary fiber**.
- Proven technologies have been developed for **anti-oxidant herbal drink** and **health care products** from jute leaf.

## Jute Geotextile: The Fabric Of Sustainable Infrastructure

- **Jute Geotextile (JGT)** is a diversified jute product belonging to the class of **technical textiles**.
- It is produced from jute fibres through **special treatment and weaving processes**.
- JGT is used in **civil engineering, soil erosion control, road pavement construction, and protection of river banks**.
- It is gaining acceptance because of functional effectiveness, environmental compatibility, favourable techno-physical properties, and price competitiveness.
- JGT is **fully biodegradable** and improves **soil structure and fertility**.
- It is recognised globally as a natural solution for **soil stabilisation, erosion control, and ecological restoration** in degraded landscapes.
- JGT regulates the micro-environment by **maintaining lower soil temperatures** and **reducing surface disturbance**, supporting seed germination and early plant establishment.

## Applications Of Jute Geotextile

- JGT is used for **slope protection** in earthen embankments, hill cuttings, riverbanks, canals, and rainwater harvesting structures.
- Its high initial tensile strength and surface roughness enhance soil stabilisation and mitigate erosion.
- In **road and haul road construction** and **railway track formation**, JGT improves load distribution and controls settlement, reducing maintenance requirements and lifecycle costs.
- In **concealed drainage systems**, especially in hilly regions, it enables regulated water flow while preventing soil migration.
- JGT stabilises soft soils through **prefabricated vertical drains**, accelerating consolidation and improving bearing capacity.
- The **National Jute Board** provides technical support on design specifications, procurement processes, testing protocols, and installation practices.

## From Farm To Market: Government Interventions For The Jute Industry

- The **Minimum Support Price - MSP** for raw jute has been fixed at **₹5,925 per quintal** for **FY 2026-27**.
- This MSP ensures a **61.8% return** over the all-India weighted average cost of production.
- The MSP represents an increase of around **147%** from **₹2,400 per quintal** in FY 2014-15.
- MSP amount paid to jute farmers rose from **₹441 crore during FY 2004-05 to FY 2013-14** to **₹1,342 crore during FY 2014-15 to FY 2025-26**.
- Under the **Jute Packaging Materials (Compulsory Use in Packing Commodities) Act**, jute packaging is mandatory for **100% food grains** and **20% sugar**.

## Jute Corporation Of India

- The **Jute Corporation of India Ltd. - JCI** is the **sole nodal agency for implementing the MSP policy for raw jute**.
- JCI procures directly from farmers when market prices fall below MSP.
- Its **Departmental Purchase Centers - DPCs** are located in rural areas and purchase raw jute directly from farmers.
- JCI has around **110 DPCs** in **West Bengal, Assam, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and Tripura**.
- Government efforts to support jute cultivation and processing have been integrated under the **National Jute Development Programme**.

## National Jute Development Programme

- The **National Jute Development Programme - NJDP** is the umbrella scheme for development and promotion of the jute sector.
- It is implemented by the **National Jute Board** with an outlay of **₹485.58 crore** from **FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26**.
- It focuses on improving **farm productivity**, **farmer incomes**, and diversification through **JUTE-ICARE** and the **Jute Diversification Scheme**.
- It supports seminars, exhibitions, and awareness campaigns to promote jute as an alternative to plastics.

## National Jute Board

- The **National Jute Board - NJB** is the nodal agency for the **development, promotion, and diversification** of the jute sector across the value chain.
- Its functions include improving cultivation and fibre quality, enhancing productivity and standardisation, promoting domestic and export markets, supporting research and value-added product diversification, and strengthening **MSMEs and artisans** through capacity building and market development.

## JUTE-ICARE (Improved Cultivation And Advanced Retting Exercise) Scheme

- **JUTE-ICARE** was launched in **FY 2015-16** to improve **jute productivity** and fibre quality.
- It promotes scientific cultivation, mechanisation, and improved retting techniques.
- It supports small and marginal farmers through subsidised certified seeds, field demonstrations, and mechanised interventions.
- It is implemented with the **Central Research Institute for Jute and Allied Fibers - CRIJAF** and the **Jute Corporation of India**.
- The scheme facilitates construction of retting ponds through convergence with the **Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana** and **Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana**.

## Jute Diversification Scheme

- The **Jute Diversification Scheme** includes schemes supporting jute processing, marketing, and sales.

### Intervention For Supporting Jute Processing: Jute Raw Material Bank

- The **Jute Raw Material Bank - JRMB** ensures raw jute availability to the **unorganised sector and small production units** manufacturing jute diversified products.
- It supplies raw jute at the economic rate of **mill-gate price plus actual transportation costs**.
- It supports artisans, women **SHGs**, and **MSMEs** through regular access to quality raw materials and promotes rural livelihoods.

### Interventions For Manufacturing, Marketing, And Sales

- **Capital Subsidy for Acquisition of Plant & Machinery** provides a **30% subsidy** on machinery costs for jute mills and MSMEs producing **Jute Diversified Products - JDPs**.
- **Jute Resource-cum-Production Centres** provide cluster-based training and production support to artisans, women SHGs, entrepreneurs, NGOs, and cooperative society members.
- **Jute Retail Outlets** receive **25% sales assistance**, up to **₹9 lakh per unit**, for retail and showroom sales of JDPs.
- **Production Linked Incentive for JDP Exports** provides exporters incentives linked to raw-material usage in exported JDPs and value realisation from exports.

## Interventions For Branding, Quality Assurance, And Market Promotion

### Jute Mark India Logo

- The **Jute Mark India logo** represents authenticity, quality assurance, and sustainability in jute and jute-blended products.
- Its stylised jute sheaves in a structured framework represent natural fibre strength, craftsmanship, and standardisation across the value chain.
- It certifies compliance with quality standards and verifies jute content in blended products.
- It enhances consumer confidence and strengthens brand recognition.

- These included the **India International Trade Fair, Surajkund Mela, and Bharat Tex**.

- **Bharatiya Gramothan Sanstha, Rishikesh; Hayden Hall Institute, Darjeeling; Jute Artisans Guild Association, Lucknow; and Rural Organisation for Social Action, Cuttack** were trained under National Jute Board programmes.

- Participation enabled direct buyer interactions, bulk export orders, stronger marketing skills, higher individual earnings, improved production capacity, and greater social recognition.

## Digital And Technological Interventions Across The Supply Chain

### JUTE-SMART

- Since **2016**, the Office of Jute Commissioner has managed procurement and supply of jute sacking bags for food grains.
- The end-to-end e-governance portal **JUTE-SMART** was developed and implemented in **November 2016**.
- By **August 2026**, more than **20 State Procurement Agencies**, including Punjab, Haryana, Odisha, Telangana, West Bengal, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, and **FCI**, had procured **303 lakh bales of B.Twill Jute Bags** through the portal.
- Each bale contains **500 bags**, and the procurement value exceeded approximately **₹1,00,000 crore**.
- In **2026**, JUTE-SMART was upgraded to digitise supply-chain processes and onboard transporters, raw-jute traders, and importers.
- Registration of raw-jute traders, importers, and jute mills has been completely digitised, and required returns are fully online to improve ease of compliance.

### Jute Crop Information System

- The **Indian Space Research Organization - ISRO**, through the **National Remote Sensing Centre - NRSC**, developed the **Jute Crop Information System** in collaboration with JCI and NJB.
- The system monitors jute cultivation through **remote sensing and field data**.
- **BHUVAN JUMP** is a mobile application for on-field jute monitoring.
- **PATSAN - Prospective Assessment of Jute Using Mobile App-Based Field Observations** is a web-based platform providing **near-real-time jute surveillance and analytics** to support informed decision-making by officials and stakeholders.

## Market Development & Promotion Scheme

- Under NJDP, the **Market Development & Promotion Scheme** supports JDP entrepreneurs and exporters in promotion and sale of JDPs in domestic and export markets.

## Workers’ Welfare Scheme (Scholarship Scheme)

- The **National Jute Board Act, 2008** provides for securing better working conditions, amenities, and incentives for workers engaged in the jute industry.
- NJB provides **scholarship and incentive support** to girl children of workers of jute mills and JDP-MSME units after they pass **Secondary and Higher Secondary examinations**.
