## From Awareness To Action For Cleaner Air

- **The International Day of Clean Air for Blue Skies is observed globally on 7 September** and highlights the need for urgent action against air pollution.
- **India’s multi-sectoral response includes the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) across 130 cities.**
- In **FY 2025-26**, **108 cities** recorded lower **PM10** levels than in **2017-18**; **72 cities** achieved reductions above **20%**, **26 cities** above **40%**, and **14 cities** met the **National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)**.
- India’s clean-air efforts include **strengthened monitoring, cleaner mobility, city-specific action and scientific interventions**.

## Towards Cleaner Air: A Global And National Commitment

- **Clean air is fundamental to human health, environmental sustainability and economic well-being.**
- The **United Nations General Assembly designated 7 September in 2019** to strengthen global action against air pollution, raise public awareness and promote actions to improve air quality.
- India observes the day as **Swachh Vayu Diwas**, linking the global campaign with national priorities.
- The Government’s **comprehensive, multi-sectoral approach** combines **air-quality monitoring, emission control and city-specific action plans**.
- The Central Government supports States and cities through targeted programmes and policy interventions, while **State Governments and district administrations** implement measures on the ground.
- These efforts address pollution sources and strengthen institutional capacity for sustained air-quality management.

## Understanding Air Pollution

- **Air pollution** occurs when harmful substances accumulate in the atmosphere at levels affecting human health and the environment.
- Pollutants may be **gases, liquid droplets or solid particles** and can originate from natural processes and human activities, including **transport, industries, construction and burning**.
- **Particulate Matter (PM)** consists of tiny solid or liquid particles suspended in air.
- **PM10** comprises particles measuring **10 micrometres or less** in diameter.
- **PM2.5** comprises finer particles measuring **2.5 micrometres or less** in diameter.
- A human hair is about **100 micrometres wide**; almost **40 PM2.5 particles** could be placed across its width.
- Fine particles can penetrate deeper into the respiratory system, and some can enter the bloodstream, increasing potential health impacts.
- Controlling particulate pollution is important for **cleaner air and healthier communities**.

# India In Action To Mitigate Air Pollution

## National Air Quality Monitoring Programme (NAMP)

- The Central Pollution Control Board initiated the **National Ambient Air Quality Monitoring (NAAQM) programme**, subsequently renamed **NAMP**.
- Its objectives are to periodically evaluate air pollution, determine the status and trend of ambient air quality in urban areas, estimate future changes, and identify violations of prescribed ambient air-quality standards and health hazards.
- As of **March 2026**, India’s ambient air-quality monitoring network comprised **1,646 stations across 603 cities in 28 States and 7 UTs**, including **manual stations** and **real-time stations**.
- Manual stations collect and analyse samples periodically, while **Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Systems (CAAQMS)** provide continuous real-time air-quality data.
- The **SAMEER mobile application and web portal** provide near real-time **AQI** information for more than **292 cities**.
- **Public Grievance Redressal Portals** have been established in all NCAP cities to enable public participation in pollution-control efforts.

## Air Pollution Monitoring In India

- India uses **CAAQMS**, **Ambient Air Quality Monitoring System (AAQMS)** and **Online Continuous Emission Monitoring System (OCEMS)** to monitor ambient air quality and pollution from industrial sources.
- **CAAQMS** provides automated real-time monitoring of **PM2.5, PM10, SO2, NOx, CO, O3 and NH3**.
- **AAQMS** covers manual and automated monitoring and detects pollutants including **PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, VOCs** and pollutants specified under **NAAQS**, along with meteorological parameters such as **temperature, humidity, wind direction and wind speed**.
- In manual monitoring, air samples are collected for **24 hours** and analysed in laboratories for long-term assessment of air quality.
- **OCEMS** monitors emissions directly at industrial sources and transmits data continuously to regulatory authorities and the **CPCB server**.
- OCEMS is installed at facilities in **17 categories of highly polluting industries**.
- In **Delhi-NCR**, OCEMS is also mandated for medium and large-scale red-category polluting industries, food and food-processing industries, and metal units.
- OCEMS helps ensure that industrial emissions and effluent discharges remain within **prescribed environmental standards**.

## Air Quality Index (AQI)

- **AQI** is a tool for citizens to understand air quality based on pollutant concentrations and potential health impacts.
- It uses **eight pollutants**: **PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3 and Pb**.
- The **worst-performing pollutant** determines the overall AQI.

|**AQI Category**|**AQI Range**|
|---|---|
|**Good**|**0-50**|
|**Satisfactory**|**51-100**|
|**Moderate**|**101-200**|
|**Poor**|**201-300**|
|**Very Poor**|**301-400**|
|**Severe**|**401-500**|

## National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)

- **NCAP** was launched in **2019** in **130 cities** to address urban air pollution in mission mode.
- It is a **multi-sectoral initiative** involving Central and State Governments, **Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)** and other stakeholders.
- NCAP emphasises **source-specific mitigation measures** through city-, state- and national-level clean-air action plans.
- Its implementation is monitored by committees at the **Central, State and District levels**.

## Key Achievement Of NCAP

- **24 States/UTs and 130 cities** have prepared **State and City-specific Clean Air Action Plans** for key pollution sources through short- and long-term sectoral measures.
- **₹16,423.55 crore** was provided to **130 cities** during **FY 2019-20 to FY 2025-26** through air-quality performance-linked grants.
- Cities reported utilisation of **₹11,575.54 crore**, or **70.5%** of these funds.
- In **FY 2025-26**, **108 cities** reduced annual PM10 concentrations compared with **2017-18**; **72 cities** achieved over **20% reduction**, **26 cities** over **40% reduction**, and **14 cities** met **NAAQS**.

## Digital Tracking Of Clean Air Progress

- **PRANA - Portal for Regulation of Air-Pollution in Non-Attainment Cities** manages and monitors NCAP implementation.
- It tracks the **physical and financial status** of city air action plans and disseminates information on air-quality management efforts to the public.
- Information on NCAP activities and stakeholder progress is available on the portal to support future policy decisions.

## Swachh Vayu Survekshan

- **Swachh Vayu Survekshan** is an annual assessment of **130 NCAP cities**, launched in **2022-23** as an initiative of the **MoEFCC**.
- It assesses measures addressing major pollution sources and implementation of **City-specific Clean Air Action Plans**.
- It promotes **healthy competition, innovation and accelerated action** among cities to improve air quality.

## Champions Of Swachh Vayu Survekshan 2025

- The **2025 Survekshan** recognised **11 best-performing cities** in three population categories.
- **Category 1** included **47 cities** with populations of **10 lakh or more**.
- **Category 2** included **44 cities** with populations between **3 lakh and 10 lakh**.
- **Category 3** included **40 cities** with populations below **3 lakh**.
- **Indore** ranked first in Category 1, **Amravati** topped Category 2, and **Dewas** led Category 3.

## Champions Of Swachh Vayu Survekshan 2026

- **Ward-level Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan** advances the **bottom-up approach** and recognises action at the root level.
- It was conducted in more than **7,400 wards** across the country.
- The **2026 Survekshan** recognised **10 best-performing cities** across three categories.
- **Lucknow** ranked first in Category 1, **Rourkela** topped Category 2, and **Kalinganagar** led Category 3.
- **10 wards** were awarded at the national level across three categories: **Category 1** population above **30,000**, **Category 2** population between **10,000 and 30,000**, and **Category 3** population below **10,000**.
- **Ward 70, Lucknow** ranked first in Category 1; **Wards 30 and 36, Bhubneshwar** topped Category 2; and **Ward 13, Rourkela** led Category 3.
- A further **69 wards**, with **3 wards from each State/UT**, were awarded from **23 States/UTs**.

# Sector-Wise Key Mitigation Measures Taken To Improve Air Quality

## Vehicular Emissions

- **BS VI compliant vehicles** were introduced across the country from **April 2020**.
- **Electric vehicles** are promoted through the **PM E-Drive scheme** and **PM E-Bus Sewa**.
- **Charging infrastructure** for e-vehicles is being established under Ministry of Power guidelines.
- City action plans include **traffic decongestion and junction improvement measures**.
- **MoRTH** issued notifications for **Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facilities (RVSFs)** and **Automated Testing Systems (ATS)** to enable the Vehicle Scrapping Policy.
- MoRTH is improving and strengthening the **Pollution Under Control (PUC) Certificate System**.
- **PARIVARTAN** is a time-bound fleet-modernisation scheme to reduce vehicular pollution in NCR by incentivising replacement of **BS-IV and older trucks and buses** with **BS-VI, CNG or electric vehicles**.
- In Delhi-NCR, CAQM directed migration of public transport, especially buses, to cleaner modes and augmentation of public-transport infrastructure.
- Only **BS-VI/CNG/EV public transport buses**, including tourist buses, may enter Delhi from **1 November 2026**.
- CAQM directed fast-tracking of cleaner mobility for services provided by **Motor Vehicle Aggregators, Delivery Service Providers and E-Commerce Entities** in Delhi-NCR.
- Only **electric L5 category three-wheelers**, for passenger and goods use, are to be inducted in Delhi, followed by high-volume NCR districts - **Gurugram, Faridabad, Sonipat, Ghaziabad and Gautam Buddh Nagar** - and other NCR districts in a graded manner.

## Industrial Emission

- **MoEFCC** has notified environmental standards for industrial sectors; industries without specific standards must follow applicable general standards under the **Environment Protection Rules, 1986**.
- **CPCB** classified **419 industrial sectors and sub-sectors** by pollution potential: **125 Red, 137 Orange, 94 Green, 54 White and 9 Blue**.
- In Delhi-NCR, **CAQM** notified a standard list of approved industrial fuels and requires identified industries to install **OCEMS** connected to CPCB servers.
- CPCB directed **17 highly polluting industry categories** to install OCEMS.
- CAQM revised particulate-matter emission norms to **50 mg/Nm3** for polluting and large industries, applicable from **1 October 2026**.
- **Air Pollution Control Devices** are being installed in identified industrial units.
- State Pollution Control Boards have been advised to take strict monitoring action in non-conforming industrial areas.
- **CEPI-based categorisation** classifies industrial clusters as **Critically Polluted Areas (CPAs)** or **Severely Polluted Areas (SPAs)**, where action plans are mandated to reduce pollution load and improve environmental quality.
- In **2022**, MoEFCC notified stricter **CPCB IV+ emission standards** for power generators up to **800 kW** operating on diesel, gasoline or dual-fuel mode.

## Road Dust And Construction

- CPCB issued guidelines for environmentally sound management of **Construction and Demolition (C&D) waste** and dust control from construction materials and C&D waste.
- **SPCBs and PCCs** must deploy **anti-smog guns** and adequate dust-control measures at construction sites exceeding **20,000 sq. metres**.
- **70 Dust Control and Management Cells (DCMCs)** have been established across NCR and are regularly reviewed by CAQM.
- An online mechanism monitors compliance with dust-mitigation measures at Delhi-NCR construction sites above **500 sq. metres** plot area.
- Delhi-NCR measures include road redevelopment, deployment of **Mechanized Road Sweeping Machines (MRSMs)** and strengthened C&D-waste collection infrastructure.

## C&D Waste

- Construction and road dust contribute around **40-50% of PM10** in most Indian cities, according to **Source Apportionment studies** under NCAP.
- City Clean Air Action Plans prioritise **road improvement, traffic decongestion, junction improvements and greening of open spaces** to reduce dust and improve urban air quality.

## Open Waste Burning

- Measures include enforcement of **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016, revised in 2026**, **Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016, amended in 2022**, **Hazardous Waste Management Rules, 2016**, **E-Waste Management Rules, 2022**, **Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022**, and **Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016**.
- **Market-Based Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** requires producers to manage post-consumer waste from plastic packaging, e-waste, batteries, tyres and used oil.
- EPR promotes recycling, reduces landfill pressure and curbs emissions from informal waste handling.
- **12 single-use plastic items** with low utility and high littering potential, including plastic cutlery, straws and thermocol decorations, were banned from **1 July 2022**.

## Crop Residue And Biomass Burning

- CPCB supports paddy-straw pelletisation and torrefaction plants through one-time financial assistance.
- CAQM directed **5-10% biomass co-firing** in thermal power plants within **300 km of Delhi**, captive plants and brick kilns in NCR.
- **₹37.16 crore** has been sanctioned for **34 paddy straw-based pelletisation and torrefaction plants** in NCR States - **27 in Punjab** and **7 in Haryana**.
- The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare provided **₹4,233.84 crore** for crop-residue management initiatives from **2018-19 to 2025-26**.
- More than **3.53 lakh crop-residue management machines** were distributed and over **43,535 Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs)** established in **Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and the NCT of Delhi**.
- In **2025**, paddy stubble-burning instances declined by **93% in Punjab** and **91% in Haryana** compared with **2021**.

## Afforestation And Green Cover

- The **Green India Mission** focuses on forest quality, ecological resilience and carbon sequestration; **₹1,019.26 crore** was released to States as of **March 2026**.
- Under **CAMPA**, more than **3.20 lakh hectares** of compensatory afforestation were undertaken between **FY 2020-21 and FY 2024-25**.
- **Nagar Van Yojana**, launched in **2020**, aims to develop **1,000 Nagar Vans and Vatikas by 2026-27**.
- As of **31 March 2026**, **₹557.62 crore** had been released for developing **626 Nagar Vans/Vatikas**.
- The **Aravalli Green Wall initiative** targets restoration of **6.31 million hectares** across **Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana and Delhi**.
- It has established **435 nurseries** with a combined capacity of **393.24 lakh seedlings**; about **36,025 hectares** were restored during **2025**.
- Under **Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam**, **262.4 crore saplings** were planted by **December 2025**.

# Institutions Driving India’s Clean Air Goals

- India has a **multi-level institutional framework** involving national regulators, State agencies, urban local bodies and specialised institutions for regulation, monitoring, enforcement, scientific assessment and coordinated action.

## Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)

- **CPCB** is a statutory organisation established under the **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974**.
- It has powers and functions under the **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981**.
- CPCB works to improve air quality and prevent, control and abate air pollution while providing technical services to **MoEFCC**.

## Commission For Air Quality Management (CAQM)

- The **Commission for Air Quality Management in National Capital Region and Adjoining Areas Act, 2021** established **CAQM** as a dedicated statutory body.
- CAQM coordinates air-pollution management across the **NCR airshed** and coordinates implementation of **GRAP**.

## Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP)

- **GRAP** is an emergency-response framework for worsening air quality in the **National Capital Region**.
- It enables multiple government agencies to take graded and targeted measures based on pollution severity.
- Measures are triggered when Delhi’s AQI reaches or is expected to reach specified levels.
- GRAP uses **air-quality data, weather forecasts and scientific assessments** for timely action.
- Its four stages range from **Poor - AQI 201-300** to **Severe+ - AQI above 450**.
- Measures become progressively stricter as air quality deteriorates and include controlling dust and emissions, regulating construction and industrial activities, and restricting certain vehicles.
- CAQM coordinates implementation across NCR.

## State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)

- **SPCBs** are key regulatory bodies at the frontline of environmental regulation.
- They operate under the **Water Act, Air Act and Environment Protection Act** and also monitor noise, manage hazardous and plastic waste, maintain inventories, set standards and ensure compliance across an expanding industrial base.
- Amendments were made to the **Air Act, 1981** and **Water Act, 1974** to improve transparency, consistency and efficiency in industrial environmental regulation.
- Uniform consent guidelines were notified - **Control of Air Pollution (Grant, Refusal or Cancellation of Consent) Guidelines, 2025** on **29 January 2025**, and **Water Pollution (Grant, Refusal or Cancellation of Consent) Guidelines, 2025** on **30 January 2025**.
- **Unified Consent and Authorization Management System (UCAMS)** was launched by CPCB for end-to-end online processing of grant, refusal or cancellation of consent applications by SPCBs and PCCs.
- UCAMS provides uniformity in **fees, siting criteria and timelines**, enhancing transparency and traceability.

# Way Forward

- India’s clean-air efforts will focus increasingly on **sustained, source-specific and science-based interventions**.
- Strengthened air-quality monitoring will improve identification of pollution sources and emerging hotspots.
- Greater use of **real-time data, forecasting and scientific modelling** can support timely and targeted interventions.
- States, cities and district administrations will remain central to translating national programmes into measurable ground-level improvements.
- **Cleaner mobility, improved public transport and greater adoption of clean fuels** can reduce transport emissions.
- Stronger **dust management, waste processing and industrial emission controls** will address major urban pollution sources.
- **Urban greening and ecosystem restoration** can provide environmental benefits and improve resilience.
- Innovation can enable cleaner technologies and more efficient pollution-control systems.
- **Public awareness and citizen participation** remain essential for sustained behavioural change.
- **Collaborative governance and measurable outcomes** can accelerate progress towards **cleaner air and healthier communities**.
