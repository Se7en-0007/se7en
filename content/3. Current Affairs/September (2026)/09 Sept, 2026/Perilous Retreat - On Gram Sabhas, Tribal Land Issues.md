### Gram Sabhas Must Have A Veto

- **Tribal organisations** should view the Indian state’s attempt to lower legal barriers created by the **Forest Rights Act (FRA)** as a **threat to their survival**.
- The **Committee on Public Undertakings** has suggested that a project need obtain consent from **only most, rather than all, affected gram sabhas**, although the FRA intends the latter **in spirit**.
- This could mark a **significant jurisprudential change**.
- Treating the acquiescence of **70%-75% of affected gram sabhas** as sufficient would reduce gram sabhas from **democratic bodies protecting the constitutional rights of their specific inhabitants** to **corporate stakeholders**.

### Bypassing Directly Affected Villages

- A project developer could bypass consent from a village located directly within a proposed **reservoir**, while securing **no-objection certificates** from less affected villages.
- The issue is connected with the political contest over classifying tribal communities: by changing the **demographic composition** of those qualifying as community members, the state could more easily **split a village assembly** and **engineer consent**.

### Dangerous Regulatory Vacuum

- The **Tribal Affairs Ministry’s** refusal to claim jurisdiction creates a **dangerous regulatory vacuum**.
- This leaves the **Centre and State governments** unchecked to rewrite rules governing the diversion of **forested land for non-forest use**.
- Although the FRA does not contain a clause requiring a **no-objection certificate from every affected gram sabha**, it is untrue that there is currently **no legal requirement** preventing gram sabha decisions from being bypassed.

### Historical Injustices And Energy Infrastructure

- The government is rapidly expanding **energy infrastructure**, including the long-stalled **Teesta-IV project**, to meet **macroeconomic goals**.
- At the same time, it had enacted the FRA to undo **“historical injustices”** inflicted on **forest-dependent communities**.
- Pursuing the position that consent was never part of the FRA is described as **plainly ignoble**.

### Open And Democratic Change

- If the **Environment Ministry** considers altering the consent requirement, it must amend the relevant **rules or legislation openly and democratically**.
- The **Tribal Affairs Ministry’s** use of a technicality involving the FRA and the Environment Ministry’s rules to recuse itself is scarcely better.
- Such bureaucratic manoeuvring could deprive forest-dependent communities of their **most potent legal instrument**.

### Land, Livelihoods And Political Autonomy

- **Land** is the foundation of forest-dependent communities’ **livelihoods, culture, identity, and political autonomy**.
- These communities have resisted **displacement and dispossession** and sought to secure **customary collective ownership**.
- In recognition of the **social and environment services** that **ST communities** continue to provide, and their contribution to national development, including helping India meet its **climate commitments**, the Centre and States must strengthen gram sabha powers under the **Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act** and the **Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act** in **Scheduled Areas**.
