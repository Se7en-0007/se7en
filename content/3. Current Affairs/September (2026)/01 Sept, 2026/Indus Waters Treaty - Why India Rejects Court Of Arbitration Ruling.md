- The **Permanent Court of Arbitration at The Hague** ruled that the **Indus Waters Treaty (IWT)** between **India and Pakistan remains fully in force** and asked India to continue to **“observe its obligations under the Treaty”**.
- The Court interpreted India’s decision to hold the Treaty in **“abeyance”** as an attempt to **“suspend or terminate”** it.
- It ruled that the Treaty does not provide for either party to **terminate**, **suspend**, or place it in **“abeyance”**.
- The Court also directed India to **temporarily restrict certain construction work** on the **Ratle hydro-electric plant** in Jammu and Kashmir.
- India **does not recognise the Court of Arbitration**, which was formalised in **2023** at Pakistan’s request. India has never participated in its proceedings and regards its pronouncements as **“null and void”**.

## **What Has The Arbitration Court Ruled On Indus Waters Treaty ?**

- On **August 31**, the Court issued **two decisions** in proceedings initiated by Pakistan.
- The first decision concerned the status of the **Indus Waters Treaty**.
- On **April 23, 2025**, following the **Pahalgam terror attack**, India announced that the Treaty would be held in **abeyance** until Pakistan **“credibly and irrevocably”** ended its support for **cross-border terrorism**.
- The Court examined whether this action could amount to the Treaty’s suspension or termination and concluded that it could not.

### **Treaty Provisions**

- The Treaty does not allow either country to **unilaterally suspend or terminate** it.
- Under the Treaty, it remains in force unless **India and Pakistan jointly modify or terminate it through a further treaty**.
- The Court considered grounds publicly referred to by India:
    
    - **Sovereignty**
    - Pakistan’s alleged **material breach** of the Treaty
    - **Terrorism**
    - **Demographic and technological changes**
    - **Climate change**
    - **Armed conflict**
- It found that none of these grounds justified the Treaty’s **suspension or termination**.
- The Court concluded that the Treaty has **neither been terminated nor suspended** and remains **fully in force**.
- India therefore remains bound by its obligations relating to:
    
    - The **design and operation of hydro-electric projects** on the **Western Rivers**
    - The Treaty’s **dispute-settlement mechanisms**

## **Why Is The Ratle Dam Part Of This ?**

- The second decision concerns the **Ratle Hydro-Electric Plant**, a **run-of-the-river** project on the **Chenab**.
- A **run-of-the-river** project follows the **natural flow of river water**.
- Pakistan has challenged certain design features of:
    
    - The **Ratle Hydro-Electric Plant**
    - The **Kishenganga Hydro-Electric Plant**
- Pakistan argues that these features do not comply with the Treaty.

### **Neutral Expert Process**

- These technical issues were also referred to a **Neutral Expert** appointed by the **World Bank** at India’s request.
- The Neutral Expert process was separate from the **Court of Arbitration proceedings**.
- After India placed the Treaty in **abeyance** last year, the **Neutral Expert process was also discontinued**.
- Pakistan sought **interim measures** from the Court.

### **Court’s Directions On Ratle**

- The Court ordered India not to **concrete the Ratle dam wall and power intake structure above specified levels**.
- India was also directed to report any changes in the **Ratle construction schedule**.

## **Why Does India Reject The Ruling ?**

- India has consistently argued that the **Court of Arbitration is illegally constituted**.
- India says its proceedings run parallel to the Treaty-consistent **Neutral Expert process**.
- In **July 2023**, the government stated that the Treaty did not allow **parallel proceedings on the same issues** and maintained that the **Neutral Expert process** was the appropriate mechanism.
- In **May 2026**, after the Court issued an award on **maximum pondage** - the maximum volume of water a reservoir can hold - India reiterated that:
    
    - It had **never recognised the Court**
    - Its proceedings, awards, and decisions were **“null and void”**
    - Its decision to keep the Treaty in **abeyance remained in force**

## **So What Happens Now ?**

- India’s decision to **scrap the Treaty** has rattled Pakistan, which depends on India’s adherence to the Treaty and on **regular information-sharing** for its water security.
- Pakistan has repeatedly raised the issue on international fora, from the **UN to other bodies**, seeking to portray India as the aggressor taking unfair advantage of its **upper riparian position**.
- Pakistani authorities have also been **sabre-rattling** about the **“national security”** dimensions of the Indus Waters Treaty being violated.
- India has held firm, insisting that **“blood and water cannot flow together.”**
- New Delhi has stated that it repeatedly sought renegotiation of the Treaty even before **Pahalgam**, because ground realities have changed substantially since the Treaty was negotiated in the **1950s**.
- According to India, Pakistan did not respond to those demands.
