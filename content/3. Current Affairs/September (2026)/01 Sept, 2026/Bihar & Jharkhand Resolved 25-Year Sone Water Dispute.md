- The **25-year-old dispute** between **Bihar and Jharkhand** over sharing **Sone River water** was resolved through an **inter-state Memorandum of Understanding (MoU)** signed in **Delhi**.
- Under the MoU, **Bihar will receive 5.75 million acre-feet (MAF)** and **Jharkhand 2 MAF** of water.
- The agreement is expected to enable delayed **irrigation, reservoir, and river-linking projects** in the **drought-prone regions** of both states.

## What Is The History Of The Sone River Dispute?

- The dispute began in **November 2000**, when **Jharkhand was carved out of Bihar**.
- Jharkhand inherited the **major upper catchment areas and tributaries** of the **Sone River**, while the **lower riparian agricultural hubs** remained in Bihar.
- Its core lay in the **1973 Bansagar Agreement**, a **tripartite agreement** involving **Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and undivided Bihar**.
- Under this agreement, undivided Bihar was allocated **7.75 MAF** of Sone water from the **total basin yield**.
- After the division of Bihar, there was **no binding formula** to divide this **7.75 MAF allocation** between Bihar and newly formed Jharkhand.
- Over the following two decades, several **inter-state committees** attempted mediation, but negotiations repeatedly stalled over:
    
    - **Reservoir height**
    - **Land submergence**
    - **Exact volumetric splits**
- Discussions between senior officers of both states rarely reached the **Chief Minister level**.
- Different political alliances in Bihar and Jharkhand delayed the pact until the current leadership broke the long-standing stalemate.

## How Did The Two States Eventually Arrive At A Consensus?

- Bihar’s allocation under the pact is **5.75 MAF**, while Jharkhand’s allocation is **2 MAF** of Sone water.
- The agreement clears the way for full operation of the **Indrapuri Barrage**, also called the **Indrapuri Dam** or **Sone Barrage**.
- The barrage is located across the Sone River in **Rohtas district**, near **Dehri-on-Sone**, around **5 km upstream**.
- It lies near the border connecting Jharkhand’s northern districts of **Garhwa and Palamu**.
- Built in the **late 1960s**, the barrage is the primary diversion structure for the extensive **Sone Canal network**.
- The canal network irrigates the **Shahabad agricultural belt**, comprising **Rohtas, Bhojpur, Buxar, and Kaimur districts**.
- Bihar will receive additional water from the barrage to irrigate local farmland.
- Jharkhand agreed to specified **reservoir water levels** to minimise **land submergence**, with clear **rehabilitation provisions**.
- The pact guarantees critical irrigation supplies to **South Bihar**, including **Rohtas, Bhojpur, and Aurangabad**.
- It also enables new canal networks in drought-prone **Palamu and Garhwa** districts of Jharkhand.
- **Samrat Choudhary** and **Hemant Soren** played key roles in finalising the pact, with the **Ministry of Jal Shakti** presiding over the process.

## Did The Dispute Create Political Tensions Between The Two States?

- There were **no major state-level confrontations** between the Chief Ministers of Bihar and Jharkhand.
- However, the dispute frequently became a political issue during election seasons, especially in Bihar’s **Shahabad region** and Jharkhand’s **Palamu region**.
- Leaders across party lines in Bihar’s **Shahabad and Magadh belts** - including **Rohtas, Bhojpur, and Aurangabad** - routinely criticised the state government over water shortages in the **Sone canal system** during the **summer sowing season**.
- Leaders from Jharkhand’s **Palamu and Garhwa** regions raised concerns over displacement.
- They feared that raising the height of the **Indrapuri Dam** would submerge local agricultural land and displace thousands without fair compensation.
- Different political coalitions in **Patna, Ranchi, and New Delhi** over the past 25 years created friction between the Centre and the states.
- This stalled inter-state coordination and led governments to blame one another for delayed agricultural development.

## How Did The Central Water Commission Help Enable The Pact?

- The **Central Water Commission (CWC)** provided the technical frameworks, **hydrology studies**, and modified **dam-height specifications** acceptable to both states.
- Its intervention motivated both governments to prioritise a resolution for long-term irrigation guarantees in their agrarian regions.
- The CWC had made several previous attempts to resolve the dispute, but without success.
- Under the current pact, Jharkhand’s water-security interests in drought-prone **Garhwa and Palamu** are addressed.
- The negotiated reservoir water levels are intended to protect local communities from **land submergence**.
