- **Semicon 2.0** outlines a **three-part push** to:
    
    - Build **domestic chip-design companies**
    - Support **strategic semiconductor technologies** and **commercially viable chips**
    - Deploy these products in the market
- **Semiconductor design and production** are critical for technology products such as **laptops, phones and AI models**.
- India is a major global base for **chip-design engineers**, but much of their work is presently undertaken for **multinational semiconductor companies**.

## What Is The Government Offering For Chip Design?

- Semicon 2.0 seeks to create more **Indian-owned intellectual property** and **fabless semiconductor companies**.
- **Fabless companies** design and sell chips while outsourcing their **physical fabrication**.
- The operational framework, notified on **August 31**, puts chip design at the front of the new semiconductor mission.
- Of the programme’s **six pillars** and **10 categories**, the **first pillar** is wholly focused on design.
- It has separate tracks for:
    
    - Chips needed for **national and strategic priorities**
    - **Commercial semiconductor products**
    - A **deployment-linked incentive** for newly developed chips

## The First Track

- The first track targets semiconductor technologies important for **national security** and **critical infrastructure**.
- The government will identify **IP, chips, systems-on-chip (SoCs) and modules** for development.
- These include building blocks for:
    
    - **Compute**
    - **Memory**
    - **Radio frequency**
    - **Power**
    - **Networking**
    - **Sensors**
- Participating companies must:
    
    - Be **incorporated and headquartered in India**
    - Have a **significant operational and manpower presence** in India
    - Be **owned and controlled by Indian citizens**
- Such companies may work with **global companies, research organisations and academic institutions** in consortiums.
- The **Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC)** under the **IT Ministry** will invite bids and provide:
    
    - **Financial support**
    - Access to **chip-design infrastructure**
- Resulting IP will be **jointly owned by C-DAC and the selected company**.
- **C-DAC will not commercially exploit the IP** unless the selected company fails to meet its contractual obligations.

## The Second Track

- The second track is meant for **commercial chip-design companies**.
- The government will provide access to:
    
    - Expensive **electronic design automation tools**
    - **Multi-project wafer fabrication services**
    - Other systems
- **Start-ups and MSMEs** can receive milestone-linked seed funding of up to **Rs 15 crore** or **50% of the project cost**, whichever is lower.
- The government can make **equity co-investments** alongside **venture capital** and **private equity investors**.
- Larger eligible companies can choose **royalty financing**:
    
    - They will pay **5% of net revenue** from the supported product.
    - Payments continue until **1.5 times the government assistance** has been recovered.
- Eligibility for this commercial category has been widened to companies **owned and controlled by Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs)**, provided they:
    
    - Are **incorporated and headquartered in India**
    - Maintain a **significant operational and manpower presence** in India
- The **IP** and associated **design and development files** must remain in India.
- The broader Semicon 2.0 programme could catalyse more than **Rs 5 lakh crore** in cumulative private and industry investment over the next **five to seven years** across:
    
    - **Fabs**
    - **ATMP/OSAT assembly and testing**
    - **Equipment and materials**
    - **Design**
    - **R&D**
    - The wider **supply chain**
- Opening the scheme to OCI-owned companies is expected to bring **rich global experience** that can help create advanced design companies in India.

## How Will The Scheme Help Indian Chips Find Buyers?

- Semicon 2.0 introduces a separate **deployment-linked incentive** to address adoption at scale after a chip has been designed.
- Eligible **semiconductor IP, chips and SoCs** launched after the scheme’s announcement will receive reimbursement equal to **9% of net sales** for **five years**.
- The incentive is capped at:
    
    - **Rs 30 crore per application**
    - **Rs 120 crore for a company**, including its group entities, across multiple products
- This is particularly relevant for young **fabless companies**, which may struggle to persuade electronics manufacturers to switch from established overseas semiconductor suppliers to a newly developed Indian chip.

## And What Is The Broader Ecosystem Focus?

- The scheme also seeks to fill gaps across the **semiconductor supply chain**.
- Companies establishing the following can receive **30% capital expenditure support**:
    
    - Semiconductor-equipment **R&D facilities**
    - Plants manufacturing **wafers, photomasks, photoresists, substrates, chemicals and gases**
    - **Testing facilities**
    - Units producing **semiconductor equipment and components**
- Equipment manufacturers will additionally receive a **production-linked incentive** that declines from **10% to 2% over five years**, based on the value of their **bill of materials sourced domestically**.
- **Large silicon wafer fabs** will receive **40% capex support**.
- **Compound semiconductor, photonics, sensor and discrete semiconductor fabs** can receive **35% capex support**.
- **Advanced chip-packaging facilities** will receive **35% support**, compared with **25%** for **legacy packaging**.
- These incentives are lower than the uniform **50% capex subsidy** offered under the first iteration of the scheme, introduced in **2021** to build a **full-stack chip ecosystem**.
- The first semiconductor mission has approved **12 manufacturing projects**, including:
    
    - **Micron’s assembly and testing facility**
    - **Tata Electronics’ wafer fab**, being developed with Taiwan’s **PSMC**
- **Micron, Kaynes Semicon and CG Semi** have started commercial production.
- The **Tata fab** remains under construction.

## What About Talent?

- **Talent development** has been made a separate pillar under Semicon 2.0.
- The government intends to train:
    
    - **Chip designers**
    - **Engineers**
    - **Researchers**
    - **Technicians**
    - **Shop-floor operators** needed across fabrication and packaging plants
- India achieved its target of developing **85,000 semiconductor engineers over 10 years** in only **four years**.
- It has set a new target of developing **one lakh more engineers**.
- Students from **tier-II and tier-III cities** have designed more than **250 chips**.
- India’s developing semiconductor ecosystem is expected to create **50,000-60,000 direct jobs**.
- On the design side, support will include:
    
    - Access to advanced **chip-design tools**
    - **Multi-project wafer services**
    - **Post-silicon validation facilities**
- On the manufacturing side, the scheme will:
    
    - Support dedicated **training infrastructure**
    - Strengthen existing **nanofabrication and process laboratories**
- Eligible talent-development projects can receive support of up to **75% of their cost**, including **state incentives**.
