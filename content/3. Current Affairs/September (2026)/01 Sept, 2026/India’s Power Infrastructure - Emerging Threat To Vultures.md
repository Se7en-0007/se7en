- For decades, the chief concern about **vulture safety** was **chemical threats**, but **electrocution posed by India’s expanding power infrastructure** may supersede it.
- In the **1990s**, India’s vultures suffered one of the world’s worst population crashes:
    
    - Their population declined by **99.5% by 2007**, from around **four crore in the 1980s**.
    - **White-rumped, Indian, and slender-billed vultures** were nearly wiped out.
    - The culprit was **diclofenac**, a cattle painkiller that **wrecks vulture kidneys**.
- The government banned **diclofenac**, followed by additional bans in **2023** to mitigate threats from **aceclofenac**, **ketoprofen**, and some other **NSAIDs**.
- The decline of vultures left **carcasses exposed for longer**, supporting **feral dog populations** and leading to **rabies outbreaks**.
    
    - A **2024 study** estimated that human mortality consequently rose by **4%**.
    - The associated damages cost India **$69.4 billion a year**.
- Although **electrocution** has not become as deadly as **NSAIDs**, the fragility of vulture populations and the proliferation of unsafe power infrastructure warrant **early action**.
- A **2025 official survey** reported that vultures were nesting at only **50% of their historic nesting sites**.

## **Threat of Electrocution**

- Vultures’ **wide wingspans** allow a large individual to contact **two conductors at once**.
- Raptors also:
    
    - Perch on **elevated structures**.
    - Are attracted to **open landscapes**.
    - Congregate where food availability is predictable.
- The risk is enhanced when people dump **food waste around electrical installations**.

## **Livestock Carcass Dump**

- A recent study found that relocating a **livestock carcass dump** to **2.4 km away** from **high-tension power infrastructure** near **Dehradun, Uttarakhand**, may have saved large scavengers from **electrocution**.
- An assessment prepared for the **State Climate Resilient Power System Development Project** recorded the electrocution of:
    
    - An **Egyptian vulture**.
    - Some **steppe eagles**.
- These birds were electrocuted on **medium-voltage**, rather than **high-voltage**, lines.
- Persistent mortality due to **electrocution alone** could render a **local population extinct**.
- **Avian electrocution** is likely under-documented in India because dead birds may be removed by humans or scavenged.

## **Measures To Protect Raptors**

- **Separate vulture feeding sites from power infrastructure**.
- Other protective measures, particularly near settlements:
    
    - **Insulate conductors**.
    - Increase the gap between **energised and grounded components**.
    - Otherwise prevent a bird from bridging these components, a non-trivial task given **vulture wingspans**.
    - Install **safe perches**.
