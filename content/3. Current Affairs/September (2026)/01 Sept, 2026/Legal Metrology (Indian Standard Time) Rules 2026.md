- On **August 27**, the **Union Consumer Affairs Ministry** notified the **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules 2026**.
- The rules designate **Indian Standard Time (IST)** as the **sole reference** for **administrative, legal, commercial and other official purposes** across the country.
- The rules will come into force **180 days after publication in the Official Gazette**.
- During this period, systems in **government departments, businesses and other institutions** must be aligned with the rules.
- The rules require **time-dependent systems** in **government and critical sectors** to use a **traceable, authorised IST reference**.
- They are part of India’s effort towards **“One Nation One Time”**, announced in **January 2025**.

## First, How Did We Arrive At IST?

- The **CSIR National Physical Laboratory** is the custodian of India’s **official time reference**.
- It maintains **UTC(NPLI)**, India’s realisation of **Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)**.
- **Indian Standard Time** is **UTC(NPLI) plus five hours and 30 minutes**.

## White Rabbit Technology-Based IST Dissemination Demonstration Network

- After a successful test in which IST was disseminated between the **Regional Reference Standard Laboratory (RRSL), Bengaluru**, and the **NSE, Chennai**, the Consumer Affairs Ministry commissioned a **White Rabbit Technology-based IST Dissemination Demonstration Network** at the **RRSL in Bengaluru** in **July 2026**.
- The network was developed jointly by the **Department of Consumer Affairs**, **CSIR-NPL** and **ISRO**.

## How Does White Rabbit Keep Computers On The Same Time ?

- **White Rabbit** is an **open-source networking technology** designed to synchronise computer clocks through **fibre-optic networks**.
- It sends a **timing signal** through a fibre-optic cable and measures the time taken by the signal to reach another computer.
- As travel time can vary slightly, White Rabbit **measures the delay** and adjusts for it, enabling clocks at different locations to remain synchronised.
- It does not merely send a time signal and assume that it arrived at the expected time - it **measures the delay and corrects for it**.
- It was initially developed by the **European Organization for Nuclear Research (CERN)** for scientific experiments requiring **thousands of computers and other devices** to maintain time with one another.
- Ordinary internet networks can synchronise clocks, but data travel time can vary as data moves through a network. **White Rabbit** is designed to account for these delays, allowing **more precise synchronisation**.
- Fibre-optic networks can distribute time without requiring a **satellite signal at every location**.
- Satellite signals can be vulnerable to **jamming** or **spoofing**, where a false signal is made to appear genuine.
- A physical fibre link does not face these particular wireless vulnerabilities, although it can still be disrupted and the wider timing network needs **safeguards and backup sources**.
- White Rabbit carries the **official time** from the **national reference** to other systems.
- The **CSIR-NPL** maintains the reference time; organisations connect their computer systems to **approved time servers or networks** that distribute it.
- Machines regularly check against this source and adjust their clocks when needed.

## Why Is Precise Synchronisation Necessary ?

- Systems across multiple organisations, including **banks, telecommunications, power grids and defence systems**, need to coordinate with one another.
- A **unified time source** helps establish the **sequence of events**, coordinate activities across locations, and reconstruct **failures, transactions or cyber incidents**.
- According to the **Telecommunication Engineering Centre**, inconsistent clocks across networks can make **server logs difficult to correlate**.
- Differing server clocks can make it difficult for investigators to determine which subscriber was using an **IP address at a particular time**.
- Financial markets already operate under precise clock-synchronisation requirements.
- Since **2013**, **SEBI** has directed stock exchanges to synchronise their system clocks with **atomic clocks** before the market opens.
- Stock-exchange clocks must have a precision of at least **one microsecond** and an accuracy of at least **+/- one millisecond**.
- Financial systems can generate and process orders in **fractions of a second**.
- Accurate timestamps establish the sequence in which orders and trades occurred, and can be important when regulators investigate **unusual activity or disputes**.

## Why Is India Now Pushing For Domestic Timing Infrastructure ?

- The push for IST uniformity accompanies India’s effort to reduce reliance on **external satellite navigation systems** and their timing signals.
- **NavIC**, India’s satellite navigation system developed by **ISRO**, provides an indigenous source of **positioning, navigation and timing signals**.
- **Terrestrial systems** such as White Rabbit can distribute IST through **fibre-optic networks**.
- Together, NavIC and terrestrial systems provide India with **more than one domestic route** to supply precise time to critical infrastructure.
- The aim is to build a **more resilient system** with multiple means of keeping critical infrastructure synchronised.
- If one timing source is disrupted, for example through **jamming or spoofing**, critical systems can use another route to maintain a **reliable time reference**.
