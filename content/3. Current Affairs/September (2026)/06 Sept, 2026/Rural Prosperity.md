## Vibrant Villages Programme

### Strengthening India's Frontiers Through Inclusive Development

- **Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP)** has redefined India’s border villages from **remote outposts** to **strategic partners** in **national security and development**.
- Implemented through **VVP-I** and **VVP-II**, with a combined outlay of **Rs. 11,639 crore**.
- Expands **road connectivity, electricity, telecommunications, healthcare, education and livelihood opportunities** in villages along India’s international land borders.
- Seeks to build **vibrant, resilient and prosperous border communities**, improve quality of life, create sustainable economic opportunities and strengthen border security by sustaining thriving frontier communities.

## Why The Border Village Matters

- India shares over **15,000 km** of international land border with **seven neighbouring countries**.
- Border lengths:
    
    - **Bangladesh - 4,096.7 km**, the longest stretch.
    - **China - 3,488 km**.
    - **Pakistan - 3,323 km**.
    - **Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan and Afghanistan** account for the remainder.
- Border villages historically faced **difficult terrain, weak connectivity, limited infrastructure and few livelihood opportunities**, resulting in **out-migration** and sparsely populated frontier areas.
- **VVP** was launched in **2023** to accelerate comprehensive development of selected border villages and recognise border residents as the **eyes and ears** of border guarding forces.
- The programme treats border settlements **not as the nation’s last villages, but as its first**.
- Beginning with northern border villages, the Government expanded the programme in phases across the entire land frontier to build a **secure** and **strong Viksit Bharat@2047**.
- During the **1999 Kargil intrusion**, local residents first reported suspicious movement in the Kargil sector on **3 May 1999**, highlighting border communities as the nation’s **first line of defence**.

## Two Phases, One Frontier Mission

- **VVP-I** covers villages along the **northern border**.
- **VVP-II** extends the approach to the **other international land borders**.
- Together, the phases aim to cover more than **2,616 villages** with a combined outlay of **Rs. 11,639 crore**.
- **VVP-I** is a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme** implemented with States.
- **VVP-II** is a **Central Sector Scheme** funded fully by the Centre.
- Both phases aim to:
    
    - Improve **living conditions**.
    - Generate **local livelihoods**.
    - Empower **border communities**.
    - Develop “**first villages**” as vibrant centres of growth and strengthen border security.

## VVP-I: Strengthening Northern Border Communities

- **VVP-I** was announced in the **Union Budget 2022-23** for villages along the northern border.
- It was launched at **Kibithoo**, **Anjaw district, Arunachal Pradesh**, on **10 April 2023**.
- Villages in **46 blocks** abutting the northern border across **4 States**, **1 Union Territory** and **19 districts** were identified.
- **662 strategic villages** were prioritised for comprehensive development, covering about **1.42 lakh population**.
- Covered States and Union Territory:
    
    - **Arunachal Pradesh**
    - **Himachal Pradesh**
    - **Sikkim**
    - **Uttarakhand**
    - **Ladakh**
- Approved outlay: **Rs. 4,800 crore** for **2022-23 to 2026-27**.
- **Rs. 2,500 crore** was earmarked for **road connectivity**.
- The programme seeks to bridge development gaps and provide border villages with **quality infrastructure and essential services comparable to those available elsewhere in the country**.

### Kibithoo

- **Kibithoo** was selected for the launch because of its **strategic location, history and development needs**.
- Located in **Anjaw district, Arunachal Pradesh**, it is among India’s **easternmost inhabited villages**.
- The area witnessed fierce fighting during the **1962 India-China War**.
- Its development was constrained by **remote terrain, sparse connectivity and out-migration**.
- Launching the programme from Kibithoo reflected the vision of bringing development first to India’s frontier.

### Key Interventions Under VVP-I

- Supports:
    
    - **All-weather roads**
    - **Drinking water**
    - **Reliable electricity**
    - **Mobile and internet connectivity**
    - **Essential healthcare and education infrastructure**
    - **Tourism facilities**
    - Multi-purpose **community centres**
- Livelihood support follows a **hub-and-spoke model**.
- It promotes:
    
    - **Social entrepreneurship**
    - Empowerment of **youth and women**
    - Promotion of **tourism and local culture**
    - **One-block-one-product** enterprises
- VVP-I established a **convergence-based delivery model** that VVP-II carries to the rest of the land border.

### Border Area Development Programme

- **Border Area Development Programme (BADP)** was launched in **1986-87** along the western border.
- It was subsequently expanded to villages within **10 km from the first habitation from the international border**.
- It covered **111 districts** across **16 States and 2 Union Territories**.
- More than **39,000 development works** have been sanctioned since **2004-05**.
- BADP is in its **sunset phase**.
- Building on this foundation, VVP focuses on selected border villages and seeks to ensure their saturation with every applicable government scheme, helping people remain in villages and strengthen border security.

## VVP-II: Extending The Model To All Land Borders

- The Union Cabinet approved **VVP-II** on **2 April 2025**.
- VVP-II is fully funded by the Centre and implemented by the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)**.
- It was formally launched on **20 February 2026** at **Nathanpur village**, **Cachar district, Assam**.
- Outlay: **Rs. 6,839 crore** until financial year **2028-29**.
- It identifies **1,954 villages** for comprehensive development in **334 blocks** abutting international land borders.
- These blocks are in **15 States** and **2 Union Territories**:
    
    - **Arunachal Pradesh**
    - **Assam**
    - **Bihar**
    - **Gujarat**
    - **Manipur**
    - **Meghalaya**
    - **Mizoram**
    - **Nagaland**
    - **Punjab**
    - **Rajasthan**
    - **Sikkim**
    - **Tripura**
    - **Uttarakhand**
    - **Uttar Pradesh**
    - **West Bengal**
    - **Jammu and Kashmir**
    - **Ladakh**
- Northern-border blocks already covered under **VVP-I** are excluded to avoid duplication.

### Interventions Under VVP-II

- VVP-II provides a comprehensive package supporting:
    
    - **Infrastructure**
    - **Value chains** through cooperatives and **self-help groups (SHGs)**
    - **SMART classrooms**
    - **Tourism circuits**
    - **Sustainable livelihoods**
    - **Road connectivity**
    - **Healthcare**
    - **Financial inclusion**
    - **Skill development**
    - **Telecom connectivity**
- Its objective is to improve living conditions, generate livelihoods, encourage people to remain in border villages and extend VVP across India’s entire land border under a unified development framework.
- VVP-II gives special emphasis to preventing and controlling **trans-border crime** through stronger border communities.

## Delivering Development Through Scheme Convergence

- Implementation is guided by **Village Action Plans** based on **border-specific, State-specific and village-specific interventions** suited to local needs.
- The programme follows a **convergence-based approach** to ensure saturation of schemes under existing norms.
- All-weather roads are developed under **Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY)-IV** of the Ministry of Rural Development.
- A **High-Powered Committee**, chaired by the **Cabinet Secretary**, may recommend relaxations in scheme guidelines to address the unique challenges of border villages.
- The **Ministry of Home Affairs** is the nodal ministry and works with **State Governments, district administrations and border guarding forces** for coordinated planning and implementation.

## VVP: Impact Beyond Infrastructure

### Security

- A populated and confident border belt supports border guarding forces.
- Both phases aim to build a strong frontier population as a **strategic asset** for the nation.

### Demography & Reverse Migration

- Livelihood opportunities and improved connectivity provide families reasons to remain in border villages.
- In **Arunachal Pradesh**, people from border villages in **Kurung Kumey, Dibang Valley and Shi-Yomi districts** are returning to their villages.

### Economy

- **Border tourism, cooperatives, SHGs and Farmer Producer Organisations** are creating local income bases in areas where economic opportunities were previously limited.
- Local procurement by border guarding forces creates an assured market within the village.
- In Arunachal Pradesh, the **Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP)** procures **milk, vegetables, eggs and grains** directly from vibrant villages, making a border post a dependable customer for nearby farms.

### Governance

- The saturation approach brings every eligible household in border villages within the reach of existing Central and State schemes.
- **Village Action Plans** provide district administrations a single instrument to sequence work across multiple departments.
- **Development and security** are not competing claims on the frontier but reinforce one another.

## Towards A Connected And Self-Reliant Frontier

- VVP seeks to ensure that India’s frontier communities fully share in national progress.
- **Better infrastructure, sustainable livelihoods and convergence of government schemes** are creating opportunities closer to home.
- **Border tourism and local enterprise** are unlocking the region’s potential.
- More vibrant, prosperous and self-reliant border villages will strengthen India’s borders and advance the vision of a **secure, inclusive and Viksit Bharat by 2047**.
