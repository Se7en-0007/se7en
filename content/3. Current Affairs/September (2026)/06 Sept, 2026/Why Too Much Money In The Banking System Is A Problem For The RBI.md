- **Banking-system liquidity climbed to a four-year high of about Rs 10.3 lakh crore on September 3**, the **highest level since May 2022**.
- **Foreign-currency inflows through the recent swap facility** and liquidity released through the RBI’s **foreign-exchange operations** have created a large pool of rupee funds with relatively limited avenues for deployment.
- While **core liquidity crossed Rs 10 lakh crore**, the **daily average surplus was Rs 3.67 lakh crore in August**, more than **three times July’s Rs 1.07 lakh crore**.

## Pressure On Overnight Money-Market Rates

- Huge banking-system liquidity generally puts **downward pressure on overnight money-market rates**, potentially pushing them **below the repo rate** unless the RBI absorbs the excess.
- **Persistently loose liquidity conditions** can add to **inflationary pressures**.
- The RBI must drain excess liquidity without causing a **sharp rise in interest rates** or unsettling the **government securities market**.

## Inflation And Policy Outlook

- The situation arises while global central banks are mostly keeping rates high or tightening cautiously to curb inflation.
- **Headline inflation is projected to peak at as high as 5.9% in Q3 2026-27**.
- RBI Monetary Policy Committee members have indicated that a **“case for a hike may emerge”** during the year.

## Potential Rise In Core Liquidity

- Even after accounting for:
    
    - The festive-season increase in **currency in circulation**.
    - Maturity of the RBI’s short dollar positions in forward markets.
- **Core liquidity could rise from Rs 8.1 lakh crore in mid-August to about Rs 13-14 lakh crore by December-end**, if the RBI undertakes no liquidity-management operations.

## Forex Swap Facility And Rupee Liquidity

- The RBI’s special **US dollar-rupee forex swap facility** drew foreign-exchange inflows of **$136.377 billion through August 31**.
- **FCNR(B) deposits** formed the overwhelming share, at **$127.226 billion**.
- Conversion of these dollars released rupees into the banking system.
- Since these deposits were exempt from **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** and **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** requirements, the liquidity entered the system **unencumbered**.
- These inflows strengthen the **capital account** and the overall **balance of payments**, but are also likely to add significantly to domestic liquidity.

## Factors That Could Offset Liquidity

- **Currency in circulation (CiC)** could rise by around **Rs 1.1 lakh crore from June levels by December**, due to seasonal festive-period demand.
- Maturity of the RBI’s short positions in the forward market could create an additional liquidity drag of around **Rs 3 lakh crore**.
- About **$22 billion** of the short-forward book is set to mature within **three months**.
- **CRR accretion arising from deposit growth** could further reduce core liquidity by **Rs 70,000 crore**.

## RBI Tools To Manage Excess Money

- The RBI cannot leave liquidity excessively loose if it:
    
    - Adds to **inflationary pressures**.
    - Pushes market rates too far below the **policy rate**.
- Near-term measures may include a **temporary CRR hike** or an **Incremental CRR (I-CRR)**, similar to the measure introduced in **2023**.
- Such a move would have an immediate impact, but could be viewed as unwinding the RBI’s earlier decision to exclude these deposits from **CRR and SLR requirements**.

## Liquidity Management Tools

- The RBI may initially use a combination of:
    
    - **Longer-tenor Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) operations**.
    - **Open Market Operation (OMO) sales**.
- If surplus liquidity remains persistent, these may be followed by stronger liquidity-absorption tools such as the **I-CRR**.

## Variable Rate Reverse Repo Auctions

- The RBI has already been conducting back-to-back **VRRR auctions**, including:
    
    - A **7-day Rs 6 lakh crore** operation.
    - An **overnight Rs 4 lakh crore** operation on the same day.
    - **Rs 5 lakh crore** and **Rs 6 lakh crore** overnight operations on successive days.

## Cash Reserve Ratio

- Raising the **CRR** is considered the cleanest method to extract **durable liquidity**.
- The CRR is currently at **3.00% of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)**, after last year’s **100 basis points cut**.
- The RBI has room to increase it.
- Absorbing liquidity equivalent to **2% of NDTL through CRR** would drain a very large and durable amount from the system.

## Open Market Operations

- **OMO sales** are a calibrated middle path: **reversible in principle, market-priced, and stance-neutral if communicated well**.
- The RBI had injected **Rs 2 lakh crore** of durable liquidity through **OMO purchases only last winter** and can now conduct the operation in reverse.

## Other Measures

- Gradual sale of dollars by the central bank in the **spot market**, along with allowing **forward-book shorts** to mature, could help reduce excess liquidity by **late FY27**.
- If the RBI tightens policy in **H2FY27**, overall liquidity will also need to be tightened to steer **call rates towards the repo rate**.
