- **India will host the 18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi on September 12-13, 2026**, as the grouping completes **20 years since its formalisation in 2006**.
- In **2000**, **Brazil, Russia, India and China** accounted for roughly **23% of global GDP on a purchasing power parity - PPP basis**, while the **G7 held nearly 52%**.
- By **2024**, the expanded **11-member BRICS** accounted for approximately **36.8% of global GDP - PPP**, while the **G7’s share fell below 29%**.
- BRICS represents roughly **a quarter of global trade**. Its **intra-BRICS merchandise trade** reached **$1.17 trillion in 2024**, a nearly **13-fold increase** from **$84 billion** over two decades.
- BRICS holds an estimated **$5.2 trillion in combined foreign reserves** and accounts for **78.2% of global mineral coal production**.
- BRICS nations are projected to grow by an average of **3.8% in 2025** and **3.7% in 2026**, more than **three times the G7 average**.
- The **G7’s per-capita GDP** averages around **$53,000**, compared with roughly **$8,200 for BRICS**. BRICS’ aggregate weight is driven by **population and resource scale**, not **individual prosperity**.

## From Vision To Reality

- BRICS began as **BRIC - Brazil, Russia, India and China - in 2006**, following a **Goldman Sachs projection** that identified these economies as future engines of global growth.
- It evolved from a **foreign ministers’ dialogue** into a **leaders’ forum**, with its first summit held in **Yekaterinburg, Russia, in 2009**.
- **South Africa joined in 2010**.
- BRICS expanded in **2024** with the addition of **Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the UAE**.
- **Indonesia became the 11th full member in January 2025**.
- What began as an economic grouping has become a geopolitical entity of considerable weight and increasingly presents itself as a voice of the **Global South**.
- Its central grievance remains that **global institutions have not kept pace with the redistribution of economic and political power**.
- The first summit called for:
    
    - **Reform of international financial institutions and the United Nations**
    - **Greater energy security**
    - A more democratic **multipolar world order**
- These demands remain strikingly relevant **two decades later**.

## India’s Strategic Engagement

- India’s involvement in BRICS reflects its strategy of **multi-alignment**, underpinned by **strategic autonomy**.
- India participates simultaneously in **BRICS, the G20, the Quad and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization**, without being tied to alliance commitments.
- BRICS emerged during an early phase of **India-US strategic convergence**. India’s multi-alignment is less about choosing between competing power centres and more about creating **room for manoeuvre**.
- At the **2012 New Delhi summit**, India proposed a **BRICS development bank**, which became the **New Development Bank - NDB**.
- By **mid-2026**, the NDB had approved approximately **$44 billion across 141 projects**.
- India seeks to shape debates on **climate change, sustainable development and trade**, thereby enhancing its **agenda-setting capacity**.

## Divergences Within BRICS: Anti-West Bloc Or Economic Forum?

- A major tension in BRICS is the fundamental disagreement over its purpose.
- **China and Russia** increasingly treat BRICS as a **counterweight to Western dominance**, a platform to challenge **US-led institutions** and the **dollar’s hegemony**.
- **Beijing** has driven BRICS expansion, viewing a larger grouping as a vehicle to project leadership of the **Global South** and limit Western influence.
- **Moscow**, excluded from **SWIFT** and facing sweeping sanctions, sees BRICS as a critical **economic lifeline**.
- **India and Brazil** view BRICS primarily as an **economic and reform-oriented grouping**.
- India does not share the **revisionist enthusiasm for confronting the West**.
- In **2023**, Russia demanded that India pay for oil in **yuan**. India refused, insisting on **dollars or rupees only**.
- In **February 2026**, India signed a **framework trade agreement with the United States**, reportedly agreeing to halt purchases of **Russian oil** in exchange for reduced tariffs. The move underscored India’s willingness to prioritise **national interest over bloc solidarity**.

## Iran And The UAE: Members On Opposing Sides

- BRICS expansion has created sharp geopolitical contradictions that test the grouping’s cohesion.
- At the **BRICS Foreign Ministers’ meeting in New Delhi in May 2026**, members failed to agree on a **joint declaration** - the first such failure in BRICS history.
- The breakdown resulted from a direct confrontation between **Iran and the UAE**, two member states on opposite sides of an active conflict.
- India, as host, issued a **chair’s statement** acknowledging **“differing views among some members regarding the situation in West Asia”**.
- The episode revealed a structural vulnerability: BRICS operates through **consensus**, and expansion has made consensus on geopolitical crises much more difficult.

## The Dollar Question

- Western countries view BRICS with both concern and pragmatism - not as an immediate replacement for the existing international order, but as a platform capable of gradually reshaping the distribution of geopolitical power.
- US President **Donald Trump** threatened **100% tariffs** on BRICS nations if they created a new currency or backed another currency to replace the **US dollar**.
- These threats had a sobering effect, and the idea of a **single BRICS currency** has largely been shelved.
- BRICS members have instead moved towards **bilateral settlement of trade in national currencies**, a more pragmatic and decentralised approach.
- **Russia and China** now settle over **90% of their bilateral trade** in **ruble and yuan**.
- **Russia and India** have moved roughly **90% of direct payments** to national currencies, facilitated by **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts** authorised by the **Reserve Bank of India**.
- **India and the UAE** have operated a **rupee-dirham local currency settlement system** since **July 2023**.
- **India and Indonesia** operationalised a **rupee-rupiah settlement framework in July 2026**. Local-currency trade surged **163% year-on-year** to **$8.45 billion in the first two months of 2026**.
- The **BRICS Pay system**, to be unveiled at the **September 2026 summit in New Delhi**, will link national payment rails:
    
    - **Russia’s SPFS**
    - **China’s CIPS**
    - **India’s UPI**
    - **Brazil’s Pix**
- BRICS Pay will enable member states to settle trade without routing transactions through **dollar correspondent banks**.
- This represents **de-dollarisation through the back-door**.
- India has pushed for **interoperable central bank digital currencies - CBDCs**, rather than a supranational currency, reflecting a digital payment philosophy shaped by the success of **UPI**.
- Limitations remain:
    
    - The dollar accounted for **57.13% of global central bank reserves in Q1 2026**
    - No BRICS member is building **rupee, yuan or rand reserves at meaningful scale**

## Pakistan’s Membership Aspirations

- Pakistan formally applied for **BRICS membership in November 2023**.
- Its membership remains contentious because of:
    
    - Recurring **balance-of-payments crises** and regular dependence on **IMF assistance**
    - Its record on **terrorism**
    - **Political instability** and weak **policy continuity**
    - Deep dependence on **China**, which could further increase Beijing’s influence within BRICS
- Pakistan has so far had limited capacity to contribute to BRICS’ **economic and institutional agenda** or shape **global governance**.

## New Delhi’s BRICS Summit Priorities

- As host of the **2026 summit**, India faces a demanding agenda.
- Conflicts involving **Iran, Israel and the US**, along with the **Russia-Ukraine war**, will test India’s ability to build consensus on a **joint declaration**.
- The visit of **Chinese President Xi Jinping** and the presence of **Russian President Vladimir Putin** could create opportunities for high-level consultations.
- Beyond geopolitics, the summit will focus on:
    
    - **Resilient and diversified supply chains**
    - **Reform of global financial institutions**
    - **Strengthening the NDB**
    - **Expanding intra-BRICS trade**
- Over the next decade, India can preserve BRICS’ original **reform agenda** while making the grouping more practical.
- **Digital public infrastructure, climate finance, health cooperation, critical minerals, local-currency payments and inclusive connectivity** can provide BRICS with a concrete **developmental agenda**.
