- **The early resolution of water-related issues in the southern region is well meaning but easier said than done.**
- **Linking major rivers from the Brahmaputra to the Godavari and the Cauvery** has been presented as an option to ensure that **India faces no water shortage for 100 years**.
- **Water issues are not easy to solve**, as illustrated by the handling of the **Pennaiyar river water dispute**.

## Pennaiyar River Water Dispute

- **Tamil Nadu**, alleging Karnataka’s **“violation” of the 1892 inter-State agreement**, sought the constitution of a tribunal in **November 2019** and also moved the **Supreme Court**.
- Since then, **two negotiation committees** were formed and **11 meetings** were held.
- In **February**, the Supreme Court directed the Centre to form a tribunal within **one month**, subsequently extending the deadline by **six months**.
- **The adjudicatory body has still not been put in place.**
- The Centre asked whether the dispute could be referred to the **Mahadayi Water Dispute Tribunal** instead of creating a new tribunal.
- This suggestion **defies logic**, since the two disputes have **nothing in common** and the **Interstate River Water Disputes Act, 1956** does not permit such a referral.
- The suggestion creates the impression that the Centre is **not keen on expeditiously implementing a judicial verdict** in an inter-State river dispute.
- The Central government has also **not replied** to Tamil Nadu’s demand, made in **March**, for a tribunal on Karnataka’s proposed **Mekedatu dam project**.

## A Complex Problem

- Proponents of river linking, including **Tamil Nadu**, argue that it does not seek to disturb a river’s **natural flow**, but only to divert part of its **surplus water**.
- Many experts are unconvinced, fearing that beneficiary regions may demand water **even in times of distress**, thereby depriving the **original beneficiaries** of their quota.
- River linking also raises concerns about **possible adverse consequences on ecology**.
- **Keralam** strongly opposes the **Pamba-Achankovil-Vaippar link proposal**, stating that it would affect the **Vembanad wetland system**, into which the **Pamba** and **Achankovil** rivers drain.
- The **National Water Development Agency** states that it has accounted for improving river flows during **lean periods**.

## Inter-Basin Transfer Projects

- Over the last **130-odd years**, India has had only a **handful of inter-basin transfer projects**, most of them in **south India**.
- Projects regarded as successful examples of **inter-basin transfer of river waters** are:
- **Mullaperiyar dam**
- **Parambikulam-Aliyar project**
- **Krishna Water Supply Project**
- **Indira Gandhi Canal Project**

## Special Committee For Interlinking Of Rivers

- After the **Bharatiya Janata Party** returned to power in **2014**, the Union government formed the **Special Committee for Interlinking of Rivers**.
- The committee has held **over two dozen meetings**, but there has been **not much headway**.
- In **2024**, the foundation stone was laid for the **₹44,000 crore Ken-Betwa Link Project**.
- The project has led to **agitations by tribal populations in Chhatarpur**.

## Demand Side Management

- Governments at the Centre and in States, along with civil society, should focus on **demand side management** rather than continually seeking **supply side interventions**.
- With land becoming **scarcely available** and public resistance to **land acquisition** increasing, the era of implementing **mega irrigation projects** is almost over.
- The priority must be to **conserve what is available** and use it **judiciously**.
- A massive programme should sensitise and incentivise farmers towards the **optimal use of water**.
- **Indiscriminate extraction of groundwater**, enabled by **free electricity for agriculture** in many States, is paving the way for **ecological disaster** and must be curbed immediately.
- Unless **water conservation** becomes the genuine practice of all stakeholders, **disputes will be the norm**.
