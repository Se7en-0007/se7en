- **Kanthapuram A.P. Aboobacker Musliar’s directive restricting Muslim women’s participation in public celebrations** has unsettled **Keralam** because it concerns **women’s right to participate in public life**.
- The issue goes beyond the manner in which Muslims should mark **Prophet Muhammad’s 1,501st birth anniversary**. It tests the extent to which **religious authority** can intrude into women’s lives in a State that takes pride in its **progressive social history**.
- Kanthapuram maintains that he is only reminding followers of **established religious norms**. His supporters argue that **Milad celebrations** have moved from **mosques and madrasas** to large public events, and that scholars must caution against such departures.
- This argument is rejected: communities may preserve traditions and regulate religious practices, but these must conform to the **values enshrined in the Constitution**.
- When women are asked to remain at home because their presence could cause **“chaos”**, the matter ceases to be one of religious observance. It becomes inconsistent with **constitutional equality** and Keralam’s long struggle to widen public space for women disadvantaged by **social customs and religious traditions**.

## The Muslim Community

- The **Muslim community is not a monolith**; it includes both **orthodox and progressive currents**.
- More Muslim women are breaking barriers in **education, employment, politics and public life**.
- This progress faces a **counter-current of renewed orthodoxy and social control**.

## Political Responses

- The **CPI(M)**, Keralam’s main Opposition party and a long-standing ally of the **Kanthapuram-led faction of Samastha Kerala Jamiyyathul Ulama**, responded sharply.
- The **Congress**, which leads the ruling coalition, was initially largely guarded, until **V.D. Satheesan** described the decree as **antediluvian**.
- The **Indian Union Muslim League’s silence** is criticised as difficult to defend. A party with an active **women’s forum** and, for the first time, a **woman legislator in the State Assembly** cannot equivocate when women’s right to public participation is challenged.
- Its caution may reflect political pressure relating to **Waqf, Vande Mataram and PM-SHRI**, or an attempt to improve ties with the **Kanthapuram faction** amid strained relations with the rival **Samastha faction**.
- Whatever the political calculation, **silence carries a cost**.

## Religious Freedom

- **Religious freedom** does not give religious authorities the right to regulate women’s **freedom of movement, association and degree of participation in society**.
- During **Onam**, social media carried videos of young Muslim girls celebrating the **harvest festival**.
- Kanthapuram’s remarks also drew criticism from a section of **Muslim women on social media**.
- **Keralam cannot claim to be progressive while making exceptions for orthodoxy.**
