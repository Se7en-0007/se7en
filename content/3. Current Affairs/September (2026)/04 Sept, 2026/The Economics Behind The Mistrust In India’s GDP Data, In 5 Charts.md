- The Union government pegged **economic growth in the April to June quarter at around 7.8%**, a **historically very robust** growth rate.
- Yet, **a section of the public is unwilling to accept** this official figure.
- The mistrust is notable because many people place trust in **last year’s official data**, even though it belongs to a **now-discarded data series**.
- The questioning was triggered by a **patently flawed understanding of data** by a former Finance Secretary.

## Old Vs New Series

- India’s **GDP**, or **gross domestic product**, measures **total economic output**.
- For several years, India’s GDP data has been questioned by academics and international institutions, including the **International Monetary Fund**.
- Critics, including some who earlier held senior government positions, offered **well-founded counters** to official calculations and argued that India was **overstating its economic output**.
- In **February**, the government revised the **GDP series** and other key data series such as **retail inflation**, while introducing new surveys to capture the economy more accurately.
- The first quarterly GDP release under the **new and reformed data series** has nevertheless encountered mistrust.
- While critics may still seek **greater transparency and improvements**, none argues that the new series is worse than the earlier one.
- The government’s own revision showed that the **previous series was overstating GDP**.

## What Data Show

- The central explanation is that the **GDP growth rate does not tally with the lived experience of the people**.
- Five factors indicate the economic underpinning of this mistrust in government data.

### Employment Has Stagnated

- **Employment has stagnated.**

### The Labour Force Has Declined

- The **labour force has declined**, indicating that fewer people are actively demanding jobs.
- This is most likely because **discouraged workers stop actively looking for work**.

### Wages Have Been Heading South

- **Wages have been heading south**, according to data from the **Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy (CMIE)**.
- The **average wage rate declined by 1.5% in July**.
- This was the **fifth monthly decline** during the **12-month period ending July**.
- Wages declined **twice in the last three months**.
- As a result, the **recent three months have been particularly stressful on the wage rate front**.

### Retail Inflation Has Been Rising

- **Retail inflation has been rising**.
- The spike in **food inflation** has been sharper, increasing from **around 2% in January** to **over 5% in July**.

### Consumer Sentiment Are In Negative Territory

- All manners of **consumer sentiment are in negative territory**.
- **July 2026 was not the best of times for Indian households**:
    
    - **Employment had stagnated** and the **labour force had shrunk**.
    - **Consumer sentiments were weak**.
    - **Inflation was above 4% for a second consecutive month**.
    - A **delayed monsoon** had seriously hampered the **kharif sowing schedule**.
    - **Youth protests in the capital** had intensified dramatically.
