- **India’s Gen Z is gaining political attention but has not yet consolidated into a viable electoral bloc.**
- Political parties, Ministries, Departments, think tanks and media organisations are increasingly engaging with the theme of **youth**.
- **Education and examination systems, skills and coaching processes, and unemployment** are under renewed scrutiny.
- Political parties are intensifying conversations on **digital platforms**, where young people are assumed to reside.
- Leaders are reshaping their communication models and mimicking the candid vocabulary of **Generation Z - 14-to-29-year-olds**.
- Political personalities are increasingly present on college and school campuses, suggesting an intensified, even desperate, attempt to connect with youth.

#### There Have Been Youth Initiatives

- Governments, past and present, have undertaken **youth-development initiatives**.
- The last **12 years** have seen numerous schemes covering **employment, entrepreneurship, skill enhancement, education, internships, sports and volunteering**.
- **National Education Policy 2020**, despite criticism in some respects, focuses on **quality learning geared towards innovation and the empowerment of young citizens**.
- The proposed **National Youth Policy 2026** seeks to prepare the **15-29 age group** for the **new digital age**.
- There remains a lag in understanding **Gen Z** and **Gen A - those born after 2010** - who have begun demanding **accountability**.
- A **NITI Aayog** report identifies **8.7 crore people aged 15-29** as **not in education, employment or training - NEET**.
- Although the government has clarified that **NEET does not necessarily mean joblessness**, a staggering amount of youthful energy still awaits an anchor.
- The gap between **formal degrees** and **job prospects** remains a daunting problem.
- Following recent street protests, the Opposition has advanced a narrative that the ruling dispensation is hostile to younger generations, raising the question of whether it has a genuine **blueprint for the country’s youth** or is merely making political capital from current ferment.
- The youth chorus in **Delhi and other places** has served as a wake-up call for those in power and those seeking power.
- Although India’s youth furore is not comparable with cataclysmic developments in parts of the **South Asian neighbourhood**, political parties and leaders now appear to be grappling with youth issues, even if prompted by electoral politics.

#### More An Elusive Vote

- India has the world’s largest youth population: **65% of its nearly 1.5 billion people are below 35 years of age**.
- Around **23% of the electorate** belongs to the **18-29 age category**.
- Recent Assembly elections in **Tamil Nadu, Assam, West Bengal, Keralam and Puducherry** showed **no discernible trend** of young people voting in a particular political direction.
- Tamil Nadu showed some signs of **youth consolidation**, but these were far from being based on an alternative **youth agenda**.
- In last year’s **Bihar election**, political groups’ noticeable efforts to advance a youth agenda did not generate much appeal.
- Certain studies suggest that young voters partly influenced verdicts in the **2014 and 2019 general elections**.
- Across India’s **75-year electoral history**, votes have been mobilised around **community, caste, class, farmers, industrial workers and government employees**, but youth have not acquired a distinct bloc identity.
- Established electoral patterns include votes of **Dalits, minorities, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes**, the **Yadav vote in Uttar Pradesh**, and the **M-Y vote in Bihar**.
- The **women’s vote** has become an additional significant electoral category.
- **Housing, toilets, cooking gas, electricity and direct cash transfers** can capture women’s imagination across regions, with gratitude expressed through the ballot.
- **Caste and community**, along with **gender identity**, continue as assured electoral aggregators and have substantially subsumed **youth identity**.
- **Employment and education** remain only a loose overhang rather than a cohesive youth electoral identity.
- Youth participation in elections has remained **low-key** compared with other demographic categories, though it has improved in recent years largely due to mobilisation by the **Election Commission of India - ECI**.
- The ECI has strengthened electoral-literacy campaigns in schools and colleges to orient students to the **electoral system, processes and practices**, and secure their informed participation.

#### Old Equations Do Matter

- The upcoming Assembly elections in **Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Manipur and Goa** will be the earliest test of whether the streets’ noisy youth voice becomes an organised political preference and produces greater **youth candidature**.
- Roughly **a quarter of Assembly seats** in these States, especially in politically muscular **Uttar Pradesh**, were won by margins of around **5%** and could be swung by **Gen Z voters**.
- Youth across States do not share a common predicament: the youth of **Uttar Pradesh and Punjab** differ, as do Gen Z in **Goa and Manipur**; the same applies to youth in **Gujarat and Himachal Pradesh**, which go to polls later in **2027**.
- Current political attention to youth can therefore be seen as an attempt to **cut possible losses while making a moderate investment**.
- Political parties cannot abandon time-tested voting blocs for an **undependable, heterogeneous multitude** that may or may not emerge from its **Instagram residency**.
- **Old social calculations and booth management** may still hold in actual polls, while the youth awakening may remain a story on the side.
- Parties and poll campaigners will watch cautiously for the emergence of a real **Gen Z vote**, adjusting plans and campaigns only modestly for now.
- In the Union government’s vision of a **developed India by 2047**, youth are described as **Amrit Peedhi - the generation of the nectar**.
- In realpolitik, forthcoming State elections may show which side this nectar sweetens, or whether it does so at all.
- The electoral power of youth may only now be **crystallising in India**.
