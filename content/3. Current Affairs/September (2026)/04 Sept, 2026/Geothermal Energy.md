## A New Chapter In India's Clean Energy Journey

- **Geothermal energy** is a **clean**, **round-the-clock available** source of power derived from the **intense heat in the Earth's interior**.
- India’s **rich geothermal resources** hold immense promise, and the **National Policy on Geothermal Energy** seeks to make geothermal energy a key part of the country’s renewable energy story as India moves towards **Net Zero by 2070**.
- The **commissioning of the first two geothermal wells in Ladakh** marks the beginning of India’s geothermal energy journey.

## Harnessing Geothermal Energy For A Sustainable India

- **Geothermal energy** is a **constant, low-carbon source of energy** located deep below the Earth’s surface.
- In locations with suitable **geology and tectonics**, it can be tapped for **power generation** or used directly on the ground.
- Unlike solar and wind energy, geothermal energy is **renewable energy available round the clock**.
- The Government notified the **National Policy on Geothermal Energy** in **September 2025**.
- The policy provides a roadmap for exploring and developing India’s geothermal resources.
- The **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)** is the **nodal ministry** for implementation of the policy.
- In **July 2026**, the **ONGC Energy Centre** commissioned India’s **first two geothermal wells** in **Puga Valley, Ladakh**.
- **Puga Valley** is located at an altitude of over **14,000 feet above sea level** and is known for its **hot springs** and **underground heat reserves**.
- The two wells are **1,000 metres deep** and will support **reservoir evaluation**.
- They also pave the way for **India’s first 1-megawatt demonstration geothermal power project**.
- Geothermal power offers a **dependable, indigenous** option to diversify India’s renewable energy mix.

## Understanding Geothermal Energy: An Overview

- **Geothermal energy** is a **steady, low-carbon and weather-independent energy source**.
- It has a **vast untapped resource base** that can strengthen India’s clean energy future and reduce reliance on **fossil fuels**.

## Formation Of Geothermal Energy

- The Earth’s interior has four main layers:
    
    - **Crust** - outermost, solid layer.
    - **Mantle** - middle, semi-solid layer.
    - **Outer Core** - dense liquid layer.
    - **Inner Core** - solid layer.
- Temperature rises with depth:
    
    - The **mantle temperature** reaches about **3,700°C** at its boundary with the core.
    - The **core temperature** reaches about **5,000-6,000°C**.
- Earth’s interior heat is generated by:
    
    - **Radioactive decay** of elements such as **uranium, thorium and potassium**.
    - **Residual heat** left from the planet’s formation.
- This heat generates large **convection currents in the mantle**, where hot material rises from the core boundary toward cooler upper parts of the mantle and crust.
- The material contains water trapped as **hydroxides**.
- These convection currents cause tectonic activity, including **earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain building**.
- In geologically active regions, **faults and fractures** allow hot material to move closer to the surface.
- This creates reservoirs of **hot water and steam** trapped within **permeable rock**.
- The thermal energy harnessed from within the Earth is known as **geothermal energy**.

## From Geothermal Energy To Power Production

- Geothermal power generation converts the Earth’s natural heat into electricity by tapping **hot water and steam** beneath the surface.
- **Deep wells** bring hot water and steam to the surface.
- Steam drives a **turbine** connected to a **generator**.
- The process resembles conventional thermal power generation but replaces **fuel combustion** with naturally occurring underground heat.
- After power generation, used water is **condensed and reinjected** into the reservoir.
- Reinjection supports **resource replenishment** and enables continued energy production.
- Geothermal energy also supports direct heat applications in:
    
    - **District heating**
    - **Agriculture**
    - **Aquaculture**
    - **Space heating and cooling**

## Inside The Earth

- **Inner Core**:
    
    - A solid ball of **iron and nickel**.
    - About **2,400 km wide**.
- **Outer Core**:
    
    - A liquid layer of molten **iron and nickel**.
    - Roughly **2,400 km thick**.
- **Mantle**:
    
    - Composed mostly of **hot, dense rock**.
    - Extends nearly **2,900 km**.
    - Forms the Earth’s **largest layer**.
- **Crust**:
    
    - Thin, solid outer layer.
    - About **5-8 km thick beneath oceans**.
    - About **24-56 km thick beneath continents**.

## India's Geothermal Resource Base

- The **Geological Survey of India (GSI)** has mapped **381 hot springs** and identified **10 geothermal provinces** across India.
- India’s theoretical geothermal prospect is nearly **10.6 GW**.
- **42 sites** have been recognised as promising for **power generation** and **direct heat use**.
- The **10 geothermal provinces** are:
    
    - **Himalayan Geothermal Province**
    - **Naga-Lusai**
    - **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**
    - **Son Narmada Tapi (SONATA)**
    - **West Coast**
    - **Cambay Graben**
    - **Aravalli**
    - **Mahanadi**
    - **Godavari**
    - **South Indian Cratonic regions**
- India’s geothermal rift basins have strong scope because of favourable **geology and tectonics**.
- Suitable projects can arise wherever geothermal resources exist.
- **Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS)** and **Advanced Geothermal Systems (AGS)** can widen exploration opportunities.

## Repurposing Abandoned Oil And Gas Wells

- Geothermal energy can be extracted from **abandoned oil and gas wells**.
- Inactive or unproductive wells may be repurposed for geothermal initiatives, subject to **technical and operational feasibility**.
- This approach uses existing:
    
    - **Drilling expertise**
    - **Well data**
    - **Infrastructure** from the oil and gas sector

## India’s Geothermal Expansion

- India is moving from **resource assessment** to **field demonstration**.
- Between **July and August 2025**, MNRE sanctioned **five research and development projects**.
- These projects cover:
    
    - **Resource assessment**
    - **Indigenous technology development**
    - **Field demonstration**
    - **Electricity generation**
    - **Heating and cooling applications**
- The projects are generating technical data and field experience required for future **commercial deployment**.

|**Project**|**Current Status Of Government-Supported Projects**|
|---|---|
|**Ankleshwar, Gujarat**|**Pilot project** evaluating electricity generation by retrofitting **unproductive oil and gas wells**. **Field investigations** and baseline **geological and production data** have been completed.|
|**Gandhinagar, Gujarat**|Demonstration project integrating **solar and geothermal energy**. It has drilled **three geothermal wells** producing geothermal fluid at **70-80°C** and demonstrated electricity generation of **50-90 kilowatts**.|
|**Tawang, Arunachal Pradesh**|Studies are underway at **five geothermal sites** through field investigations and **geochemical analysis**.|
|**Hyderabad, Telangana**|Research project developing a **geothermal cooling system** for air-conditioning applications through modelling and performance analysis.|
|**Shallow Geothermal Energy**|Pilot study developing analytical models to evaluate shallow geothermal systems using **Borehole Heat Exchangers** for heating and cooling applications.|

- Significant geothermal resources, advancing technologies and early field activity are laying the foundation for geothermal energy in India.
- **Commercial-scale power generation is yet to be established**.
- Expanding well fields, demonstration activities, and research and development initiatives are strengthening the ecosystem for wider deployment.

## Unlocking India’s Geothermal Potential: Policy & Partnerships

- Geothermal energy is a **proven, established renewable technology worldwide**.
- Global installed geothermal capacity reached **15.67 GW by the end of 2025**.
- **Indonesia, the US, the Philippines, Türkiye and New Zealand** together hold around **67% of global capacity**.
- India is building an ecosystem to move from resource assessment to real-world deployment through national programmes and international partnerships.
- These efforts support **research**, **technology development** and **knowledge exchange**.

## National Policy On Geothermal Energy, 2025

- The policy aims to establish geothermal energy as a **major renewable pillar**.
- It promotes systematic exploration and deployment of India’s geothermal resources.
- It prioritises:
    
    - **Research**
    - **Drilling**
    - **Reservoir management**
    - **Direct-use applications**
- It supports **Ground Source Heat Pumps (GSHPs)** for heating and cooling.
- A clear regulatory framework encourages participation by both the **public** and **private sectors**.
- The policy facilitates repurposing **abandoned oil and gas wells** for geothermal use.
- It supports:
    
    - A **national geothermal data repository**
    - **Pilot projects**
    - **Centres of Excellence**

## Renewable Energy Research And Technology Development Programme (RE-RTD)

- MNRE is implementing the **RE-RTD scheme** through research institutions and industry partners.
- The initiative aims to develop **indigenous, cost-effective technologies** for renewable energy, including geothermal energy, throughout the country.

## International Collaborations

- Under cooperation mechanisms with **Australia** and **Iceland**, geothermal energy has been identified as a key area of cooperation.
- An MoU with the **Kingdom of Saudi Arabia** has designated geothermal energy as a focus area of bilateral engagement.
- These initiatives provide a coordinated platform for converting India’s geothermal resources into **scalable solutions across sectors**.

## New Dimension In India’s Renewable Energy Mix

- India has moved from planning to implementation through the **National Policy on Geothermal Energy** and the commissioning of the first two geothermal wells at **Puga Valley, Ladakh**.
- India has a theoretical geothermal resource potential of nearly **10,600 MW**.
- Pilot projects and international collaborations are expanding geothermal applications in:
    
    - **Power generation**
    - **Heating**
    - **Geo-tourism**
    - **Agriculture**
- Geothermal energy is emerging as a valuable addition to India’s renewable energy portfolio.
- As India advances toward its **2070 Net Zero target**, geothermal energy will remain a **steady, indigenous pillar** of the country’s energy transition and progress.
