### **Bishkek Meeting**

- **Prime Minister Narendra Modi attended the annual Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) leaders’ meeting in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan**, which marked **25 years of the Eurasian grouping**.
- The meeting had a **multi-pronged agenda**, including Modi’s first meetings with counterparts since the **U.S.-Israel war on Iran began more than six months earlier** and imposed **economic costs on the entire grouping**.
- The SCO includes **Iran** and **Pakistan**, with Pakistan having emerged as a **mediator between Iran and the U.S.**

### **Bishkek Declaration**

- The **Bishkek declaration** condemned the **U.S. and Israel’s attacks on Iran**.
- It supported **Iran’s right to peaceful nuclear technology under the NPT**.

### **BRICS Cooperation**

- Several SCO members are also in **BRICS**, including **Russian President Vladimir Putin, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian**.
- Modi will host them in **Delhi on September 12-13**.
- **New Delhi is keen to forge consensus on a joint declaration**, but this has been difficult because of **UAE-Iran tensions**.
- Modi’s discussions with **Masoud Pezeshkian** included **BRICS cooperation**.

### **Central Asian Republics**

- India’s ties with the **Central Asian Republics** have flagged because of other preoccupations.
- Modi’s visits to **Tashkent and Bishkek**, and his meetings with regional leaders, were important for reviving discussions on **trade, critical minerals, tourism, and other areas**.

### **Pakistan Chairmanship**

- As **Pakistan takes over the SCO chairmanship**, the Bishkek Summit enabled Modi to lay out **India’s agenda for the coming year**.
- Modi stressed the need to dismantle the ecosystem of **“terrorist financing, recruitment, radicalisation, and safe havens”**.
- He called on the SCO to eschew **“double standards” on terrorism**.
- Modi also sought a strong message to countries that **“use terrorism as an instrument of policy and provide safe haven and support to terrorists”**, a pointed reference to India’s repeated concerns about **Pakistan**.
- New Delhi welcomed the Bishkek declaration’s strong language on **“all forms” of terrorism**, including **cross-border terrorism**.
- It remains uncertain how far the **Pakistan presidency** will advance these ideas.

### **Altered Geopolitical Framing**

- Unlike the **high-octane optics** and the **Modi-Putin-Xi handshake** at the **2025 Tianjin SCO Summit**, the current summit was relatively **sombre**.
- **U.S. tariffs**, its **war with Iran**, and **sanctions on Russia** have harmed **economic security, energy security, and connectivity**, including through the **Hormuz Strait blockade**.
- These developments pose major challenges for the SCO grouping.
- For India, the past year has been sobering after tensions with Pakistan peaked following the **Pahalgam attacks** and the **four-day conflict**.
- Pakistan’s diplomatic engagements with the **U.S.**, along with its new security pact with **Saudi Arabia and Türkiye**, have added to India’s concerns.
- In this altered geopolitical framing, **India must broaden its engagement with all regional multilateral organisations such as the SCO**.
