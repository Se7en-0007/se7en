- **Climate shock events across South Asia expose an unequal and unfair world**, where risks may be shared but **vulnerabilities differ sharply**.
- The **Rasuwa floods in Nepal** have caused **over 1,250 deaths**, while **over 4,000 people are still missing**.
- The disaster affected **locals, tourists on holiday, and pilgrims**. It triggered government outreach for citizens, rescue assistance, rehabilitation contributions, and widespread searches for missing loved ones.
- A climate shock’s **full story rarely fits into one dramatic headline**, as its human consequences, rescue needs, and rehabilitation burdens unfold across many locations and communities.

## Nepal’s Negligible Contribution To Climate Change

- Nepal accounts for only **0.1% of total global greenhouse gas emissions**.
- This negligible contribution to climate change should be considered before attention is drawn towards **unsubstantiated claims or conspiracy theories**.
- At least **900 workers** were believed to be trapped at **12 hydropower projects** affected by flash floods in the districts of **Rasuwa and Nuwakot**.

## Cyclone Ditwah

- The situation in Nepal recalls **Cyclone Ditwah**, which hit Sri Lanka in **late 2025**.
- The disaster showed that while climate risks may be common, **vulnerabilities are different**.
- Among those most affected were Sri Lanka’s **Malaiyaha Tamil community**, especially people working in **tea and rubber estates** across the **central and southern hill country**.
- The community is **historically marginalised**, faces **exploitation**, and is among those most vulnerable to climate shock.
- The **United Nations sought to mobilise $35 million** for humanitarian assistance.
- Sri Lanka is still rebuilding after the **unprecedented floods and landslides**.
- Some affected families remained in **temporary shelters for months** and struggled to commute to the tea estates where they earned a **daily wage**.
- Concerns regarding **compensation** and transition to **safe, permanent homes** persist.
- Even **ten months later**, some communities say that **the cyclone is yet to pass**.

## One Climate Shock After Another

- Major climate shock events attract global attention, but less dramatic signs occurring periodically are also significant.
- In **late 2018**, flash floods severely hit districts across Sri Lanka’s **Northern Province**.
- **Tens of thousands of people were affected**, crops were damaged, and villages were inundated.
- In Kilinochchi, people were receiving **drought relief even days before the flood suddenly hit**.
- This reflected how people and authorities were compelled to combat **one climate shock after another**.
- As Sri Lanka prepares for the possible effects of **El Niño**, this pattern remains relevant.

## South Asia As An Ecologically Interconnected System

- The region has experienced major climate shocks in quick succession: the **Pakistan floods of 2022**, floods in different parts of **India over the last few years**, the **Sri Lanka cyclone of 2025**, and Nepal’s staggering floods.
- A **2009** piece in _Himal Southasian_ argued that **South Asia is an ecologically interconnected system** and called for a **collaborative response to the climate crisis**.
- However, reporting from the region has contained more stories about **natural disasters** than about **collective strategies**.
