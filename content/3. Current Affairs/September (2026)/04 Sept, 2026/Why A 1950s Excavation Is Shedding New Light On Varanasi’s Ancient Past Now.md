## Rajghat Artefacts

- **Banaras Hindu University (BHU)** recently examined **22 boxes** stored for around **70 years** and found **more than 10,000 artefacts**, including **painted black pottery believed to be 3,000 years old**.
- The artefacts were excavated from **Rajghat**, one of the oldest known settlements in the **Varanasi region**, between **1957 and 1969**.
- Earlier in August, experts opened a box containing a **human skeleton** recovered from the same site.

## Where Is Rajghat And Why Is It Significant For The History Of Varanasi?

- **Rajghat** is a neighbourhood in **eastern Varanasi**.
- The **Rajghat archaeological site** refers specifically to an ancient settlement on the **northeastern outskirts of the city**, near the confluence of the **Ganga** and **Varana** rivers.
- Rajghat is identified as the capital of the **Kashi Janapada**.
- **Janapadas** were territorial kingdoms or political units that emerged in ancient India, particularly during the **later Vedic period**.
- Some janapadas became larger and more powerful kingdoms called **mahajanapadas** by the **6th century BCE**.
- Early **Buddhist and Jain texts** mention **Kashi** as one of the **16 mahajanapadas** in the **Indo-Gangetic plain**.
- The Kashi region, more popularly known as **Varanasi**, has several ancient settlements, including **Aktha, Ramnagar, Sarnath, and Rajghat**.
- While **Sarnath** emerged as a pilgrimage site, **Rajghat** developed as the main urban centre.
- The other settlements, dated several centuries earlier, were peripheral and rural regions of Kashi.

## When Was Rajghat Excavated And What Did The Excavations Reveal?

- Rajghat first drew archaeological attention in **1940**, when a railway contractor reported archaeological remains while excavating earth for the extension and remodelling of **Kashi Railway Station**.
- **Krishna Deva** conducted a trial excavation on behalf of the **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** in **October-November 1940**.
- In **1957**, **A K Narain** and colleagues from BHU’s Department of **Ancient Indian History, Culture and Archaeology** excavated three mounds at Rajghat.
- The excavation took place in **four phases over 12 years** and yielded a large number of significant artefacts.
- The lowest and oldest layer yielded **pre-Northern Black Polished Ware (pre-NBPW)**, pottery associated with an earlier phase of the **Iron Age**.
- On this basis, the origins of the Rajghat settlement were dated to around **800 BCE**.
- In the early **2000s**, Rajghat was re-excavated to verify the dating of its oldest layer.
- Excavation began with layers from the **Kushana period**, dated from the **1st century BCE to the 3rd century CE**, and proceeded downwards.
- Scientific analysis of artefacts from these layers confirmed the oldest layer’s date as around **800 BCE**.

## Artefacts From Rajghat

- The site has yielded **hundreds of seals and sealings**.
- Many seals carry **Greek inscriptions**.
- Several seals carry the names of **administrative units** and their **officials**.
- Other finds include **ivory and iron objects, punch-marked coins, and terracotta figurines**.
- The finds are significant for tracing the development of **human civilisation in the subcontinent**.

## Mythology And Archaeology

- The antiquity of Varanasi is well established in **mythological and religious texts**.
- An important question during the Rajghat excavation was whether archaeological evidence from the region corresponded with these textual traditions.
- **Mythology and archaeology operate on very different bases**.
- There is a tendency to describe a site as **pracheen** when it has to be established as important.
- The connection between **Varanasi, Shiva, and the Ganga** is a product of **late medieval texts**.
- Archaeology dates settlements through their **material remains**.
- Remains from Rajghat and other Varanasi sites can be dated as far back as **1800 BCE**.
- The gap between archaeological dates and the period of textual traditions must be kept in mind while reconstructing Varanasi’s history.

## Why Is BHU Documenting The Artefacts Now?

- The documentation is part of the **centenary celebrations** of the concerned BHU department.
- Its purpose is to preserve the department’s work of the last **100 years** and place it in the public domain for future researchers.
- There are plans to develop a **museum** in the future.
- Documentation began in **January** and has already recorded about **4,000 artefacts**.
- There are believed to be close to **10,000 objects from Rajghat alone**.
- Rajghat excavation reports are already in the public domain, and the artefacts have long been available to researchers.
- Some researchers have studied the material, but this is the **first concerted and organised effort** to document all the findings.

## The Human Skeleton

- The human skeleton is the **only artefact opened for the first time**.
- When it was excavated in the **1960s**, technology to analyse human remains was unavailable.
- Advances in **DNA sampling and analysis** may now yield information about the **age, ancestry, and lifestyle** of people who lived in the region in antiquity.
