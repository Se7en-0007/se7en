- **A strengthening El Niño** and **escalating geopolitical tensions in West Asia and Russia-Ukraine** could push up **food inflation**, which has remained relatively under control so far.

## This Year’s Southwest Monsoon

- **June** normally sees an average of **three low-pressure systems (LPS)** covering about **11 days**. This year, **not a single LPS formed**.
- An **LPS** brings rain when **warm, moist air near the ground rises**, cools and condenses into clouds.
- Consequently, **all-India rainfall in June was 38% below the normal long-period average (LPA)**.
- In **July**, **four LPSs** formed, close to the climatological average.
- These systems persisted longer, taking total **LPS days to 24**, against a monthly average of **13.56 days**.
- India received rainfall **1% above the overall LPA** in July.
- In **August**, the **effects of El Niño started showing up**.
- **Six LPSs** formed, above the climatological average of **5.38**, while **LPS days reached 26**, above the normal **16.3 days**.
- Yet, India recorded an **all-India rainfall deficit of 16.3% over the LPA**.
- LPS formation did not ensure rainfall because **El Niño weakened the easterly trade winds**, reducing the transport of **moisture-laden air** towards the Indian subcontinent.
- Cumulative all-India rainfall for the four-month season was **14.7% below the LPA as on September 13**.
- **Twenty-four of 36 meteorological subdivisions** recorded rainfall deficiency of more than **10%**.
- The **southern states**, along with **Marathwada and Vidarbha** in Maharashtra, were the worst affected.

## Impact Of The Deficient Monsoon

- Total kharif sowing stood at **1,096.5 lakh hectares** as of **September 11**, only **1.4% below** the **1,112.5 lakh hectares** sown in the corresponding period of **2025**.
- However, the acreage shortfall may not show the full picture. **Fertiliser sales** are a better indicator.
- Sales of **di-ammonium phosphate**, **muriate of potash**, and **complex fertilisers** containing nitrogen, phosphorous, potassium and sulphur were significantly lower during **April to July 2026** than in **April to July 2025**.
- Only **single super phosphate** sales were marginally higher.
- **Urea sales declined by 6.6%**, despite government interventions to ensure natural-gas availability for domestic production and imports amid **West Asia-induced supply shocks**.
- Commodity prices indicate bullish sentiment and expectations of a smaller crop:
- **Maize** in Chhindwara, Madhya Pradesh: about **Rs 2,625 per quintal**, versus **Rs 2,165** a year earlier.
- **Arhar** in Akola, Maharashtra: **Rs 8,650 per quintal**, versus **Rs 6,200**.
- **Soyabean** in Dewas, Madhya Pradesh: **Rs 6,150 per quintal**, versus **Rs 4,300**.
- The **United States Department of Agriculture** forecast India’s **rice production** at **147 million tonnes**, down from the previous year’s all-time high of **154 million tonnes**.
- It forecast **maize production** at **50 million tonnes**, down from **55.1 million tonnes**.
- The headline kharif sowing gap appears small, but the **uneven temporal distribution** of rainfall, with extended dry spells interspersed with heavy downpours, and its **uneven spatial distribution** are likely to lower yields and harvested produce.
- Rainfall was largely confined to **Odisha, Chhattisgarh, eastern Madhya Pradesh, Gangetic West Bengal, Jharkhand and Uttar Pradesh**.

## Looking Ahead

- **El Niño** is in a **strong state**, with average sea-surface temperatures in the equatorial Pacific off Ecuador and Peru about **1.8 degrees Celsius above normal**.
- The **US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration** projected a **more than 90% chance** of a **very strong El Niño** event, with sea-surface temperatures more than **2 degrees Celsius above normal**, developing and lasting from **September through January**.
- El Niño is projected to stay **strong**, with temperatures more than **1.5 degrees Celsius above normal**, until **March**, and **weak-to-moderate**, at **0.5 degrees Celsius to 1.5 degrees Celsius above normal**, until **May**.
- El Niño suppresses rainfall and raises temperatures in India.
- If it becomes very strong over the next **four to five months**, India could experience a **relatively short and warm winter**.
- The upcoming rabi crops likely to bear the brunt include **wheat, rapeseed-mustard, chana, masoor, matar, potato, onion, garlic, jeera, saunf and dhaniya**.

## World Prices

- The West Asia conflict has not produced a dramatic spike in world food prices like Russia’s invasion of Ukraine in **2022**.
- The reason has been **ample stocks** following back-to-back bumper crops in **2024-25** and **2025-26**, including record output of **wheat, rice, maize or corn, sugar, soyabean, rapeseed and palm oil**.
- This situation is changing as stocks run down, trade logistics are disrupted by geopolitical tensions, and El Niño strengthens.
- The **FAO food price index** was **133.3 points in August**, its highest since **November 2022**.
- The index is a weighted average of world prices for a basket of food commodities, with the base-period value for **2014-16 set at 100**.
- It remained below the all-time high of **160.2 points in March 2022**.
- The FAO vegetable-oils index in August was the highest since **June 2022**, while the cereals index reached a **27-month high**.

## Inflation Pressures

- Inflation pressures are currently concentrated in **vegetable oils**.
- Landed Mumbai prices of imported crude oils were above their September 2025 averages:
- **Crude palm oil:** **Rs 1,285 per tonne**, versus **Rs 1,164 per tonne**.
- **Crude soyabean oil:** **Rs 1,300 per tonne**, versus **Rs 1,182 per tonne**.
- **Crude sunflower oil:** **Rs 1,450 per tonne**, versus **Rs 1,293 per tonne**.
- Cereal markets are also tightening:
- Argentine wheat export prices increased from **Rs 228 to Rs 262 per tonne** in one year.
- European Union wheat rose from **Rs 226 to Rs 290 per tonne**.
- Australian wheat rose from **Rs 251 to Rs 319 per tonne**.
- US wheat rose from **Rs 235 to Rs 354 per tonne**.
- Corn export prices rose to **Rs 219 per tonne** from Argentina and **Rs 238 per tonne** from Brazil, compared with **Rs 200** and **Rs 210 per tonne**, respectively, a year earlier.
- **Food inflation is likely to return and remain elevated going ahead.**
