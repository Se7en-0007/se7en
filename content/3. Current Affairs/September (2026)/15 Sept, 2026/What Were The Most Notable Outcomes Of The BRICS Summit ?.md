- **The 18th BRICS Summit**, marking **two decades of the group’s formulation**, concluded on **September 12-13**.
- Hosted by **India**, it brought together the **heads of states of all 11 member nations** for **multilateral deliberations** and significant **bilateral engagements**.
- The summit tested the effectiveness of an **expanded and increasingly diverse BRICS**, including whether and to what extent **consensus could be formulated**.
- Two elements stood out from **India’s 2026 BRICS presidency**: the **New Delhi Declaration** and **diplomatic activity on the sidelines**.

## New Delhi Declaration

- The summit navigated **sharp differences among members** and adopted the **140-point New Delhi Declaration**.
- Despite significant divergences over **ongoing conflicts**, the document used **carefully calibrated language** and avoided naming individual countries as offenders, enabling members with competing positions to remain within a **common framework**.
- The declaration endorsed **China as the 2027 chair**.
- Unlike the **Rio Declaration** last year, the New Delhi Declaration explicitly condemned the **April 2025 terror attack in Pahalgam**: the terrorist attack in **Jammu and Kashmir on 22 April 2025**, in which **26 people were killed** and many more were injured.
- It addressed the continuing conflict in **West Asia**. As **Iran and the UAE** are now both BRICS members and directly affected by the conflict, the text expressed concern over the **escalation of tensions**, without naming parties.
- It reaffirmed the **UN Charter** and called for the exercise of **maximum restraint**.
- The **Rio Summit** also addressed West Asia, but none of the countries directly involved in the conflict were BRICS members at that time.

## Bilateral Level

- The summit provided a platform to engage **key partners**.
- The meeting between **Prime Minister Narendra Modi** and **Chinese President Xi Jinping** signaled continued efforts to stabilise **India-China ties**, following the **2020 Galwan Valley clash** that led to severe deterioration in the relationship.
- The two sides discussed **trade, business links, supply chains, and people-to-people exchanges**.
- They reiterated that **peace and tranquility along the border** remain essential to the development of bilateral ties.
- Their statements differed in **emphasis and tone**.

## BRICS Continuity And Implementation Mechanism

- India sought to address the tendency for initiatives launched during one presidency to **lose momentum under the next**.
- PM Modi proposed the **BRICS Continuity and Implementation Mechanism**.
- Under the mechanism, the **troika** - the outgoing, current and incoming chairs - will track progress and systematically follow up on outcomes.
- The proposal’s real test will come after the handover to **China for the 2027 chairship**.

## BRICS Economic Partnership 2030

- India’s chairship attempted to move BRICS beyond **leader-level statements** towards mechanisms with a more direct impact on **trade, investment and businesses**.
- This included advancing the **BRICS Economic Partnership 2030** and working on **development finance, infrastructure and investment**.
- The practical significance of these initiatives should not be overstated because several proposals, including the **investment platform**, remain under development.

# How Was India’s 2026 Presidency Different From Its Previous Chairship In 2021?

## Composition Of The Grouping

- In **2021**, BRICS comprised only the **five original members**.
- In **2026**, it had expanded to **11 members and 10 partner countries**.
- This increased the group’s **economic weight** but made **consensus more difficult**.

## Different Contexts

- The **2021 Summit** was held virtually following the outbreak of the **Covid-19 pandemic** and against the backdrop of the **India-China border standoff**.
- In **2026**, India hosted an extensive programme of **over 350 meetings across 30 Indian cities**.
- The **2021 Summit New Delhi Declaration** focused on **Global Health Challenges** and combating the pandemic, particularly **vaccine production and distribution**.
- In **2026**, India placed greater emphasis on **science and technology, agroecology, fintech, and economic cooperation**.
- India continued its push for **UN and multilateral reform** around **representation, responsiveness, and rulemaking**.

## Economic Relationship Within BRICS

- The economic relationship within BRICS has **deepened**.
- Indian trade with BRICS members more than doubled from **203 billion dollars in 2020-2021** to **417 billion dollars in 2025-2026**.
- Recognising the expanded potential for **intra-bloc trade** and market opportunities in these developing economies, BRICS emphasised **customs cooperation, trade finance, supply chains and digital payments**.
- It also addressed **trade asymmetries**.

# What Are The Developments In The Cross-Border Payments Systems?

## Cross-Border Trade In National Currencies

- Despite expanded efforts to boost **intra-bloc trade** and streamline payment mechanisms, BRICS does not yet have an **operational system for cross-border trade in national currencies**.
- The **BRICS Payment Task Force** will continue to explore practical solutions for **efficient cross-border payments** and greater interoperability of **payment and messaging channels**.
- This builds on the **BRICS Cross-Border Payments Initiative** referenced in the **Kazan and Rio Declarations**.

## More Ambitious Approach

- India pushed for linking the **fast-payment systems** of BRICS countries and examining interoperability between **central-bank digital currencies**.
- The objective is to make payments **faster, cheaper and more reliable**.
- Implementation is complicated because members have different **exchange rate regimes, capital controls, exposure to sanctions, banking regulations, and payment standards**.
- **Intra-BRICS trade** is highly asymmetric, with **China’s dominance** accounting for a substantial share and several members running **large trade deficits** with it.

## Common BRICS Currency

- Discussions on trade in national currencies distinguish what BRICS is realistically pursuing from the often-repeated misconception of a **common BRICS currency**.
- Members have not agreed on a **common technical architecture**, a **shared settlement mechanism**, or the launch of the payments platform, **BRICS Pay**.
- Therefore, trade in national currencies has not materialised beyond existing initiatives at **bilateral levels**.

# Core Concerns Of Development, Trade And Practical Cooperation In Technology

- India attempted to keep BRICS focused on its core concerns of **development, trade and practical cooperation in technology**.
- It also sought meaningful language on conflicts despite a **fragmented geopolitical environment**.
- Ambitious efforts must materialise on **payment systems interoperability, MSME financing, greater investment announcements, and meaningful bilateral development** under future presidencies.
