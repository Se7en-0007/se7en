- Chief ministers of **Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan and Delhi** signed an **MoU on September 15** for the long-pending **422 MW Kishau Multipurpose Project**.
- The project has an estimated cost of **Rs 15,000 crore**.
- It will be placed before the **Union Cabinet** for approval.

## What Is The Kishau Multipurpose Project?

- A proposed dam and hydropower project on the **Tons River**, a major tributary of the **Yamuna**, along the Himachal Pradesh-Uttarakhand border.
- It envisages a **232.6-metre-high concrete gravity dam** and will generate **1,476 million units of clean hydropower**.
- The site straddles **Sirmaur district, Himachal Pradesh**, and **Dehradun district, Uttarakhand**.
- Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand signed an MoU to form a joint venture in **June 2015**; **Kishau Corporation Ltd** was incorporated in **January 2017**.
- The Chairperson of Kishau Corporation Ltd is held by the Chief Secretaries of Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand in **two-year rotations**.
- The project is intended to:
    
    - Generate electricity
    - Create irrigation potential of about **97,076 hectares**
    - Provide drinking and industrial water to **Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh**

## Why Was The Kishau Dam Required?

- Kishau was one of **three storage projects** planned in the upper reaches of the Yamuna and its tributaries after the **May 12, 1994 MoU** on allocation of Yamuna water.
- The Yamuna basin States under the MoU were **undivided Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh and the National Capital Territory of Delhi**.
- The other projects were **Lakhwar** and **Renukaji**; their MoUs have been signed and they are under implementation.
- There are no storage facilities on the **Upper Yamuna**, stretching from the **Yamunotri glacier** in the lower Himalayas to the **Okhla barrage** in Delhi.
- The Upper Yamuna has a catchment area of about **30,000 square kilometres**.
- The 1994 MoU made an interim seasonal allocation of the Yamuna's annual utilisable flow among the five states.

### Annual Utilisable Water Of The Yamuna

- Total annual utilisable water: **11.983 billion cubic metres (BCM)**.
- Maximum allocation:
    
    - Haryana: **5.730 BCM**
    - Uttar Pradesh: **4.032 BCM**
    - Rajasthan: **1.119 BCM**
    - Delhi: **0.724 BCM**
    - Himachal Pradesh: **0.378 BCM**
- After Uttarakhand was created in **2000**, undivided Uttar Pradesh's share of **4.032 BCM** was allocated as:
    
    - Uttar Pradesh: **3.721 BCM**
    - Uttarakhand: **0.311 BCM**
- A **Detailed Project Report** of the Kishau project was circulated among the concerned states earlier this year but is yet to be finalised.

## Why Was The Signing Of The MoU Delayed?

- For **eight years**, Himachal Pradesh held out on signing the MoU due to:
    
    - The project-cost burden, particularly the **power component**
    - Concern that the state would not receive **proportionate benefits**
- The previous BJP-led Himachal Pradesh government had agreed to contribute about **Rs 800 crore** towards the project cost.
- The current Congress-led government rejected the arrangement, stating that it was not in Himachal Pradesh's financial interest and that it would suffer substantial land submergence and displacement.
- A **2020 feasibility study** estimated that the project would submerge around **2,950 hectares** of land, affecting **17 villages** and about **5,500 people** across Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand.
- Himachal Pradesh argued that it should not invest heavily when the water benefits would largely go to downstream states such as **Delhi, Haryana and Rajasthan**.

## How Was A Solution Reached?

- In **June 2026**, the Centre announced that it would bear **90% of the project cost**.
- The six states will share the remaining **10%**.
- Himachal Pradesh's share of the project expense will be borne by:
    
    - Delhi: **75%**
    - Rajasthan: **25%**
- Delhi and Rajasthan will bear Himachal Pradesh's expense because they will receive water supply.
- Himachal Pradesh does not require power supply at present and will receive money for providing power to neighbouring states.

### Storage Capacity And Water Distribution

- The project will have a storage capacity of **1,786 million cubic metres (MCM)** but will actually store **1,324 MCM**.
- Water distribution under the joint agreements:
    
    - Haryana: **633 MCM**
    - Uttar Pradesh: **411 MCM**
    - Rajasthan: **124 MCM**
    - Delhi: **80 MCM**
    - Uttarakhand: **34 MCM** for drinking and industrial purposes
    - Himachal Pradesh: **MCM water**, with no figure specified in the statement
