- **Punjab is facing a power shortage**, triggering a **war of words between the state government and the Centre**.
- The **state government** has primarily blamed **disruption in coal supplies**, while the **Centre** has maintained that **adequate coal has been made available** to Punjab’s power plants.
- The situation has worsened because of **high demand during the paddy season** and **lingering summer heat**.
- **Generation from state-owned thermal plants** was lower than expected during the **peak summer months**.

## How Serious Is The Current Power Shortage?

- Between **June and August**, Punjab’s two state-owned thermal plants - **Guru Gobind Singh Super Thermal Power Plant (GGSSTP), Ropar**, and **Guru Hargobind Thermal Power Plant (GHTP), Lehra Mohabbat** - generated **much less power than their available capacity**.
- In **early September**, several units were **shut**, while others operated at **reduced load**.
- Punjab’s private **Independent Power Producers (IPPs)** - **Nabha Power Limited, Rajpura**, and **Talwandi Sabo Power Limited** - performed relatively better until August, but their coal stocks later declined sharply.
- By **September 13**, **Nabha Power** had coal for around **seven days**, while **Talwandi Sabo** had coal for around **four days**.
- The shortage has resulted in:
    
    - **Restrictions on industrial supply**
    - **Load-shedding in some areas**
    - **Reduced power availability for agriculture**
- Demand has touched **17,000 megawatts (MW)**, while Punjab’s total thermal generation capacity is around **5,680 MW**.

## Why Did The State’S Plants Generate Less Power?

- **Coal availability is only one of several factors** behind lower generation.
- Coal allocation is generally made when a thermal plant is set up, with the **Union Ministry of Coal** allotting coal from India’s coalfields.
- Punjab’s state-owned plants receive coal from the **Pachhwara mine in Jharkhand**.
- Until **2022**, when the Pachhwara mine was not operational:
    
    - The two private plants received **imported coal**
    - State plants used coal allotted by **Coal India Limited**
- After supplies began from Pachhwara, coal allotted for Punjab’s state-owned thermal plants was **diverted to private thermal plants**.
- Plants are advised to maintain coal stocks for **21 days**, but stocks fell short because of **high peak demand**.
- This time, **heavy rains** affected operations at the Pachhwara mine:
    
    - Parts of the mine were filled with water
    - Mining and transportation were affected
    - The government could not fully draw coal because **loaders and unloaders were unavailable**
- In Punjab, **contractual employees at state thermal plants remained on strike for about a month**, affecting operations.
- Despite these problems, coal stocks at the two state-owned plants were still **relatively comfortable by early to mid-September**, but their **utilisation remained low**.

## What About The Private Thermal Plants?

- **Nabha Power Limited** and **Talwandi Sabo Power Limited** together account for around **3,380 MW** of generation capacity.
- Both maintained relatively good generation through **August**, but their coal stocks later **declined rapidly**.
- The **Central Electricity Authority** prescribes coal-stock norms for thermal plants.
- For plants located more than **1,500 km** from their coal source, the prescribed coal stock is **three weeks**.
- The private plants therefore need a **sizeable coal buffer** before the peak-demand period.
- In **2014**, the **Punjab State Electricity Regulatory Commission** constituted a **Standing Committee** to address issues related to the Nabha project, including **alternate or imported coal**.
- The committee comprises the **Secretary, Power**, **CMD of Punjab State Power Corporation Limited (PSPCL)**, and the concerned **plant CEO**.
- The present situation has highlighted the need to monitor **coal stocks and consumption well before demand peaks**.

## What About Punjab’S Hydro Power?

- **Hydropower** could have provided relief during high demand because it is generally a **low-cost source** and provides flexibility when thermal generation fluctuates.
- However, some hydro capacity has remained **underutilised**.
- In the **Upper Bari Doab Canal (UBDC) system**, a problem in its feeding canal in **August 2025** affected the plant.
- The plant has continued to operate at around **half its capacity**.
- Lower hydro generation has added pressure on thermal plants and increased the need for **power purchases**.

## What Has The Impact Been Like?

- The impact has affected all three major consumer categories - **industry, households and agriculture**.
- **Industry** has faced supply restrictions, affecting production and increasing costs.
- **Consumers** have experienced unscheduled power cuts.
- **Agriculture feeders** have faced restrictions when paddy requires its final rounds of irrigation.
- For **PSPCL**, lower generation from its own plants has increased dependence on **short-term power purchases**.
- Market power has cost around **Rs 6 per unit**, compared with an estimated variable cost of around **Rs 3.85 per unit** for PSPCL’s own generation.
- This has imposed an additional burden of around **Rs 389 crore over three months**.

## What Has The Chief Minister Said?

- **Bhagwant Mann** denied the extent of the problem and said the **coal shortage at thermal plants is easing**.
- He said flooding at coal mines in Jharkhand had caused a **temporary shortage**.
- Mann said he had spoken with **Jharkhand Chief Minister Hemant Soren** and would visit **Ranchi within two to four days**.
- According to Mann, coal from the **Pachhwara coal mine** could earlier be used only by Punjab’s **state thermal plants**, not by its two private plants.
- **Coal India** has agreed, following the Punjab government’s request, to allow the coal’s **private use** as well.
- Mann said that **Hemant Soren** had also granted the required **local approval from the Jharkhand government**.
