- **Indian Railways carries over 20 million passengers every day** and is among the world’s largest freight transportation systems.
- **Freight revenue accounts for over 65% of the Railways’ total earnings** and subsidises passenger travel.
- Expansion of the **National Highways network by over 60% in the past decade** has intensified competition for Railways in increasing its share of overall freight transport.
- Rail freight is **cheaper** and **environmentally sustainable due to very low emission**, but **network congestion** and **inadequate last-mile connectivity** restrict its potential.
- Railways plans to **quadruple or four-line all seven High Density Networks**, which account for **over 40% of Railways traffic**.
- The commissioning of the **Vaitarna-JNPT section** completed the **1,506-km Western Dedicated Freight Corridor**.
- The **1,337-km Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor** was fully operationalised by the end of **2023**.
- Railways is expediting a **third Dedicated Freight Corridor** connecting **Dankuni, West Bengal, to Surat, Gujarat**.

## High Density Network

- **High Density Networks**, or **high density routes**, are passenger-freight corridors where trains run **beyond capacity in most sections**.
- On the **28-km Karjat-Lonavala section** near Mumbai, an average of **67 trains run each way per day**, against a maximum capacity of **40 trains**.
- Overutilisation causes **congestion**, delays in passenger and freight trains, and reduces overall network efficiency.
- Of the **69,000-plus km** Indian Railways network, the High Density Network covers **11,051 km**, nearly **16%** of the total network.
- Despite their relatively small extent, these corridors are the **backbone of the Railways** because they carry **over 40% of total traffic**.

### Seven High Density Networks

- **Howrah-Delhi:** **1,422 km**
- **Howrah-Mumbai:** **2,039 km**
- **Mumbai-Delhi:** **1,322 km**
- **Delhi-Guwahati:** **1,876 km**
- **Delhi-Chennai:** **2,037 km**
- **Howrah-Chennai:** **1,117 km**
- **Mumbai-Chennai:** **1,238 km**

### Line-Capacity Utilisation

- Railways considers operation at **70-80% of total capacity** necessary to avoid congestion and enable seamless train movement.
- Only **4.6%** of the High Density Network operates below **80% capacity**.
- **18.89%** operates at **80-100%** line-capacity utilisation.
- **32.75%** operates at **100-120%** utilisation.
- **29.53%** operates at **120-150%** utilisation.
- **14.11%** operates at **over 150%** of route capacity.
- Frequent congestion particularly delays freight trains, whose **average speed remains only 25 kmph**.

## Multitracking

- Multitracking is necessary for both **passenger convenience** and **higher freight loading**.
- Railways’ **modal share** in India’s freight transportation is around **27%**.
- Railways carried **1,670 million tonnes of freight in 2025-26**.
- It has set a target of increasing freight loading to **3,000 million tonnes by 2030**.
- High-density routes must be converted into **normal routes** to achieve this objective.

### National Rail Plan

- Without multitracking of High Density Networks, by **2031**:
- **50% of the network** will have line-capacity utilisation of **over 150%**.
- **39% of the network** will operate at **100-150% utilisation**.
- **9% of sections** will operate at **70-100% of capacity**.

### Construction Targets

- In **FY27**, Railways has targeted:
- **500 km of new lines**
- **2,400 km of doubling of lines**
- In **FY26**, the revised targets were:
- **2,600 km of doubling**
- **700 km of new line construction**

### Cabinet-Approved Projects

- The Union Cabinet approved **eight multitracking projects** covering **1,196 km**, worth **Rs 20,804 crore**.
- Multitracking is expected to **decongest sections** and **reduce India’s logistics cost**.
- Of the **11,000 km High Density Corridor**:
- About **2,000 km** is already **four-line**
- Work is underway on **5,000 km** of sanctioned projects
- **Detailed Project Reports** are being prepared for **4,000 km**, for future approval

### Logistics Cost

- Lower logistics cost can result from **minimising transit delays**, reducing **inventory holding requirements**, and cutting **wastage and degradation of goods during transit**.
- Road transport costs approximately **Rs 3.60 per tonne per kilometre**, compared with **Rs 1.60 per tonne per kilometre by rail**.
- India’s logistics cost is currently **12-13%**.
- Shifting more freight to rail could reduce logistics cost to **8-9%**.

## Freight Corridors

- Freight corridors are **special lines for goods trains**.
- In some sections, they run parallel to high-density corridors; in others, they take detours to bypass **passenger-dominated terminals**.
- The Eastern and Western Dedicated Freight Corridors were conceived because the existing **Howrah-Delhi** and **Mumbai-Delhi** trunk routes had become oversaturated.
- Their line-capacity utilisation ranged between **115% and 150%**.

### Eastern And Western Dedicated Freight Corridors

- Both corridors are fully operational and run an average of **420 freight trains per day**.
- The **Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor** runs from **Ludhiana, Punjab, to Sonnagar, Bihar**.
- It primarily caters to **coal and mineral traffic from eastern India**.
- Traffic on the Western corridor mainly consists of **ISO containers**.
- **ISO containers** are standardised steel shipping boxes designed to move goods safely between ships, trains and trucks **without unloading cargo**.
- Western corridor containers originate from **JNPT and Mumbai Port in Maharashtra**, and **Pipavav, Mundra and Kandla ports in Gujarat**.
- They move to Inland Container Depots in north India, mainly at:
- **Tughlakabad, Delhi**
- **Dadri, Uttar Pradesh**
- **Dhandari Kalan, Punjab**
- **Khatuwas, Rajasthan**

### East-West DFC

- Railways is moving ahead with the proposed **1,738-km East-West Dedicated Freight Corridor** from **Dankuni to Surat**.
- Major traffic generators include **coal mines, iron ore, bauxite, manganese, steel plants, ports, fertilisers and FCI godowns**.

### Projects In The Pipeline

- **East Coast Corridor:** From **Kharagpur, West Bengal, to Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh**, parallel to the existing **Kharagpur-Vijayawada coastal railway line**.
- **North-South Corridor:** From **Itarsi, Madhya Pradesh, to Vijayawada via Nagpur**.
- These two projects have **not yet been sanctioned** and may be taken up in the future.
