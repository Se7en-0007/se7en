- The government announced the **merchant discount rate (MDR) regime for UPI transactions** on **September 15**.
- **Person-to-person (P2P) payments** are exempt from MDR **irrespective of the transaction amount**.
- MDR will essentially apply to **payments to merchants**, or **person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions**, of **over Rs 2,000**, at **0.4%**.
- The announcement followed the government’s exemption of **all UPI payments up to Rs 2,000** and **RuPay debit card transactions** from any bank charge.

## Within The Merchant Payment Ecosystem

- The government described MDR as a charge **“within the merchant payment ecosystem”**, not a charge on customers making UPI payments.
- Banks have been advised to ensure that **merchants do not pass MDR charges to customers**.
- UPI application providers are **expressly prohibited** from imposing **platform fees or hidden charges**.

## When Do The Updated MDR Provisions Take Effect?

- The finalised **MDR framework and threshold structure** will take effect from **October 15**.
- The NPCI said this provides **acquiring banks, payment aggregators, fintech applications, and corporate accounting platforms** adequate time to update their **software engines and billing systems**.

## How Much MDR Will Be Charged On Payments?

- **P2P UPI transactions** will remain **completely free**, irrespective of the amount transferred, for both the **sender and receiver**.
- **P2M payments up to Rs 2,000** will remain free.
- **P2M payments above Rs 2,000** will attract **0.4% MDR**.
- For high-value UPI merchant payments exceeding **Rs 75,000**, the **0.4% MDR** will be capped at **Rs 300 per transaction**.
- Payments for **railways, telecommunications, insurance, fuel, utility bills** - including **electricity, water, and piped gas** - **educational transactions**, and **agricultural inputs** will attract a flat **Rs 5 MDR per transaction**, instead of the **0.4% levy**.
- Payments relating to **mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers, and dealers** will attract **0.02% MDR**, capped at **Rs 300 per transaction**.
- **Monthly utility bills, OTT subscriptions, recurring investments**, and similar payments made through automated recurring standing instructions - **UPI Mandates or AutoPay** - will not face MDR charges.

## Will Consumers Have To Pay Any Fee On UPI?

- Ideally, consumers will not pay a fee, as banks have been advised to prevent merchants from passing the additional MDR cost to consumers.
- However, it remains to be seen whether merchants pass the MDR charge to consumers even when the price charged is below the **maximum retail price**.
- According to NPCI, merchants absorb nominal digital processing costs to increase business volume.
- Such costs are considered standard operational overheads, offset by **increased footfall, higher average ticket values, and reduced cash-handling risks**.
- NPCI said the proposed UPI MDR is significantly lower than credit-card fees and applies only above specified transaction thresholds; therefore, shopkeepers have no economic incentive to inflate retail shelf prices.
- Consumers will continue paying the **exact listed price** for goods and services.

## What About Micro Merchants And Small Vendors?

- **Small merchants**, including **street vendors**, receiving up to **Rs 1 lakh per month** through UPI QR codes into their personal bank accounts will not face MDR charges.
- They fall under the special **Person-to-Person-Merchant (P2PM)** category.
- Small merchants do not need to replace or re-register their QR codes under the new MDR framework.
- Banks and payment service providers use a **transaction velocity check** to monitor the **Rs 1-lakh-per-month inflow threshold**.
- Merchants whose aggregate inward UPI payments exceed **Rs 1 lakh per month consecutively for 3 months** are moved to the regular **P2M** category.
- Such P2M merchants are liable for **0.4% MDR on transactions above Rs 2,000**.

## Why Charge MDR On UPI Transactions?

- UPI processes **billions of transactions every month**.
- NPCI argues that MDR will support further investment in **infrastructure resiliency, innovation, cybersecurity, and customer service**.
- NPCI describes UPI as a **home-grown payment system** and says its MDR is much lower than charges associated with **credit cards, debit cards, wallets**, and other payment instruments.
- The charges are intended to remain reasonable and apply only to transactions above **Rs 2,000** so that UPI remains the most affordable mode of accepting payments.
- The government said MDR will effectively apply to only about **4% of merchant transactions**.
- About **96% of merchant transactions** will remain unaffected because they are either below the **Rs 2,000 threshold** or covered by the **zero-MDR framework for small merchants**.
- The framework seeks to protect **individuals, micro-enterprises, and small businesses** while introducing a limited charge on larger merchant transactions.

## How Does UPI MDR Compare To Debit And Credit Card MDRs?

- Traditional card-based transaction fees typically range from **1.5% to 2.5% per transaction**.
- **Debit card MDRs** are capped at up to **0.90%**.
- The baseline **UPI MDR** is **0.4% on transactions above Rs 2,000**.
- For high-value purchases, UPI MDR is capped at **Rs 300**.
