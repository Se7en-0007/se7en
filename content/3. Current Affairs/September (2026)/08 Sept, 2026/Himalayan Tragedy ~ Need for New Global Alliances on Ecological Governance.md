## The Scale Of The Disaster

- The Himalayas have unleashed a series of **flash floods** - in **Kedarnath, Chamoli, and Sikkim** on the India side, and now in **Rasuwa** on the Nepal side.
- As of **September 7**, as many as **1,356 people** have been confirmed dead in Nepal, while **4,994 remain missing**.
- Scientific terms like **"hazard cascades"** and **GLOF (glacial lake outburst flood)**, or dramatic epithets like **"Himalayan tsunami"**, now sit alongside older, forgotten terms like **bhisyari** (rooted in oral histories of mountain flash floods) and **mahapralaya** (the Hindu mythology on apocalypse).

## Mountains Have Their Own Alphabets And Memories

- Society has become accustomed to translating catastrophe into the **language of accounting** - numbers on losses and compensations.
- Local communities have long carried knowledge that **narrow valleys should never have been crowded with permanent settlements**, and that **slopes should not have been cut haphazardly** for infrastructure.
- Such wisdom surfaced during the **pandemic lockdowns**, and the killer waves of the **Lhende Khola** recall mythologies of the **Ganga descending directly from heaven**.

## Language Of Global Climate Compensation

- As Nepal grapples with the disaster, its leaders increasingly speak the language of **global climate compensation**.
- **Prime Minister Balen Shah** is set to address the **United Nations General Assembly** later this month.
- **Popular intellectuals, activists, and Gen Z icons** of last year's youth revolution are publicly demanding that **Nepal's losses be recognised within the larger architecture of global climate injustice**.
- The political moment is difficult: **multilateral governance** is being pushed aside by **unilaterally minded leaders**, and even on issues of **genocide and war**, the global order appears "taken hostage," making it impossible to translate **moral consensus into collective action**.
- **Euro-America** has not acted on global humanitarianism even when ardently demanded by its own youth.
- On global warming, the **Paris Agreement** appears ineffective, as news of **global temperature rise exceeding the 1.5 degrees Celsius target** has failed to make headlines even while mainstream global media streams apocalyptic reels from Nepal.

## Reaching Out To Polar Communities

- The question raised: can the **Global South, especially South Asia**, still hope to reshape the balance between **Euro-America and the rest of the world**?
- The risks are no longer about **territorial or civilisational power games**; what is at stake is the **evolutionary geographies of ecology**, where cycles of **water, ice, and temperature** are creating **new frontiers and generating new alliances**.
- The **nation-state model**, anchored in the **Westphalian imagination of sovereignty** and reproduced through **New World nationalisms**, was contested earlier by **postcolonial countries** but is now seen "riding into Global South psyches."
- The Himalayan tsunami must be a **wake-up call** to bring **South Asia together as a post-nation-state and imperial entity**, placing **ecology at the centre of diplomacy**.
- The Himalayan region should build solidarities not only with **plains and oceanic shores downstream** but also with scientists, policymakers, and dreamers at the **North and South Poles**.
- The **Arctic, Antarctica, and the Himalayas** are distant ecological worlds, brought together by the existential challenge of **global warming**.
- The Himalayas must assert their own location as the **"Third Pole of Earth"** - not as rhetoric but as a **political and scientific proposition**, enabling a **polar solidarity** connecting mountain communities, researchers, and climate institutions around a shared agenda of **ice, water, adaptation, and survival**.

## Partisanship At Home

- The trauma of the **2015 earthquake**, which killed **8,000 people**, still lingers for Nepal.
- That earthquake produced a **rare national unity**, bringing rival political forces to negotiate a **constitutional settlement** and complete the **decade-long transition** following the **Maoist insurgency**.
- The promised **"peace dividend"** never became the economic take-off many hoped for; an additional decade of **stagnation** brewed **profound generational disillusionment**, exploding into the **Gen Z uprising of last September**.
- The **2015 rhetoric of rescue, relief and reconstruction** had brought of age a generation that gained a foothold in **Euro-American aid institutions and networks**, using them to mobilise resources and bargain entry into **national politics**.
- The **politics of disaster relief** in Nepal is inseparable from the **politics of legitimacy**.
- The **Finance Minister** rattles off numbers about the **Prime Minister's Relief Fund**, while the **Home Minister** inserts himself into rescue and relief distribution, scoring **social media brownie points** - political parties performing the **"anti-politics of disaster aid."**
- Criticisms have surfaced about **delays in rescue operations**; desperate social-media appeals from people searching for loved ones trapped beneath collapsed buildings or flood-struck tunnels have at times been met with **inhuman trolling and abuse on Facebook and X**.
- The **Prime Minister** himself had to publicly call for an **end to such cruelty**.
- Nepal is described as a **"nation of many revolutions"** - the **Maoist revolution**, the **constitutional revolution of post-war state-building**, and the **Gen Z revolution** - each promising a new beginning, yet each inheriting **unresolved failures** from the previous one, keeping the country in an **unresolved, unending transition**.

## A Different Kind Of Ultimatum

- The floods present a different **ultimatum**: rebuilding cannot mean simply reconstructing infrastructure; it must mean a **new imagination of earth and ecology**, and a **new way of being**.
- This must translate into an **implementable set of actions** that brings a **meaningful conclusion** to the country's protracted **political transition**.
- The question posed: can **South Asian civilisation** reach out to the wounded Himalayas of Nepal and **lead new global alliances around ecological governance**?
- The scale of devastation is too large for **partisan ownership**; South Asia's **organic anatomy** is laid bare by **melting glaciers**, **flash floods in the Chure and Shivalik ranges**, and the **poisoned waters of the rivers on the Gangetic plains**.
- The **"alphabet"** being written by the flash floods in Nepal remains **incomplete** - it can be read merely as a **record of disaster**, or as a **warning and a calling**.
- The Himalayas suggest that the **old political map is no longer sufficient**, and that communities cast out to **opposite poles** may ironically be the **closest of kin in survival**.
