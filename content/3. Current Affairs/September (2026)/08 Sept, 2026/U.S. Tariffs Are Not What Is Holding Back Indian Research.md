- **Tariff wars with the United States have ebbed and flowed for years.** After months of escalation, **Washington and New Delhi stepped back**: the penalty tied to India’s purchases of Russian oil was removed, the reciprocal tariff was cut, and **medicines and most electronics remained exempt**.
- The concern that high American tariffs will **choke research** in affected industries and hinder India’s move into **higher-value production** rests on a **mistaken assumption**: sectors exposed to U.S. tariffs are not the sectors where India conducts most of its research.

## The Reality

- The **2019 loss of duty-free access** and the **2025 tariff hit** affected sectors such as **organic chemicals, plastics, base metals, machinery, auto components, and leather**.
- These industries were already **research-thin**:
    
    - **Indian metals firms** spend about **0.4% of sales on R&D**, against a global average of nearly **1.6%**.
    - **Auto and parts makers** spend a little over **2%**, versus a global average of **5%**.
    - **Electrical equipment firms** spend less than **2%**, against the same **5% global benchmark**.
- **The sectors most exposed to trade shocks and the sectors that invest in research barely overlap.**
- India’s research effort is concentrated in **pharmaceuticals and automobiles**, while almost everything else does little research. Therefore, **a tariff on chemicals or steel cannot cut research spending that was never there.**
- **Patenting and research spending** in the exposed sectors show **no clear break during the tariff years** attributable to duties. These sectors had followed a **low-research path well before 2018** and remained on it.
- Tariffs are a **symptom of a trading relationship under strain**, not the principal reason for weak Indian innovation.
- Research is a **fixed and risky investment** that pays off only when a product sells at scale. When export markets shrink, firms can either:
    
    - Retreat to **cheaper, undifferentiated goods**, or
    - Invest in products that a tariff cannot easily replace.
- **Indian industry has chosen the first path for decades**, and tariffs do not change that habit.

## Where Tariffs Do Matter

- Tariffs matter in a **narrow** part of India’s research base.
- Of the two sectors accounting for most Indian research:
    
    - **Pharmaceuticals** secured an exemption in the February deal.
    - **Automobiles** did not.
- The **25% U.S. duty on auto parts remains**, while metal tariffs increase input costs for **downstream engineering and component firms**.
- Thus, the live risk to India’s research base runs through **autos**, not the broader manufacturing economy.

## The Real Problem

- India spends a **very small percentage of its output on research**, well below countries it seeks to compete with.
- The **private industry share** of research spending is also far below global leaders.
- What industry spends mostly goes towards **routine development and testing**, rather than research that creates **new products**.
- By one comparison, **Nvidia alone spends nearly as much on research as all Indian industry combined.**

## Research, Development And Innovation Scheme

- The government’s **₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation scheme**, launched late last year, offers **long-tenure, low-cost capital** to firms in sunrise and strategic areas:
    
    - **Artificial intelligence**
    - **Semiconductors**
    - **Quantum technology**
    - **Biotechnology**
- Its ambition is **overdue**, but its funds are directed at the **frontier**.
- The industries most exposed to trade shocks, and most in need of moving up the value chain, are **older sectors** for which the scheme is not designed.
- A **chemicals or auto-parts maker** that has never run a research programme will not begin one merely because a loan is aimed at **deep tech**.

## The Opportunity The Tariff Episode Offers

- A trade shock shows an exposed industry that its products are **vulnerable**.
- The useful response is to shift firms towards **differentiated products that a tariff cannot easily undercut**, requiring investment in research.
- Incentives can be:
    
    - Weighted towards **core research** rather than routine testing.
    - Directed at **exposed sectors** instead of being spread thin.
- The higher costs caused by metal tariffs for downstream engineering firms can be offset for firms that **maintain research spending**.
- **Pharmaceuticals and autos** deserve protection in further negotiations because tariffs in these sectors can affect innovation.
- Sheltering exposed industries without demanding anything in return preserves **low-value production**.
- Support should be tied to **research effort**, so relief rebuilds capability rather than freezing the status quo.

## Time To Measure Research Better

- India’s official research figures arrive **years late** and **undercount private spending**.
- Without knowing where research is going, a country cannot steer it.
- A faster, **firm-level record** linking research spending to exports would allow policymakers to act before damage appears.

## The Tariff Truce Gives India Time

- **Tariffs were never the main factor holding back Indian research.**
- The central task is to address an economy in which **exposed industries do too little research**, while research strength is concentrated in a few sectors that may be vulnerable to the next trade shock.
