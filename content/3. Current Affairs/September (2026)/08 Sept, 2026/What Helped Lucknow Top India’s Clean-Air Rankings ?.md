- **Lucknow topped the Swachh Vayu Survekshan-2026** among cities with a population of **more than 10 lakh**.
- It scored **198 points**, narrowly ahead of **Indore (197)** and **Jabalpur (195)**.
- The ranking reflects a **multi-pronged approach** targeting **road dust, vehicle emissions, open waste burning and waste management**.
- Key interventions include **electric vehicles** for public transport and garbage collection, **mechanised sweeping machines**, **end-to-end road paving**, **scientific waste processing**, **Miyawaki plantations** and **vertical gardens**.

## **From Garbage Collection To Electric Mobility**

- Around **1,100 electric vehicles** are used for **door-to-door waste collection**, ensuring regular lifting of garbage while reducing emissions from the municipal fleet.
- Around **90 per cent** of door-to-door garbage collection vehicles are now **electric**.
- The remaining garbage collection vehicles are expected to be replaced within the next **six months**.
- Lucknow has **140 electric buses** currently operating as part of its electric public transport fleet.
- To support the transition, the city has established **12 charging stations** with **916 charging points**.

## **A Direct Fight Against Road Dust**

- The Municipal Corporation has deployed **97 electric mechanised sweeping machines** for road cleaning.
- About **559.85 km of roads** have been paved **end-to-end**.
- Paving reduces exposed surfaces and helps prevent dust from being thrown into the air by passing traffic.
- The combination of **paving** and **regular mechanised sweeping** has been important in controlling **re-suspended road dust**.

## **Stopping Garbage From Becoming Smoke**

- The administration has sought to address pollution generated by the **burning of municipal waste**.
- Lucknow has arrangements for **100% scientific processing of municipal waste**.
- The waste-processing capacity is **2,550 tonnes per day**.
- Preventing **open waste burning** is a key component of the effort.
- The Municipal Corporation seeks to ensure that garbage collected from homes is not burnt in **open plots** or by the **roadside**, which releases **smoke and fine particles** into the air.

## **Expanding Green Cover In A Growing City**

- As Lucknow grows, creating new green spaces within a **densely built urban environment** is becoming increasingly difficult.
- The clean-air strategy includes **Miyawaki plantations** and **vertical gardens**.
- These interventions add greenery where conventional **large-scale plantations** may not always be possible.
- The objective is not only to improve the city’s appearance but also to expand its **green cover**.

## **The Clean-Air Battle Reaches The Mohalla**

- Lucknow’s efforts have moved beyond city-level plans to the **ward level**.
- **Ward-70 in Zone-4** was ranked **No. 1 nationally** among urban wards with a population of **more than 30,000**.
- At this level, important measures included:
- **Preventing garbage burning**
- **Keeping roads and local stretches free of dust**
- **Ensuring that waste does not pile up**

## **The Challenge Now**

- The challenge is to sustain these measures and translate the **No. 1 ranking** into a long-term reduction in **particulate pollution** as the city expands.
- Lucknow has not yet achieved the **PM 10 annual ambient air quality standard of 60 ug/m3**, prescribed under national standards.
- Since the National Clean Air Programme was initiated in **2019**, annual **PM 10 concentrations** have declined from **250 ug/m3 in 2017-18** to **137 ug/m3**.
- This represents a **45.2% reduction** in annual PM 10 concentrations.
