- **On the day ISRO launched its first geosynchronous imaging satellite, EOS-05, using a temperamental GSLV**, its employee organisations sought **clarity on its long-term plans**.
- Since the **Indian Space Policy of April 2023**, the message has been that ISRO would, at some unspecified future point, **stop making commercial satellites and launch rockets**.
- **Inspired by NASA**, ISRO is expected to focus on **ambitious exploratory missions** and **moonshots**.

## Letter Co-Signed By Nine Employee Associations

- The letter to the Chairman seeks clarity on:
    
    - **Staff strength**
    - **Recruitment**
    - **Outsourcing of core functions**
- ISRO has said it will **“not be privatised or reduced”**.
- **IN-SPACe**, created to facilitate private participation, has said ISRO will **“not be diminished”**, but that **industry’s role must grow**.
- Both remain silent on the central concern: **potential job losses**.

## Founding Principles

- ISRO faces an underlying tension: whether its vision for India’s space sector aligns with its founding principles of:
    
    - **Not being enamoured of space races**
    - Being **laser-focused on space technology as a tool for social development**
- The alternative vision is one centred on the **image of power** - India as one of a handful of **space-faring countries**, supported by a mushrooming of **“space startups”**.

## NASA And ISRO

- NASA’s budget is **16 times that of the Department of Space**: **$24.4 billion**, compared with **₹13,705 crore**.
- NASA has scaled back since the 1960s:
    
    - Its share of American GDP fell from **0.7% in 1966** to **0.1% now**.
    - Its civil-servant workforce fell from **36,000 at Apollo’s peak** to about **14,000 today**.
- Unlike NASA, which initially designed and made **every nut and bolt**, ISRO has always maintained a manufacturing relationship with the private sector, including **Walchandnagar** and **L&T**.

## New Entrants With Fleeting Foreign Capital

- The present buzz is not about established companies building on experience, but about **new entrants with fleeting foreign capital**.
- These entrants are most interested in **satellite data as a service**.
- No sector can remain static, and survival can require **significant changes to how it operates**.
- There has been **no serious reckoning with the might of China’s space programme**.

## India’s Space Sector In 2035

- If India’s space sector by **2035** becomes:
    
    - A source of **export earnings**
    - A nucleus of **value-added services**
    - A sector that generates **jobs** and absorbs **skilled labour**
    
    then hard choices taken today may be worthwhile.
    
- But joining a space bandwagon as fuel for the **science fiction fantasy of billionaires**, **colonial notions of conquest**, or a misplaced idea of belonging to an **“exclusive” club** would be **foolhardy**.
- ISRO must have a **transparent policy** that clearly spells out its direction.
