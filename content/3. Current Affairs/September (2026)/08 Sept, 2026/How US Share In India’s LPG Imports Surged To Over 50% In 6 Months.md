- **Stifled liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) supply from West Asia**, owing to the regional conflict, caused the **US share in India’s LPG imports to rise above 50%** in the six months after the conflict began, from **less than 10%** in the preceding six months.
- The **West Asia war** began when the **US and Israel struck Iran in late February**. It disrupted India’s traditionally Gulf-dominated LPG import basket, led by the **UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, and Saudi Arabia**.
- In **March-August**, India’s total LPG imports fell by **over 40% sequentially**, while imports from the **US nearly quadrupled**, as India sought alternative supplies unaffected by geopolitical conflict.

## Strait Of Hormuz

- The war brought about an **effective halt in vessel movement through the Strait of Hormuz**, a critical chokepoint that carries **one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG) flows**.
- The **Strait of Hormuz** is a narrow waterway linking the **Persian Gulf** with the **Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea** and is the primary route for evacuating energy supplies from the broader Gulf region.
- India’s **crude oil and LNG supplies** were also affected, but **LPG supplies were hit the most**.
- Regularly, India sourced around **40% of crude oil imports**, **60% of LNG imports**, and **90% of LPG imports** from West Asia through the Strait of Hormuz.
- India’s import dependence is **over 88% for oil**, **60% for LPG**, and **about 50% for natural gas**.
- Thus, the Strait of Hormuz effectively handled supplies equivalent to around **54% of India’s LPG consumption**.

## US Dominates As West Asia Falters

- In the **six months to August**, India’s overall LPG imports declined **43.1%** from the previous six months, **September-February**, to **7.14 million tonnes**.
- LPG supplies from the **US surged 281.1%** to **3.78 million tonnes**, accounting for **53%** of India’s LPG imports during **March-August**.
- During **September-February**, the US supplied about **993,000 tonnes**, constituting only **7.9%** of India’s LPG imports.
- The **UAE**, India’s biggest LPG source before the war, saw its share fall to **13.4%** in the six months to August, from **37.8%** in September-February.
- LPG imports from the UAE crashed **79.8%** to about **958,000 tonnes**.
- LPG imports from **Qatar** fell **84.7%** to about **405,000 tonnes** in March-August.
- Qatar’s share in India’s LPG import basket declined to **5.7%**, from **21.1%** in the preceding six months.
- **Kuwait’s** share contracted to **4.9%** in March-August, from **15.1%** in September-February.
- LPG import volumes from Kuwait declined **81.7%** to about **346,300 tonnes**.
- LPG import volumes from **Saudi Arabia** declined **76.1% sequentially** to about **423,700 tonnes** in the six months to August.
- Saudi Arabia’s share in India’s LPG import basket shrank to **5.9%**, from **14.1%** in September-February.
- The rise in US LPG imports was driven particularly by **constrained Middle East supply**. The US, a major global LPG supplier, became a **readily available option** for India in the constrained supply environment, similar to **Russian oil barrels flocking to India** amid West Asia supply disruption.

## Hormuz Factor Remains Critical

- American LPG flows to India are expected to remain strong until **West Asian supply normalises**, although there is **no clarity** on when that will occur.
- **Washington was already the world’s largest LPG exporter before the crisis**, and **US propane was cheaper than Asian supplies before the war**.
- After shortages emerged and nearby options were unavailable, India was willing to pay more to bring LPG from farther away because **keeping cooking gas available was the priority**.
- The US share could decline if West Asian supplies recover meaningfully, but security around the **Strait of Hormuz remains the big uncertainty**.
- For **2027 contracts**, the government has reportedly asked **Indian Oil, Bharat Petroleum, and Hindustan Petroleum** to secure at least **15% of next year’s LPG imports through annual US deals**.
- Nothing has been finalised, but this indicates an intention to build **diversification** into India’s purchasing plans.
- Higher purchases of American energy could also support negotiations with Washington on the **US-India trade deal**.

## Supply Management Measures

- The disruption in LPG supplies from West Asia forced India to **ration LPG supplies to industrial and commercial consumers** to prioritise the **crores of households** dependent on cooking gas.
- India has **over 33 crore households with LPG connections**.
- As a demand-management measure, the **minimum gap between household LPG refill bookings was increased**.
- Domestic refineries have been **maximising domestic LPG production** to partly offset lost imports, while refiners have sought LPG cargoes from **alternative geographies**.
- Some emergency measures have been withdrawn as domestic production rose and alternative imports, predominantly from the US, increased.
- However, the **supply gap remains too wide to be fully bridged**.

## India’s LPG Consumption

- India’s annual LPG consumption was **a little over 33 million tonnes**, or about **90,000 tonnes per day**, with an **import dependency of 60%**.
- Following disruption of flows through the Strait of Hormuz, LPG consumption declined to about **80,000 tonnes per day**, according to estimates shared by the **Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas in May**.
