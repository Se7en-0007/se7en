- **A fall in unemployment may indicate distress-driven entry into low-productivity jobs rather than genuine employment creation.**
- The government has found a way to measure the **statistical elephant of unemployment**, but the measurement may be **incomplete**, like blind men mistaking parts of a pachyderm for the whole truth.

## Current Weekly Status

- The **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** approach determines a person’s activity status based on the **preceding seven days**.
- Its challenge is to assess not only whether people are employed, but also:
    
    - The **nature of their jobs**
    - The **frequency and regularity of payments**
    - The **overall quality of employment**
- In an economy where a **vast majority** are involved in **informal work**, employment cannot be analysed through a **headline rate** of a high-frequency indicator alone.

## Database And Labour Market

- Transforming quarterly or yearly unemployment data into a **monthly indicator** is **economically desirable**.
- However, its credibility is questionable because the database seldom accounts for **circular migration**.
- Efforts have been made to strengthen databases using:
    
    - **EPFO** payroll data
    - **ESIC** payroll data
    - **GST-based enterprise information**
    - **Income tax records**
    - **Corporate payroll data**
    - **Gig economy employment data**
    - **Rural wage indicators**
- These sources remain **fragmented**.
- Unlike mature labour markets with **payroll surveys, unemployment insurance records, formal contracts, and extensive administrative databases**, India’s large **informal employment market** is difficult to track.
- Most advanced nations use a **Labour Force Survey** based on the **International Labour Organization (ILO)** definition.
- The **U.S., Japan, EU, and U.K.** possess decades of household-survey data covering **full-time versus part-time jobs, hourly wages, job duration, labour mobility, and unemployment spells**.

## A Leading Indicator

- The latest **Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)** recorded an unemployment rate of **5.1%** for people aged **15 years and above** in **July**, a **four-month low**.
- The review period coincided with the **peak kharif season**, when demand for agricultural labour increases for:
    
    - **Land preparation**
    - **Transplanting**
    - **Allied activities**
- **Construction, agriculture, small trade, logistics, and local services** experience seasonal fluctuations.
- Therefore, July’s decline is likely to reflect higher hiring in these segments rather than a **broad-based expansion in formal-sector jobs**.
- **Monthly unemployment** should at best be a **leading indicator**, not a comprehensive measure of **labour-market health**.

## Informal Sector And Seasonal Movement

- Various reports suggest that **90% of the population** is involved in the **informal sector**.
- Agricultural employment remains **seasonal**, wage payments are **negotiated informally**, and **underemployment/disguised employment** is widespread.
- Available data has limited granularity and remains sketchy on:
    
    - **Wage growth**
    - **Hours worked**
    - **Job quality**
    - **Occupational shifts**
    - **Sector-wise employment trends**
- Consequently, monthly unemployment data reflects **seasonal movements** rather than **structural improvement**.
- A fall in unemployment may result from **distress-driven entry into low-productivity jobs**, not genuine employment creation.

## Rural Areas And Labour Force Participation

- The overall decline in unemployment was driven by **rural areas**, where unemployment fell from **5% to 4.5%**.
- This should not be read as a major improvement in the job market.
- Independent labour studies estimate that **30 to 35 million seasonal labourers** move across India annually, forming the **invisible backbone of urban construction and infrastructure**.
- July data showed an increase in the **labour force participation rate**, a positive **supply-side signal**.
- However, **stronger investment and job creation** must precede sustainable employment recovery.
- The challenge is to convert participation into **stable, high-productivity jobs**.

## Revamped PLFS

- The **revamped PLFS** is a significant improvement over earlier **low-frequency labour statistics**.
- Its success depends on whether it gauges the **reality of the job market**, rather than merely measuring it more frequently.
- A credible **labour-market barometer** is needed to capture the livelihoods of **millions of workers**.
