- **Justice Sandeep Mehta**, a Supreme Court judge who previously served in the Rajasthan High Court, reportedly wrote **three letters on August 2, 10 and 17** to the **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** seeking the **immediate transfer** of **Justice Sanjeev Prakash Sharma**, the Acting Chief Justice of the Rajasthan High Court.
- The letters reportedly referred to complaints concerning Justice Sharma's functioning, including:
    
    - **Victimisation of judges**
    - **Maladministration**
    - Improper **"shifting of cases"** to his Bench
    - **Nepotism** in appointments to the **Permanent Lok Adalat**
    - Intimidation of colleagues through threats of **retributive measures**, including transfers, based on his claimed proximity to the CJI
    - Alleged favouritism towards a chosen few lawyers and misuse of power as the **"master of the roster"**
- Justice Mehta's last letter reportedly raised concern over the CJI's alleged inaction, particularly because Justice Sharma was due to retire on **September 26**.
- There has been **no official denial** of the reported contents of the letters. Justice Sharma has publicly denied the accusations as **baseless**.
- The Rajasthan High Court had functioned under an Acting Chief Justice for about **11 months**, which is described as not being a good institutional gesture.
- Justice Sharma was transferred to the **Patna High Court in 2022**. His request for repatriation to the Rajasthan High Court was rejected by the Collegium in **2023**, which instead proposed his transfer to the **Punjab and Haryana High Court**.
- Reports indicate that Justice Mehta questioned how Justice Sharma was retransferred to the Rajasthan High Court in **2025** and then allowed to serve as Acting Chief Justice with **wide-ranging administrative and judicial powers**.
- Lawyers at the Rajasthan High Court staged a **sit-in protest** on the High Court premises, after which Justice Sharma opted out of hearing cases.
- On **August 31**, the Supreme Court Collegium recommended **Justice Sanjay Agrawal** as the new Chief Justice of the Rajasthan High Court, and he was subsequently appointed.
- The episode is distinctive because a **sitting Supreme Court judge** reportedly alerted the CJI about the conduct of an Acting Chief Justice of a High Court, supported by **verifiable material**.
- For several weeks, no cogent action was reportedly initiated by the CJI. The CJI reportedly said that allegations could not be treated as findings and **"must be dealt with strictly through the established institutional mechanism"**.
- Given Justice Sharma's impending retirement, early action on Justice Mehta's letters could perhaps have averted subsequent disturbing events.
- **Transparency International's 2002 report**, based on a household survey, identified the judiciary as the **second-most corrupt institution** in certain South Asian countries, including India.
- However, the situation is described as more complex and multi-dimensional, and the integrity and public image of many fine judges should not be tarnished because of aberrations elsewhere, whether on or off the Bench.

## Institutional Responsibility, Structural Issues

- Repeated episodes of judicial corruption reflect fundamental problems in the functioning of India's higher judiciary.
- **Lack of criteria** in judicial appointments, along with unavoidable collaboration between the **Collegium** and the **Executive at the Centre**, makes appointments **highly partisan, opaque and often arbitrary**.
- The existing mechanism and processes are considered incapable of identifying the **cleanest and most meritorious candidates**.
- Corruption in the judiciary is inseparable from the present method of selecting candidates for judicial office.
- A system of inviting applications, as in **Canada**, or an **independent body for judicial selection**, as in the **United Kingdom**, remains unthinkable in India.
- The absence of an effective mechanism for dealing with corruption in the higher judiciary has perpetuated the menace.
- The **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968** is inadequate and has, for practical purposes, become **defunct**.
- In **K. Veeraswami vs Union of India (1991)**, the Supreme Court held that registering a **First Information Report** against a higher judiciary judge requires the CJI's permission as a necessary precondition.
- This creates a practical obstacle: police may not approach the CJI without **clinching evidence**, but cannot obtain such evidence without investigating.
- The **Judicial Standards and Accountability Bill** was passed by the Lok Sabha under the United Progressive Alliance government in **2012**, but lapsed when the **15th Lok Sabha** was dissolved in **2014**. A later attempt to revive it with changes remained a **non-starter**.
- Impeachment under **Articles 124(4) and 217(1)(b)** of the Constitution has proved to be a failure.
- The Supreme Court's **in-house mechanism**, adopted in **1999**, has not proved fault-free or effective.

## Earlier Crises, Different Responses

- The lack of a permanent systemic solution should have been an additional reason for the CJI to act **expeditiously and diligently**, using all authority available to take remedial measures. The situation required **judicial statesmanship**.
- In **1990**, while an inquiry into alleged misconduct by **Justice V. Ramaswami** was underway, then CJI **Sabyasachi Mukharji** advised him to take leave and stay away from judicial work.
- After the impeachment motion against Justice Ramaswami failed in Parliament because of the Congress party's **strategic abstention**, he tried to resume judicial work with about **nine months** of tenure remaining.
- Then CJI **Venkatachaliah** refused to allocate any cases to Justice Ramaswami for hearing.
- Last year, after partly burnt currency notes were reportedly found in the outhouse of **Justice Yashwant Varma** at his Delhi residence, then CJI **Sanjiv Khanna** quickly transferred him to the Allahabad High Court, where he was practically not allocated judicial work.
- In **XXX vs Union of India (2025)**, the Supreme Court emphasised the CJI's responsibility regarding **judicial integrity** and allegations of **corrupt practices**.
- Justice Sharma's alleged claim of proximity to the CJI generated undesirable and avoidable speculation.
- Given the nature and immediate verifiability of the allegations, the CJI needed to act **promptly and convincingly** to reduce, if not altogether prevent, further damage to the system and the institution.
