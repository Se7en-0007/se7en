### Raman And The Supreme Court

- On **June 9**, a **Supreme Court Division Bench** stated that it could not remain a **mute spectator** in matters concerning animals, because their **wellbeing is of paramount importance**.
- The observation arose in **Jayakrishna Menon vs. Krishnankutty & Ors**, an **ownership dispute** over **Raman**, Kerala’s tallest elephant, who was being used for **temple activities**.
- Raman’s **temporary custodian** was responsible for his **care and upkeep**, but the elephant continued to be used for temple activities despite a prohibition by the Supreme Court.
- The Supreme Court ordered the **Kerala government** to take custody of Raman, stating that it would fail in its duty towards **“voiceless animals”** if it ignored such **defiance of court orders**.

### Two Different Approaches

- The Court’s observation recognises an animal’s **propensity to suffer** and stresses the need to secure animal wellbeing.
- This approach sharply contrasts with the ruling on street dogs in **Re: City Hounded By Strays, Kids Pay Price, 2026**.
- In the street-dog matter, a **three-judge Bench** adopted a **much narrower interpretation of the law**, resulting in the removal of a **large number of dogs** from public institutions.
- The judiciary’s differential treatment of **elephants** and **street dogs** raises moral and legal questions:
    
    - Why is one elephant’s wellbeing of **“paramount importance”**, while the welfare of **lakhs of street dogs** is disregarded?
    - Why does the same institution use **contrasting, if not contradictory, approaches** in matters involving similar moral considerations?
    - Is the judiciary best positioned to decide cases that determine animals’ fates?

### Legal Protections And Human Interactions

- The two cases and the animals involved are **not identical** because their **legal protections** and **interactions with humans** vary significantly.
- The **Indian elephant** is a **charismatic animal**, listed as an **Endangered Species** on the **International Union for Conservation of Nature’s Red List**, and explicitly protected under the **Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972**.
- The **street dog** is legally perceived as a **problem that needs to be tackled**.
- The elephant is expected to live in the **wild**, away from human habitation, whereas street dogs share public spaces with humans, requiring people to learn to **coexist in shared spaces**.
- While these differences must be recognised, the key question is whether they are substantial enough to justify **completely different moral and legal considerations**.

### Equal Consideration

- According to moral philosopher **Peter Singer**, the basic principle of equality does not require **equal or identical treatment**; it requires **equal consideration**.
- **Equal consideration** extends to every being that:
    
    - Has **interests**
    - Has some **subjective awareness**
    - Is capable of feeling **pleasure or pain**
- Equal consideration for different beings may result in **different treatment** and **different rights**.
- In the two judgments, it is the **absence of equal consideration** that appears to produce differential treatment.
- Equal consideration in both cases would require assessing the **needs of the animals** and then determining an appropriate **course of action**.

### Anthropocentric Bias

- The **Wildlife Protection Act** applicable to elephants and the **Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960** applicable to street dogs reveal an **anthropocentric bias** in evaluating animals’ worth.
- This bias determines the **nature and level of protections** to which animals are entitled.
- The contrasting Supreme Court approaches expose a gap in **legislative guidance** for ensuring animal wellbeing, despite differences in animals’ legal categorisation.

### Animal Dignity And Legislative Foundation

- The **Swiss Constitution** protects not only animal wellbeing but also **animal dignity**, recognising animals’ **inherent worth**.
- The responsibility lies with **Parliament** to extend protection to every being worthy of **moral consideration**.
- A strong **legislative foundation** is needed to provide moral consideration to **all animals** and create pathways for **ethical coexistence**.
