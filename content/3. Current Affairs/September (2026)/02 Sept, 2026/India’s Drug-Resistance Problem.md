## **Carbapenem Resistance And Patient Survival**

- **A major study of hospitalised patients across India found that infections caused by antibiotic-resistant Gram-negative bacteria are more likely to be fatal and more expensive to treat than infections caused by drug-susceptible strains.**
- **Gram-negative bacteria** can cause infections in the **lungs, urinary tract, wounds and bloodstream**. Major hospital pathogens include:
    
    - **Escherichia coli - E. coli**
    - **Klebsiella pneumoniae**
    - **Acinetobacter baumannii**
    - **Pseudomonas aeruginosa**
- **Carbapenems** are an important group of antibiotics used for serious infections. When bacteria become **carbapenem-resistant**, doctors have **fewer effective medicines** available.
- The study was conducted by the **ICMR antimicrobial resistance surveillance network** across **20 tertiary-care hospitals**, from **April 2022 to April 2025**, covering **1,59,336 hospitalised patients**.
- It compared patients with **carbapenem-resistant bacteria** and those infected with **susceptible strains of the same bacteria**.
- **Mortality was consistently higher** among patients with carbapenem-resistant infections across all four major Gram-negative pathogens.
- The **relative risk of death was 1.16-1.43** across these organisms.
- Resistance should not be treated as an entirely independent cause of death because outcomes are also shaped by:
    
    - **Severity of illness**
    - **Timing of appropriate therapy**
    - **Source control**
    - **Other patient factors**
- However, **carbapenem resistance is an important marker of excess mortality and a critical clinical threat**.

## **More Than 61% Of Gram-Negative Infections**

- **More than 61% of Gram-negative infections** in the study were **carbapenem-resistant**.
- This figure must be interpreted in context because the hospitals were **tertiary-care hospitals** managing **referred and often critically ill patients**.
- Therefore, the resistance rate should **not be considered representative of the general community or all Indian hospitals**.
- Nevertheless, the scale of resistance in this hospital population warns of the importance of:
    
    - **Healthcare-associated transmission**
    - **Invasive devices**
    - **Recent surgery**
    - **Antibiotic pressure**
    - **Gaps in infection prevention**
    - **Delayed or inadequate diagnosis**
- **Antimicrobial resistance - AMR - is not merely a problem of antibiotic overuse.** It results from a complex interaction between:
    
    - **Antibiotic selection pressure**
    - **Infection-control practices**
    - **Transmission within healthcare settings**
    - **Delayed or inadequate diagnosis**

## **Mortality From Bloodstream Infections**

- A bloodstream infection caused by a **carbapenem-resistant Gram-negative organism** is a **clinical emergency**.
- Mortality from resistant bloodstream infections ranged from approximately:
    
    - **39% in resistant E. coli**
    - **51% in resistant A. baumannii**
- Patients can deteriorate because:
    
    - The infection may be **recognised late**
    - **Effective treatment options** are limited
    - Resistance may not be identified **quickly enough**
    - Critically ill patients may have **multiple comorbidities**
    - Patients may require **invasive devices**
- The two key interventions are:
    
    - **Rapid, accurate diagnostics**
    - **Prompt delivery of an appropriate antibiotic** when resistance is suspected or confirmed
- These must operate alongside **infection prevention**, because **preventing a bloodstream infection is always better than trying to rescue a patient after it has occurred**.

## **Treating Resistant Infections With Antibiotics Alone**

- Treating resistant infections with antibiotics alone cost **1.1-2 times more** than treating susceptible infections.
- The cost estimates were **conservative** because they considered only the cost of antibiotics available under the **Jan Aushadhi scheme**.
- They excluded:
    
    - **ICU costs**
    - **Bed and room charges**
    - **Diagnostic investigations**
    - **Procedures**
    - **Supportive care**
    - **Consultation costs**
- Therefore, the real economic burden on **patients, families and the healthcare system** is likely to be **substantially greater**.

## **Ceftazidime-Avibactam**

- **Ceftazidime-avibactam** was used more frequently for resistant **E. coli** and **K. pneumoniae** infections.
- It was associated with **better outcomes in some resistant E. coli and K. pneumoniae infections**, including **bloodstream infections**.
- This should be treated as an **important signal**, not definitive evidence that the drug is superior.
- The study was **observational** and could not fully adjust for:
    
    - **Disease severity**
    - **Timing of appropriate therapy**
    - **Source control**
    - **Resistance mechanisms**
    - **Other factors influencing treatment outcomes**
- The findings reflect **real-world treatment patterns in India** and do not prove that one drug is **universally superior** to another.
- Better **diagnostics** and information on **resistance mechanisms** are needed to guide treatment rather than simply escalating to **newer and broader antibiotics**.

## **Antibiotics Cannot Compensate For Failures In Infection Prevention**

- **More than 85% of bloodstream infections** in the study were classified as **healthcare-associated**.
- Healthcare-associated infections constituted a **very large proportion** of infections caused by these organisms.
- Hospitals must place **infection prevention and control** at the centre of their AMR response, including:
    
    - **Hand hygiene**
    - **Device-associated infection prevention**
    - **Appropriate insertion and early removal of invasive devices**
    - **Environmental cleaning**
    - **Surgical infection prevention**
    - **Surveillance of healthcare-associated infections**
- **Antimicrobial stewardship** is equally important.
- The response to rising resistance cannot simply be to use stronger antibiotics. It must involve:
    
    - **Preventing infections**
    - **Diagnosing them quickly**
    - Using the **narrowest effective antibiotic**
    - Using it for the **shortest appropriate duration**

## **What Needs To Happen Next**

- Priorities must be **integrated rather than pursued in isolation**.
- India should move from **isolate-based AMR surveillance** to **integrated surveillance** that links:
    
    - **Laboratory results**
    - **Patient outcomes**
    - **Mortality**
    - **Treatment**
- Faster and more accurate diagnostics are needed to:
    
    - Distinguish **bacterial infection** from **non-bacterial illness**
    - Identify **resistance early**
- **Antimicrobial stewardship** must become **routine clinical practice**, not an optional hospital programme.
- **Infection prevention** must receive as much attention as antibiotic prescribing, especially because a large share of these infections are **healthcare-associated**.
- India needs **responsible access to effective newer antimicrobials**, coupled with stewardship, so that these drugs remain effective when patients genuinely need them.
- **AMR cannot be solved by discovering another antibiotic every time an old one fails.** The central response is to **prevent infections, diagnose them early and preserve the antibiotics that remain effective**.
