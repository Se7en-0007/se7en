The article argues that the **south's "political retreat"** from its once-influential national role comes amid growing **anxieties over representation and federalism**.

## Historical Context

- The **national movement** was dominated by leaders from **northern, western and eastern India** - **Gandhi, Nehru, Netaji, and Patel** were the tallest leaders.
- Southern leaders like **C. Rajagopalachari** (Madras), **T. Prakasam** (Andhra), and **E.M.S. Namboodiripad (EMS)** (Keralam) never had comparable national presence.
- Despite this, the presence of a **galaxy of leaders from the south** helped the original **Indian National Congress** be seen as an **all-inclusive umbrella party**.

## A North-Dominated Political Landscape

- **Demographics** and the **penchant for a 'national' language** are cited as reasons for northern political domination.
- Both the **Indian National Congress** and the **Jana Sangh** (later **Bharatiya Janata Party/BJP**) have been dominated by **northern leadership**.
- In **nearly eight decades of India's independence**, only **one Prime Minister from the south completed a full term** in office.
- The **Nehru-Gandhi family's domination** of Congress and the **ideological dominance of Hindi** and the **Hindi heartland** in the BJP have overshadowed southern leadership.
- Congress had powerful regional southern leaders: **K. Kamaraj** and **C. Subramaniam** (Tamil Nadu); **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy, Kasu Brahmananda Reddy, Marri Chenna Reddy, Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy** (Andhra Pradesh); **S. Nijalingappa, Devaraj Urs, S.M. Krishna** (Karnataka); **K. Karunakaran** (Keralam).
- The **BJP** has **no south Indian leader of stature** in its leadership ranks; **B.S. Yediyurappa**, who rose within party ranks, has **quickly faded away**.
- Distinguished southern national leaders mostly came from **regional parties**: **M.G. Ramachandran, Jayalalithaa** (Tamil Nadu); **Namboodiripad** (Keralam); **N.T. Rama Rao (NTR)** (Andhra Pradesh); **K. Chandrashekar Rao** (Telangana); **H.D. Deve Gowda, Ramakrishna Hegde, Siddaramaiah** (Karnataka).
- **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, the **first south Indian Prime Minister** (aided by his **Hindi-speaking skills**), was **denied due place** in Congress's pantheon of PMs.

## Southern Leaders and Delimitation

- At the **31st meeting of the Southern Zonal Council**, southern politicians **failed to speak with one voice** on **delimitation**.
- **Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar** made the strongest intervention, demanding **1971 Census data** be used for delimitation for **another 25 years**.
- Shivakumar also demanded the **Lok Sabha's strength be retained at 543 seats** and implementation of **women's reservation**.
- **Keralam CM V.D. Satheesan** (Congress) made only a **tepid mention** of delimitation.
- **Tamil Nadu CM C. Joseph Vijay** softened his earlier strong opposition, only seeking assurance States retain their **present proportional share** of Lok Sabha representation.
- **Telangana CM A. Revanth Reddy** (Congress) **missed the meeting**, deputing the **Deputy Chief Minister** instead.
- Other issues raised concerned **financial devolutions, river water disputes**, and administrative matters, but **delimitation did not heat up the meeting**.
- The five southern Chief Ministers failed to **close ranks** on a common platform.

## Dependence on New Delhi

- Southern States' varying dependence on **New Delhi** has led them to **supplicate for funds** and echo **BJP's ideological obsessions**.
- **N. Chandrababu Naidu** and **Pawan Kalyan** (CM and Deputy CM of Andhra Pradesh) have become **more communal** in articulation than some BJP allies in the **NDA**.
- Naidu's **desperation for financial support from Delhi** has diminished his stature as representative of the **Telugu Desam Party's** once-proud Telugu identity.
- **NTR** once said, **"The Centre is a conceptual myth,"** a powerful federalism statement that prompted PM **Indira Gandhi** to appoint the **Sarkaria Commission** on Centre-State relations in **June 1983**.
- Last year, the **DMK government in Tamil Nadu** appointed a **committee on Centre-State relations chaired by Justice Kurian Joseph**, which **did not receive due attention** in media or among southern leadership - reflecting **abdication of responsibility**.
- In Keralam, **EMS** and **K. Karunakaran** made national impact; in **1968-69**, EMS mobilised **K. Brahmananda Reddy** (Andhra Pradesh CM) and **Mohanlal Sukhadia** (Rajasthan CM) to get the **National Development Council (NDC)** to endorse the **Gadgil Formula** for Plan fund distribution.
- This contrasts with the **submissive approach** of former Keralam CM **Pinarayi Vijayan** (CPI-M) to the Union government.
- Under PM **Narendra Modi**, the **NDC has not met even once**, with **little disquiet** expressed across the political spectrum.

## Shrinking Space

- Southern/peninsular India's political presence is **weak even with the present Lok Sabha seat distribution**.
- Earlier, Congress, the **Janata Party**, and **Janata Parivar coalitions** were **not entirely dismissive** of southern concerns (language, Centre-State relations, fund allocation, railway lines).
- These parties had **consequential presence** in southern States and included **influential regional leaders**, resulting in **less Hindi-heartland-centric policy direction**.
- The entire southern region could become **politically inconsequential** against the strength of just **two-and-a-half to three Hindi heartland States** in the Lok Sabha.
- The **delimitation design pushed by the BJP-led Union government** will **heighten political anxieties**, eventually **overwhelming the present crop of supplicant Chief Ministers**.
