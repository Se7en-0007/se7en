- **Changing the GDP base year** is not only about changing the calculation of **economic growth**. It can also alter the estimated rupee size of the economy, known as **nominal GDP**.
- A better picture of the economy through **new data, improved methods and wider coverage** requires a review of economic estimates.
- Under **international statistical practice**, revisions need not move in one direction: the estimated size of the economy may rise or fall depending on what new data and methods reveal.

## Recent Base Revision Of GDP

- Across the three overlapping years, India’s **nominal GDP** was revised down by about:
    
    - **2.7% in 2022-23**
    - **3.5% in 2023-24**
    - **3.8% in 2024-25**
- Similar rebasing exercises also revised earlier nominal GDP levels in **Nigeria (2014), Indonesia (2014), Brazil (2015), South Africa (2018), Mexico (2019), China (2021), and Spain (2024)**.
- India’s earlier shift from **base year 2004-05 to 2011-12** also changed the estimated size of the economy.
- The important issue is not simply why the number changed, but **what changed in the data or methods that produced the new number**.
- The **February 27, 2026 Press Release** on the **New GDP Series with base year 2022-23** had already identified the methodological improvements and updated data sources behind the nominal-GDP revisions. It also included an **economic activity-wise comparative table** of revisions and their reasons.

## Better Evidence

- Media focus on the headline downward revision has overlooked substantial upward revisions in several sectors.
- A proper assessment requires a **sector-wise picture**, because revisions are far from uniform.
- **Agriculture and allied activities** were revised up by about **3.8%-5.9%**.
- **Financial services and real estate, professional services and ownership of dwellings** were revised up by roughly **7.8%-9.0%** over comparable years.
- **Trade and transport and storage** experienced sharp downward revisions of around **23%-26%**.
- **Trade GVA (Gross Value Added)** was revised downward by **36%**.
- **Road transport** was revised downward by **16.9%**.
- **Hotels and restaurants’ GVA** was revised upward by **5.7%**, mainly because of revised estimates of the **unincorporated sector**.

## The Unincorporated Sector

- In the **2011-12 series**, the unincorporated sector was estimated by moving forward **benchmark estimates** through **proxy indicators**.
- In the new series, **ASUSE (Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises)** and **PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey)** data provide a more direct basis for measuring the unincorporated sector.
- Improved measurement of India’s **unincorporated services sector** is a major driver of the revision in nominal GDP.
- The **World Bank’s April 2026 India Development Update** noted that India’s nominal GDP was revised down by **3%-4% in each of the four years from FY23**, mainly due to a reassessment of the **informal economy**.
- It also noted that quarterly growth from **FY 2023-24 to FY 2025-26** is **less volatile and more broad-based** in the revised series than previously estimated.
- Revisions in the unincorporated sector are **not uniform** and vary from sector to sector.

## Annual And Quarterly Estimates

- India’s quarterly and provisional GDP estimates are built from the previous year’s quarterly figures and subsequently updated using information such as **GST collections** and **industrial production**.
- When the **FY 2022-23 GDP estimate** was revised under the new methodology, the change was naturally carried forward into subsequent years.
- Consequently, later annual and quarterly estimates also moved down.
- This does not mean that the economy suddenly became smaller or slowed down in those years; part of the change reflects that the calculation now begins from a **different, better-measured number**.

## The New GDP Series

- The new GDP series should be judged not by a single headline revision, but by its effort to make measurement of the Indian economy **more current**.
- The real test of a statistical system is not whether its numbers remain unchanged, but whether it is willing to change them when **better evidence** becomes available.
