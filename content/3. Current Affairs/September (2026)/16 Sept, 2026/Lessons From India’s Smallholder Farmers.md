- **Conflict, climate stress, food insecurity, entrenched poverty, and fiscal pressure** are exposing the limits of old **development** models.
- Serious development must begin with **honest evidence, practical experience, and results that hold up**.
- Useful lessons for rural development may come from **India’s smallholder farmers**, rather than only from **donor strategies**.

## Smallholder Agriculture

- Across rural India, **farmers, women’s groups, producer organisations, and rural enterprises** demonstrate that **smallholder agriculture** can move beyond **poverty and subsistence** towards **prosperity**.
- This transition rests on **organisation, commercial opportunity, and resilience at scale**.
- India is showing that rural transformation can be **practical, inclusive, and commercially serious**, even in **resource-constrained contexts**.
- India’s approaches combine **infrastructure, enterprise development, skills, finance, and market access** in increasingly commercial ways capable of delivering **results at scale**.

## Challenges Remain

- Smallholder farmers, located at the **first mile of food systems**, continue to face unequal access to:
    
    - **Finance**
    - **Technology**
    - **Markets**
    - **Land**
    - **Water**
    - **Relevant infrastructure**
- **Climate change** intensifies these pressures through **less predictable rainfall, rising temperatures, and weakening soils**.
- These conditions show why rural transformation cannot depend on **isolated projects alone**.

## No Single Intervention Is Enough

- **Roads, irrigation, power, and digital connectivity** are important, but they transform lives only when farmers can organise into **collectives**.
- Collectives provide:
    
    - **Real bargaining power**
    - Access to **finance and technology**
    - The capacity to **reach markets**
    - The ability to build **viable food enterprises**

## Redefining Development

- Programmes financed by the **International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD)** illustrate these connections.
- Nearly **five decades** of the **IFAD-India partnership** include:
    
    - **Self-help groups accessing commercial credit in Maharashtra**, supporting **thousands of enterprising rural women**
    - A **hub-and-spoke economic development model in Meghalaya**
    - **Youth- and women-led farmer producer organisation enterprises in Uttarakhand**
    - **Climate-resilient Zau farming in Mizoram**, protecting the **environment** as well as **traditional communities**
    - Engagement with young people in **Jammu and Kashmir** to harness their **entrepreneurial spirit** and unlock the region’s **vast agricultural potential**

## The Ecosystem

- India has built much of the ecosystem needed to link smallholders with services, finance, and markets through:
    
    - **Government bodies**
    - **NGOs**
    - **Private enterprises**
    - **Agri Stack digital public infrastructure**
    - Institutions such as **NABARD**
    - **Self-help groups**
    - **Farmer producer organisations**
    - **Cooperatives**
    - **Micro, small and medium enterprises**
- Together, these institutions help smallholders access **services, finance, and markets**.
- **Digital tools** improve **efficiency and accountability**.
- The new **IFAD-India eight-year strategy**, launched in **May**, aligns with the **Viksit Bharat@2047 vision** and focuses on strengthening this architecture.

## Global South’s Rural Development Conversation

- India is an important voice in the **Global South’s rural development conversation** and is taking on a broader leadership role.
- In the spirit of **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam**, India’s rural experience can become a shared resource for the wider family of nations.
- **IFAD**, the only international financial institution devoted to **rural transformation**, has a presence in **92 countries**, while many more are reached through the wider **UN system**.
- IFAD provides the channel through which India’s lessons can travel beyond its borders.

## What Can Travel

- India’s models cannot be copied exactly.
- The transferable principles are:
    
    - **Build strong local institutions**
    - **Connect them to markets and finance**
    - **Invest in rural enterprises**
    - **Use digital systems intelligently**
    - Treat smallholders, including **women and youth**, as **economic actors** rather than **passive beneficiaries**
