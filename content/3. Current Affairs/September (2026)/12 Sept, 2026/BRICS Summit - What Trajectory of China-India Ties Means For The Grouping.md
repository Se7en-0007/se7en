## China’s Growing Might

- **Chinese President Xi Jinping landed in India for his third visit since assuming China’s presidency and his first after a gap of seven years.**
- The stated purpose was **participation in the BRICS leaders’ summit**, but the visit was significant bilaterally because of the **2020 border standoff**, which marked a low point in India-China relations.
- The relationship’s **fault line**, combined with a **turbulent global scenario**, was expected to heavily influence the summit.
- In **2001**, Goldman Sachs analysts termed **India, China, Brazil and Russia** the world’s **“emerging economies”**, giving rise to the **“BRIC”** grouping in the immediate aftermath of the **9/11 attacks**.
- Over the next **25 years**, their trajectories diverged:
    
    - **China** surged ahead to become the **second-largest economy after the US**.
    - **India** grew at a modest rate but was outpaced by Beijing by a mile.
    - **Russia** faced challenges due to wars of its own making.
    - **Brazil** floundered amid frequent regime changes and scandals.
    - **South Africa**, a later entrant, also did not live up to expectations.
- Since the first **BRICS leaders’ summit in 2009**, held in the shadow of the global financial crisis, **China emerged stronger** after weathering that crisis better than many Western nations.

## Increasing Assertions

- Alongside its economic rise, **China has grown militarily**, with **big-power ambitions to dethrone the US**.
- These ambitions are reflected in China’s increasingly **aggressive and assertive stance** in its neighbourhood, the **South China Sea**, the **Indo-Pacific**, and on its borders with India.
- India has faced frequent border standoffs, but under **Xi**, the sense of belligerence from the Chinese state has been greater than at any time in the recent past.
- After the **1962 war** and the **1967 border clashes in Sikkim**, major border standoffs included:
    
    - **2013** - **Depsang, Ladakh**
    - **2014** - **Chumar, Ladakh**
    - **2017** - **Doklam**
    - **Since 2020** - **Eastern Ladakh**
- The eastern Ladakh standoff remains ongoing, with deployment of about **50,000 Indian troops** along the **Line of Actual Control (LAC)**.
- The **June 2020 clashes** killed **20 Indian defence personnel**, including a **Colonel-rank officer**, and at least **four Chinese defence personnel**.
- Years of diplomatic and military-level talks led to a **troop disengagement in 2024**.
- Incremental measures to stabilise ties have included:
    
    - **Resumption of direct flights**
    - **Easing visa restrictions**
    - **Restarting the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra**
    - **Relaxing restrictions on Chinese investments**
- Significant structural differences remain:
    
    - A **massive trade deficit**
    - **Lack of market access for Indian businesses**
    - India acting against Chinese firms for **tax evasion**
- **Strategic and political trust** has diminished during the border standoff, now in its **seventh year**.
- China-Pakistan collusion during India-Pakistan military hostilities under **Operation Sindoor** further exacerbated the crisis of **trust and credibility**.

## What It Means For BRICS

- The summit, attended by leaders of **over 30 countries and groupings**, is taking place against this backdrop.
- The picture has been complicated by the US government’s actions under **President Donald Trump** over the last **one-and-a-half years**.
- Trump’s **arbitrary tariff moves** have cornered countries worldwide, including **China and India**, placing them on the same side in trying to counter their effects.
- After years of high economic growth, **China has revised its expectations for the near future**.
- Trump’s war on **Iran** has raised questions about powerful countries violating others’ **territorial integrity and sovereignty**.
- However, **China and Russia** are also guilty of the same in their respective neighbourhoods.
- The war in **West Asia** has imposed economic costs on all countries:
    
    - **Energy prices** have risen.
    - **Inflation** has risen.
    - BRICS countries, as part of the **Global South**, are naturally bearing the brunt.
- The key question is whether shared interests and concerns between **India and China** can become an opportunity to improve ties and bring other developing countries together on common concerns.
- **Xi’s visit** is being viewed as a positive signal, but only to a degree.
- BRICS offers potential areas for cooperation, but Beijing has so far viewed itself as **“first among equals”** in the grouping.
- China long supported expanding BRICS membership beyond the original five, helping drive the **2023 expansion** that brought the grouping to its current strength of **11 members**.
- China and Russia have tried to frame BRICS as an **“anti-West”** grouping, while India sees it as a **“non-Western”** grouping.
- These divergent perspectives are complicated by US actions against fellow members, including:
    
    - **Tariffs on Brazil**
    - **Political disagreements with South Africa**
- India has built robust ties with the US over the last **25 years** and is seeking closer partnerships with **Europe, Australia, Canada and other Western nations**.
- India is decisively not **“anti-West”** because it recognises the need to turn to the West for:
    
    - **Capital**
    - **Technology**
    - A destination for its people, more than China or Russia
- By hosting the summit, India also seeks to signal to the US that it has other partners in fields ranging from **technology to fuel security**.
- Other BRICS partners offer specific opportunities:
    
    - The **UAE and Saudi Arabia** have been investing in the Indian economy.
    - **Russia** has stepped in as an energy supplier amid the closure of the **Strait of Hormuz**.
    - **Brazil** is ready to partner on **critical minerals**.
- The war has also polarised BRICS members:
    
    - **Iran** is attacking US military bases in West Asia.
    - The **UAE** hosts several military bases and personnel.
- India needs **China’s might** to move the two partners towards cooperation and demonstrate a more unified flank.
- Whether India and China can reach consensus on these issues and build on gains made so far will affect both their bilateral ties and **BRICS’ efficacy as a grouping**.
