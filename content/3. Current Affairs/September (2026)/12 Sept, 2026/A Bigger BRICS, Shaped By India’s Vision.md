- The **BRICS summit in New Delhi on September 12-13, 2026** is a landmark event amid **geopolitical turbulence** caused by **raging wars and conflicts**, a **breakdown in global governance**, and the **changing nature of global alliances**.
- **BRICS has 11 prominent members** and **about 10 partner countries**.
- Its collective economic output in **purchasing power parity** is greater than that of the **G-7**, making it one of the largest and most important **transcontinental groupings outside the West**.
- **Artificial intelligence** and **quantum computing** are being weaponised as the great powers compete for **tech supremacy**.

## India’s BRICS Moment

- India followed the successful template of its **G-20 Summit in New Delhi in 2023**.
- It conducted around **350 meetings** across Indian cities, including **22 ministerial-level meetings**.
- India’s focus is **“building resilience, innovation, cooperation and sustainability”**, giving a new meaning to the **BRICS acronym** and seeking to **refocus and recalibrate the BRICS agenda**.

## Three Pillars

- BRICS has traditionally rested on three pillars:
    
    - **Political and security**
    - **Finance and the economy**
    - **Culture and people-to-people exchanges**
- India’s agenda is oriented more towards the **second and third pillars** than the first.
- Leaders will largely determine the approach to major geopolitical issues, but substantive outcomes are expected from the India-chaired meetings.
- BRICS is now **too big to either ignore or fail**.

## Building Resilience, Driving Innovation

### Resilience

- India’s stewardship has led to:
    
    - The launch of the **BRICS Digital Centre of Excellence for Smart Grids and Energy Storage**
    - Adoption of the **BRICS Logistics Supply Chain Cooperation Framework**
    - Establishment of **Centres of Excellence on Agro-Ecology and Regenerative Agriculture**
- Once operationalised, these initiatives can provide the ballast for BRICS countries to become **truly resilient**.

### Innovation

- India has advanced proposals for:
    
    - A **start-up innovation fund**
    - An **incubator network**
    - A **digital public infrastructure repository**
    - A **science and research repository**
    - The action plan of the **BRICS Working Group on research infrastructures and mega science projects**
- These measures will benefit all BRICS members, particularly because they are at differing levels of development in **scientific research**.

### Cooperation

- Leaders may endorse recommendations to:
    
    - **Revitalise the multilateral trading system**
    - Welcome the **network of centres of excellence on mental wellness**
    - Establish the **BRICS urbanisation forum**
    - Enhance the **digital capacity of women**
    - Intensify cooperation in **education**, **youth skilling**, and the **BRICS Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Cooperation Portal**
- These initiatives offer concrete benefits to ordinary people in BRICS countries.

## Sustainability

- **Sustainability** is central to India’s agenda, building on Brazil’s BRICS agenda of the previous year.
- Areas of cooperation include:
    
    - **Combating desertification**
    - Developing guidelines for **disaster management** and **forest-fire preparedness**
    - The **BRICS forum on sustainable aviation fuels**
    - Advancing **climate resilience** through **people-centric and community-based adaptation**
- **People-centric and community-based adaptation** is critical because international fora tend to focus almost exclusively on **mitigation**.

## A Collaborative Platform

- The central tenet of India’s agenda is to create a **collaborative platform** that:
    
    - Enables the sharing of **knowledge and best practices**
    - Facilitates **joint research**
    - Promotes **capacity building**
- This represents a **people-centric, humanity-first approach**.

## Keeping BRICS On Course

- BRICS is here to stay and can gradually help members:
    
    - **Enhance their strategic options**
    - Drive geopolitics towards a **truly multipolar world**
- These are organising principles of **India’s foreign policy**, and other countries also see significant value in such a foreign-policy approach amid geopolitical uncertainty.
- BRICS must avoid being caught in geopolitical currents that are neither of its making nor of strategic value to its members.
- The larger objective is for BRICS to **live up to its full potential**, with the Delhi Summit making **concrete progress** towards that goal.
