- A **mother elephant and her baby** entered a farm in **Kodaikanal** during the day, where the mother **fed the infant until it fell asleep**.
- The incident reflected the **trust developed over many years**: the mother was teaching her baby that the **human-occupied farm was a safe space**.
- In a world where **human development is forcing elephants to alter their ancient migratory routes**, this was seen as a **milestone**.

## The Original Encroachers

- The farm shares a border with **Tamil Nadu’s Anamalai Reserve Forest**, which is home to the **Bengal tiger, Indian elephant, gaur, leopard**, and several other endangered species.
- Preventing **poachers** and the **timber mafia** from entering the forest contrasted with the later realisation that **farming with wild animals is difficult** and that keeping them out is **expensive**.
- A **Permaculture Design course** led to the idea that it might make more sense to **farm side by side with large animals** rather than try to exclude them.
- The idea of **ownership**, particularly ownership of property, is questioned: the wild has an **obvious first claim** to land it has occupied for generations.
- **Agriculture** is identified as the **first cause of habitat loss and degradation** and the **original encroachment into wild ecosystems**.
- Humans too have a right to wildlife because they are part of the delicate ecosystem and **as much a part of Nature as the elephant**.
- However, elephants were responsible for the **forest in which the farm existed**, making humans **beholden to them**.

## Bona Fide Ecosystem Engineers

- Elephants move in **matrilineal tribes** along migratory routes believed to be **thousands of years old**.
- **Matriarchs** lead herds across **hundreds of kilometres**, following seasons and remembering where to find water.
- Migration is necessary because elephants require **enormous amounts of food**.
- By moving along these routes, elephants create **highly biodiverse forests**:
    
    - Seeds swallowed with fruit are carried over long distances and deposited through **dung**.
    - This enables plants to grow away from the parent tree and supports **genetic diversity through movement**.
- During summer, elephants move through thick vegetation as **natural pruning agents**, allowing in **sunlight and air** and even creating **grasslands for other animals**.
- Elephants are **keystone species** that support other wildlife and are **bona fide ecosystem engineers**.
- In the presence of a wild elephant, **fences, property papers, and money** become meaningless: one simply **makes way**.

## ‘Owning’ The Planet

- The **Natural Commons** holds that every human being has a **nature-given right to air, water, forests, and wildlife** simply by being born on the planet.
- In reality, many of these are now **packaged and sold to the rich in their most pristine form**.
- **Land and a space to live** should also be part of these commons.
- Early human communities shared land within the tribe.
- **Plato**, the first Western philosopher to write on private land ownership, strongly opposed it because he believed it would lead to **corruption and greed**; he proposed **communal property for the ruling class** in his ideal republic.
- **Private property**, defined as property owned by commercial entities, emerged with the great European trading companies of the **17th century**, marking the beginning of **colonialism**.

## Sacred Economics

- In **Sacred Economics**, Charles Eisenstein limits the private right to **“use and abuse”** property by proposing a penalty for **socially and environmentally harmful activities**.
- This approach separates, as far as possible, the **“enjoyment”** or fruits of ownership from the fruits of **labour and creativity** added to what is owned.
- **Permaculture** combines **community-based land stewardship** with ecological design to create and maintain **natural commons**.
- Under this approach, land is held in trust for **shared ecological and community benefit**, rather than private ownership.

## Protector, Guardian, And Steward

- A fence around a **man-made boundary** does not separate land from the rest of the planet.
- Landholding is compared to having a child: one can be its **protector, guardian, and steward**, but can never claim to **own** it.
- The central argument is that **land can never really be yours**.
