- At the **2026 BRICS Summit**, **Prime Minister Narendra Modi** and **Chinese President Xi Jinping** shook hands before a high-quality projection of the **1,212-pillar corridor** of Tamil Nadu's **Ramanathaswamy temple**, located in **Ramanathapuram district**.
- **Tamil Nadu** was also where Modi and Xi last met in India, in **October 2019**. In a photograph from **September 12**, Modi appeared to be describing the corridor to Xi.
- Although the reason for choosing this backdrop is unclear, the temple and its corridor hold deep significance. The corridor is an iconic motif because of the **visual depth** it creates.

## Ancient Beginnings

- The **Ramanathaswamy temple** is located at **Rameswaram** on **Pamban Island**.
- According to an **1885 report** by **Henry Hardy Cole**, a superintendent with the **Archaeological Survey of India**, the temple in its current form was rebuilt in the **17th century** after repeated sackings over earlier centuries.
- The temple is an important religious site and one of the **12 jyotirlingas**, a series of shrines across India dedicated to **Shiva**.
- Its history is said to extend much further back than the 17th century. **Lord Ram** is believed to have worshipped **Lord Shiva** at the site after returning from **Lanka**.
- The **Sethupathi rulers of Ramanathapuram** were particularly important in shaping the temple's present form.
- The temple's **third and outermost corridor**, which contains over a thousand pillars, was added much later than the original structure.

## A Corridor At Scale

- The famous outer corridor is largely credited to **Muthuramalinga Sethupathi**, the **18th-century** ruler and zamindar of the **Ramnad estate**.
- It contains **1,212 pillars**, each rising to approximately **6.9 metres**.
- The combined length of the temple's corridors is commonly cited as about **3,850 feet**, or roughly **1.17 km**.
- It is unclear why the exact number of **1,212 pillars** was chosen.
- The corridor is constructed as a sequence of **stone columns** standing on **raised platforms**, which support the **beams** and **continuous roof**.
- The pillars serve more than a functional purpose. Each is individually **carved and ornamented** with **floral motifs** and figures of **mythical creatures**.
- The pillar rows appear to recede towards a single distant point. Their similar brown forms seem progressively smaller as their parallel lines converge in the distance.

## A Magical Visual Effect

- Every pillar supports the beams and roof while fitting into a carefully organised **grid**.
- The **columns, capitals, brackets and beams** had to function together structurally, while their ornamentation maintained the temple's visual language.
- Although the units are repetitive, together they create a changing spatial view of the corridor.
- The corridor was built to be **walked through**, rather than merely admired from a distance.
- The repeated columns generate immediate depth on a screen, while their enormous scale and distinctive **Dravidian architecture** make the image recognisable.

## More Than A Backdrop?

- The Ramanathaswamy Temple is notable not merely for having **1,212 pillars**, but because it represents the culmination of centuries of **temple-building traditions**.
- Its association with **Rama and Shiva** gives it sacred importance, while its corridors show how sacred space was transformed into **monumental architecture**.
- In the Modi-Xi image, the corridor places a diplomatic encounter against an architectural tradition in which **scale, symmetry, repetition and movement** were used to turn stone into an experience of space.
