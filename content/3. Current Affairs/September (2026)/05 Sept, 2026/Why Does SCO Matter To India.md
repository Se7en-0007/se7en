## Bishkek Declaration

- The **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit** hosted by **Kyrgyzstan** adopted the **Bishkek Declaration**.
- It called upon the international community to combat **terrorism**, including the **cross-border movement of terrorists**.
- SCO member states condemned the **“military strikes” on Iranian territory**.
- **Prime Minister Narendra Modi** stressed resolving conflicts through **“dialogue and diplomacy”** and called for a **“strong message”** to countries using terror **“as an instrument of state policy”**.
- **Pakistan** is scheduled to host the **SCO summit in 2027**.

## India’s Objectives

- India joined the SCO primarily to reach out to the **Central Asian countries**, considered part of India’s **extended neighbourhood**.
- India shares **cultural, historical and civilisational ties** with Central Asia and maintained **dynamic and robust ties** with the region until the Soviet period in the **1990s**.
- Central Asian countries possess major **natural resources**, including **mineral resources, oil, gas, critical minerals and uranium**.
- The SCO provides India a platform to connect with Central Asian states at the **highest level** and promote bilateral relations.
- India’s central concerns include **terrorism, security, stability, peace, double standards in dealing with terrorism and cross-border terrorism**.
- The **Bishkek Declaration** explicitly mentions these issues.

## Connectivity

- India has **de jure access to Tajikistan through Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK)** but lacks **de facto connectivity** because the region is currently occupied by Pakistan.
- India is pursuing access through **Iran**, **Chabahar** and the **International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC)**.
- The **INSTC** is a **7,200-km freight corridor** comprising **ship, rail and road routes** between **India, Iran, Azerbaijan, Russia, Central Asia and Europe**.
- Connectivity featured in the Declaration and in Modi’s discussions with **Vladimir Putin**, **Masoud Pezeshkian** and **Shavkat Mirziyoyev**.
- The article describes **INSTC and Chabahar** as a **lifeline** for Central Asian countries and important even for **Afghanistan**.

## Bridge-Builder And Strategic Autonomy

- Amid global fragmentation into **East and West** and **North and South**, the SCO gives India an opportunity to demonstrate its role as a **bridge-builder**.
- India is presented as being at ease with **Russia**, **Iran** and, following the **normalisation of relations**, **China**.
- The platform serves an optical purpose by showing that India has **options**.
- The bonhomie among **Modi, Putin and Xi Jinping** conveys India’s **strategic autonomy**.
- India is also contributing through cooperation on **civilisational dialogue**, **Buddhist heritage**, **traditional medicines**, the **Young Scientists Forum** and the **Young Authors Forum**.
- Most Indian objectives have been **satisfactorily realised**, although this remains **work in progress** amid rapid global change.

## Pakistan And The 2027 SCO Summit

- It is considered **too early** to determine whether Modi will attend the **2027 SCO summit in Pakistan**.
- Bilateral relations may remain stable at their current **adversarial level** or see a slight improvement; the government would assess all **pros and cons**.
- India’s position on **double standards** and **cross-border terrorism** is consistent, irrespective of whether Pakistan’s Prime Minister is present.
- India will continue to raise terrorism at both **SCO** and **BRICS** forums.
- India hosts the **2026 BRICS Summit on September 12-13, 2026**, where the issue is also expected to arise.

## India, Iran, US And Israel

- India’s relations with the **US and Israel** are not exclusive; its strategic autonomy means cooperation based on **India’s interests** and the principles of the **UN Charter**.
- India also maintains good relations with the **Palestinian Authority**, supports it and recognised it long ago.
- India has **civilisational and historical ties** with Iran.
- Iran matters for India because of **energy access through the Strait of Hormuz**, the welfare of Indian **seafarers**, and the safety and security of **10 million Indians in the Middle East**.
- The **2025 Tianjin SCO declaration** explicitly named **Israel and the US** in relation to attacks on Iran during the **12-day war in June 2025**.
- The **Bishkek Declaration** uses a more moderate formulation: it condemns attacks on Iran without naming the US or Israel, despite the conflict having continued for **more than six months** and involving greater aggression.
- Consequently, the article argues that the US and Israel are unlikely to be particularly upset.
- The four Central Asian SCO members - **Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan** - maintain good relations with the US and follow a **multi-vector foreign policy**.
- These states may also support moderation of language on such issues.

## China, Russia And India’s Place

- The SCO is dominated by **China** and, to a lesser extent, **Russia**, but its pivot is the **four Central Asian countries**.
- India’s major strength is its **historical, traditional and civilisational ties** with Central Asia, giving it strong **soft power**.
- The central challenge is converting this soft power into **hard power** through stronger **economic ties, projects, connectivity and innovation**.
- India’s relevant strengths include its growing economy, **fintech**, **Digital Public Infrastructure**, **pharmaceuticals**, **critical minerals** and **uranium**.
- Since the beginning of the **Russia-Ukraine conflict in 2022**, Central Asian countries have sought to reduce dependence on China and Russia and diversify their options.
- **Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan**, especially, follow a **multi-vector foreign policy**, and India is described as ideally fitting this requirement.

## Uzbekistan

- Modi’s visit to **Uzbekistan** was described as very successful.
- **UPI** was adopted in Uzbekistan.
- India and Uzbekistan reached a **long-term arrangement for uranium supply**.
- Their **strategic partnership** was upgraded to a **comprehensive strategic partnership**.
- A **Coordination Council** was established.
- Ongoing dialogue mechanisms will be upgraded to the **ministerial level**.
- Though implementation remains the test, there is determination on both sides, and India is **finding its feet and spreading its influence**.

## English As An SCO Language

- Since becoming an SCO member in **2017**, India has sought to make **English** a medium of communication in the organisation.
- Earlier, only **Russian and Mandarin** were used.
- At the Bishkek meeting, it was agreed that **English would be an SCO language**.

## Central Asia And Global Connectivity

- All Central Asian countries are **landlocked**.
- **Uzbekistan** is **doubly landlocked**.
- They lack access to the **warm waters of the sea**, while the **Caspian Sea** has no access to maritime shipping routes.
- Connectivity is therefore vital for the region.
- China’s **Belt and Road Initiative** plays an important role, but India does not support it because it does not respect India’s **sovereignty and territorial integrity**: the **China-Pakistan Economic Corridor** passes through **PoK**.
- Current circumstances may limit progress in the near term, but India will continue pursuing connectivity with its Central Asian partners.
