- **Tommy Koh**, a **lawyer and legal scholar**, chaired a pivotal negotiating committee at the **Third United Nations Conference on the Law of the Sea**.
- As **President of the Conference in 1982**, he led **more than 150 nations** to adopt the **United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS)**.
- The recognition of his role signifies an endorsement of efforts to build a world governed by **law and not by force**.

## **Seabed Mining**

- The principal challenge in the final negotiations was the **Reagan Administration’s radical demands on seabed mining**, which were rejected by the **developing countries**.
- **Ronald Reagan** changed the U.S. position that had existed during the **Jimmy Carter era**.
- He objected to **Part XI** of UNCLOS, which treats the **deep seabed** as the **“common heritage of mankind”**, viewing it as contrary to **free-enterprise** and **private-property norms**.

## **Near Universal**

- **172 countries** are parties to **UNCLOS**, making it **near universal**.
- Although the **United States** is not a party, it regards UNCLOS as the **authoritative law of the sea**.

## **Strait Of Hormuz**

- The priority is to **end the war**.
- Before the war, there was **no trouble in the Strait of Hormuz**.

## **Transit Passage**

- **Iran** is not a party to **UNCLOS**.
- However, the regime of **transit passage** has become part of **customary international law** and is therefore **binding on Iran**.

## **Constitution Of The World’s Ocean**

- UNCLOS remains the **“constitution” of the world’s ocean** despite contemporary disputes involving the **exclusive economic zone**, **seabed**, the **South China Sea**, and **Indian Ocean boundary questions**.

## **Dark Fleet**

- States’ punitive and material action against ships dubbed as the **dark fleet**, including action on the **high seas**, is a major problem for the **International Maritime Organization (IMO)**.
- This problem should be addressed at the **IMO**.

## **Compulsory Dispute Settlement**

- Unlike other treaties, **States Parties** under UNCLOS are bound by a system of **compulsory dispute settlement**.
- This mechanism has **worked very well**.
