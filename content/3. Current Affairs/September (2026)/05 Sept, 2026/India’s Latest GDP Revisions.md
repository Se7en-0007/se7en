## Extent Of Past Revisions

- **Revisions to past GDP numbers, especially quarterly numbers, happen regularly.**
- Quarterly GDP estimates use a **“benchmark-indicator approach”**, in which movements are guided by high-frequency indicators such as **crop production, cement production, finished steel consumption, and commercial vehicle sales**.
- Annual GDP estimates are based on **actual output and numbers**. As more actual data becomes available from **companies’ financial results** and the statistics ministry’s surveys, quarterly estimates are revised.
- These factors led to the large upward revision in GDP growth for **January-March 2026 (Q4 of FY26)** from **7.8% to 8.6%**.
- Revisions to older data were mainly due to the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation - **MoSPI** - shifting to a new indicator, the **Producer Price Index (PPI)**, to adjust **nominal GDP** to **real GDP**.

## Deflating To Get ‘Real’

- On **February 27**, MoSPI unveiled a new GDP data series after changing the **base year** from **2011-12 to 2022-23**.
- Base-year revisions incorporate **new data sources**, improve methodology, and capture data more accurately.
- While rebasing, MoSPI overhauled methods of calculating GDP, including the process of removing inflation from **nominal GDP** to arrive at **real GDP**.
- GDP is calculated by:
    
    - Summing the value created - **Gross Value Added (GVA)** - by every sector
    - Adding indirect taxes collected by the government
    - Subtracting subsidies given
- **GVA** is the difference between the value of a sector’s production - **output** - and the raw materials it uses - **inputs**.
- This is **nominal GVA**, or GVA measured at **current prices**.
- Until now, input and output values for each sector and subcategory were generally deflated by the **same number**, except in **agriculture** and **mining and quarrying**.
- This number could be the **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**, **Wholesale Price Index (WPI)**, or one of their sub-indices.
- The real input value was subtracted from the real output value to obtain **real GVA**. This process is called **single deflation**.

## Single Vs Double Deflation

- **Single deflation** works when input and output prices change at the **same rate**. It creates a problem when their rates of price change differ.
- Ideally:
    
    - Inputs should be adjusted by **input inflation**
    - Outputs should be adjusted by **output inflation**
- This method is called **double deflation** and is meant to ensure accurate real GVA.
- In the **2022-23 GDP series**, MoSPI moved to double deflation after repeated criticism that single deflation could lead to **underestimation or overestimation of real growth**, depending on movements in input and output prices.

### Example

- A sector uses **Rs 100** of inputs to produce **Rs 200** of output in one financial year.
- Its **nominal GVA** is therefore **Rs 100**.
- In the following financial year, input and output values rise by **20%** to **Rs 120** and **Rs 240**, respectively.
- Nominal GVA becomes **Rs 120**, implying **nominal GVA growth of 20%**.
- Assume input prices rise by **5%** and output prices rise by **2%**.

|Method|Deflation Process|Real GVA|Real GVA Growth|Deflator|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Single Deflation**|Uses a common **3% deflator** for both inputs and outputs|**Rs 116.5**|**16.5%**|**3%**|
|**Double Deflation**|Deflates inputs by **5%** and outputs by **2%**|**Rs 121**|**21%**|**-0.8%**|

## Double-Deflation Implication

- When **input prices rise faster than output prices**, single deflation can **underestimate real GVA growth**.
- A **negative implicit deflator** prevents low nominal growth from incorrectly penalising real growth when firms cannot fully pass higher costs to consumers.
- This was relevant in **April-June 2026**, when nominal growth was **10.3%**.
- The manufacturing deflator was negative in **six of the 13 quarters** for which data under the new GDP series is available, beginning **April-June 2023**.
- The manufacturing GVA deflator moves inversely with the year-on-year change in the price of **India’s crude oil basket**, a crucial input for Indian manufacturers: **the manufacturing deflator falls when crude-oil prices rise**.
- According to economists at **ICICI Securities Primary Dealership**, a negative deflator means **“pipeline price pressures are strong”**.
- If companies have pricing power, they may pass these costs to final-goods prices in subsequent quarters. **Nominal growth could then pick up**, while **real GDP may suffer somewhat**, depending on the **price elasticity of demand**.

## So What’s The Correct Deflator?

- When updated numbers were first released in February, MoSPI carried out double deflation using the **CPI, WPI, and their sub-indices**.
- The later revisions to past GDP growth data resulted from using the **PPI** for double deflation.
- The **PPI** measures prices received by producers at the **factory gate** and excludes **net tax** as well as **trade and transport margins**.
- Inputs and outputs of each sector should be deflated using the **correct index**. This was a major issue in the old series, particularly for the **services sector**.

### Services Sector

- In the last quarterly GDP numbers under the old **2011-12 base-year series**, for **July-September 2025**, the services-sector deflator implied by nominal and real growth rates was only **1.2%**.
- This appeared too low for services inflation because the implicit services deflator was largely based on the **WPI**.
- The WPI contains **only goods** and **no services**.
- When wholesale inflation was low because of soft commodity prices, services-sector nominal GVA was deflated by a number lower than the inflation actually faced by the sector.
- This made real services GVA larger than it should have been, causing an **overestimation of real growth**.
- The opposite would occur when commodity prices rose too rapidly.

## PPI And Accuracy

- With PPIs, MoSPI now uses **more than 300 deflators** to estimate different real GVA values, compared with about **180 deflators** under the old series.
- This means GVA across more sectors and sub-sectors is being measured more accurately.
- The new series and deflator processes do **not** mean that India’s GDP numbers have reached **peak accuracy**.
- The **input PPI** remains at a **trial stage** and exists only for the **manufacturing sector**.
- More clarity is expected from MoSPI’s **‘Sources and Methods’** document, which will detail the compilation process, data sources, and methodologies used in the new series.
