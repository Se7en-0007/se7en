- The **United Nations** adopted a resolution to formally **phase out the 16th-century Mercator map** in favour of the **2018 Equal Earth design**, which more accurately depicts **Africa’s size**.
- At the **UN General Assembly**, **164 countries**, including **India**, voted for the resolution; the **United States voted against** it and **six countries abstained**.
- **UN resolutions are not binding**, but the vote could lead to changes in **classroom curricula** and **technology**.
- **No flat map of the globe can ever be truly accurate**. Every conversion of a **three-dimensional sphere** into a **two-dimensional surface** involves distortions and trade-offs depending on its purpose.
- The **Mercator projection** makes **Africa** appear close in size to the much smaller **Greenland**. The **Equal Earth projection** also has limitations, but its supporters say it can help reverse perceptions of Africa and **“decolonise” global curriculums**.

## The Origins Of The Mercator Projection: A Map For Sailors

- **Gerardus Mercator (1512-94)**, a mathematician and engraver, created the **Mercator projection in 1569** to help sailors navigate the globe.
- Its first version consisted of **18 sheets**, forming a wall-size mosaic of **48 inches high by 80 inches wide**.
- Mercator represented the Earth’s surface as though it had been **unwrapped from a cylinder around the globe**.
- The map used a grid of **longitudes or meridians** and **latitudes or parallels** intersecting at **90-degree angles**.
- **Lines of longitude** were evenly spaced, while the distance between **lines of latitude** increased away from the **equator**, placed at the centre.
- This ensured that a **straight line between two points** on the map gave sailors the **correct direction** to follow using compasses.
- The Mercator map revolutionised travel and became the standard world map for **atlases** and **wall maps** by the **19th century**.

## Distorting The Real Size

- Representing the nearly spherical Earth as a flat rectangle left large gaps near the poles. Mercator stretched the northern and southern ends of the globe to fill these gaps and produce a usable map.
- As a result, places closer to the poles - including **Canada, Russia, the United States, and Europe** - appear larger than they actually are.
- **Africa**, which straddles the equator, appears smaller than its real size. Tropical countries such as **India** also look smaller.
- **Greenland**, which is about the size of the **Democratic Republic of the Congo**, appears roughly as large as the entire continent of **Africa**.
- Mercator’s projection enabled Western cartographers to easily mark **towns, cities, and roads** in their part of the world.
- The **Greenwich-anchored world map**, especially when combined with the Mercator projection’s enlargement of western Europe, has been criticised as an example of **Western cultural hegemony**.

## Why This Matters

- An episode of the American political drama **The West Wing** depicted socially minded cartographers explaining the bias implicit in the Mercator map.
- Critics argue that the map’s diminution of Africa contributed to the continent’s exploitation by the colonial West.
- The standard projection has been described as a political tool contributing to the **scramble for Africa**, also called the **partition of Africa**, when Western European powers colonised the continent.
- The argument is that making Africa look **small and conquerable** then, and **small and irrelevant** now, affects perceptions of the continent.
- Advocacy groups **Africa No Filter** and **Speak Up Africa** led the **Correct the Map** campaign, endorsed by the **African Union**.
- The rejection of the Mercator map has been presented as a step towards reclaiming **Africa’s rightful place on the global stage**.

## The Other Popular Types Of Projections

- **Areas and shapes can be depicted accurately only on a globe**. Every flat projection makes trade-offs according to whether its purpose is to measure **distances, directions, angles, areas**, or display thematic data such as **population**.
- Every map projection distorts at least one of four aspects:
    
    - **Area** - how large a landmass is relative to others
    - **Shape** - the true outline and appearance of a country
    - **Distance** - the actual space between two points
    - **Direction** - the correct compass bearing from one location to another
- In the **Mercator projection**, the **shapes** are prominently distorted.
- Map projections include **cylindrical, conic, and azimuthal** types, along with projections based on these types or their combinations.

### The Gall-Peters Projection

- In **1855**, Scottish clergyman **James Gall** presented a new projection at a science convention.
- In **1973**, German historian **Arno Peters** reintroduced a very similar map, bringing it widespread attention.
- The **Gall-Peters projection** preserves **area** accurately but severely distorts the **shape** of countries, making them appear stretched or squashed.
- Schools in **Boston** introduced this projection in **2017**.

### The Robinson Projection

- In **1963**, geographer **Arthur H. Robinson** devised the **Robinson projection** to better balance the sizes and shapes of landmasses and address the **“Greenland problem”**.
- Its meridians curve gently, while the poles are stretched into long lines rather than shown as points.
- Distortion near the poles is severe, though it moderates away from them.

### The Winkel Tripel Projection

- In **1921**, German cartographer **Oswald Winkel** devised the **Winkel Tripel projection** to reduce distortion across **area, distance, and direction**.
- In **1998**, it replaced the Robinson projection in world maps made by the **National Geographic Society**.
- Schools, textbooks, and the **World Bank** also adopted it.
- The Winkel Tripel reduces overall distortion but does not preserve **area, shape, distance, or direction** perfectly.
- Its distortion is more evident near the poles.

### The Equal Earth Projection

- The **Equal Earth projection** was developed in **2018**.
- It presents continents in mostly correct **relative sizes** while avoiding some of the severe shape distortions of the **Gall-Peters projection**.
- The UN resolution described Equal Earth as offering a more accurate representation of the sizes of continental landmasses and as a relevant option for improving the accuracy of world maps.
- It is not free of criticism: **shapes and distances** become distorted towards the poles.
- The **World Bank** already uses the **Winkel-Tripel** or **Equal Earth** projections for static maps and is phasing out Mercator on web maps.
- **Google Maps** switched from Mercator on desktop to a **3D globe view** in **2018**, though users can switch back to Mercator.
- On the Google Maps **mobile app**, the **Mercator projection remains the default**.
