- **The United Nations Environment Programme’s report, _Limiting Overshoot_, warns that breaching the 1.5°C global-warming limit would cause irreversible losses that adaptation cannot undo.**
- **Even if all countries fulfilled their climate pledges today, global heating is projected to reach 1.8°C; under current policies, it is poised to reach 2.6°C.**
- **The 1.5°C limit has already been breached.**
- Ongoing heating in the **central equatorial Pacific** is expected to develop into **one of the strongest El Niños in recent history**, likely causing another breach in **2027**.
- The report accepts the scientific reality that the world is in a **1.5°C world**, but rejects the conclusion that nothing more can be done.

## Overshoot, Peak And Decline

- The report proposes an **“overshoot, peak and decline” pathway**:
    
    - **Average global temperatures rise above 1.5°C**.
    - Countries collectively keep the **peak as low as possible**.
    - They then bring temperatures **back below 1.5°C by the end of the century**.
- **Neutralising the heating effect of even one-tenth of a degree is far harder than preventing that rise in the first place.**

## Greenhouse Gases Beyond Carbon Dioxide

- The report’s new emphasis is on **greenhouse gases beyond carbon dioxide**.
- **Methane**, responsible for about **0.5°C of current warming**, is considered the **most effective means to slow warming in the near term**.
- More than **155 countries** have joined the **Global Methane Pledge**.
- The pledge aims to cut **anthropogenic methane** emissions by at least **30% below 2020 levels by 2030**.
- **India is not among the countries that have joined the Global Methane Pledge.**

## Mitigation And Adaptation Finance

- India’s absence reflects the familiar tension between reports emphasising **mitigation** and major developing economies such as India.
- Developing economies stress that they are:
    
    - **Already moving away from fossil fuels**.
    - **Vulnerable to the impacts of historical stocks of carbon**.
    - Entitled to greater **financial support** and **affordable technology** from richer developed countries responsible for the crisis.
- The latest report has **little to say on adaptation finance**, although previous assessments have addressed the issue.

## COP31 And India’s Position

- Countries are set to convene in **Türkiye in November for COP31**, where familiar disagreements are likely to resurface.
- In the aftermath of the **Bhotekoshi disaster in Nepal**, India’s position has become more complicated.
- Nepal’s Foreign Affairs Minister, **Shisir Khanal**, has said that major industrial emitters such as **China, the United States and India** must consider the impact of rising temperatures on small countries such as Nepal.
- Despite India’s position as a **victim of climate change** and its **below-world-average per capita emissions**, a section of the world sees India as **part of the problem**.
- **India must take such perceptions seriously and calibrate its messaging to adapt to the reality of a 1.5°C world.**
