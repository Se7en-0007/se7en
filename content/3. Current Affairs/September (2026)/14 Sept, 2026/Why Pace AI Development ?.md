- **Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei** has called on leading AI companies to **slow the pace of increasing the capabilities of their most powerful models**.
- He argues that recent AI advances are **outstripping the industry’s ability to understand, test and control** the systems it builds.
- His position has received backing from **OpenAI’s Sam Altman** and **xAI’s Elon Musk**.
- In his essay, **“We Must Pace the Frontier,”** Amodei says his view hardened because of **two developments**:
    
    - AI systems are increasingly helping to build their own successors, creating the beginnings of **“recursive self-improvement”** and potentially accelerating AI progress.
    - In a recent **OpenAI-Hugging Face experiment**, AI agents carried out cyberattacks beyond their assigned task, suggesting that a more capable system with similar behaviour could cause much greater damage.
- Amodei warned that within **six to 12 months**, sufficiently powerful **misaligned agents** could potentially create **persistent botnets** and inflict **economic damage running into hundreds of billions of dollars**.

# Pacing The Frontier

- Amodei is **not calling for an outright halt** to AI development.
- **“Pacing the frontier”** means giving **safety research, model evaluation and oversight** more time to catch up with rapidly advancing AI capabilities.
- He argues that current models are an **“almost endless gold mine”** of insight into both building AI well and understanding what can go wrong when it is not built well.
- According to Amodei, if slowing down bought even **one or two extra years** before models reached critical capability levels, and that period was used to advance **alignment**, it could greatly reduce the risk of something going seriously wrong.
- Pacing would enable frontier labs to achieve:
    
    - Greater **operational excellence**
    - Better **alignment with human values**
    - Improved **interpretability**, the science of understanding what happens inside AI models
    - More time for **testing and evaluating models**
- Amodei said laws can take time while AI is advancing very quickly; therefore, alongside regulation, AI companies should **voluntarily work together to set standards**, aided by the verifiability of **permanent embedded evaluators**.

# Dario Amodei’s Three-Step Plan

- The plan seeks to **pace the frontier**, not halt model training or technical progress.
- Its purpose is to ensure companies take adequate time to **align and safeguard models**, while **third-party evaluators** confirm this.

## Embedded Evaluators

- Each frontier AI company would give a team of **embedded third-party evaluators** ongoing, employee-like access.
- Their role would be to:
    
    - Verify adherence to **safety practices and commitments**
    - Report incidents
    - Assess the alignment of completed AI models as well as **training pipelines and processes**
- They could check, at the **nuts-and-bolts level**, whether a company follows its claimed **training, deployment, operational and safeguards practices**.
- Evaluators would have **desks in company offices, access badges, company laptops, and access to workspaces, tools and permissions** mostly comparable to those available to internal risk-assessment teams.

## Democratic Coordination

- Frontier AI companies in **democratic countries** would coordinate to establish:
    
    - Common **safety standards**
    - Limits on the rate of **unchecked AI progress**
- Some forms of coordination that could effectively pace AI are **legally challenging** and would require **government support**.

## Global Coordination

- The **US and other democratic governments** would attempt coordination with **authoritarian governments**, while taking seriously the difficulty of verifying compliance.

# Global Cooperation

- **China** is the biggest obstacle to Amodei’s proposal.
- Any meaningful effort to slow frontier AI development would eventually require coordination between **Washington and Beijing**.
- However, US companies should not slow down unilaterally if Chinese labs are free to keep advancing.
- This produces a **strategic tension**:
    
    - Slowing AI could reduce safety risks.
    - Slowing too early could allow China to close the technological gap with the US.
- Amodei supports:
    
    - Continued restrictions on exports of **advanced AI chips and chipmaking equipment** to China
    - Stronger action against **chip smuggling** and **remote access to overseas data centres**
    - Safeguards against **model theft**
    - Measures to prevent Chinese companies from cheaply replicating frontier systems through unauthorised **distillation**
- He argues that widening the US lead over China over the next **three to five years** could create more room for both sides to negotiate limits later.
- He proposes beginning with narrow agreements, including:
    
    - Banning AI assistance for **biological weapons**
    - Requiring **pre-release testing** of powerful models for **cybersecurity, biological and alignment risks**
- More ambitious measures could include a mutually agreed **“speed limit” on recursive self-improvement**.
- A full global pause remains unlikely because verifying compliance would be **extremely difficult**.
