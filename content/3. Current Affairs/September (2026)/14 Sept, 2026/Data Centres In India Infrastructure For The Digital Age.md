- **Data centres are secure facilities housing computing, storage and networking equipment** that **store, process, manage and distribute large volumes of digital information**.
- They power **online banking, e-Governance, digital payments, cloud computing and other emerging technologies**.
- With rapid digitalisation, India requires continuously operating, secure and scalable infrastructure for **computing capacity, data storage and high-speed connectivity**.
- **Data centres provide the critical foundation for digital services driving India’s economy and governance**.
- Modern data centres integrate **computing, networking, power, cooling, security and management systems** to ensure **reliable operations, data security and uninterrupted digital services**.
- **Resilient data centres** are becoming strategic infrastructure for **innovation, competitiveness and sustained digital growth**.

## Inside A Data Centre: The Technologies Powering Digital Services

### Reliable Power Infrastructure

- Data-centre computers must operate continuously and **cannot turn off even for a millisecond**.
- **Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS)** provide immediate backup during electrical interruptions.
- **Backup generators** and **electrical distribution systems** provide resilience during prolonged power disruptions.

### Cooling Systems

- Continuous operations require cooling systems to maintain suitable conditions for computing equipment.
- **Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning (HVAC)** systems regulate temperature and airflow.
- **Advanced airflow management** and **liquid cooling** improve thermal efficiency and reliability.

### Information Technology Infrastructure

- **Servers** process information and run digital applications.
- **Storage systems** securely hold large data volumes using **Hard Disk Drives (HDDs)** and **Solid-State Drives (SSDs)**.
- **Security systems** protect equipment, networks and data from physical and digital threats.
- **Networking equipment**, including routers and high-speed switches, enables data exchange within and beyond the facility.
- **Fiber-optic connections** provide high-speed communication between the data centre and external networks.

### Management Software

- **Management software** monitors systems and optimizes computing-resource use.
- **Virtualisation** enables multiple virtual machines to run on one physical server, improving resource use.
- **Automation tools** manage workloads, support maintenance and identify operational problems.

## From Click To Response: How A Data Centre Handles Your Request

- A digital request may begin with **opening an app, checking a bank balance or accessing a document**.
- The request travels through a network to a data centre, where systems process it securely and efficiently.
- The **security layer** checks the request and protects the system from cyber threats.
- The **load balancer** directs the request to an available server and distributes workload efficiently.
- The **application server** processes the request and determines the required information or service.
- If needed, the application server uses an **Application Programming Interface (API)** to communicate with other applications or services.
- The **database server** retrieves the required information and sends it to the application server.
- Processed information travels back through the network to the user’s device.
- The entire process can take **1 to 2 seconds**.
- This chain, **Connect → Receive → Network → Process → Store → Secure → Respond**, enables **cloud services, e-governance platforms and Artificial Intelligence applications** to deliver information reliably and at scale.
- Modern data centres are not merely storage facilities but **digital infrastructure enabling everyday online services**.

## In Simple Terms: The Behind-The-Scenes Team

- **Network:** The Corridors and Intercom that connect the whole team and enable instant information transfer.
- **Security Layer:** The Security Guard that inspects a request at the entrance for safety.
- **Load Balancer:** The Traffic Cop that directs incoming digital traffic so that no server is overwhelmed.
- **Application Server:** The Chef that processes the order and determines the required information.
- **API:** The Waiter acting as a messenger.
- **Database Server:** The Digital Vault where details are secured and retrieved when required.

## Status Of Data Centers In India

- India’s data-centre journey has evolved with the expansion of **e-Governance and digital services**.
- Rising citizen expectations for online services are increasing demand for robust data-centre infrastructure.
- The **National Informatics Centre (NIC)** has established National Data Centres at its headquarters in **Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad and Bhubaneswar**.
- NIC has also established **37 smaller Data Centres** at various **State Capitals**.
- These facilities provide reliable digital services to Government institutions, supporting **round-the-clock operations, system management and skilled onsite personnel**.
- Installed data-centre power capacity increased from around **375 MW in 2020** to **1.57 GW as of August 2026**.
- Capacity is projected to reach nearly **8 GW by 2030**.
- Investments of nearly **USD 70 billion** are already underway in India’s data-centre sector.
- An additional **USD 90 billion** in projects has been announced, reflecting strong investor confidence.

## Enabling Policy Environment For Data Centres

- In the **Union Budget 2022–23**, data centres were included in the **Harmonized List of Infrastructure**.
- This gave data centres **infrastructure status**, facilitating greater access to credit and supporting investment and capacity expansion.
- Infrastructure status treats data centres like **national highways, airports and power grids**, enabling easier and cheaper long-term loans.
- The **Union Budget 2026–27** introduced a **tax holiday for eligible foreign cloud service providers until 2047**.
- The incentive applies to global operations using India-based data-centre infrastructure.
- It covers **Tax Years 2026–27 to 2046–47** for notified foreign companies.
- These measures strengthen India’s investment environment for data centres and cloud infrastructure.

## Linked Ecosystem Schemes Supporting Data Centre Growth

- **Semicon India Programme (Semicon 2.0)** was approved by the Union Cabinet on **15.07.2026** with an outlay of **₹1,27,500 crore** under the **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)**.
- Semicon 2.0 aims to holistically build a **trusted and resilient semiconductor ecosystem** in the country.
- The **IndiaAI Mission** has a total budget outlay of **₹10,371.92 crore** to build a comprehensive AI ecosystem.
- The AI ecosystem fostered under the IndiaAI Mission is a key driver of data-centre growth.
- The **Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS)** allocation increased from **₹22,000 crore** to **₹40,000 crore**.
- **PLI Scheme 2.0 for IT Hardware** aims to build a robust domestic manufacturing ecosystem for IT hardware such as servers, attract large investments, reduce import reliance and make India a trusted global supply-chain hub.

## Sustaining The Data Centre Sector: Energy And Water Planning

- Rapid expansion of data-centre capacity makes **energy and resource efficiency** increasingly important.
- Data centres require reliable electricity and efficient cooling systems for continuous operations.
- According to the **Central Electricity Authority (CEA)**, Ministry of Power, data-centre power demand could reach **17 GW by 2031–32**.
- The Government is promoting cleaner energy and efficient resource use for sustainable sectoral growth.

### Renewable Energy & Clean Power

- Key initiatives include the **Green Energy Open Access Rules**, **Green Energy Corridor Scheme** and **National Green Hydrogen Mission**.
- The **National Programme on High Efficiency Solar PV Modules** and **Solar Park Scheme** also support clean-energy deployment.
- These measures improve access to cleaner and more reliable power for energy-intensive digital infrastructure.

### SHANTI Act & Clean Power

- The **Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India (SHANTI) Act** strengthens the clean-power ecosystem.
- It supports reliable nuclear power for emerging sectors, including **Artificial Intelligence and data centres**.
- It enables future deployment of **Small Modular Reactors** and **Micro Nuclear Reactors**.

### BIS Standards For Data Centres

- The **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)** has developed standards for data-centre efficiency.
- Its technical committee **LITD 31** covers **Cloud Computing, Information Technology and Data Centres**.
- BIS standards cover **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)**, **Carbon Usage Effectiveness (CUE)**, **Cooling Efficiency Ratio (CER)** and **Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE)**.
- **PUE and WUE** measure how efficiently a facility uses electricity and water.
- **CUE** measures carbon pollution produced for every unit of electricity used by data-centre computers.
- **CER** measures how efficiently cooling systems remove heat relative to the electricity consumed.

### Energy And Water Efficiency Standards

- The **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)** notified the **Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC) 2017**.
- It also notified the **Energy Conservation and Sustainable Building Code (ECSBC) 2024**.
- These codes prescribe **energy-efficiency and water-conservation norms** for buildings and include provisions relevant to data-centre infrastructure.

### Advanced Cooling And Water Management

- Data-centre water requirements depend on the cooling technologies deployed.
- **Groundwater extraction**, including for industrial purposes, is regulated under guidelines issued by the **Ministry of Jal Shakti**.
- AI infrastructure is driving adoption of **direct-to-chip liquid cooling, adiabatic cooling and immersion cooling**.
- **Closed-loop liquid cooling systems** are emerging as resource-efficient solutions.

## Way Forward

- India’s data-centre growth reflects a policy journey towards **resilient and sovereign digital infrastructure**.
- Data centres are increasingly important for **data sovereignty, digital trust, economic competitiveness and technological self-reliance**.
- Expansion of cloud and Artificial Intelligence infrastructure is creating new opportunities for innovation and economic growth.
- Growth requires responsible governance of **data, technology, energy and digital infrastructure**.
- A **technology governance approach** can balance **innovation, security, sustainability, accountability and public interest**.
- This requires **strong standards, robust cybersecurity, responsible data practices and efficient resource management**.
- India also requires greater domestic capabilities in **computing, semiconductors, energy and critical digital infrastructure**.
- As India advances towards **Viksit Bharat by 2047**, data centres can serve as a foundational layer of the digital economy.
