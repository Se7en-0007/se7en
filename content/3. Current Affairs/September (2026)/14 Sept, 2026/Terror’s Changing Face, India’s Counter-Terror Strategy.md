- The **September 11, 2001 al-Qaeda attack on the Twin Towers in New York** was neither the first major terrorist attack globally nor the first against the United States, but it **fundamentally changed how terrorism would be fought** in the following decades.
- Over the past **25 years**, the global and Indian security landscapes have changed irreversibly: **forms of terrorism, instruments of terror, and counter-terror strategies** have all evolved.
- Large terror groups led by figures such as **Osama bin Laden** of al-Qaeda and **Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi** of the Islamic State, who called for an **Islamic Caliphate**, are no longer prominent in the same form.
- Terror groups have become **smaller and more autonomous**, while **lone-wolf attacks** are becoming the norm.
- **Drones, cyber capabilities and artificial intelligence** are being used both by terror groups and by States combating terrorism.
- For India, **9/11** brought greater attention to concerns over **cross-border terrorism**, especially in **Kashmir**. However, global solidarity and acknowledgment of India’s fight came only after the **26/11 Mumbai terror attacks of November 2008**.
- India’s approach has evolved from **merely warning Pakistan without punishing it** to treating terrorism supported from across the border as an **“act of war”**.

## The Starting Years Saw A Passive Response

- Cross-border terror began in Kashmir in the **early 1990s**, but the first major terror attack did not come from the Kashmir Valley.
- On **May 21, 1991**, former Prime Minister **Rajiv Gandhi** was assassinated at **Sriperumbudur, Tamil Nadu**, by the Sri Lankan **Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam**.
- India lacked a clearly defined **counter-terror strategy** while terror networks in Kashmir were rapidly expanding.
- The **March 1993 Mumbai serial blasts** killed **over 250 people** through **13 coordinated blasts** across the city.
- The **February 1998 synchronised blasts in Coimbatore** and the Mumbai attacks exposed weaknesses in India’s internal-security mechanisms for detecting and combating terror.
- Pakistan’s direct role was first exposed through the hijacking of **Indian Airlines flight IC-814** on **December 24, 1999**.
- Under public pressure and with few alternatives, India released Pakistan-based terrorists including **Ahmed Omar Sheikh** and **Masood Azhar** in exchange for **more than 160 civilian hostages**.
- The **December 13, 2001** attack on Parliament in New Delhi by the Pakistan-based **Jaish-e-Mohammed** led to large-scale military mobilisation under **Operation Parakram**.
- Since India and Pakistan had acquired nuclear weapons, fears of escalation prompted international intervention to ease tensions.
- After nearly **two years** of tense standoff, military disengagement occurred without any direct punishment for Pakistan.
- The **26/11 Mumbai terror attacks**, regarded as India’s **“9/11 moment”**, also did not produce military action against Pakistan.
- Other major attacks included:
    
    - **2002 Akshardham Temple attack**
    - **2006 Varanasi serial bomb blasts**
    - **2010 German Bakery blast in Pune**
    - **December 22, 2000 Red Fort attack**, in which **Lashkar-e-Taiba** terrorists targeted an Army garrison, killing **three soldiers**
    - **October 2005 Delhi serial blasts** in markets including **Sarojini Nagar** and **Paharganj**, killing **over 60 people**
    - **September 2008 Delhi blasts** in commercial areas including **Connaught Place, Greater Kailash and Karol Bagh**
    - **September 7, 2011 Delhi High Court briefcase bomb**, which killed **15 people** and was claimed by **Harkat-ul-Jihad Islami**, an al-Qaeda-affiliated group largely based in Pakistan
- Despite conclusive proof of Pakistan’s direct support in these and other attacks, India did not respond directly and decisively.

## Taking The Fight Across The Border

- After the change of government at the Centre in **May 2014**, India adopted a strategy based on the resolve that terror would not go unpunished.
- The **September 18, 2016 Uri attack** by **Jaish-e-Mohammed** terrorists on an Army camp in Kashmir led to the first cross-border **surgical strikes** on **September 28-29, 2016**.
- The strikes conveyed a strong message and a clear statement of intent.
- After the **February 14, 2019 Pulwama attack** on a **Central Reserve Police Force convoy**, the **Indian Air Force** struck a JeM terror camp at **Balakot**.
- This was the first time the Indian Air Force crossed into Pakistani airspace to strike a terror target.
- The **April 22, 2025 Pahalgam terror attack** generated unprecedented national outrage.
- Prime Minister **Narendra Modi**, then visiting Saudi Arabia, returned to India; the armed forces subsequently launched **Operation Sindoor** on **May 6-7, 2025**.
- Within **96 hours**, the operation destroyed the leaders and headquarters of **Lashkar-e-Taiba** and **Jaish-e-Mohammed**, and pulverised Pakistani military assets.

## Where India Stands On Terror Now

- India has moved from a **passive and reactive policy** to becoming a leading voice in the global fight against terrorism.
- After **Operation Sindoor**, India announced three intentions:
    
    - The era of **restraint and patience is over**, and Pakistan will pay a direct and heavy price for future terror attacks.
    - Any future cross-border terrorism emanating from Pakistan will be treated as an **“act of war”**.
    - **Pakistani nuclear blackmail** will no longer restrain India.
- On **February 23, 2026**, India unveiled **PRAHAAR**, its first comprehensive **National Counter-Terrorism Policy and Strategy**.
- PRAHAAR provides a national framework to prevent and respond to **terrorist activities and radicalisation** through coordinated **“whole-of-government”** and **“whole-of-society”** approaches.

## A Four-Fold Strategy

- Although terror-related incidents in Kashmir have declined drastically, terrorists may still infiltrate across the **Line of Control** and evade intelligence and security agencies.
- India’s four-fold strategy should be:
    
    - **Hunt down and eliminate** remaining terror-network elements in the country, particularly in Kashmir.
    - Take **pre-emptive military action** against developing threats across the LoC, including at terrorist launch pads, aided by experience and a deeply embedded intelligence network.
    - Conduct an exhaustive **de-radicalisation programme** nationwide to bring young people into the mainstream and make joining or supporting terror outfits unattractive and prohibitively costly.
    - Cut off **terror financing** in close coordination with friendly countries.

## Twenty-Five Years After 9/11

- Terrorism has not been eliminated globally but has **morphed into new forms**.
- At the **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation Summit in Bishkek** on **September 1**, Prime Minister Modi criticised double standards and called for a strong message to countries that use terrorism as an instrument of policy or provide terrorists safe haven and support: **terrorism can never be a strategic asset for anyone**.
- The **Delhi Declaration** of the **18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi** included an exclusive paragraph on the **Pahalgam terror attack**, indicating a growing global consensus on combating terrorism.
