- **Reformed multilateralism** amid a world **riven with unilateral actions by superpowers** emerged as the binding theme of the **18th BRICS Summit** and the **New Delhi Declaration**.
- The Summit’s success was measured by **three main parameters**:
    
    - **Participation of members at the highest levels**
    - Importance given to **intra-BRICS trade cooperation mechanisms**
    - **Consensus** forged by Indian negotiators on a joint statement **in a time of war**

## Iran And The UAE

- **Iran and the UAE**, rivals in the **West Asian conflict**, held their **highest-level bilateral meeting since the war began** on the occasion of the Summit.
- The **New Delhi Declaration** was issued despite scepticism over the grouping’s internal differences, presenting BRICS as a **formidable front in its 20th year**.
- **Saudi Arabia’s** position remained ambiguous as it has **yet to formally join** the group; its Foreign Minister arrived **after the main session**, where the joint statement was adopted.
- The declaration omitted references to:
    
    - **U.S.-Israel attacks on Iran**
    - The **assassination of Iran’s Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei**
    - Iran’s retaliatory attacks on its neighbours, especially the **UAE**
- This omission was understood to result from a **last-moment compromise** between **Iran and the UAE** to forge an agreed text.

## The New Delhi Declaration

- The declaration called for **Security Council reform** and supported a **greater role for Brazil and India**.
- It recommended **new initiatives for global governance**.
- It condemned **western sanctions and tariffs**.
- BRICS resolved to improve **intra-BRICS trade** and undertake more transactions in **local currencies**.
- It condemned **unilateral wars**, without criticising any member.
- It took an **exceptionally strong position** on Israel’s actions in **Palestine and Lebanon**.

## India’s Fourth BRICS Chairship

- India’s **fourth BRICS chairship** strengthened its reputation as a **Global South balancer** with easy access to both sides of a divided world, while also delivering **bilateral benefits**.
- Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s close attention to Iran’s President **Masoud Pezeshkian** could help assuage Iran’s hurt feelings after New Delhi appeared to be tilting towards the **U.S. and Israel** during the war.
- Mr. Modi’s meeting with Chinese President **Xi Jinping** allowed India and China to reaffirm their **political will to normalise ties**.
- Meetings with important leaders from the **ASEAN grouping** and **African nations** gave much-needed attention to India’s ties with them.

## Shift In Traditional Power Equations

- The Summit represented a moment of solidarity among countries with many differences that nevertheless seek a shift in **traditional power equations** towards an order driven more by their **emerging economies**.
