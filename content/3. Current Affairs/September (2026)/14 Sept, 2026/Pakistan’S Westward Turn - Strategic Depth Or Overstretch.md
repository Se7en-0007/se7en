- **Pakistan’s defence cooperation agreement with Kuwait, signed on August 27, 2026**, reflects its **rapidly expanding strategic footprint in West Asia**.
- Its significance is heightened by Pakistan’s membership in the **Saudi Arabia-Türkiye-Pakistan defence arrangement** and its recent role as an **intermediary between Iran and the United States**.

## Enduring Links

- Pakistan’s orientation towards West Asia has **deep historical roots**. From its inception, it sought to combine its **South Asian geopolitical identity** with that of a **major Muslim power**.
- During the **Cold War**, Pakistan, Iran and Türkiye were linked through the **Central Treaty Organization** and subsequently through the **Regional Cooperation for Development**.
- Pakistan’s decisive turn towards the Arab world followed its **defeat and dismemberment in 1971**.
- It tried to compensate for its diminished South Asian position by stressing its **Islamic identity**, symbolised by the **Islamic summit in Lahore in 1974**.
- Pakistan also developed extensive military relationships with **Saudi Arabia** and the **Gulf monarchies**.
- Islamabad nevertheless sought to avoid becoming a party to the **Saudi-Iranian rivalry** because of its Saudi relationship.
- In **2015**, Pakistan’s Parliament refused to send troops for the **Saudi-led Yemen war**.
- Its caution stemmed from a **substantial Shia population** at home and a **long and occasionally turbulent border with Iran**.

## Increasing Institutionalisation

- What is new is not Pakistan’s involvement in West Asia, but its **increasing institutionalisation**.
- The **Makkah Agreement of August 7** stipulated that an **armed attack against one member** would be regarded as an **attack against all**.
- At the **August 31 meeting in Istanbul**, the Foreign and Defence Ministers and military chiefs of the three countries decided to:
    
    - Establish a **permanent secretariat in Saudi Arabia**.
    - Appoint a **Pakistani as the first Secretary-General for three years**.
    - Strengthen **military interoperability**.
    - Pursue **defence-industrial cooperation**, including **joint technology development and production**.
- The arrangement is potentially formidable because it combines:
    
    - **Saudi financial power**.
    - **Türkiye’s conventional military capability** and rapidly expanding **defence industry**.
    - **Pakistan’s large armed forces** and **nuclear capability**.

## Contradictions In Pakistan’S Expanding West Asian Role

- The new arrangement is already exposing Pakistan to the contradictions of its expanding West Asian role.
- Renewed **Houthi missile and drone attacks against Saudi Arabia** created the first serious test of the **Makkah pact**.
- The Houthis’ recent capture of **Mocha port** and **Perim island** has increased their ability to choke Saudi shipping through the **Bab al-Mandab**.
- With the **Strait of Hormuz effectively closed by Iran**, Saudi Arabia may relaunch a **full-fledged war against the Houthis**.
- This would create a major dilemma for Pakistan, particularly if Riyadh invokes the **collective-defence provision** of the Makkah Agreement.
- Pakistan seeks simultaneously to be:
    
    - **Saudi Arabia’s security partner**.
    - **Türkiye’s strategic ally**.
    - **Iran’s friend and interlocutor**.
    - **Washington’s useful intermediary**.
    - An increasingly important **security provider to the Gulf monarchies**.
- These roles are compatible when regional tensions are manageable, but become **irreconcilable** when tensions intensify.

## What It Means For India

- Pakistan’s westward turn presents India with both a **challenge** and a potential **relief**.
- Pakistan’s embedding in West Asian security relationships may provide it **defence in depth**.
- **Saudi financial resources**, **Turkish defence technology**, and Pakistan’s expanding ties with Gulf militaries could theoretically increase Islamabad’s resilience in a confrontation with India.
- Türkiye is already Pakistan’s most vocal major supporter on **Kashmir**.
- Pakistan’s **nuclear status** adds to its strategic value for Arab states concerned by an increasingly unstable regional order resulting from **nuclear-armed Israel’s aggressive policies**.

## Strategic Overstretch

- **Strategic depth can easily become strategic overstretch**.
- Pakistan’s security establishment has historically concentrated overwhelmingly on **India**.
- Greater commitments of diplomatic, military and intelligence resources to **Iran, Saudi Arabia, the Gulf, Yemen and the Red Sea** could leave Pakistan with fewer resources to focus on India.
- The Saudi-Houthi confrontation, and the possibility that it may produce a war, demonstrates the danger of **strategic overstretch**.
- The more deeply Pakistan is embedded in West Asian security arrangements, the harder it becomes for it to remain aloof from the region’s conflicts.
- If the Houthi-Saudi confrontation escalates, Islamabad may have to choose between:
    
    - Upholding the credibility of its **collective-defence commitments**.
    - Maintaining its carefully cultivated relationship with **Tehran**, the Houthis’ principal ally.

## India’S Approach

- India should not interpret Pakistan’s growing West Asian role in purely negative terms.
- **Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and other Gulf states** have major economic and strategic relationships with India that they are unlikely to sacrifice for Pakistan.
- **New Delhi should continue deepening these relationships** to counter Pakistan’s expanding footprint in West Asia.

## The Paradox

- Pakistan’s consolidated West Asian relationships undeniably give it greater **strategic depth vis-à-vis India**.
- However, those same relationships expose Pakistan to some of the world’s most **combustible rivalries**.
- A Pakistan embedded in a West Asian security structure may appear strategically stronger in theory, but its mutually contradictory commitments could leave it **indefinitely stuck in a quagmire of its own choosing**.
- **It is far more difficult to get off the tiger than to get on it.**
