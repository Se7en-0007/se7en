## Punjab Agricultural Policy 2023

- **Punjab is primarily an agrarian state**, yet it has **never had a comprehensive agricultural policy**.
- The previous **Congress ruled government** attempted to formulate one, but the draft **never translated into a policy**.
- The **AAP government**, which came to power in **2022**, constituted an **11 member Agricultural Policy Formulation Committee** on **January 13, 2023**, under **Sukhpal Singh**, chairman of the **Punjab State Farmers’ and Farm Workers’ Commission**.
- The committee prepared the **200 page Punjab Agricultural Policy 2023**.
- It included **K A P Sinha**, then Special Chief Secretary, Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare, **Satbir Singh Gosal**, Vice Chancellor of Punjab Agricultural University, **Inderjeet Singh**, Vice Chancellor of Guru Angad Dev Veterinary and Animal Sciences University, and experts including **Sucha Singh Gill, B S Ghuman, Gurkanwal Singh, and Balwinder Singh Sidhu**.
- Around **1.02 lakh suggestions** were received from organisations and stakeholders and examined before the policy was prepared.
- The draft was submitted to the Punjab government on **October 13, 2023**, but **implementation did not follow**.
- Although the policy was prepared by experts, submitted to the government, discussed with stakeholders, and examined by a legislative committee, Punjab still lacks a **clear implementation road map**.
- The issue is more significant because the government is set to complete its term in a few months and **Assembly elections are round the corner**.

## Policy Proposals

- The **Punjab Agriculture Policy 2023** goes beyond **crop diversification** and addresses **groundwater depletion, farm debt, farm incomes, insurance, labour, research, marketing, livestock, and horticulture**.

### Water And Diversification

- The policy targets at least **30% water saving initially**.
- It proposes restricting **long duration paddy varieties** and stopping **paddy cultivation in critically stressed areas**.
- It promotes **canal irrigation, micro irrigation, and water saving technologies**.
- It encourages alternatives to the **wheat paddy cycle**, including **cotton, maize, sugarcane, pulses, oilseeds, vegetables, and horticulture**.
- It calls for **timely canal water** and **improved cotton seeds** in cotton growing areas.

### Debt, Pension, And Insurance

- It proposes a **one time debt settlement**, **debt swapping**, and relief for **small farmers**.
- It recommends a **Punjab specific crop insurance scheme and fund**, as well as **livestock insurance**.
- It proposes a **pension after 60** for farmers owning up to **five acres** and for **farm labourers**.

### Research, Seeds, And Markets

- The policy proposes **13 Centres of Excellence**.
- It seeks to make Punjab a **seed hub** through **region specific clusters**, **continuous seed research**, stronger **agricultural research and extension**, and **market research institutions**.

### Cooperatives And Rural Economy

- It seeks to strengthen **3,523 multi purpose cooperative societies**.
- It promotes **organic and natural farming, horticulture, livestock, dairy, mechanisation, women’s role in agriculture**, and **modernisation of land lease laws**.

### Agricultural Tubewells And Power Subsidy

- The policy recommends **grid connected solar systems for agricultural tubewells** to reduce the government’s long term financial burden.
- According to the Commission, the scheme would need substantial initial investment, but could recover its cost over time and subsequently generate revenue worth **thousands of crores of rupees**.
- The policy links water conservation with Punjab’s agricultural power subsidy burden, which was around **Rs 8,000 crore**.
- It argues that reducing groundwater extraction and promoting water efficient agriculture could eventually reduce the state’s expenditure on **subsidised farm power**.

## The 2026 Committee

- The issue received a fresh push from the **Punjab Vidhan Sabha’s Committee on Agriculture and Allied Activities**, headed by **MLA Gurpreet Singh Banawali**.
- The committee held **16 meetings** before submitting its **fourth report** to the Assembly on **March 12, 2026**.
- At its meeting on **July 8, 2025**, it heard representatives of the Commission and examined the policy’s recommendations.
- The committee **unanimously recommended** implementation of the policy.
- It indicated that the government need not implement every recommendation simultaneously and could proceed in **phases** if full implementation is not possible at once.

## The Hurdles

- Although phased implementation is possible, a **first phase has not been clearly defined**.
- **Finances** are the first constraint: pension, debt settlement, crop insurance, compensation, research, water conservation, and other measures would require substantial public expenditure when Punjab’s finances are already under pressure.
- **Diversification** is politically and economically difficult because farmers have relatively assured procurement for **wheat and paddy**, whereas alternative crops need comparable **price support, markets, and infrastructure**.
- Some recommendations would require support and coordination from the **Centre**.
- The policy requires a clear **implementation architecture** consisting of **budgets, timelines, departmental responsibilities, measurable targets, and accountability mechanisms**.
- The policy explains **what needs to be done**, but the government has yet to specify **how, when, and with what money** it will be done.

## Farmers’ Organisations

- Farmers’ organisations have repeatedly called for implementation of the policy.
- **Bhartiya Kisan Union (Dakuanda)** stressed that Punjab needs a dedicated agricultural policy and that successive governments must prepare a road map and secure funding for **debt settlements, an assured minimum support price, and pension plans** to make the proposals a reality.
