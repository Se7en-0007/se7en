- The **New Delhi Declaration**, adopted at the **18th BRICS Summit**, made **energy security** and the **protection of critical energy infrastructure** key priorities.
- It called for **stable energy markets**, **uninterrupted energy supplies**, and protection of important energy infrastructure.
- The declaration described energy security as a crucial foundation for **social and economic development**, **national security**, and the **welfare of all nations**.
- It stressed the need for:
    
    - **Energy market stability**
    - **Undisrupted flows of energy from diverse sources**
    - Stronger **value chains**
    - Greater **resilience**
    - Protection of **critical energy infrastructure**, including **cross-border infrastructure**

## **West Asia Conflict**

- The ongoing **West Asia conflict** has affected major oil and gas producers that are BRICS members, including **Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates**.
- **US and Israeli forces** attacked Iranian energy infrastructure, while **Iran** and **Tehran-backed non-state actors** targeted **Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and other Gulf countries seen as US allies**.
- Examples include:
    
    - The **US strike on Iranian oil tankers** earlier in the week.
    - A **Tehran-backed Houthi militia attack** that damaged **oil and gas infrastructure in Saudi Arabia**.
- **Russia**, a BRICS founding member, has also suffered damage to its energy infrastructure during its war with **Ukraine**.
- **China and India**, both founding BRICS members, are among the world’s top oil and gas importers. India has repeatedly called for the protection of energy infrastructure and the **unimpeded global flow of energy**.

## **Strait Of Hormuz**

- The West Asia conflict has severely constrained global energy flows, with vessel movements through the **Strait of Hormuz** reduced to **a trickle**.
- Before the war, then in its **seventh month**, the narrow waterway between **Iran and Oman** carried **one-fifth of global crude oil and liquefied natural gas - LNG - flows**.
- The disruption has caused **supply tightness** and **elevated energy prices globally**, particularly affecting countries such as **India**, which depend heavily on energy imports.
- The emphasis on uninterrupted energy flows and protection of critical infrastructure is significant because a large part of the energy crisis is unfolding **within BRICS itself**.

## **Declaration’s Position**

- The declaration does not name any country or refer to any individual attack on oil infrastructure.
- Instead, it reflects the risks confronting global energy markets:
    
    - **Disruption of supply routes**
    - **Attacks on energy installations**
    - **Threats to shipping**
- BRICS members expressed **deep concern** about escalation in the West Asia conflict and called for:
    
    - **Maximum restraint**
    - Avoidance of actions that could further aggravate the situation
    - International cooperation for the smooth flow of **energy**, **international trade**, and **supply chains**
- The declaration urged protection of **civilians and civilian infrastructure** in accordance with the purposes and principles of the **Charter of the United Nations**, including respect for the **sovereignty** and **territorial integrity** of states.
- It encouraged greater efforts through **dialogue and diplomacy** toward a long-term understanding that contributes to lasting **peace, security, and stability** in the region.
- It stressed the need to maintain smooth global flows of **trade, supply chains, and energy**, in accordance with applicable international law.

## **Oil Prices**

- A fresh escalation in the West Asia conflict pushed oil prices above **$100 a barrel**, their highest levels in nearly **three-and-a-half months**.
- The **$100 threshold** was breached on Wednesday.
- **Brent futures** remained above $100 until Friday, closing at about **$105 per barrel**, the highest level since **May**.

## **Dilemma And A Test For BRICS**

- **Saudi Arabia** temporarily shut its **East-West oil pipeline** after multiple attacks on the crucial network on Thursday.
- The East-West pipeline is one of the most important **Hormuz-bypass routes** for exporting Saudi crude.
- Alongside attacks on the pipeline system, **Iran-backed Houthi forces** have tightened their grip around the **Bab el-Mandeb and Red Sea shipping route**, potentially reducing oil flows further, especially from **Saudi Arabia**, the world’s largest exporter.
- Saudi Arabia had relied on the East-West pipeline for oil exports amid the effective halt in shipping through the **Strait of Hormuz**, the main outlet from the **Persian Gulf**.
- **Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, and the UAE** have traditionally relied heavily on the Strait of Hormuz to reach global markets, especially major importers **China and India**.
- Along with continued disruption in the Strait of Hormuz, the widening conflict is placing the other regional chokepoint, **Bab el-Mandeb**, under pressure.

## **Different Sides Of The Conflict**

- The West Asia crisis has produced an internal dilemma for BRICS because some members are on different sides of the conflict.
- **Iran** is an oil producer and a principal party to the conflict.
- **Saudi Arabia and the UAE** are major oil exporters, US allies, and targets of Tehran.
- **China and India** are major energy importers with their own strategic interests and preferences in West Asia.
- Other BRICS members are also growing energy consumers and industrial economies.
- Consequently, the declaration avoids assigning blame for attacks on energy infrastructure or for shipping disruption in the Strait of Hormuz.
- It instead focuses on principles that can be universally agreed upon:
    
    - **Restraint**
    - Protection of **civilian infrastructure**
    - Respect for **sovereignty**
    - **Uninterrupted trade and energy flows**
    - Resilience of **supply chains**
- The declaration creates a basis for cooperation, but its practical ability to safeguard energy infrastructure and normalise energy flows remains uncertain because BRICS’s West Asian members hold opposing positions and stakes in the conflict.
