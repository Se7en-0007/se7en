- **Leaders of 11 BRICS countries** arrived in India for the **18th edition of the summit** to discuss ways to boost the **growth prospects of the Global South**.
- From India’s perspective, the most consequential engagement was expected to be the **bilateral talks between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping**.
- There were signs of a **thaw in India-China ties**:
    
    - **Resumption of direct flights**
    - **Reopening of border trade via Nathu La Pass after six years**
    - **Easing of restrictions on foreign direct investment from land-bordering countries**

## Unpredictable American Trade Policies

- **Unpredictable American trade policies** and **two ongoing wars that have rattled oil and bond markets** appear to have nudged India and China towards easing tensions.
- The timing of the bilateral engagement is significant because the rise of **aggressive industrial policies globally** has placed pressure on **Global South nations**.
- International trade experts see cooperation between **Asia’s two largest economies** as **strategically necessary** in this environment.

## Trade Cooperation

- India and China have an important trade relationship, but India faces a **large bilateral trade deficit with China**.
- The two countries have sought to improve economic and commercial ties amid global tariff uncertainty, including through discussion of **expanded trade and investment**.
- India’s interest in trade cooperation is shaped by the need to navigate a global environment marked by **tariffs, sanctions, supply-chain pressures, and market disruptions**.

## China’s Dominance Poses A Challenge

- The principal challenge for India is not merely restoring bilateral trade, but **competing with Chinese goods in overseas markets**.
- China’s industrial and export dominance poses a challenge for India because both countries seek space in markets across the **Global South**.
- While greater India-China cooperation may be strategically necessary amid global economic pressures, India must contend with the fact that China has **far outpaced India economically** and maintains a large trade surplus with it.

## Limits To A Reset

- The bilateral relationship continues to face deep constraints:
    
    - **Disputed border issues**
    - **India’s large trade deficit with China**
    - **Restrictions on Chinese investment and technology**
    - **Competing strategic interests across Asia**
- The cautious thaw follows the **2020 border clash**, which damaged relations and led to a prolonged military standoff.
- The key question is whether the recent improvement in engagement can become a **broader stabilisation of ties**, despite persistent strategic mistrust and economic asymmetry.
