- The **45-page BRICS declaration** achieved consensus through **careful diplomatic calibration**, **nuancing the most divisive issues** and enabling every participant to have its **stated goals acknowledged** while avoiding its **triggers**.
- Its **140 paragraphs** focused extensively on **trade, health, AI and tourism**, making the document **cooperative rather than confrontational**.
- On several issues, it **echoed the 2025 Rio declaration**, including **Gaza**, the **Pahalgam terror attack**, and **UNSC reforms**.

## Trade And Tariff

- The declaration avoided naming **Trump**, but repeatedly referred to **trade** and **tariff**.
- All participants have faced **US tariffs**, while **Russia** and **Iran** have repeatedly confronted **US economic sanctions**.
- **Paragraph 22** condemns **“unilateral coercive measures”**, **“unilateral economic sanctions and secondary sanctions”** - standard BRICS shorthand for **Western sanctions regimes**.
- **Paragraph 27** describes **Latin America and the Caribbean** as a **zone of peace** and refers to the **“tightening of unilateral blockade measures”** on **Cuba** - an unnamed but pointed reference to the **US embargo**.
- The declaration highlights the **“proliferation of trade-restrictive actions”** and **“indiscriminate rising of tariffs”**, an oblique nod to **Trump-era tariff policy**.
- It opposes **“carbon border adjustment mechanisms”**, targeting **US/EU trade-climate measures**.
- The **2025 Rio declaration** had only expressed concern that the **multilateral trading system** was at a crossroads and about rising **unilateral tariff and non-tariff measures** that distort trade and are inconsistent with **WTO rules**.
- **Paragraph 20** acknowledges **China** for expanding **zero-tariff treatment** to **53 African countries** with which it has diplomatic ties, portraying China as a benefactor to the **Global South** amid unilateral tariffs and sanctions.

## No Ukraine

- The **Delhi 2026 declaration** contains **no mention of Ukraine**.
- In contrast, the **Rio 2025 declaration** had an anodyne paragraph recalling members’ **national positions concerning the conflict in Ukraine**, as expressed in appropriate fora including the **UN Security Council** and **UN General Assembly**.
- With **Russia** at the high table as India’s **long-time partner**, any reference to Ukraine was avoided.
- No paragraph in the **45-page declaration** mentions **Ukraine**, **Russia’s invasion**, or related sanctions by name.

## Terrorism

- BRICS leaders reached consensus on terrorism in **Delhi 2026**, using language similar to the **2025 Rio declaration**.
- They expressed **strong condemnation** of all acts of terrorism as **criminal and unjustifiable**, regardless of motivation and wherever or by whomsoever committed.
- They condemned in the strongest terms the terrorist attack in **Jammu and Kashmir on 22 April 2025**, in which **26 people were killed** and **many more injured**.

## Iran-UAE Balance

- The declaration reflects consensus between **Iran** and the **UAE** despite tensions over the West Asia war.
- It expresses concern over continuing tensions and calls for **maximum restraint**, avoidance of further escalation, protection of **civilians and civilian infrastructure**, and respect for **sovereignty and territorial integrity**.
- It urges **dialogue and diplomacy** toward a **long-term understanding** for lasting **peace, security and stability** in the region.
- It stresses working together to maintain the **smooth flow of global trade, supply chains and energy**, in accordance with applicable international law.
- **Paragraph 20** strongly supports **Iran’s bid for accession to the WTO**, alongside **Ethiopia’s**.
- The declaration flags **deliberate attacks on civilian infrastructure and peaceful nuclear facilities under full safeguards of the IAEA** as violations of international law - a reference to the **June 2025 Israeli/US strikes on Iranian nuclear sites**.

## Gaza And Lebanon

- As in the **2025 Rio declaration**, the **Israel-Gaza conflict**, which began in **October 2023** with the Hamas terror attack in Israel, is named directly.
- The Delhi 2026 declaration cites **UNSC Resolution 2803**, opposes the **forced displacement of Palestinians**, and criticises arrangements that may **legitimise or prolong occupation**.
- It refers to the **International Court of Justice case** brought by **South Africa against Israel**, reaffirming Israel’s **legal obligation** to ensure **humanitarian aid**.
- On **Lebanon**, it calls on Israel to respect agreed terms and to **withdraw its occupying forces**.
- This Lebanon formulation is the **same as in 2025**.
