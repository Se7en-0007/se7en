- **BRICS adopted the New Delhi Declaration on September 12**, setting out common positions on **trade, terrorism, artificial intelligence, institutional reform, cross-border payments and investment**.
- Against **tariffs, trade tensions and geopolitical fragmentation**, the grouping opposed **unilateral trade restrictions** and sought to ease **cross-border payments and investments** among BRICS countries.
- The declaration included India-specific outcomes on **cross-border terrorism**, **digital public infrastructure**, **health** and **finance**.
- Achieving consensus was significant because BRICS foreign ministers had failed to issue a joint statement in **May** amid differences between **Iran and the UAE**. The final language addressed wars and tensions across **West Asia** without directly assigning responsibility to individual countries.

## **Pushback Against Unilateral Tariffs And Restrictive Trade Measures**

- Since early **2025**, US President **Donald Trump** announced escalating tariffs against several US trading partners to extract concessions and correct a perceived imbalance and “return” money to the US.
- The declaration condemned **“unilateral coercive measures”** as contrary to **international law** and the **United Nations Charter**, and called for their elimination.
- Without naming the US president, BRICS acknowledged that its members are affected differently by **“unjustified unilateral protectionist measures”** inconsistent with **WTO rules**.
- BRICS called for a **just and fair, stable and predictable environment** for mutually beneficial sustainable development, positioning itself as a collective counterweight to growing trade and financial restrictions by major Western economies.
- The language stopped short of announcing a **retaliatory trade mechanism**.
- It explicitly opposed **carbon border adjustment mechanisms** that are **unilateral, punitive, discriminatory or protectionist**.

## **Strong Condemnation Of Terrorism, Especially Pahalgam Attack**

- The declaration reaffirmed commitment to combating terrorism in **“all its forms and manifestations”**.
- It condemned in the **“strongest terms”** the **April 2025 terrorist attack in Pahalgam, Jammu and Kashmir**, in which **26 people were killed** and several others injured.
- It singled out the **cross-border movement of terrorists**, **terrorism financing** and **safe havens**, expressed **zero tolerance for terrorism**, and rejected **double standards** in countering terrorism.
- The wording was notable because BRICS operates through consensus and includes **China**, whose position on Pakistan-based terrorists at the UN has at times differed from India’s.
- The declaration reiterated that terrorism must not be associated with any specific **religion, nationality, civilisation or ethnic group**.
- It called for closer international counter-terrorism cooperation and acknowledged cooperation through the **BRICS Counter-Terrorism Working Group (CTWG)**.
- It identified **extremism and radicalisation among younger generations** as a specific issue requiring attention.

## **Common Ground On The War In West Asia**

- The presence of **Iran and the United Arab Emirates**, on competing sides of the war in West Asia, made consensus difficult.
- The declaration expressed **“deep concern”** over escalating tensions across West Asia, called for **maximum restraint**, and sought resolution through **dialogue and diplomacy**.
- Without assigning responsibility to individual countries, it condemned attacks on **civilian infrastructure** and **safeguarded nuclear facilities**.
- It stressed uninterrupted **trade, supply chains and energy flows**.
- It expressed grave concern over the humanitarian situation in **Gaza** and the **Occupied Palestinian Territory**.
- It called for maintaining the **Gaza ceasefire**, ensuring **unhindered humanitarian access**, and opposed the **forced displacement of Palestinians**.
- It condemned violations of international human rights law, including:
    
    - **Use of starvation as a method of warfare**
    - **Attacks targeting civilians and civilian objects**
    - Attacks on **healthcare facilities** and other civilian infrastructure
    - **Unlawful obstruction of humanitarian access**
- BRICS affirmed support for **UNRWA - United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East**.
- It noted the **International Court of Justice’s provisional measures** in South Africa’s case against Israel, which reaffirmed Israel’s legal obligation to ensure humanitarian aid in Gaza.
- The declaration reiterated support for a **two-state solution**, with a Palestinian state within the **1967 borders** and **East Jerusalem as its capital**.
- It called on Israel to adhere to the **Lebanon ceasefire**, withdraw from remaining Lebanese territory, and supported an **inclusive political transition in Syria**.

## **Local Currencies, But No Common BRICS Currency**

- The declaration did not address a **unified BRICS currency**, despite longstanding speculation.
- BRICS instead focused on facilitating **trade settlements and investments in members’ own currencies** for **faster, cheaper and safer cross-border payments**.
- It acknowledged discussions on promoting **BRICS local currencies**, while respecting national priorities and recognising that there is **“no one-size-fits-all approach”**.
- The **BRICS Payment Task Force**, comprising central bank experts, has been studying how members’ payment and messaging systems can work more easily with one another.
- Its stated aim is to make cross-border payments among BRICS countries **faster, cheaper, more accessible and safer**.
- The declaration encouraged the **New Development Bank** to:
    
    - Expand **local-currency financing**
    - Diversify its funding sources
    - Mobilise resources for development and infrastructure projects
- BRICS will continue technical work on the proposed **New Investment Platform**, following a **consensus-based, phased approach** rather than launching a new investment mechanism at the summit.
- Members expressed interest in India’s proposal for a **BRICS Risk Lab** at the **GIFT City International Financial Services Centre in Gujarat**.

## **A Global South Position On AI**

- The declaration viewed **artificial intelligence** as an economic and governance opportunity for the **Global South**.
- It called for wider access to AI resources while emphasising **safety, security, inclusiveness, reliability, energy efficiency and “trustworthy AI”**.
- It congratulated India for hosting the **AI Impact Summit in February 2026**.
- Members committed to implementing the **BRICS Leaders’ Statement on the Global Governance of Artificial Intelligence**.

## **The Indian Imprint On The Summit**

- India prioritised initiatives relating to **digital infrastructure, health and development cooperation**.
- On **digital public infrastructure**, BRICS noted a proposal for a **BRICS repository for digital public infrastructure (DPI)** and pilot projects using DPI solutions across member countries.
- On health, the declaration supported:
    
    - A **BRICS Training Hub Network**
    - A network of **Centres of Excellence on mental well-being**
    - **Bengaluru-based NIMHANS** as the coordinating centre
- In industry, BRICS welcomed the **India Centre for BRICS Industrial Competencies** to help manufacturing firms and small businesses adopt **Industry 4.0** and advanced digital technologies.
- **Industry 4.0** refers to the integration of **technology and automation with manufacturing**.
- BRICS also welcomed:
    
    - A new **Task Force on Growth and Development**
    - The **BRICS-New Development Bank Knowledge Portal** to share policy experiences and lessons from development projects across member countries
