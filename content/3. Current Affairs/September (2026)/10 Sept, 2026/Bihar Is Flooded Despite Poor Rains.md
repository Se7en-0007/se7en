- **Between June 1 and September 8, Bihar received 601.1 mm of rainfall, 27% below normal.**
- **As late as September 1, the rainfall deficit stood at 30%.**
- Despite this deficit, **rivers rose sharply** and the **Ganga crossed its previous highest flood level (HFL) at Patna and Bhagalpur**.
- At **Gandhi Ghat, Patna**, the Ganga reached **50.58 metres**, which was **6 cm above** the previous HFL of **50.52 metres recorded in 2016**.
- At **Sultanganj, Bhagalpur**, it touched **36.80 metres**, **5 cm above** the earlier HFL of **36.75 metres set in 2021**.
- By **September 9**, floods had affected **84 circles, 518 panchayats and 2,157 villages across 14 districts**, affecting about **40.51 lakh people**.

## Nepal Disaster, Gandak And Ganga’s Unusual Flood Sequence

- Normally, rivers entering Bihar from Nepal - **Bagmati, Kamla, Kosi and Gandak** - rise first, followed by the **Ganga**.
- **This time, the Ganga became the main source of concern first.**
- The Ganga’s catchment extends far beyond Bihar and receives water generated by **upstream rainfall, including from Uttar Pradesh**.
- **Rainfall around the Allahabad and Varanasi region** and the resulting **downstream discharge** were important causes of the Ganga’s rise.
- For other rivers as well, **discharge from neighbouring states** was a major factor.
- Bihar also received **heavy, localised rainfall** when flooding worsened, including individual rain events of **214.92 mm in East Champaran** and **154.55 mm in Sitamarhi**.
- While cumulative rainfall remained **27% below normal up to September 8**, rainfall during **September itself was 31% above normal**.
- On **August 26**, a **glacial lake outburst in Tibet** caused severe flooding in Nepal.
- The Bihar Water Resources Department opened **all 36 gates of the Valmikinagar Barrage on Gandak**, although the river was below its normal levels.
- Gandak’s discharge at the barrage reached **1,50,200 cusecs at 11 pm on August 26**, lower than officials had expected, and then declined.
- **Flooding nevertheless continued.**
- The unusually high Ganga produced a **“backwater effect”** for rivers such as the **Gandak and Punpun**.
- As tributaries must drain into the Ganga, their drainage became difficult once the Ganga swelled.
- The **Punpun** was also affected by **heavy rainfall in Jharkhand**.

## The River Network Explains The Spread

- The **Ganga enters Bihar at Buxar** and flows eastward through a large floodplain covering **Bhojpur, Patna, Vaishali, Saran, Begusarai, Lakhisarai, Munger, Bhagalpur and Katihar**.
- North Bihar’s major rivers - **Gandak, Kosi, Bagmati and Burhi Gandak** - descend from **Himalayan and Nepal catchments** into the state’s **extremely flat plains**.
- The **Kosi** remained above the danger mark at **Baltara in Khagaria** and **Kursela in Katihar**.
- The **Gandak** was above the danger mark at **Dumaria Ghat and Hajipur**.
- The **Burhi Gandak** was above the danger mark at **Khagaria**.
- The **Bagmati** was above the danger mark at **Benibad**.
- The **Punpun** was above the danger mark at **Sripalpur**.
- The **Ghaghra** was above the danger mark at **Darouli and Gangpur Siswan**.
- The **Ganga** itself remained above the danger mark at **Digha, Gandhi Ghat and Hathidah in Patna**, as well as at downstream gauges in **Munger, Bhagalpur and Kahalgaon**.
- A river flood is a **moving event**: the peak at one gauge can be transferred downstream, affecting a much larger area than locations that received the heaviest rainfall.

## Embankments Can Hold And Flooding Can Still Occur

- Bihar has built **more than 3,730 km of river embankments**, which have historically protected around **3,600 sq km of land**.
- Flooding still affected low-lying areas through **seepage, local damage, overtopping of older structures and drainage problems**.
- The department stated that **no major modern scientific embankment suffered a large breach** during the episode.
- However, embankments do not remove the underlying flood vulnerability of one of India’s **flattest, most sediment-heavy alluvial landscapes**.
- The **Ganga, Gandak, Kosi, Bagmati and other rivers** carry enormous quantities of sediment.
- When sediment accumulates in channels over years, the **riverbed can rise relative to the surrounding land**.
- **Silt is a “permanent issue” for Bihar’s rivers.**
- **Sediment accumulation** and **downstream hydraulic conditions around Farakka** could potentially influence Ganga levels and backwater conditions.

## Farakka Barrage

- State leaders contend that, over the **five decades since its construction**, the **Farakka Barrage** has trapped massive quantities of silt along the Ganga.
- This sediment accumulation has made the riverbed **dramatically shallower**.
- Consequently, even normal seasonal monsoon flows can spill over the banks and trigger **severe annual flooding across Bihar’s plains**.
- As the **1996 India-Bangladesh Farakka Treaty** approaches expiry in **December 2026**, Bihar’s political corridors are witnessing demands to **review and scrap the pact**.

## Diara Lands

- The **Ganga, Gandak and Kosi** constantly reshape their channels and create **diara lands** - shifting riverine islands and low-lying tracts.
- These lands are cultivated and inhabited but remain **highly vulnerable to rising water**.
- Bihar’s **high population density** means that many communities live close to rivers, while thousands live on these dynamic lands.
- When river levels rise, people may have to be evacuated even when the main embankment remains intact.
