- **Copper prices surged to a record high of $14,708 per tonne on Tuesday, September 8**, despite global growth prospects being clouded by **trade tensions, the West Asia conflict and uncertainty**.
- **Three-month copper futures** on the **London Metal Exchange (LME)** stayed above **$14,000 per tonne for most of August** and climbed further in September.
- The rally followed a volatile phase: copper crossed **$12,000 per tonne in December last year**, marking its **biggest annual gain since 2009**, before easing to **$11,929.5 per tonne in March**.
- The March decline reflected concerns that **higher energy costs stemming from the West Asia conflict** could hurt global growth and weaken demand for industrial commodities.

## Why Copper Price Rise Matters

- Copper is essential to the modern economy, with uses in **housing, manufacturing, power grids, clean energy, artificial intelligence and defence**.
- Its price is often viewed as a **barometer of economic health**: rising prices usually signal **robust economic growth**, while falling prices raise concerns about an **economic slowdown**.
- However, the current rally is driven less by expectations of stronger global growth and more by concern over **potential US tariffs**, which could take effect from **January 2027** and are already influencing **copper trade flows and inventories**.

## US-Led Factors

- US President **Donald Trump** imposed a **50% tariff on semi-finished and derivative copper imports in August last year**.
- **Refined copper** has so far been exempt from these duties, but a proposal would impose a **15% tariff on refined copper imports from January**, rising to **30% in 2028**.
- In anticipation of US tariffs, material is being moved from **LME warehouses** to **US COMEX - Commodity Exchange - warehouses**.
- This movement has reduced copper stocks available for trading on the LME, pushing up **futures prices**.

## Inventory Buildup And Arbitrage

- Inventories have accumulated heavily in the United States:
    
    - **LME inventory: 265,000 tonnes**
    - **Shanghai Futures Exchange - SHFE - inventory: around 63,000 tonnes**
    - **US COMEX inventory: around 700,000 tonnes**
- The disparity in inventories has created **arbitrage opportunities**.
- An **arbitrage opportunity** occurs when the same commodity is priced differently in two markets: traders buy copper in the cheaper market and simultaneously sell it in the more expensive market to profit from the price gap.
- Copper is traded across multiple exchanges, including the **LME in London** and **COMEX in the US**.
- The current arbitrage difference between LME and COMEX is around **$400-$500**.
- However, **arbitrage is only one factor** behind the recent rise. The more significant driver is **stockpiling in anticipation of tariffs**, which has pushed up prices.

## Constrained Mine Supply And Geographical Rebalancing

- Copper prices have also remained elevated due to **constrained mine supply** and the ongoing **geographical rebalancing of inventories**.
- **COMEX stocks rose sharply** because refined copper was **front-loaded in the US market amid tariff uncertainty**.
- Relatively low inventories on the **London and Shanghai exchanges** indicate tighter copper availability outside the US.
- These factors could keep copper prices elevated in the short term.
- However, a **major delay in US tariffs** or a tariff lower than expected could reverse inventory flows, release accumulated stocks into global markets and put downward pressure on **copper prices** and **upstream producers’ profitability**.

## Copper In US-China Trade War

- Last year, Trump’s **50% tariff on semi-finished and derivative copper imports** prompted US buyers to stockpile copper before its implementation on **August 1**, contributing heavily to the price surge.
- The move followed a report by the **US Secretary of Commerce**, which found that copper imports threatened to impair **US national security**.
- In **2025**, the United States imported **$16.2 billion worth of copper products**.
- Sources of US copper-product imports were:
    
    - **China: 20%**
    - **Mexico: 17%**
    - **European Union: 14%**
    - **Canada: 10%**
    - **Vietnam: 8%**
- In April that year, Trump announced:
    
    - A **50% tariff on the full value of semi-finished copper products**
    - A **25% tariff on the full value of copper-intensive derivative products**
    - A reduced **10% tariff** for derivative products where at least **95% of the copper, steel and aluminium** used was sourced from the US
- These tariff measures form part of broader **US-China trade tensions**. **China is the world’s largest consumer of copper.**
