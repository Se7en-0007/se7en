# Evolution, Cooperation And India's Leadership

- **BRICS has evolved from a four-country dialogue into a geopolitical bloc spanning four continents.**
- **India is chairing BRICS for the fourth time since its inception.**
- Successive membership expansions brought in **oil states, African economies and Southeast Asian partners**.

## BRICS: Shaping An Inclusive Global Order

- **BRICS is a key platform for coordinating economic policy among major emerging economies** and serves as their principal forum for shaping a common position on global governance.
- It advances the interests of the **Global South** within multilateral institutions through sustained dialogue.
- Its agenda covers **development, finance, technology, trade and people-to-people exchanges**.
- **The grouping reflects the growing economic weight and aspirations of emerging economies globally.**
- It enables members to share experiences and address common challenges.

## 2026 Presidency

- Under its **2026 Presidency**, India will host the **18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi on 12-13 September 2026**.
- India’s Chairship theme is **“Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability.”**
- The theme focuses on strengthening existing achievements and institutional effectiveness, while placing **development, sustainability and innovation** at the centre of the agenda.
- It highlights the aspirations of the **Global South** and seeks to reinforce BRICS as a **constructive and solutions-oriented forum**.

## BRICS Membership

- BRICS comprises **11 countries**:
    
    - **Brazil**
    - **China**
    - **Egypt**
    - **Ethiopia**
    - **India**
    - **Indonesia**
    - **Iran**
    - **Russia**
    - **Saudi Arabia**
    - **South Africa**
    - **UAE**
- Collectively, BRICS members represent:
    
    - **49.5% of the global population**
    - **40% of global GDP**
    - **26% of global trade**
- Expanded membership has widened BRICS’ geographical reach across **Asia, Africa, the Middle East and Latin America**.
- **This diversity brings together major markets, natural resources, technological capabilities and varied development experiences.**

## The Origin

- The acronym **‘BRIC’ was coined in 2001 by Goldman Sachs** in its Global Economics Paper, **‘The World Needs Better Economic BRICs.’**
- Based on econometric analysis, the paper projected that **Brazil, Russia, India and China** would be among the world’s largest economies over the following **five decades**.
- Their growing economic weight and vast geographic reach across **three continents** highlighted their emerging global significance.
- **South Africa joined in 2010**, transforming **BRIC into BRICS**.
- This marked the transition from an economic grouping identified by analysts to a formal platform for **political, economic and development cooperation**.

## The Aim, Objective And Areas Of Cooperation

- BRICS was initially conceived as a forum for dialogue on major issues shaping the international agenda.
- Its early focus was developing common positions on global political and economic matters and promoting a **more democratic, legitimate and balanced international order**.
- **The grouping emphasised sustainable economic and social development, social inclusion and shared prosperity among emerging economies.**
- Across successive summits, cooperation expanded to strategic global issues and practical collaboration.
- BRICS’ agenda covers:
    
    - **Terrorism**
    - **Climate change**
    - **Food and energy security**
    - **Trade and agriculture**
    - **Labour and employment**
    - **Telecommunications**
    - **International finance**
    - **Global economic situation**
- **BRICS advocates greater representation and equity in international institutions, including the WTO and global financial architecture.**
- These efforts aim to make global governance **more inclusive, effective and responsive to contemporary challenges**.

## BRICS Through The Years: From Crisis To Collective Action

- The **2008 global financial crisis** was a defining moment in BRICS’ evolution, exposing gaps in the existing global financial architecture.
- The four BRIC countries called for:
    
    - A **more representative international financial system**
    - Greater voice for emerging economies in the **IMF** and **World Bank**
    - Opposition to **protectionism**
    - An **open, rules-based multilateral trading system**
- This coordination laid the foundation for the **first BRIC Summit in Yekaterinburg, Russia, in 2009**.
- **BRIC was formally established in 2006** as a political cooperation forum among **Brazil, Russia, India and China**.
- **South Africa became a full member in 2011**, transforming **BRIC into BRICS** and expanding its geographical representation across continents.
- The grouping broadened its agenda beyond economic issues to include **development, finance, health, climate action, technology and trade**.

## A Wider BRICS: Expanding Reach, Deepening Engagement

- A major expansion began in **January 2024**, when **Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the UAE** became full members.
- **Indonesia joined as the eleventh full member in January 2025.**
- The expansion significantly increased BRICS’ geographical and economic reach.

### Partner Country Category

- A **Partner Country category** was created alongside membership expansion.
- **Ten countries joined this framework during 2025**:
    
    - **Belarus**
    - **Bolivia**
    - **Cuba**
    - **Kazakhstan**
    - **Malaysia**
    - **Nigeria**
    - **Thailand**
    - **Uganda**
    - **Uzbekistan**
    - **Vietnam**
- The framework provides an additional channel for structured engagement without altering the full-membership structure.
- It strengthens opportunities for **South-South cooperation** across **Asia, Africa, Latin America and Eurasia**.
- It creates scope for collaboration on **trade, finance, climate action and sustainable development**.
- **It reflects growing interest among emerging economies in participating in discussions on global governance.**

## BRICS Presidency: Rotation And Responsibilities

- The BRICS Presidency rotates annually according to the letters of its acronym.
- Each Presidency runs from **1 January to 31 December** of the respective year.
- The Presidency-holding country:
    
    - Sets the annual agenda priorities
    - Organises the annual Summit
    - Steers key ministerial and working group meetings
- With BRICS’ recent expansion, member countries are discussing a **new rotation formula** for the Presidency.

## India At The Helm Of BRICS: From Continuity To Consensus

- India previously chaired BRICS in **2012, 2016 and 2021**.
- **India’s repeated leadership reflects its sustained engagement, commitment to constructive multilateralism and the priorities of developing economies.**

## 2012: Laying The Foundation For Stronger Cooperation

- India hosted the **Fourth BRICS Summit** in **New Delhi on 29 March 2012**.
- The theme was **“BRICS Partnership for Global Stability, Security and Prosperity.”**
- Discussions focused on **global governance reform, sustainable development, food security and energy security**.
- The Summit adopted the **Delhi Declaration** and an **Action Plan for strengthening BRICS cooperation**.

### Major Outcomes

- The Summit proposed exploring a **new Development Bank** to mobilise resources for infrastructure and sustainable development projects in BRICS, emerging economies and developing countries.
- The proposed institution was intended to complement existing multilateral and regional financial institutions supporting global growth and development.
- Member nations signed the **Master Agreement on Extending Credit Facility in Local Currency** to boost intra-BRICS trade.
- The Summit laid the foundation for the **Contingent Reserve Arrangement**, a financial safety mechanism providing short-term liquidity support and helping members address financial instability.
- India institutionalised joint tracks for practical cooperation in **agriculture, health and science**.

## 2016: Goa Summit Expanded BRICS Outreach

- India hosted the **Eighth BRICS Summit** in **Goa in October 2016**.
- The theme was **“Building Responsive, Inclusive and Collective Solutions.”**
- India’s Chairship focused on strengthening intra-BRICS cooperation and expanding outreach.
- It advanced collaboration on **counter-terrorism, trade, innovation and multilateral coordination**.

### Major Outcome

- The operationalisation of the **New Development Bank** was solidified through its first set of financial loans, primarily supporting **renewable energy projects**.
- BRICS elevated **renewable energy access, clean energy transition and energy security** as shared priorities.
- Greater focus was placed on combating **HIV and tuberculosis**.
- India secured stronger BRICS language on combating terrorism and called for adherence to **FATF - Financial Action Task Force - standards**.
- The **BIMSTEC Outreach Summit** expanded engagement between BRICS and countries of the wider Bay of Bengal region.
- BIMSTEC Outreach discussions covered **counter-terrorism, trade, energy, investment, environment, technology and infrastructure**.
- Agreement was reached to establish the **BRICS Agriculture Research Platform, BRICS Railway Research Network, BRICS Sports Council** and youth-centric cooperation forums.

## 2021: Consolidating Cooperation In A Changing World

- India hosted the **13th BRICS Summit virtually on 9 September 2021**.
- The Summit marked the **15th anniversary of BRICS**.
- The theme was **“BRICS@15: Intra-BRICS Cooperation for Continuity, Consolidation and Consensus.”**
- India focused on institutional continuity, deepened cooperation and greater consensus across **health, digital technologies, counter-terrorism and sustainable development**.

### Major Outcomes

- India led the adoption of a unified **BRICS Counter-Terrorism Action Plan**.
- India hosted the **first-ever BRICS Digital Health Summit**.
- A **BRICS Vaccine Research and Development Center** was launched to address current and future pandemic responses collectively.
- Agreement was reached on cooperation for the **BRICS Remote Sensing Satellite Constellation**.
- The constellation enables members to share remote-sensing data for **environmental protection, disaster management and climate monitoring**.
- India introduced the **BRICS Alliance for Green Tourism** and the first **BRICS Water Ministers Meeting** for sustainable resource management.

## BRICS 2026: Key Outcomes Emerging From Ministerial Meetings

- India assumed its **fourth BRICS Chairship on 1 January 2026**.
- India convened ministerial meetings across BRICS priority areas, advancing cooperation through three fundamental pillars:
    
    - **Political and security cooperation**
    - **Economic and financial cooperation**
    - **Cultural and people-to-people exchanges**
- More than **350 meetings and high-level engagements** were held across **25 cities** during India’s Chairship.
- The meetings produced practical and development-oriented outcomes across key areas.

### Agriculture

- **BRICS Centres of Excellence on Agro-Ecology** were agreed upon for joint research, knowledge sharing and capacity building in **natural, organic and regenerative farming practices** to promote climate-resilient and sustainable agriculture.
- They are initially coordinated by **ICAR-Indian Institute of Farming Systems Research, Modipuram**.
- A **BRICS Network on Digital Agriculture**, initially coordinated by **IIT Delhi**, will deepen cooperation and scale **AI-driven decision-support systems, geospatial intelligence, IoT-enabled monitoring and advanced data analytics** in agriculture.
- **BRICS AGRIN - Agro-Inputs, Genetic Resources and Information Network** was agreed upon as a voluntary, farmer-centric framework for cooperation in **seeds, germplasm and agricultural inputs**.

### Health

- The **BRICS Mission for Healthy Lifestyle** was launched with the **BRICS Roadmap for a Joint Initiative on Healthy Lifestyle Promotion 2026-2029**.
- It promotes technical cooperation, knowledge exchange and coordinated awareness initiatives, with preventive and life-course approaches to **obesity and other non-communicable diseases**.
- The **BRICS Expert Working Group on Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine** will support policy dialogue, research and evidence generation, capacity building, academic exchange, digital knowledge platforms, quality and regulatory standards, and protection of **traditional and indigenous knowledge**.
- **Focus on Mental Health** will operate through the **BRICS Training Hub Network** and **BRICS Network of Centres of Excellence**, with **NIMHANS India** as coordinating centre.
- It aims to optimise mental-health human resources through **upskilling, task-sharing and standardised training**, and integrate mental well-being into primary healthcare.

### Traditional Knowledge And Sustainable Lifestyles

- Traditional knowledge is being mainstreamed through:
    
    - **BRICS Principles for Advancing Climate Resilience through People-centric and Community-based Adaptation**
    - **BRICS Guiding Principles on Strengthening Academic Collaboration on Traditional and Indigenous Knowledge Systems**
- Sustainable lifestyles will be advanced through the **Network of BRICS Institutions to Advance Knowledge Sharing, Research & Innovation** and **Sustainable Lifestyles Week**.
- The initiatives will facilitate knowledge exchange on policies, programmes, initiatives and leading practices; capacity-building workshops; stakeholder dialogue; and compilation of case studies.

### Skilling And Workforce

- **BRICS CONNECT for Skilling** is a cooperation network for **Capacity Building, Employability, and New Skills and Technologies**.
- Built on the **BRICS Virtual Liaison Office**, it has four components:
    
    - Capacity building for advancing **social security**
    - **Women’s participation in the workforce**
    - **Skills and employability**
    - **Digital public infrastructure**
- Cooperation under BRICS CONNECT is **voluntary and non-binding**.

### Energy And Urban Infrastructure

- The **BRICS Guiding Principles on Energy Storage and Smart Grids** were adopted.
- The **BRICS Digital Centre of Excellence for Smart Grids and Energy Storage** was launched for knowledge sharing, capacity building, policy and regulatory best-practice exchange, and pilot initiatives.
- Urban infrastructure was strengthened through:
    
    - The **BRICS Urban Mobility Hub** for sustainable urban-mobility knowledge sharing, capacity building and technical cooperation
    - **BRICS Voluntary Principles for Climate Resilient Urban Infrastructure**
    - The **BRICS Urban Research and Knowledge Network** for applied urban research, cross-border peer learning and sustained institutional collaboration

### MSMEs, Start-Ups And Science

- The **BRICS MSME Cooperation Portal** will function as a digital ecosystem for MSMEs, technology centres and clusters, trade associations, financial institutions, training and capacity-building institutions, policymakers and other stakeholders.
- An institutionalised **Dialogue on MSME Finance** and a **Workplan on the Internationalisation of MSMEs** aim to improve MSMEs’ access to markets and finance.
- The **BRICS Incubator Network** will connect National Nodal Agencies, selected incubators and start-ups, facilitate registration and links with participating incubators, and enable qualified start-ups to access incubators in other BRICS countries.
- The **BRICS Startup Innovation Fund** will catalyse financing for early- and growth-stage start-ups and foster innovation-driven entrepreneurship.
- The **BRICS Science and Research Repository** was welcomed as a structured platform for scientific knowledge storage and sharing.

### Trade, Logistics And Standards

- The **BRICS Logistics Supply-Chain Cooperation Framework** is a voluntary, consensus-based framework to strengthen cross-sector transport-logistics resilience and sustainability.
- It addresses fragmented supply chains and weak connectivity through technical exchange, knowledge-sharing on cargo and shipping services, localised assembly and manufacturing ecosystems, capacity building, and environmentally sustainable and climate-resilient logistics practices.
- The **GVC Action Plan 2026-2030**, including a **Technical Council** and **GVC Joint Study**, seeks resilient, efficient and productive global value chains, market opening and economic diversification among members.
- Members confirmed in-principle approval and willingness to sign the **BRICS Agreement on Cooperation and Mutual Administrative Assistance in Customs Matters**.
- The Agreement supports faster trade, lower transaction costs, secure borders, trusted trade facilitation, improved ease of doing business and action against illicit trade.
- An MoU signed by BRICS National Standards Body Heads created a framework for collaboration on the development, adoption and implementation of standards.
- It promotes information-sharing, exchange of best practices, technical cooperation, capacity building, quality infrastructure, consumer protection and reduction of technical barriers to trade.

### Youth And Sport Technology

- The **BRICS Youth Cooperation Framework 2026** was adopted to support continuity, coherence and structured cooperation under the BRICS Youth Track.
- A new Working Group institutionalised **sport technology cooperation** through exchange of good practices, institutional experiences, technical information and research inputs.

## 2026: Steering BRICS Towards A More Inclusive Future

- India’s **2026 BRICS Chairship** occurs amid **economic fragmentation, technological disruption, geopolitical uncertainty, climate change and resource pressures**.
- **These interconnected challenges require cooperation that is inclusive, practical and responsive to diverse national circumstances.**
- BRICS provides a forum for dialogue and coordinated action on **development, climate action, technology, energy and financial resilience**, while contributing to discussion on reforms of global institutions.
- **India’s objective is to convert greater representation into meaningful cooperation and tangible outcomes.**
- India seeks to build consensus among diverse members, strengthen existing mechanisms and reinforce BRICS as a **constructive force for a more inclusive global order**.
