- The **Centre** has offered to add a new constitutional provision, **Article 371(K)**, to extend **additional protections to Ladakh** following a meeting between the **Ministry of Home Affairs**, the **Leh Apex Body**, and the **Kargil Democratic Alliance**.
- The proposed arrangement would create a **directly elected governing body** with **legislative powers** over **land, culture and language, forests, environment, and natural resources**.
- There is **no draft yet**. Unresolved issues include the proposed body’s **executive, financial and planning powers**, and control over **police and law and order**.
- Further talks are expected in **October**. Ladakh representatives have said the offer remains incomplete without a draft specifying the elected body’s powers.
- The proposal builds on an **“in-principle understanding”** from talks in **May and July** on restoring democracy in Ladakh and creating **tailor-made constitutional safeguards under Article 371**.

## Why Has Ladakh Been Demanding Constitutional Safeguards?

- Ladakh’s demand for greater political and constitutional protection dates to the **reorganisation of Jammu and Kashmir in August 2019**.
- There was initially considerable enthusiasm, particularly in **Leh**, after Ladakh became a separate **Union Territory**. The **Buddhist-dominated Leh district** had long sought UT status, arguing that it was neglected by the erstwhile Jammu and Kashmir government.
- This enthusiasm declined when it became clear that, while the **Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir** would have a legislature, **Ladakh would not**.
- Ladakh had **four MLAs** in the erstwhile **J&K Assembly**. After 2019, its administration was largely led by **bureaucrats**, rather than **elected officials**, deepening political alienation.
- Changes to **domicile rules in Jammu and Kashmir** intensified concerns in Ladakh over **land, employment, demography, and cultural identity**.
- Ladakh currently has **two Hill Councils**, in **Leh** and **Kargil**, but neither is under the **Sixth Schedule** and their powers remain limited.
- These concerns led to demands for **statehood**, inclusion under the **Sixth Schedule**, and greater democratic control over Ladakh’s administration.

## What Is The Sixth Schedule?

- The **Sixth Schedule**, under **Article 244 of the Constitution**, provides for autonomous administrative divisions called **Autonomous District Councils**, or **ADCs**.
- ADCs enjoy a degree of **legislative, judicial and administrative autonomy within a state**.
- They can make laws, rules and regulations on **land, forests, water, agriculture, village councils, health, sanitation, inheritance, social customs, and mining**.
- The Sixth Schedule currently applies to tribal areas in **Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Tripura**.
- In **September 2019**, the **National Commission for Scheduled Tribes** recommended bringing Ladakh under the Sixth Schedule, noting that the new UT was **overwhelmingly tribal** and had a **distinct cultural heritage** requiring protection.
- **No region outside the Northeast** has so far been included under the Sixth Schedule.

## What Protections Does Article 371 Offer?

- **Articles 371 and 371-A through J** contain **“special provisions”** for specific states.
- These provisions differ considerably by state, but broadly seek to protect **local political, cultural, social or economic interests**.
- When the Constitution first came into force, **Article 371** required the creation of **development boards** for certain regions of **Maharashtra and Gujarat**. Further provisions were added as new states were created and particular political arrangements evolved.
- Under **Article 371-A**, laws made by Parliament on **Naga religious or social practices**, **customary law and procedure**, and the **ownership and transfer of land and resources** do not automatically apply to **Nagaland** unless the state Assembly agrees.
- Similar protections apply to **Mizoram** under **Article 371-G**.
- **Articles 371-B and 371-C** provide for special committees within the Legislative Assemblies of **Assam** and **Manipur**.
- **Article 371-F** provides special arrangements for representation in the **Sikkim Assembly**.
- Special provisions also exist for **Arunachal Pradesh** and **Goa**.
- If introduced, **Article 371(K)** would significantly depart from this pattern because **Ladakh is a Union Territory, not a state**.

## How Would Article 371(K) Differ From The Sixth Schedule?

- The **Sixth Schedule** creates **autonomous district-level institutions** with constitutionally defined powers.
- The proposed **Article 371(K)** appears intended to establish a **directly elected governing body at the Union Territory level** with legislative authority over specified subjects.
- It would provide Ladakh with a **separate constitutional arrangement**, rather than merely extending the Sixth Schedule to the Union Territory.
- The exact difference will depend on the wording of the draft.
- The key unresolved questions are whether the elected body will have **executive authority**, control over **budgets and planning**, and powers over **police and law and order**.
- Until these issues are settled, the extent of power that the proposed body would actually exercise remains unclear.
